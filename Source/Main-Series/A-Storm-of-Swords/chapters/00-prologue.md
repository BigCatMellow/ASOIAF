## Major sequence

- **VERIFIED:** The Watch's large ranging occupies the Fist of the First Men while scouts report Mance Rayder's vast host moving down the Milkwater. Officers contemplate striking at the marching wildlings first.
- **VERIFIED:** Chett has organized fourteen mutineers. Their plan is to kill selected leaders and witnesses, free the horses, and desert. He intends to murder Sam, whom he resents for replacing him in Maester Aemon's service.
- **VERIFIED:** Chett's history includes killing a woman who rejected him. His imagined escape to Craster's Keep and control of Craster's women reveal motives of domination, not principled opposition to command.
- **VERIFIED:** The woods fall eerily quiet; the dogs resist the hunt and wild animals seem absent. Chett assumes snow will foil his plan.
- **VERIFIED:** A horn sounds once, then twice, then three times. Chett and Sam recognize the third signal as Others, not ordinary wildling attackers. The novel stops before Chett's immediate outcome.

## Narrow observations often lost in plot summaries

| Detail | Implication / counterweight |
|---|---|
| Mutiny predates the alarm | Military disorder is already underway; supernatural danger is not the sole cause of Watch collapse |
| Mormont's planned offensive | Leadership is not merely waiting defensively; misinformation about actual enemy movement is consequential |
| Absent animal calls | Possible supernatural advance signal, but weather and observer bias remain alternatives |
| Mormont's talking raven | Repeats ominous language around a battle; **not proof** that a named greenseer controls it |
| Small Paul's desire to keep the raven | Humanizes a conspirator and creates a concrete custody question for the bird |
| Chett's grudge against Sam and Jon | Shows how small personal injuries can threaten institutions long before the main conflict |
| Three horn blasts | Direct signal within the Watch's code; Chett's recognition does not identify who blew it or exactly what that person saw |

## Narrative asymmetries

The Watch believes the main threat to be the approaching free-folk army. Readers have already witnessed Others in `AGOT Prologue`. Chett's plans would destroy the Watch from inside even without the supernatural attack.

## Theory intersections

- **Watch facing in the wrong direction:** the chapter supports a major supernatural threat beyond the Wall, but also asks whether the institution correctly understands the free folk; it does not establish an inverse original purpose.
- **Raven as Bloodraven agent:** suggestive only; needs comparison with Bran, the raven's observed limitations, and other explanations.
- **Others and animals:** retain specific behavioral observations with weather, place and POV; avoid retrofitting every unusual silence as magic.
