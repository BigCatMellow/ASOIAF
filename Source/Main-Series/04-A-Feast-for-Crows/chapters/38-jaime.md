## Evidence audit and reader-level crosschecks

**Failed Blackfish parley:** Jaime has a sincere preference for bloodless transfer, but the Blackfish's distrust is rationally grounded in Frey guest-right violations, Catelyn's broken agreement, and his obligation to keep Robb's queen Jeyne safe. Jaime's claim of unlimited supplies is knowingly false. The direwolf still flies; personal surrender offers are institutionally unenforceable in Brynden's eyes.

**The two sequential siege solutions:** Jaime first orders a dawn storming when negotiations fail, then uses Edmure's real hereditary authority to circumvent the uncle, turning siege economics into threats to hostages. Whether he would actually kill an infant or raze Riverrun is **UNKNOWN**: the threat was explicitly made and is morally consequential regardless. He uses the threatened participation of conquered riverlords, Freys, then westermen, showing a deliberate order of expendable soldiers. He simultaneously feeds, bathes and clothes Edmure. Neither simple redemption nor simple cruelty captures the action.

**Red Wedding memory as technology:** The singer, and specifically the Rains of Castamere, becomes a pressure instrument. Edmure says Roslin was forced into her part and wept; that is important testimony, but not full exoneration of all participants. The bronze-and-iron **sword-rimmed crown worn by Ryman's prostitute** is a physical lead whose recurrence must be audited against **AFFC 42 Brienne**, where Stoneheart handles a matching crown; no transfer is shown here.

**Cross-book:** [AFFC 33](33-jaime.md) documents the siege food shortages, Roslin pregnancy, Blackfish's defense and Genna's inheritance analysis. [AFFC 37](37-brienne.md) documents different hangings and an escalating Stoneheart campaign unknown to Jaime. ASOS Red Wedding chapters supply the singer's song and hospitality violations. The Blackfish's distrust of Jaime is neither omniscient knowledge nor an arbitrary obstruction.
