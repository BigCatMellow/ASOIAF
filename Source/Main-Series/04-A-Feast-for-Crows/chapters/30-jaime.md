## Complete chronological and causal reading

1. **Jaime's column arrives at Darry, where war-burned fields are being cultivated again.** Scouts report women weeding and oxen breaking ground, watched by about a dozen armed bearded men. The workers withdraw behind the walls as the host nears, showing tangible insecurity despite partial recovery.
2. **Darry initially bars its gates to a Lannister commander.** Jaime orders Ser Kennos to blow the Horn of Herrock, an ordinary military signal previously used at Harrenhal. The guarded welcome is significant because Lancel is Jaime's own cousin.
3. **Lancel flies quartered Lannister lion and Darry plowman colors.** Jaime attributes the heraldic strategy to Kevan: marriage into the former ruling family grants legitimacy the Crown's grant alone might not convey. The visual heraldry is verified; Kevan's exact design rationale is Jaime's inference.
4. **Jaime again judges Kevan more suited to be Hand than Harys Swyft.** This is a political evaluation, consistent with Cersei/Kevan's rift, not a present appointment.
5. **Jaime orders his fewer-than-a-thousand troops to camp outside with ditches and stakes**, fearing outlaws driven by hunger as much as malice. Strongboar thinks the force too formidable to attack; Jaime insists on fortification.
6. **Ser Hugo Vance carries the white Kingsguard banner, while Dermot of the Rainwood carries Tommen's stag-and-lion device.** Red Ronnet is no longer there: Jaime assigned him to **escort Wylis Manderly to Maidenpool** after the quarrel over Brienne.
7. **Wylis's escort assignment resolves an immediate gap left by AFFC 27:** an actual named carrier is appointed, but the chapter does not show arrival, embarkation or White Harbor's response.
8. **Pia rides with Jaime's squires on a horse Peck provided**, astonished by Darry's smallness relative to Harrenhal. Jaime explicitly recognizes her as traumatized and socially inexperienced outside the castle where she grew up.
9. **The chapter retrospectively confirms a concrete punishment at Harrenhal.** When a man of Gregor's company attempted to rape Pia again, Jaime **ordered Ilyn to behead him**, despite the man's assertion that he and others had assaulted her many times. The man protests habitual access as entitlement; this does not mean Pia previously consented.
10. **Ilyn presented the severed head to Pia and she smiled through broken teeth.** Her response is observed, but does not erase her earlier extreme fear or prove every future interaction is safe.
11. **Lancel is restoring Darry's war-damaged fortifications and structures**—new gates, stables, steps and shutters. The castle had been burned once and sacked at least twice; broken prosperity can be rebuilt only in a precarious military setting.
12. **The defenses mix crimson-cloaked Lannisters and blue-grey Freys.** Peasants carry improvised weapons and armed men with red seven-pointed stars also congregate inside. The latter are not merely urban demonstrators; religious mobilization now reaches a militarily contested rural lordship.
13. **Kevan is absent**, Maester **Ottomore** says, having departed after Lancel's wedding. The reader knows Kevan rejects Cersei's Handship; his present destination and exact reason for leaving Darry remain unverified here.
14. **Jaime falsely tells Ottomore that Darry lay naturally along his route**, while privately choosing a stop that could delay a direct assault on Riverrun. He considers a siege's possible end before he arrives desirable because it could spare him fighting Tullys, consistent with his vow to Catelyn.
15. **Lady Amerei Frey/Darry prepares a feast**, but says the castle cannot provision Jaime's whole force. Jaime reports he has his own supplies; hospitality and military quartering remain separate issues.
16. **Ser Harwyn Plumm, called Hardstone, is absent hunting outlaws** beyond the Trident with five knights and twenty archers. This is the garrison's reported expedition, not proof he found or killed Beric's followers.
17. **Lancel is at prayer and has forbidden disturbance.** His administrative residence is under religious control to the degree that visitors must wait or bypass his order.
18. **Darry's Plowman's Keep contains hidden history.** Jaime remembers Tyrion noticed bare patches indicating tapestries removed from the walls during Robert's visit and paid a servant to reveal their concealment in a cellar.
19. **The tapestries portrayed Targaryen kings**, including Aegon and Aenys, and had been hidden rather than destroyed. Jaime understands the old House Darry's former dynastic loyalties; neither this discovery nor their continued custody in the present visit demonstrates an active restoration plot.
20. **Ottomore says Lancel wants devotional hangings** in place of the old Targaryen imagery. This is a future decoration plan with symbolic political significance, not a completed change.
21. **Jaime recalls Cersei and Robert previously stopping here** on their trip to and from Winterfell. The same rooms and godswood will connect a new confession to the children's early river incident.
22. **Lancel now sleeps in Darry's sept rather than the lord's bed**, and seems to favor asceticism over dynastic marriage. Jaime judges this strategically foolish: a child with Darry blood would help consolidate his claim.
23. **The refurbished lord's room is deliberately austere**, with stale rushes and a rough straw pallet replacing rich furnishings. Jaime associates the absence of comfort with Lancel's penitential program.
24. **Pia assists with Jaime's bath and clothing**, while squires carry water and remove his golden hand. Jaime notices attraction and remembers her previously expressed wish to imagine him as a partner; the remembered words do not confer perpetual consent or entitlement.
25. **Jaime's bath recalls his feverish disclosure to Brienne at Harrenhal**. He now deliberately sends Pia away, prioritizing his vows and physical restraint rather than treating the bath as opportunity.
26. **Jaime encourages Peck to approach Pia only if she is willing** and emphasizes kindness and gentleness. His claim she is likely infertile because she has never conceived despite repeated assaults is his inference, not proof of infertility or a guarantee against pregnancy.
27. **Jaime offers his temporary bedchamber for Peck and Pia's possible intimacy.** The plan is a possible arrangement, **not an on-page completed encounter or an established affirmative decision by Pia**.
28. **At dinner Jaime wears red velvet and gold rather than Kingsguard white**, acknowledging a personal motive brought him to Darry. The contrast in dress marks inward conflict about oath, kinship and sexuality.
29. **Amerei reports Lancel is fasting over the death of the former High Septon**. Lancel's grieving religious dependence is a source of danger to both his health and his marriage, not proof he has physically died or become incapable.
30. **Amerei remembers her first husband Pate dying after trying to challenge Gregor Clegane** when the Freys still served Robb Stark. This corroborates her situation previously described from her father Merrett's POV in the ASOS epilogue.
31. **Lady Mariya, Amerei's mother, is Darry-born** and accompanied by roughly twenty Frey relatives, creating a heavy resident Frey presence under the combined Darry/Lannister heraldry.
32. **Amerei grieves for her father Merrett Frey**, saying he carried ransom for Petyr Pimple but was hanged by outlaws anyway. The **ASOS epilogue** independently narrates Merrett's ransom journey, Petyr's earlier hanging and Catelyn's authorization of Merrett's execution.
33. **Jaime remembers Merrett from Sumner Crakehall's household as a former bully**, and recalls the White Fawn's branding and the other squires' humiliating retaliation. This supplements Merrett's own account of his earlier career and lasting injuries.
34. **Jaime corrects Amerei's mistaken claim that he personally killed the Smiling Knight**. He names **Ser Arthur Dayne, Sword of the Morning**, as the actual victor, and recognizes the Smiling Knight as a forerunner to Gregor in violent notoriety.
35. **Ser Danwell Frey says wolf packs attacked his family's baggage on the road from the Twins**, forcing archers to kill roughly a dozen; Addam confirms wolf problems on his own march. These are reports about ordinary four-legged predators, not an independent sighting of Nymeria.
36. **Jaime still struggles to handle food and drink with the golden hand**, while listening to his captains' stories. The prosthesis's public grandeur contrasts with tactile limitations and conspicuous humiliation when he spills wine later.
37. **Mariya initially suspected Beric's outlaws murdered Merrett**, but says different leads emerged after the killing. The Brotherhood's organizational continuity is assumed by southern observers who may not recognize its leadership change.
38. **Lord Vypren tracked one fleeing outlaw band toward Fairmarket** but lost it; **Black Walder** tracked another with hounds into Hag's Mire. The peasants initially denied sightings, then under sharp questioning supplied details. Coercion can distort intelligence.
39. **The pursued band reportedly included a one-eyed man, a yellow-cloaked man and a hooded woman** with a badly scarred face who appeared to lead the outlaws. These are **peasant reports relayed by Mariya**, not Jaime's eyewitness observation.
40. **Reader-level cross-check identifies the likely figures** as **one-eyed Jack, Lem in a patched yellow cloak and the revived Catelyn Stark/Stoneheart**, from the ASOS epilogue. The new report offers a post-Oldstones route toward **the Neck**, not proof they passed through a particular crannogman refuge.
41. **Black Walder claims to have been within half a day of the band** north of Hag's Mire when they vanished into the Neck. The trail may be real while the exact hiding place, allied houses and end destination remain UNKNOWN.
42. **Mariya insists neither Beric nor the red priest was sighted with that band.** This does not prove either dead in Jaime's knowledge, but it fits the reader's evidence of Catelyn's new central agency.
43. **Strongboar repeats that Gregor killed Beric by stabbing his eye**, which men in their company reportedly witnessed. Addam counters with reports that Beric keeps returning from death. Neither is a fresh direct observation here; the chapter preserves the divergent nature of resurrection rumors.
44. **Amerei admires Ser Harwyn Plumm's promised killing of Beric**. Jaime thinks Harwyn a hard fighter but an unsuitable intimate partner and notices Amerei's attraction. Her marriage to Lancel is real; an actual affair with Harwyn is not established.
45. **Amerei pleads with Jaime to protect Darry and hunt Beric and the Hound**, touching his golden fingers; Jaime notes she cannot feel what he feels through the prosthesis.
46. **Ser Arwood Frey recounts going to ruined Saltpans with Harys and Donnel Haigh and about fifty men**, after reports of a massacre. He directly saw devastated buildings and terrified Ser Quincy within the surviving castle; this is stronger site evidence than remote gossip.
47. **Arwood reports survivor accounts of a killer in Sandor's recognizable hound helm** and horrible attacks against women. He cites a surviving girl, hidden boys, an injured woman and fisherfolk on boats. Their trauma and the destruction are meaningful; the identity behind the helm is not established by sighting it alone.
48. **Jaime questions whether Sandor actually committed the atrocities**, since the behavior sounds to him more like Gregor's, and observes that strong but slower Lyle Crakehall would be in danger against Sandor if they fought.
49. **Strongboar pledges to return after Riverrun to kill the Hound**. This is an intention from his sense of chivalry, not an accomplished hunt, and it may target the wrong culprit if the helm was used by an impostor.
50. **Arwood's claim of twenty Saltpans victims is an approximate report**, not a comprehensive death census. The strongest observed evidence is the burned village and testimony of multiple survivors, not an independently established individual perpetrator.
51. **Jaime accidentally spills wine at the high table and feels others' silence as pity**. The moment triggers his departure from dinner to seek Lancel; physical vulnerability, jealousy and wounded pride intensify the next exchange.
52. **Approximately one hundred sparrows eat sausages at Darry's cooking fires**, despite late-autumn scarcity. Jaime worries future hunger could follow if planting cannot yield another harvest; this is a logistical forecast, not current starvation of all residents.
53. **Three armed sparrows guard Lancel's sept**, one small bearded man, a huge bald man with a painted star over one eye, and a female Poor Fellow in rusted mail carrying a spiked club. The woman speaks as a guard, disrupting Jaime's first assumption she was a boy.
54. **The guards refuse Jaime access despite his royal/Kingsguard status**, insulting him as a cripple and Kingslayer. They call themselves **Poor Fellows**, marking an already locally operational religious militia distinct from Cersei's expectation that the movement would simply withdraw.
55. **Lancel opens the sept doors and invites Jaime through**, avoiding physical confrontation. The guards obey Lancel, not the visiting Lord Commander, demonstrating divided loyalties.
56. **Lancel is barefoot, shaven-crowned, white-haired near his ears, physically wasting and dressed in plain wool**. Ascetic practice and possible illness are observable; supernaturally granted visions are not independently proved.
57. **Lancel admits a quarrel with Kevan** and says he has found faith. The specific subject of the quarrel is not wholly disclosed; rejecting inheritance and paternal plans is the strongest immediate candidate.
58. **Lancel sleeps beneath a different altar each night and claims visions from the Seven**. Fasting and sleep patterns provide an ordinary possible contributor to dreams and altered perception.
59. **He claims he dreamed Jaime would come, know his sins and kill him.** This is a dream and fear; the predicted murder does not take place in this chapter.
60. **Lancel disclaims ordinary food as necessary**, insisting faith is nourishment. Jaime compares it to porridge and urges him to eat; long-term survival remains a genuine concern.
61. **Lancel says he never wanted Darry, only to be Jaime**, exposing how Jaime functioned as the model for his admired royal, martial and sexual standing.
62. **Lancel's marriage to Amerei has not been consummated, according to him**, and he says the ceremony was done to please Kevan. The wedding took place, but no physical marital relationship is independently depicted.
63. **Lancel argues that without consummation the marriage is incomplete**, citing Baelor and Daena as precedent. Whether he can legally set aside the Darry/Frey arrangement without political consequences is untested in this scene.
64. **Lancel says if Amerei loves Hardstone she may be happy with him**. The statement illustrates his renunciation, not evidence her alleged attraction has progressed to intercourse.
65. **Jaime compares his killing of Aerys to Lancel's sin**; Lancel answers that both killed kings, distinguishing an armed killing from murder involving a wineskin. This is a confession that Lancel **participated in Robert's fatal wine scheme**, consistent with Cersei's direct fear in AFFC 7.
66. **Jaime explicitly demands admission of sexual relations with Cersei**. Lancel's tearful responses, insistence he loved/protected her, and narrow assertion he never ejaculated inside her directly establish an intimate sexual relationship by **Lancel's own testimony**, not proof about Osmund or Moon Boy.
67. **Lancel argues there was no treason or bastard because of his method of avoiding conception**. Jaime mocks the rationalization; even if no child resulted, it does not nullify the affair or proven role in Robert's death.
68. **Lancel says Cersei sought his comfort while Jaime was captive and Tywin away**, and explains Tyrion exploited or pressured him into betrayal. Each explanation is Lancel's characterization, not a neutral allocation of agency.
69. **Jaime privately realizes Cersei's earlier boast that she has lied to him repeatedly may have been true**, and experiences the disclosure as personal betrayal. He continues to wonder whether Osmund is also involved; **Moon Boy** remains specifically unverified.
70. **Jaime remembers his own incestuous paternity of Joffrey, Tommen and Myrcella**. He considers confessing but does not; this is a vital contrast with Lancel's fear of a bastard.
71. **Lancel says he confessed to the *previous* High Septon**, who prayed over him while wounded and urged forgiveness of Cersei. The prior prelate is dead by this chapter, so this does not by itself show the new High Sparrow personally heard Lancel's confession.
72. **Jaime recognizes a possible lethal risk from the confession**, wondering whether Lancel understands what his words wrought. The listener and disclosure chain need tracing; the prior High Septon's knowledge must not be upgraded to an established reason for his death.
73. **Lancel openly plans to renounce the Darry lordship and Amerei marriage**, saying Harwyn can take both if he wants, and return to King's Landing the next day. This is his declared intention, **not a completed abdication or annulment**.
74. **Lancel announces he will join the restored Warrior's Sons** and says the **Poor Fellows have been revived**. This provides post-AFFC 28 evidence of active recruitment and armed religious personnel, but not proof every ancient charter or crown financial obligation has already been formally executed.
75. **Lancel cites a letter from the new High Septon saying Tommen consented** to the restoration. He offers to show the letter but Jaime does not read it in this scene. Thus **the priest has asserted written royal consent**, corroborating progress from AFFC 28's verbal bargain without supplying a witnessed royal seal.
76. **Jaime remembers ancient Targaryen suppression of the Faith Militant but cannot identify the precise king**, naming Maegor or Jaehaerys as candidates. His uncertainty is explicit and Tyrion is his absent hoped-for historian.
77. **Lancel asks Jaime why he joined the Kingsguard and surrendered inheritance and marriage**. Jaime silently knows his own oath was substantially driven by desire to stay near Cersei, complicating his claim to be objectively defending Lancel's dynastic interests.
78. **Jaime contrasts the gods' iconography with his image of Cersei**: he once imagined her as the Maid beside himself as Warrior, but now thinks her a Stranger concealing her true face. This is a psychological metaphor, not evidence Cersei is literally a divine or magical figure.
79. **Jaime refuses to pray and leaves the sept**, with bitter mockery for the armed sparrows. No reconciled confession or restored cousinly alliance is achieved.
80. **He seeks Ser Ilyn Payne and two practice swords**. Because people might overhear, the two withdraw to Darry's godswood rather than practice in the open yard.
81. **Jaime points to Raymun Darry's old bedchamber and recalls the royal family's earlier return from Winterfell**, placing the memory around Joffrey's attack on Arya/Mycah and Nymeria's bite at the Trident. Jaime's account connects his own hidden behavior with AGOT 15/16.
82. **Jaime recalls Cersei demanding the 'Stark girl' lose a hand** for an injury to royal blood. Robert opposed the cruelty. The intended target was **Arya**, not Sansa, as established in AGOT 15.
83. **Jaime admits he had intercourse with Cersei while Robert slept nearby at Darry**, and that he would have killed Robert if he had awakened. This is a candid interior/firsthand retrospective, not a completed second regicide.
84. **He recalls Cersei demanding the Stark girl be maimed or killed**, and acknowledges it was chance Stark men found Arya before he did. The exact atrocity he would have committed is conditional and **UNKNOWN**, but Jaime's past willingness to act for Cersei is plainly serious.
85. **Jaime confesses this dangerous memory to the tongueless headsman**, who makes the clacking laugh previously noted during their training. Jaime angrily imagines Payne may have been with Cersei too, another unsupported jealousy claim.
86. **The chapter ends when Jaime challenges Payne to kill him in their nocturnal practice**, striking at a tree branch and inviting combat. This is rage and self-punishment language, **not a depicted death, suicide attempt or confirmed intention to die**.
87. **End state:** Lancel has directly confessed to Robert's death scheme and sexual involvement with Cersei, promises to surrender his holding for the new Warrior's Sons, armed Poor Fellows already guard Darry, a Stoneheart-linked outlaw trail reportedly extends into the Neck, Saltpans remains an identity puzzle, and Jaime's self-conception unravels under these revelations.

## Cross-book source junctions

- **AFFC 28 (Cersei) ↔ AFFC 30:** In the previous chapter Cersei verbally offers to lift Maegor's restrictions and trade **900,674 dragons** of Faith debt for Tommen's blessing. **Now armed Poor Fellows are actually posted at Darry**, Lancel has received news of royal consent, and he intends to join Warrior's Sons. Do not incorrectly maintain that *no* militia is active after this chapter. Still **no witnessed signed royal instrument or financial accounting entry**.
- **AFFC 7 and 8 (Cersei/Jaime) ↔ AFFC 30:** Cersei worried Lancel might confess the Robert wine plot and their affair; Tyrion's accusations haunted Jaime. Lancel now confirms both, but **does not independently confirm Osmund, Moon Boy, the exact duration, or every element Tyrion said**.
- **ASOS Epilogue (Merrett) ↔ AFFC 30:** The dead man Amerei and Mariya mourn was the epilogue narrator, hanged by the transformed Brotherhood after delivering ransom. **Catelyn is directly observed revived**, ruling out Jaime's assumption the scarred female commander must merely be a surviving human outlaw of unknown pedigree at reader level.
- **AFFC 25 (Brienne) ↔ AFFC 30:** Hyle's separate information about a leader called Stoneheart matches the ASOS epilogue and Mariya's one-eyed/yellow-cloak/hooded-woman report. A plausible operational movement toward the Neck emerges, but it is **not proof Catelyn reached Greywater Watch or obtained crannogman support**.
- **AFFC 20/25 (Brienne) and ASOS 74 (Arya) ↔ AFFC 30:** Sandor's last directly witnessed condition remains grievously wounded; the distinctive hound helm can transmit an identity falsely. Arwood's direct inspection of Saltpans increases confidence in the atrocity, **not in Sandor's personal responsibility**.
- **AFFC 27 (Jaime) ↔ AFFC 30:** Jaime's earlier vague **order for Wylis's return** is now tied to **Red Ronnet as named escort**. Ronnet's removal also follows Jaime defending Brienne's dignity; it is not proof Jaime harmed or reconciled Ronnet outside the earlier strike.
- **AGOT 15 (Sansa) and the Trident aftermath ↔ AFFC 30:** Sansa witnesses Joffrey attack Mycah, Arya intervene and Nymeria bite him. Jaime's new private memory provides a **distinct hidden Lannister response**—Cersei demanded a mutilation and Jaime was willing to search for the girl. Keep Jaime's remembered conditional menace separate from injuries actually inflicted on Arya.
- **ASOS Jaime Harrenhal bath ↔ AFFC 30:** Jaime's memory of confessing to Brienne and the new bath before his confrontation with Lancel provide a structural comparison; the second time he chooses restraint without the excuse of fever.
- **Kingswood Brotherhood and Arthur Dayne ↔ AFFC 30:** Jaime's childhood campaign recollection supplies an exact model of **purchasing food, expanding grazing access and allowing limited timber and venison**, showing coercion alone is insufficient to defeat insurgency. This also corrects Amerei's inflated account of who killed the Smiling Knight.
- **AFFC 28 and the prior High Septon question:** Cersei's sinister internal remark about her predecessor becomes more consequential once the reader learns Lancel confessed the Robert plot to that very predecessor. A **possible security motive** emerges, but no actual killing or instruction is shown or authorized by Lancel's statement; search independent death circumstances.

## Most valuable next tests

1. **Lancel confession custody:** Determine whether the predecessor told anyone of Robert's murder/affair, and what Cersei could learn about such disclosure. Motive and opportunity are not proof of a particular High Septon's murder.
2. **Faith Militant legal status:** Obtain the first direct royal seal/official document or subsequent independent action. Here, **local Poor Fellows are active** and a claimed consent letter exists; contrast the two evidence grades with AFFC 28's bargain.
3. **Stoneheart whereabouts:** Track source of the band description, date, escape routes into the Neck and presence of Jack/Lem; a disappearing scent trail does not identify a fixed secret base.
4. **Darry succession:** Monitor whether Lancel actually renounces title and wife, Kevan's response, Frey marriage claims and practical legitimacy of an unconsummated marriage.
5. **Arthur Dayne model:** Look for a later comparable population-protection and grievance-redress policy; evaluate whether Jaime can implement something similar in a devastated Riverlands rather than just advocating it at supper.
6. **Darry tapestries:** Confirm whether the hidden Targaryen portraits remain in the cellar, who has access and whether earlier loyalties have political consequences; no unobserved dragon claimant is implied.
7. **Saltpans attribution:** Separate site inspection and survivor testimony from identification by helm; follow any independently witnessed movement of the physical hound helm or another wearer.
8. **Pia and Peck:** Record Pia's affirmative choices and safety, rather than assuming Jaime's private arrangement is completed or treating traumatic prior assaults as proof of barrenness.
9. **Jaime's ethics:** Compare his actual punishment of Pia's attacker, use of Gregor's men, advocacy for the smallfolk and private admission he once could have harmed Arya for Cersei. Character change is incomplete and not monotonic.
10. **Wylis:** Track Red Ronnet's escort from Harrenhal to Maidenpool and actual transport, independent of court assumptions about Wyman or Davos.
