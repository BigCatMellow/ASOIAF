## Causal interpretation and rival readings

**What the case proves.** Cersei engineers Osney's false testimony (see [AFFC 39](39-cersei.md)), receives Pycelle's damaging but limited moon-tea testimony, and orders arrests with Tommen's blank warrants. The examinations and moon tea do **not** independently prove Margaery's adultery; the Blue Bard's story was shaped through extreme torture. Her plan turns when the Faith keeps Osney and extracts his accusations about Cersei and the preceding High Septon's death. The Faith's torture of Osney, Margaery and Cersei is coercive, not an indicator that every allegation gained thereby is true.

**An institutional trap, not just poetic punishment.** Cersei tries to restore Faith jurisdiction because she believes it will kill Margaery. Her insistence on a Kingsguard champion prevents the use of Qyburn's unspecified powerful fighter when she herself is charged. The queen loses control of the Watch, council, Tommen's seal, the capital's public mood, and the dromonds. The High Septon has motives independent of Cersei, including institutional recovery of sacred jurisdiction. Whether his charges will withstand trial remains open.

**Maritime consequence.** The fleet's departure matters more than Cersei's guess about its eventual destination. Both an Aurane pirate fleet and a Stannis transfer are *explicitly rival speculations by characters*. The ironborn situation off Oldtown (AFFC45) becomes materially worse if central royal naval strength is no longer available, but the two flotillas are not observed together.

**Prophecy control.** Cersei's conclusion that Margaery's humiliation disproves Maggy is as unwarranted as a conclusion that Cersei has now proven every part of the prophecy. The valonqar remains unidentified.
