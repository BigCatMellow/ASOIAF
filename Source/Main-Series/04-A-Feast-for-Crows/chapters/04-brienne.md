## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Brienne is actively following Jaime's commission to find Sansa.** She questions inhabitants of **Rosby** for a beautiful **auburn-haired, blue-eyed thirteen-year-old** accompanied by an older portly knight or fool. **None she interviews recognizes Sansa**; a negative search of one village cannot establish the girl never passed nearby.
2. **The villagers range from goodwife, blacksmith and septon to pigkeeper and onion-gathering girl**; two House Rosby spearmen, wearing **three red chevrons on ermine**, respond with sexual insults. These mundane encounters demonstrate the lack of useful intelligence and danger to a runaway girl.
3. **Brienne notices a skinny boy on a piebald horse**, but he vanishes behind the sept before she questions him. This is an **explicit missed information opportunity**. His identity and destination are **not established by this chapter**, so avoid retrospective identification.
4. **She chooses the road northeast toward Duskendale.** The greater port traffic there contrasts with **King's Landing's destroyed quays, burnt galleys and ships unloading offshore**. Brienne has investigated the bay; she finds no source who recalls a ship departing on the Purple Wedding night. **The reader knows Sansa departed via skiff to an offshore vessel**, so the absence of a recorded departure is explicable without Sansa never sailing.
5. **Sansa's ordinary refuge options appear closed from Brienne's perspective.** Her father was executed, mother reportedly killed, Winterfell burnt, Freys dominate the Twins and ironborn **hold Moat Cailin**. Brienne doesn't know Sansa is **alive and disguised as Alayne in the Vale**. This is a clean information-asymmetry example.
6. **Brienne carries Jaime's bay mare and his oath**, while questioning whether the mission is a cruel game, then rejecting that interpretation because he gifted her a **valuable Valyrian sword and pledged sincerity**. She does not know Jaime's secret freeing of Tyrion or the subsequent deaths.
7. **She distinguishes a false Arya from Sansa.** Jaime already warned that the supposed Arya sent north to Ramsay was a **counterfeit**; Brienne therefore concentrates on Sansa, though **the real Arya is alive and on her way to Braavos** by reader knowledge.
8. **Brienne meets two hedge knights by a brook after dusk.** The larger, nearsighted and verbose **Ser Creighton Longbough** claims fame at the Blackwater, and the older, more pragmatic **Ser Illifer the Penniless** shares trout. Their actual provenance is mainly **self-description**, not independent service records.
9. **Brienne's size and armor lead strangers to take her for a man.** The chapter extensively grounds her insecurities about stature, appearance and ridicule through her own thoughts, while showing competence, endurance and courtesy in behavior.
10. **Creighton boasts of twelve noble ransoms and named victories; Illifer is skeptical.** His florid claims about the Blackwater, **Herbert Bolling** and the **Knight of the Red Chicken** are not independently corroborated. Treat them as characterization and possible fabrication, not a battle-history ledger.
11. **Illifer identifies Brienne's shield as the Lothston black bat.** Jaime had supplied an old shield from **Harrenhal's armory**, bearing a black bat against **silver and gold**. Illifer recalls his ancestors fighting the last Lothstons and cites **Lucas the Pander and Manfryd of the Black Hood**. This demonstrates **how equipment can falsely assign inherited guilt**.
12. **Illifer suspects Brienne of murdering Renly.** He draws a dagger after recognizing the alleged **Maid of Tarth**. She solemnly swears **by the Seven and by her sword** that she did not kill Renly; he sheaths the weapon and accepts that the gods can judge. **Her denial is consistent with Catelyn's eyewitness shadow account and Loras's doubts**.
13. **Brienne remembers Renly's visit to Tarth.** As a much younger girl, humiliated by others' taunts about a **rose**, she attended a feast on **Selwyn's orders**. Renly courted her graciously and danced with her, setting an example that briefly caused others to dance with her too. His courtesy underlies her intense devotion, not necessarily a romantic promise from Renly.
14. **Her vigilance is understandable after earlier attempts at sexual violence.** She stands watch over the knights and recalls dangers from **Renly's camp near Highgarden** and from the Brave Companions; she has trouble sleeping near men. This is trauma-aware risk assessment rather than unreasonable fear.
15. **Her watch ends peacefully.** The hedge knights do not steal, molest or harm her. She judges them **decent despite vanity and poverty**, preserving moral differentiation between strangers in a war-torn landscape.
16. **Ser Creighton observes travelers in the night.** He reports a **farm boy on a piebald horse**, then **six men carrying staves and scythes** who looked hard at their mounts but withdrew after his warning. This is his report, not Brienne's direct identification of any follower.
17. **Brienne has spoken to Brella, one of Sansa's former maids.** Brella said Sansa and Tyrion showed little warmth; Brienne reasons Tyrion might have forced Sansa to poison Joffrey, although the reader knows Tyrion did not administer the poison. This is an explicit **POV mistake founded on incomplete evidence**.
18. **A large pious procession appears on the road to King's Landing.** **Begging brothers**, a septon with a **crystal on a thong** and roughly **sixty men, women and children** pull a **two-wheeled wagon heaped with skulls and bones**. A shirtless man with a **seven-pointed star carved in his chest** claims to be a poor fellow.
19. **The procession's stated mission is religious justice and protection.** Members say holy men and women were murdered, septs pillaged and silent sisters assaulted; they seek aid from **the king and Blessed Baelor's church**. The recovered bones are real objects before Brienne, but precise identities and the speakers' casualty descriptions are **not all independently audited**.
20. **The septon exhorts knights to abandon worldly masters for the Faith.** This is a **political mobilization doctrine** rather than a mere procession of mourners. He calls his followers **sparrows**, defining them as humble common believers. Their emergence foreshadows a possible mass religious movement without proving its eventual leadership or success.
21. **Some poor fellows menace knights who refuse to escort them.** When **Illifer demands payment** and Creighton explains they must go to Duskendale, followers call them false knights and raise clubs; the barefoot septon calms them and allows passage. The movement mixes trauma, grievance, religious ideals and intimidation.
22. **Brienne remembers a violated septon near Maidenpool.** Brave Companions suspended a dead septon upside down and used him for archery practice. Her firsthand recollection lends weight to general claims of war crimes against clergy, even if each bone's origin is unknown.
23. **A merchant's caravan joins the travelers later that day.** Merchant **Hibald** carries goods with **six serving men and a wagon**, guarded by the small, sharp-faced knight **Ser Shadrich of the Shady Glen**, called **the Mad Mouse**. Its men initially take defensive positions with **quarterstaffs and crossbow** because highway robbery is common.
24. **Shadrich's shield bears a white mouse with red eyes** against diagonal brown and blue. He claims the colors refer to lands and rivers he has traveled. The device and alias are **witnessed/reported**, his professional history less certain.
25. **Shadrich independently identifies Sansa as a bounty target.** He says **Varys promised a substantial reward** and recognizes the specific combination of her age, auburn hair, and suspected helper **Ser Dontos**, once a knight from Duskendale. This establishes **a rival informed search**. The truth of Varys's reward amount or whether it remains active after Varys disappears is **his claim**.
26. **Brienne denies her true objective and continues using the 'my sister' cover.** Shadrich does not believe the disguise and offers to share reward money, then privately warns that searchers may mistake her supposed missing sister for Sansa. This makes him a **practical security threat**, not merely an eccentric knight.
27. **The travelers stop at the Old Stone Bridge inn**, at an actual river junction. Innkeeper **Naggle** says Creighton owes **seven silver stags** and will not lodge them for free. Brienne pays for separate rooms and food for the hedge knights, using **money Jaime provided**.
28. **Brienne carries a royal assistance letter signed by Tommen.** It commands loyal subjects to help her on the king's business; the signature is **in a child's hand**. Along with money, armor and the bay mare, this is concrete logistical support from Jaime, though **the document may expose her to political suspicion if shown indiscriminately**.
29. **Brienne corrects false tavern gossip about Jaime.** Hibald confirms Jaime's right hand is gone; Creighton invents a direwolf-bite account. Brienne says **a Qohorik sellsword cut it off**, consistent with ASOS; even well-traveled knights cannot be trusted to report recent events reliably.
30. **Her inner defense of Jaime is grounded in close combat.** She remembers fighting a weakened, chained Jaime and recognizes his former skill despite his crimes. She separates **the cruelty of the amputation** from the political morality of Jaime's earlier life.
31. **Oathkeeper is still under Brienne's control.** She keeps a **plain replacement longsword** openly and **the red-black Valyrian steel Oathkeeper** hidden in her bedroll, adorned with gold and rubies. She kneels, holds the blade and asks **the Crone** to guide her to Sansa. Its origin in **Ned Stark's Ice** gives the mission a material irony.
32. **Brienne leaves the inn during the night.** Instead of remaining close to the bounty hunter, she quietly saddles her mare and rides away, apologizing mentally to the friendly hedge knights. **No Sansa encounter occurs** and no destination beyond continued searching is verified.
33. **End state:** Brienne's search has no positive sighting, but she has learned that **another hunter knows the Dontos lead**, that the Faith is mobilizing over civilian atrocities, and that the road north/east is still dangerous. She retains Oathkeeper, a signed royal letter, money and freedom to travel.

## Cross-POV implications

- **The information trail is uneven:** Brienne follows Dontos and a possible Duskendale port; the reader knows **Dontos dead** and Sansa concealed as **Alayne** by Petyr. No ordinary witness near Rosby has the right information.
- **Who pursues Sansa?** Shadrich's pursuit is economically motivated and his knowledge dangerous, but no seizure or direct villainy toward Sansa is shown.
- **Religious grassroots movement:** the sparrows' physical presence and claims should be tested against future Faith decisions and royal responses without prematurely assigning the septon an unnamed office.
- **Brienne's oath and identity:** other travelers see only her size, armor or supposed Renly guilt, while her internal understanding shows deliberate loyalty, tactical caution and moral discernment.
