# AFFC 25 — Brienne: Meribald, the Hound reports, and broken men

- Status: FIRST-PASS COMPLETE; independent cross-POV challenge open.

## Chronological close reading

1. Hyle carries the heads of Pyg, Timeon and Shagwell back from the Whispers despite Brienne's objections. They deteriorate badly on the wet return journey; Brienne is disturbed by the trophies and dreams of their whispering.
2. Hyle credits Podrick's rock and Brienne's victory before Randyll Tarly rather than claiming the feat. Tarly nevertheless belittles Brienne and orders the heads displayed at Maidenpool's gate.
3. Brienne refuses Tarly's direction to return to Tarth. She intends to find Sansa and considers following rumors concerning Sandor Clegane.
4. Tarly describes contradictory reports of the Hound's association with the Brotherhood and a separate outlaw leader called Stoneheart. His estimates of the Hound's following differ wildly, showing that his information is insecure.
5. Tarly refuses Brienne and Podrick lodging at Maidenpool. Hyle offers to ask his cousin Alyn about the Hound and arranges to meet Brienne at the Stinking Goose.
6. The remaining inns are overcrowded with Tarly's soldiers. Podrick realizes ships may offer an affordable cabin, demonstrating useful practical initiative.
7. The Titan's Daughter leaves Maidenpool on the evening tide, a significant cross-POV recurrence of the vessel previously associated with Arya's journey to Braavos. Its departure does not prove a precise synchronized timeline.
8. On the Seastrider, Brienne hears a route connecting Oldtown, Tyrosh, Pentos, Duskendale, Gulltown, Sisterton and White Harbor. Despite temptation she does not take a northern passage.
9. Brienne buys Podrick an orange. They find accommodation on the damaged Lady of Myr, whose owner lacks money for repairs.
10. Brienne dreams of the Mummers, Tarly, Hoat and Ronnet Connington and awakens momentarily convinced Dick Crabb is present. Fear and grief continue after the Whispers fight.
11. The Stinking Goose's proprietor remembers Dick's dubious reputation but Brienne states that he was killed by outlaws.
12. Hyle brings a map and says Alyn places the Hound at Saltpans during the attack and afterward traveling west along the Trident. This is a reported identification, not an on-page sighting by Brienne or Alyn.
13. Hyle also repeats that the Hound killed three of Gregor's men at the crossroads inn. This earlier report must be distinguished from who subsequently committed the Saltpans atrocities.
14. Hyle's analysis of geography notes obstacles from the Freys, Blackwoods and Brackens, Tarly's garrison, Darry, Harrenhal, winter snow on the Vale road and mountain clans. He assumes the fugitive may be confined near the river.
15. Alyn reportedly says Beric's Brotherhood is pursuing the Hound for Saltpans rather than sheltering him. Hyle says Tarly knowingly blames the Brotherhood to erode its civilian support. Both claims are transmitted through Hyle.
16. Hyle refers to a woman called Stoneheart whose followers have been confused with Beric's men; details about her identity are rumor. The ASOS epilogue supplies a separate reader-level basis for comparison.
17. Brienne wants to investigate Saltpans. Hyle recommends Septon Meribald, a traveling priest who regularly passes the place.
18. Hyle tells Brienne he has left Tarly's service and intends to accompany her, motivated partly by the possible reward for locating Sansa. Brienne remains focused on her vow, not a bounty.
19. Brienne, Podrick and Hyle depart with Meribald, his donkey carrying food for poor riverlanders and his large friendly dog named Dog.
20. Meribald is a wandering septon without a sept. He is illiterate but knows many prayers and scripture passages from memory and has spent nearly forty years visiting isolated settlements.
21. Meribald walks barefoot as personal penance, and interprets the 'Cobbler Above' as an aspect of the Smith within the faith of the Seven Who Are One.
22. Podrick identifies himself with the Warrior; Meribald prefers the Smith for recognizing ordinary labor. The discourse distinguishes formal seven-as-one doctrine from everyday religious imagery.
23. The route runs northwest of Maidenpool through marshes, tidal pools and low dunes on paths missing from Hyle's sheepskin maps. Meribald's staff and long experience keep the party on safe ground.
24. Three marsh women bring clams to Meribald, who exchanges precious oranges. Hyle makes a Mother-Maiden-Crone analogy but Brienne observes ordinary residents.
25. Meribald says the isolated marsh dwellers have so little property that armies largely overlook them. Their local economic isolation contrasts with regions devastated by the war.
26. The septon walks a circuit of half a year or longer and knows settlements, caves, hedges and minor roads invisible on military maps. Brienne briefly wonders whether this knowledge might connect him with fugitives.
27. Podrick remembers owning a dog named Hero; Meribald says Dog belongs to himself and the Seven. The names invite thematic comparison with the Hound but establish no hidden identity.
28. Meribald has heard the howls of an immense wolf pack along the Trident. Reports claim hundreds of wolves led by an enormous grey female with unusual abilities; he has not personally seen the she-wolf or counted the pack.
29. The septon cautions Podrick about dangerous tidal mud; later a stilt-house village provides warnings of three hungry wanderers near an old watchtower.
30. Meribald proposes feeding the three men, hearing their confessions and inviting them to the Quiet Isle instead of beginning with force. Hyle favors Tarly's punitive approach.
31. Meribald carefully distinguishes dangerous 'broken men' from other outlaws: conscripted commoners can lose their families, equipment, purpose and means of returning home during long campaigns, ultimately deserting and surviving by theft.
32. Meribald does not claim the broken men are safe; he argues travelers should fear and pity them simultaneously, because social and military pressures created many of them.
33. Meribald recalls being recruited while still young for the War of the Ninepenny Kings. His brothers Willam, Robin and Owen died, and their companion Jon Pox was executed. He recalls neither meeting a king nor obtaining wealth, grounding his analysis in firsthand suffering.
34. His description of anonymous armored knights as faceless men is an ordinary image of the battlefield and is not evidence of contact with Braavosi killers.
35. End state: Brienne continues toward Saltpans with Hyle and Meribald, retaining an unresolved Hound identity problem. The giant wolf-pack reports, Quiet Isle, and the rediscovered Titan's Daughter are priority cross-POV clues.
