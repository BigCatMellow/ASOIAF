## Causal interpretation and rival readings

**Oldtown is in active danger.** The ironborn have advanced beyond opportunistic coastal theft into an organized campaign with forward bases, damaged shipping and attempted clandestine entry. The captain's report of a disguised Tyroshi ship illustrates counterintelligence, and the banner bearing the eye and iron crown points toward the Greyjoy campaign; do not claim Sam personally encountered Euron. Leyton and Malora's spell study is a **captain's report**, not witnessed successful defensive sorcery. Gunthor, Baelor, Garth and Humfrey have distinct purported jobs; a full hired fleet is not present.

**Sam's binding choices.** Sam must balance Gilly's and the substituted child's safety, his Watch vows, the unknown security of Horn Hill, Aemon's unburied/unstated final disposition and urgent political news. At the endpoint Gilly remains aboard; Sam has **not** escorted her to Horn Hill, given Jon's letters to the official Seneschal, seen Aemon's interment, or settled the baby's custody. The broken old horn brought from the Fist (AFFC35) is **not mentioned or disposed of here**; never infer its location was changed.

**Direct magic versus institutional allegation.** The black glass candle is visibly alight with nonordinary flame properties. Alleras says Marwyn foresaw Sam's arrival through it; that supports current anomalous knowledge but is not a first-person demonstration of dream invasion. Marwyn says Citadel orthodoxy engineered dragon extinction, would have killed Aemon and might poison Sam. These are **Marwyn's severe claims**; Aemon's own Wall vows and independent life history are plausible competing explanations. The candle alone cannot certify a coordinated conspiracy.

**The infiltration at the Ravenry.** The new Pate is visually encountered at the precise place where Walgrave's keys and ravens matter. The [AFFC Prologue](00-prologue.md) ends after the original Pate sells Walgrave's key to an alchemist and apparently dies. Identical name and associated access now provide a *powerful identity-substitution inference*. The source does not explicitly identify the impostor as Jaqen H'ghar or show the replacement ceremony. Alleras is Sphinx and the reversible name resembles Sarella; likely Dorne link, but [the theory case](../../../../research/theories/cases/sarella-alleras.md) must still distinguish this evidence from direct name confirmation.

**Three knowledge channels.** Quhuru holds books offered for sale, the Citadel operates ravens, and Marwyn claims glass candles permit near-instant communication. The meeting fuses Aemon's death, Daenerys intelligence and a suspected infiltrator at the very institution controlling knowledge. No one at the Citadel yet independently verifies Aemon's prophetic identification of Daenerys.
