## Causal interpretation and rival readings

**A bloodless capitulation with violent coercion.** Edmure's forced surrender saves the garrison from storming. That result does not retrospectively neutralize Jaime's threat to Edmure's unborn child or future threatening treatment. The Blackfish escapes through a carefully controlled water gate; the distinct promises matter. Jaime protects the garrison against questioning but orders lethal archery if Edmure or Jeyne attempts escape. These are selective limits, not simple innocence or total moral collapse.

**The Westerling bargain.** Sybell explicitly confirms an understanding with Tywin and says she ensured Jeyne would not have Robb's child. **Neither an administered potion nor certainty about Jeyne's reproductive state is witnessed in this chapter.** Jeyne's mourning, crown struggle and bruising show a teenager with agency in conflict with her mother, not necessarily a false wife. Differences in remembered body description warrant textual comparison, *not* automatic adoption of the Jeyne-impostor theory. Raynald's wounded plunge into the Green Fork is an unresolved survival possibility, not his documented death.

**Overlapping Brotherhood threat.** The reported hanging of Ryman near Fairmarket makes the sword-rimmed crown now held by Stoneheart in [AFFC 42](42-brienne.md) especially significant. It remains circumstantial: the killers and the artifact's transit are not narrated here. Tom o' Sevens' appearance in Riverrun is actionable infiltration evidence when cross-checked with his earlier Brotherhood role. Jaime neither identifies Tom's affiliation nor knows what reader has seen in Stoneheart's cave. The huge she-wolf echoes Arya's bond with Nymeria, but Dermot supplies the only in-chapter sighting; avoid making Jaime magically aware of Arya.

**Choosing family and self.** Jaime wishes to protect Tommen and considers a truth about paternity which could cost Tommen the throne. His mother-like visitor interrogates Tywin's family legacy; it may be grief and memory, a meaningful dream, or something more, but does not establish a ghost. The last scene is a *real decision*: Cersei's appeal arrives and Jaime orders its destruction. His choice is not proof she has already been convicted, nor proof he has ceased caring for Tommen.

**Winter signal.** Actual snow at Riverrun is observed. Vyman's letter is **not** a Citadel white raven; Jaime's initial assumption is corrected. Winter's official seasonal declaration and the severity of future crop losses remain distinct from his fear.
