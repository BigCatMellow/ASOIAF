# AFFC 28 — Cersei: the High Sparrow's bargain, the Faith Militant, and the invented Tyrell conspiracy

- Record role: `RESEARCH / SEQUENTIAL CLOSE READING`.
- Status: `FIRST PASS COMPLETE — INDEPENDENT CROSS-POV CHALLENGE OPEN`.
- Primary source: complete [AFFC 28 Cersei](../text/029-cersei.md), read 2026-10-18; [evidence standards](../../../../SOURCES.md).
- Scope: The entire ride up Visenya's Hill, approach to the Great Sept, private High Septon interview, return journey and meeting with Margaery. Spoken promises and attributed historical anecdotes are not automatically completed actions or independently confirmed history. The Cersei POV's interpretations require special separation from narrated conduct.

## Chronological close reading

1. **Cersei rides up Visenya's Hill in a litter with Taena Merryweather** while Osmund Kettleblack clears the way. The ostensible business is to visit the newly elevated High Septon, whose blessing for Tommen has not yet been delivered.
2. **Taena catalogs Margaery's thriving informal court**, including jugglers, puppeteers, singers, and performers. Hamish the Harper plays fortnightly; Alaric of Eysen also visits; the handsome **Blue Bard** is her favorite, according to Taena.
3. **Cersei turns the social report into a sexual investigation**, asking whether Margaery is still a maiden. Taena claims to have helped disrobe Renly when he married Margaery, and describes him as sexually aroused before entering their bed. That does **not** establish consummation.
4. **No bloodied sheet was displayed**, Taena says. Cersei recognizes that omission proves little, yet relies on equally poor assumptions about riding horses and maidenhood. Physical mythology, gossip and suspicious inference substitute for actual evidence.
5. Taena names additional male visitors or admirers: **Ser Lambert** with an eye patch, **Bayard Norcross**, **Courtenay Greenhill**, the **Woodwright brothers**, **Portifer**, **Lucantine**, Redwyne twins, **Ser Tallad** and others. These are social links and potential witnesses, not proof of infidelity.
6. **Pycelle visits Margaery's circle frequently**, Taena claims; Cersei instantly frames the possibility as desertion from the lion to the rose. The visit alone does not establish Pycelle's allegiance or a Tyrell conspiracy.
7. **Jalabhar Xho**, a Summer Islander dressed in feathers, also visits, as do courtiers of Margaery's cousins. Taena reports **Elinor's Ambrose betrothal**, **Megga's flirtations**, and a possible Bulwer match.
8. **Megga likes one-armed Mark Mullendore**, Taena reports, and hopes to find him a monkey. Cersei ridicules his disability, echoing her derision of Jaime's missing hand and showing how she translates social vulnerability into unworthiness.
9. **Loras visits his sister daily** unless on duty. Taena breaks off an observation about how close the siblings are, announces a wicked thought, and smiles. The scene *invites* a sexual insinuation but **supplies no evidence of incest between Margaery and Loras**. Cersei herself is implicated in precisely that kind of conduct.
10. Taena mocks the sparrows' hygiene. Cersei expects the new High Septon to be discreditable and easy to intimidate, before encountering any independent evidence of his leadership.
11. **Cersei explains that High Septons relinquish personal names** on elevation. Her account of the office's anonymity and language about the gods' avatar is an explanation of Faith practice, not evidence of an individual supernatural transformation.
12. Cersei recounts **Baelor the Blessed** elevating an allegedly illiterate stonemason as High Septon and later an eight-year-old reputed to perform miracles. **The claim that Baelor's Hand poisoned the stonemason is reported rumor**; the child's healing powers are Baelor's reported belief, not a verified miracle.
13. **Cersei privately considers selecting the next High Septon herself** and entertains replacing the current one if he continues to obstruct her. Her willingness to control religious succession is explicit; no new removal is carried out here.
14. **Cersei asks Taena to bring her son Russell from Longtable to court** as Tommen's peer, envisioning loyalty analogous to Robert and Ned's friendship and an emotional counterweight to Margaery's influence.
15. **The proposed boyhood friendship doubles as political leverage.** Cersei stresses Russell is Taena's only child and would be nearby; Taena expresses concern he knows no home but Longtable. No agreement to foster him is shown.
16. **Cersei remembers her own difficult arrival at court**, her aunt's lesson to act as a lioness, Joffrey's isolation and his search for a father figure in Sandor. Those are Cersei's retrospective interpretations of her family, not proof of Joffrey's motives.
17. **Melara enters her childhood memory as a companion who fell into a well.** This is a relevant unresolved death reference, but the chapter does **not** depict Cersei causing it or specify the circumstances.
18. At mention of a second child, **Cersei remembers Joffrey dying and Maggy's crowns, shrouds and valonqar prophecy**, including a drop of blood in a candle flame. Her sense of being endangered shapes ostensibly unrelated conversations.
19. **Sparrows impede the litter** on the crowded ascent. Cersei orders Meryn Trant to clear a route *gently*, not from sympathy but because she fears a repetition of the earlier King's Landing riot.
20. **Cersei recognizes ceremonial blessing as politically meaningful despite considering it empty religion.** She invokes Aegon I's Oldtown anointing as the convention establishing royal legitimacy in the Faith's eyes.
21. **The Faith's financial grievance arises from her own policy.** In AFFC 17 she suspended Faith and Iron Bank repayment to finance a new fleet; here she blames Braavosi demands for worsening **Gyles Rosby's** condition and insists construction had to proceed.
22. Cersei imagines **ten large royal dromonds** as an independent naval counterweight to the Tyrell-aligned Redwynes. **Lord Tywin** will be a flagship with twice as many oars as *King Robert's Hammer*; **Sweet Cersei** is to carry a gilded image of her in armor.
23. **The other stated projected ship names** are *Brave Joffrey*, *Lady Joanna*, *Lioness*, *Queen Margaery*, *Golden Rose*, *Lord Renly*, *Lady Olenna* and *Princess Myrcella*. She let Tommen choose the final five, rejecting his attempted name **Moon Boy** in favor of Myrcella at Aurane's urging. Names are plans for ships under construction, not proof the fleet is operational.
24. **The plaza is physically occupied** by hundreds of displaced sparrows amid cookfires, tents, makeshift huts, animals and refuse. Cersei has heard numerical reports from Qyburn but is visibly startled by the real crowd.
25. **Osfryd Kettleblack is now commanding the gold cloaks** and proposes summoning them to clear a path; Cersei recalls Pycelle and other councillors objected to his lack of seasoned command experience. It is an operative personnel arrangement, not evidence Osfryd is qualified.
26. **Pycelle also objected to requesting a Dornish master-at-arms**, precisely because it could offend the Tyrells; Cersei welcomes that political affront. The proposed trainer has not yet arrived.
27. **Cersei refuses to remain in the litter and walks toward the sept**, with Osmund and Meryn beside her and Osfryd guarding the litter. Her public confidence does not erase her stated recognition of the danger posed by the crowd.
28. **Cersei notices her older formal dress is now tight at the middle.** This is a physical clothing observation, not sufficient evidence of pregnancy or an unexplained disease.
29. **She recalls her wedding to Robert on the same plaza**, when the public cheered and she briefly felt happy; seeing Jaime's unhappy face ended that moment. The contrast with today's hostility is explicit.
30. **The marble statue of Baelor is surrounded by a mound of skulls and bones**, some retaining flesh. A one-legged spokesman **claims** they were recovered from murdered religious men and women throughout the realm, including violated Silent Sisters; the physical display is verified, individual provenances are not independently tested here.
31. **Cersei publicly blames Stannis, his red priestess and northern old-god worshipers** for the outrages. This is an accusation tailored to her political needs, not evidence that they committed each reported atrocity.
32. **The petitioners explicitly ask for protection of living people and sacred places, not merely revenge.** Cersei promises retribution and presents Tommen's compassion as sufficient, missing or ignoring the practical request.
33. **A man with a painted seven-pointed star challenges Tommen's fitness as a protector**. Another demands knights place Faith obligations ahead of their worldly commanders. Both reveal that the crowd's claims concern sovereignty as well as personal safety.
34. **A sparrow calls Tommen an inadequate boy king.** Meryn begins to draw his sword; Cersei intervenes because she sees that fighting among the vastly more numerous armed crowd could tear them apart. Her restraint is tactical, not evidence she accepts the criticism.
35. **Armed men wearing red stars bar the sept entrance**, demanding the Kingsguard leave their swords. The greybeard distinguishes the gods' house from the king's; the Faith is already exercising control over sacred space before any new legislation.
36. **Cersei goes inside without her armed escorts** rather than provoke violence. This is a concrete, limited assertion of the High Septon's authority over royal practice.
37. **Septon Raynard and other Most Devout are physically scrubbing the floor**, Raynard's hands blistered and bleeding. Cersei's former court-friendly contacts have been subordinated to the new leader's conception of service.
38. **The newly chosen High Septon is barefoot and simply dressed**, doing the same manual work. He calls cleaning a form of prayer pleasing to the Smith, paralleling the importance Septon Meribald accords the Smith in AFFC 25 without proving organizational coordination.
39. **Septon Torbert is in a penitent's cell on bread and water**, according to the new High Septon, because of his conspicuous corpulence in a starving realm. The described discipline is harsh and not simply egalitarian voluntary labor.
40. **The High Septon calls for rulers to bow to the Seven**, while Cersei resists kneeling in the dirty hall. The exchange previews a practical disagreement over who can command whom.
41. **The magnificent crystal-and-gold crown Tywin gave the previous High Septon has been sold**, the new High Septon says, with rings, luxurious robes and other treasures to feed the poor. Lack of a crown is visible; the particular transactions and charitable distribution are his own account.
42. **Qyburn's network reports that Septon Luceon was nine votes short of election when sparrows broke in**, bearing their favored priest with axes. This is a significant report of pressure on the Most Devout, but the exact vote count and coercion are **secondhand intelligence**, not a witnessed ballot.
43. **In the great sept the Mother and Maid have very large numbers of candles**, while the Stranger has fewer than ten. The contrast in petitionary practice may reflect devotees' fears; it does not prove anything metaphysical about the Seven.
44. **Rough hedge knights pray over weapons at the Warrior's altar**, while many sparrows worship the Mother. The movement already possesses a martial constituency and symbols before formal restoration.
45. **The High Septon kneels at the Crone's altar and leaves Cersei little choice but to kneel beside him.** A change in posture makes Cersei's court rank less controlling inside the sept.
46. **Cersei demands removal of the displaced sparrows**, and the High Septon answers that they have nowhere safe to return. His practical history of ministering to about fifty now-ruined villages explains his identification with the refugees.
47. **The High Septon identifies Baelor's plaza as the site of Ned Stark's execution** and argues it was bloodshed rather than dirt that profaned the space. Cersei regrets the *location* of the execution yet maintains Ned was a traitor.
48. **The priest cites Baelor as an example of forgiveness**; Cersei privately counters with his treatment of his sisters, whom he confined. She remembers tormenting the young Tyrion after hearing that story. Her abhorrence of coercion is selective.
49. **The High Septon reports broad devastation, hunger, murdered clergy and destroyed rural communities**. His claims align with firsthand devastation in earlier Arya, Brienne and Jaime POVs, but specific culprit allegations here remain attributed testimony.
50. **Sparrows blame some bands displaying lions**, the High Septon says. The AFFC 27 Jaime POV independently confirms that Amory Lorch and other Lannister-aligned forces brutalized areas claiming direct crown allegiance. Cersei's exclusive Stannis-and-northmen explanation is unsustainable.
51. **The High Septon specifically attributes horrifying Saltpans crimes to the Hound**, including a septon's killing and the assault and mutilation of a twelve-year-old novice. These are reports transmitted by sparrows; they do not identify the individual under Sandor's recognizable helm.
52. **Cersei depicts Sandor as a dismissed traitor currently fighting for Beric Dondarrion** and denies crown responsibility. Her claim does not establish Sandor's present location or allegiance, especially given AFFC 20, 25 and ASOS 74 identity conflicts.
53. **The High Septon invokes Jaehaerys's reported promise to protect the Faith**. Cersei confirms it aloud while admitting privately she does not know what the king promised. Her assent is political improvisation, not validation of the historical wording.
54. **The High Septon distinguishes delaying a blessing from refusing it**. He proposes prayer and fasting before blessing Tommen; no blessing has yet occurred at this stage.
55. **He recounts an Oldtown precedent before Aegon's Conquest:** an earlier High Septon reportedly prayed and foresaw destruction if Oldtown resisted, after which Lord Hightower chose not to fight and the Faith anointed Aegon. This is historical-religious testimony; neither the vision's supernatural origin nor every detail is independently confirmed here.
56. **Cersei privately imagines imprisoning and starving the obstructive priest**. Her thought about treating him as she did the last High Septon is especially concerning and may imply involvement in a predecessor's removal, but **the specific act, timing and method are not demonstrated in this scene**. Compare AFFC 7/17 before declaring a proven killing.
57. **The High Septon asks how Tommen will actually protect priests, septas and shrines** in a war-torn realm. Cersei cannot assign knights to every roadside location and does not intend to divert her armies to the work.
58. **Cersei suggests that sparrows defend themselves**, and the priest objects that **Maegor's laws forbade armed religious orders**. Cersei recognizes a legislative lever that would make the Faith responsible for much of its own protection while relieving the crown of a large military obligation.
59. **Cersei deliberately floats repeal**, drawing the High Septon to propose restoration of the **Warrior's Sons** and **Poor Fellows**. His response indicates long-suppressed institutional ambitions; her proposal creates more than a momentary crowd-dispersal bargain.
60. **The new High Septon promises that a restored Faith Militant will openly acknowledge Tommen**. This is an offer linking spiritual legitimation to coercive institutional power, not proof the order will obey the crown's commands in future.
61. **Cersei makes a concrete debt-for-armed-Faith bargain**, offering royal repeal in exchange for remission of the crown's debt to the Faith. She estimates nine hundred thousand dragons; the High Septon specifies **900,674 gold dragons**, money he says could feed the poor and rebuild septs.
62. **The High Septon verbally agrees to forgive the debt and bless Tommen**; he anticipates the Warrior's Sons escorting him and the sparrows becoming Poor Fellows. Cersei promises **papers drawn up and Tommen's royal seal**. The promise is on-page; this chapter does not show those instruments executed, armed orders fully mustered or a completed public blessing.
63. **Cersei treats the exchange as a triple victory**: sparrows supposedly leave the streets, she gains legitimacy, and the treasury escapes nearly a million gold dragons of obligation. Each expectation exceeds what has yet been observed in implementation.
64. **Cersei explains the ancient orders to Taena**: Warrior's Sons were land-and-wealth-renouncing knights with rainbow cloaks, silver armor and crystalline sword pommels; the larger and poorer **Poor Fellows** traveled armed along roads bearing red stars on white.
65. **Accounts portray those orders as uncompromising toward enemies of the Faith**, with stories of holy knights, fanatics, magicians and dragon slayers. These are Cersei's historical/legendary descriptions, not verified proof of actual medieval sorcery or dragon fights.
66. **Cersei sees Stannis and Melisandre as the most useful targets** for the restored orders; Taena encourages that inference. No binding condition limiting the Faith Militant to crown-designated enemies is stated.
67. **Cersei and Taena celebrate with hippocras** while returning in the litter. Their celebration is evidence of their confidence, not independent verification that the bargain has made Tommen safer.
68. **They encounter Margaery, her cousins, Loras and their escort returning from the kingswood**, carrying autumn flowers. Cersei catalogs Elinor and Alyn Ambrose, Alla and Tallad, Megga and Mark Mullendore, Meredyth Crane and Janna Fossoway with the Redwyne twins, Lambert Turnberry, Jalabhar Xho and the Blue Bard.
69. **The Tyrell court visibly integrates young aristocrats, entertainers and sworn protectors**, contrasting with Cersei's reliance on paid informants and Kettleblack intermediaries. Cersei again interprets ordinary public sociability as plotting against her.
70. **Loras no longer trains Tommen at arms**, yet spends time with the boy. Cersei cannot prevent the affection and interprets repeated visits as a direct contest for Tommen's loyalty.
71. **Margaery takes frequent public rides, boat trips, hunts, craft-market visits and alms-giving outings**, cultivating actual smallfolk contact. The schedule is reported through Cersei's informants; the immediate autumn excursion and large company are directly visible.
72. **Tommen wants to accompany Margaery** and argues that ordinary people will love rulers who mingle with them. Cersei cites the riot after Myrcella's departure and the killing of an earlier High Septon, emphasizing risk while ignoring the political benefit.
73. **Cersei invents a fully developed Tyrell plot from a single unexplained Gardener coin** found in missing gaoler Rugen's chambers: Margaery and Olenna allegedly arranged Joffrey's death, bribed the turnkeys, released Tyrion and sheltered Tyrion and Sansa at Highgarden.
74. **Reader-level disconfirmation is exceptionally strong.** In AFFC 8 Jaime knows **Rugen was Varys** and admits coercing Varys to free Tyrion; the Sansa/Alayne POVs establish **Sansa is in the Vale**, not Highgarden. The coin remains real but unprovenanced; Cersei's new conspiracy is not merely speculative—it conflicts with established facts.
75. **Margaery invites Cersei and Tommen on future outings and offers to share court burdens**, explicitly wishing to end talk of rivalry. Her public behavior could be diplomacy, positioning or sincere courtesy; Cersei reads only hostile competition.
76. **Cersei remembers Robert's hunting trips as opportunities for trysts with Jaime**. She ominously reminds Margaery that a royal hunting trip can conceal mortal danger, without depicting any current harm to Margaery.
77. **Margaery answers that Loras protects her**, prompting Cersei to remember Taena's earlier sexually suggestive insinuation and laugh. The implied accusation about siblings lacks evidence and is an instance of Cersei projecting her own secret onto others.
78. **The chapter closes with Cersei promising Margaery she will share the joke later.** Her private triumph has produced both a militarized, newly empowered Faith and heightened commitment to an unsupported Tyrell conspiracy; no actual accusation or arrest of Margaery occurs in this chapter.

## Cross-POV and historical comparisons

- **AFFC 7/8 (Cersei/Jaime) → AFFC 28:** The old Gardener coin was found by Qyburn in Rugen's rooms, but **Jaime knows Rugen was Varys** and forced Varys to help Tyrion escape. An inference from a coin's old Reach imagery cannot override that knowledge. Earlier white-cloak murders also prevent retrospective recovery of easy gaoler testimony.
- **AFFC 10/23 (Sansa/Alayne) → AFFC 28:** Sansa remains hidden in the Vale under Littlefinger's Alayne identity. Cersei's sudden conviction Sansa is safely installed in Highgarden is false at reader level even though Cersei feels she has resolved the mystery.
- **AFFC 17/24 (Cersei) → AFFC 28:** The previous suspension of payments to the Faith and Iron Bank creates the negotiation. Ten warships remain under construction; the naming plan and debt remission do not establish completion of a functional navy.
- **AFFC 12 (Cersei) and AFFC 25 (Brienne) → AFFC 28:** Reports from the island septry and Brienne's informants connect the Hound legend to Saltpans but do not settle who wore the helmet or conducted the assaults. Treat reported victim suffering seriously without awarding responsibility to the wrong individual.
- **AFFC 20 (Brienne), ASOS 74 (Arya), AFFC 27 (Jaime) → AFFC 28:** Rorge was reported heading to Saltpans, the original crossroads fight involved Sandor and Arya, and Jaime's garrison cannot reliably apportion three deaths. The High Septon's detailed report may contain genuine atrocity facts and still misidentify the attacker.
- **AFFC 25 (Brienne) → AFFC 28:** Septon Meribald's wandering service and interest in the Smith echo the High Septon's self-described ministry to lost villages and insistence that labor itself is prayer. This is a thematic and social comparison, not evidence the two priests secretly coordinate.
- **AFFC 27 (Jaime) → AFFC 28:** Jaime sees Lorch's persecution of crownland vassals and disfavored smallfolk; the High Septon now transmits testimony of Lannister lions devastating the Faith. Cersei disregards direct crown responsibility despite appeals for protection.
- **AGOT Ned execution and King's Landing riots → AFFC 28:** The same Great Sept plaza saw a highly visible royal beheading; political use of public religion has cumulative consequences. Ceremonies can legitimize a ruler only if the communities and clergy accept the bargain.
- **Faith Militant's legal history:** The High Septon invokes Maegor's anti-militant decrees and an Aegon-era Oldtown precedent. Do not treat every ancient timeline, prophecy or boast from interested representatives as independently proved; here the significant contemporary fact is Cersei's readiness to **reverse the legal constraint**.
- **Maggy's prophecy and Cersei's character:** She recalls Joffrey's death and child-shroud fear while seeking leverage over Taena and Tommen. Interpret later predictions against direct outcomes, not as certainty that each frightened association is fate.

## Priority disconfirmation tests and causal risks

1. **Highest priority — Faith Militant legal implementation.** Find the first on-page signed decree, actual order formation, patrols, weapons and command structures. This chapter's deal does not prove the High Septon will answer royal orders, nor that the sparrows have left King's Landing.
2. **Highest priority — who owes and who commands.** Confirm whether the financial release becomes legally binding and whether royal strategy has exchanged nearly a million dragons in obligations for a permanent independent armed religious constituency.
3. **High — Saltpans accountability.** Identify each source transmitting reports of the septon and assaulted girl; distinguish the original eye-witness, a religious relay and Cersei's interested Sandor/Beric attribution.
4. **High — Cersei's false Tyrell model.** Compare Varys's known guise, Jaime's coercion, and Sansa's Vale position against Cersei's imagined Highgarden refuge. Investigate coin custody and opportunities for planting before alleging a specific conspiracy.
5. **High — Taena's motivations.** Separate what she actually witnessed about Renly and Margaery from her suggestive phrasing about Loras; weigh courtly manipulation and common gossip as alternatives. Her access and Russell's possible fostering remain consequential.
6. **High — prior High Septon.** Cross-check Cersei's menacing 'last' remark with prior appointment, death, Qyburn information and any direct order. Do not assign a poisoning method from Baelor's separate historical rumor.
7. **Medium — naval fantasy versus capacity.** Track construction, crews, exact ship names, Aurane's command, financing and Crown's bank obligations; ships with ceremonial names are not deployed fleets.
8. **Medium — legitimacy and PR.** Compare Margaery's actual public outings and Loras's mentorship with Cersei's isolation of Tommen and attempted propaganda, without assuming Margaery's motives are universally innocent.
9. **Medium — childhood and prophecy.** The remembered Melara death, Joffrey's solitude and her aunt's lioness maxim explain Cersei's reflexes; no unsourced murder conclusion should be laundered out of her partial flashback.
10. **Medium — Faith faction diversity.** Distinguish unarmed suffering refugees, armed temple guards, ascetic clerics, former Most Devout and mounted hedge knights. Different groups sharing seven-pointed imagery need not share motives or chain of command.

**End-state receipt:** The chapter begins during the queen's climb to the Great Sept and closes after a tense, outwardly polite reunion with Margaery. A verbal agreement on repealing Maegor's restrictions and forgiving **900,674 dragons** is directly established; future implementation, political beneficiaries and religious enforcement remain open. Cersei's discovery of a supposed comprehensive Tyrell conspiracy remains **reader-disconfirmed**, not promoted to canon.
