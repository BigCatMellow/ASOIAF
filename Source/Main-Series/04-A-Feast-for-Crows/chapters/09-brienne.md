## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Brienne reaches Duskendale still using the invented 'sister' description for Sansa.** She again discovers that improvising a cover story is harder for her than straightforward oath-bound conduct.
2. **The skinny boy on the piebald horse appears yet again.** Brienne has now seen the same rider near Rosby, outside Duskendale and inside the town, turning a background detail into a persistent tail.
3. **Duskendale's market is full of looted arms, armor and clothing from the battle.** Northern, Reach, stormland and other sigils are being sold cheaply, showing how quickly war dead become an economic resource.
4. **Brienne refuses to replace Jaime's heavy oak shield even though lighter pine would be easier.** Its personal provenance matters to her in addition to practical protection.
5. **She has the Lothston bat painted over because those arms attract suspicion.** Her public identity is becoming deliberately less traceable while the underlying shield remains Jaime's.
6. **The painter's imaginary castle door includes small animals hidden in the foliage.** The detail is direct artwork, not a coded prophecy unless later evidence supplies a causal link.
7. **Brienne asks for an old shield design remembered from her father's armory.** She recalls a green tree and the path of a falling star on cracked, faded paint. The original owner's identity is not established in this chapter and should remain an open provenance clue.
8. **Duskendale preserves diluted Darklyn descent among common local families.** Darkes, Darkwoods and Dargoods claim connections after the lordly Darklyns were destroyed.
9. **The Seven Swords inn commemorates seven Darklyn Kingsguards.** The transformation of dynastic glory into an inn sign reinforces how completely the house's political power has vanished.
10. **The maester gives Brienne a detailed account of the Defiance of Duskendale.** He says local tradition blames Denys Darklyn's Myrish wife Serala, while the actual political responsibility is more complex.
11. **Serala's punishment was exceptionally gendered and brutal.** The story says Aerys had her mutilated before burning her, an important example of how misogynistic blame and royal vengeance enter local memory.
12. **Barristan's rescue of Aerys ended Darklyn leverage.** Denys surrendered rather than let Tywin storm the town, after which Aerys exterminated the principal Darklyn and Hollard lines.
13. **Dontos survived only because Barristan asked Aerys to spare him.** He was a child whose father had died before the Defiance and had no role in it.
14. **Dontos did not return to Duskendale and had no surviving local power base.** This materially weakens Brienne's assumption that following his family origin would lead to Sansa.
15. **The Duskendale maester suggests many possible destinations but has no direct sighting.** Oldtown, Essos, Dorne and the Wall are speculation rather than leads.
16. **Brella had previously told Brienne that Sansa often prayed in the godswood.** Brella therefore guessed Sansa went north, again without sighting her after the wedding.
17. **Brienne independently considers Sansa's surviving blood relations.** She thinks of Jon at the Wall, Edmure and Brynden in the riverlands and Lysa in the Vale.
18. **She recognizes the Eyrie as the easiest plausible family refuge.** This is notably close to the truth: Sansa is in the Vale, although Brienne does not know Lysa is dead or that Petyr controls her.
19. **A new reported lead pulls Brienne away from that correct inference.** A dwarf holy brother recalls a ragged fool at Maidenpool seeking passage across the narrow sea for three people.
20. **The holy brother cannot securely identify the fool as Dontos.** Details are partial, and the 'three' travelers do not fit Brienne's existing model cleanly.
21. **The same witness names Nimble Dick as a man who later boasted of 'fooling a fool.'** This creates a concrete investigative lead, but the meaning of the boast remains unknown.
22. **Brienne hypothesizes Tyrion might have been the third traveler.** This follows her incorrect model that Tyrion coerced or accompanied Sansa; it is reasoning from incomplete information.
23. **News of Tywin's death has already reached Duskendale.** Local discussion immediately raises the succession question of who will rule during Tommen's minority.
24. **Jaime remains publicly defined as an oathbreaker.** Brienne hears the innkeeper reject the idea of Jaime as ruler, contrasting with her increasingly sympathetic private view of him.
25. **Brienne dreams Renly's death again, but the dying man becomes Jaime.** The dream is psychological evidence of how Jaime has entered the emotional space once dominated by Renly; it is not a prophecy of Jaime's death.
26. **Her newly painted shield evokes a childhood memory from Tarth.** The green tree and falling-star path are important enough to survive years in her memory despite the original shield's faded condition.
27. **The northern dead at Duskendale lie in a mass grave marked only by a crude 'wolves' marker, while Tarly's dead received honored burial.** The asymmetry records the victor's control over memory.
28. **Brienne prays for the northern dead, Catelyn and Robb.** Her Sansa search remains rooted in her oath to Catelyn rather than reward or political advantage.
29. **She remembers Catelyn describing Sansa's auburn hair and Arya.** The recollection strengthens her personal commitment while also showing how little direct knowledge she has of Sansa herself.
30. **The road fork stands beside ruins bearing three golden crowns.** Brienne recognizes the place as a former Hollard castle, physically connecting the landscape to the arms the maester described.
31. **Brienne's past betrothal to Humfrey Wagstaff ended after he demanded conventional female obedience.** She accepted his proposed 'chastisement' only if he could defeat her and then broke his collarbone and ribs in a blunted fight.
32. **Ser Goodwin's training explains Brienne's combat method.** He taught stamina, caution and exploitation of male opponents' pride, giving a grounded basis for her success rather than treating her strength as the whole explanation.
33. **Brienne finally catches the piebald-horse rider and learns he is Podrick Payne.** The apparent spy is not working for Cersei or Varys.
34. **Pod identifies himself as Tyrion's squire from the Blackwater.** His presence supplies Brienne with a living connection to Tyrion that she did not know existed.
35. **Pod says he is following Brienne because Brella told him she seeks Sansa.** He hopes finding Sansa may somehow lead back to Tyrion, who left him behind.
36. **Pod's loyalty persists despite abandonment and danger.** Like Brienne, he is pursuing a person to whom he feels bound without a clear plan for what success would produce.
37. **End state:** Brienne leaves Duskendale headed toward the Maidenpool/Nimble Dick lead with Podrick revealed behind her, even though her own earlier Vale inference was geographically closer to Sansa's actual location.

## Cross-POV implications

- **Brienne is being diverted away from her best inference by a more concrete but weaker lead.** The Vale is correct at broad geographic level; Maidenpool has a named witness but no verified Sansa connection.
- **The old shield deserves provenance research without premature identification.** Its presence in Selwyn Tarth's armory and distinctive imagery are evidence; any famous former owner remains a theory until separately sourced.
- **Podrick changes the search party's information base.** He knows Tyrion directly but does not know where Tyrion went, while Brienne knows Jaime said the northern Arya is false.
- **Public Jaime and private Jaime are diverging sharply.** Duskendale still remembers 'Kingslayer/oathbreaker' while Brienne dreams of failing to save him.
