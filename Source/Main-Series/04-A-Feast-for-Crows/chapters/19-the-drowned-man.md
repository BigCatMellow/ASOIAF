# AFFC 19 — The Drowned Man: the kingsmoot, Asha's peace platform and Euron's dragon horn

- Status: FIRST PASS COMPLETE — independent challenge open.
- Primary source: complete [AFFC 19 The Drowned Man](../text/020-the-drowned-man.md), read 2026-10-18.

## Complete narrative and causal analysis
1. After the confrontation at Victarion's feast, Aeron submerges himself in cold seawater to overcome his terror of Euron. A recurring memory of a creaking iron hinge and a door evokes a trauma he refuses to name; what happened behind that door is not revealed in this chapter.
2. Aeron ascends the hill to the forty-four great stone ribs called Nagga's bones. He remembers the Grey King legend: a giant sea dragon, a thousand-and-seven-year reign, a mermaid wife, living fire, seaweed tapestries, and a tooth-made throne. These belong to religious tradition, not established eyewitness history.
3. Aeron prays all night that the Drowned God will guide the captains to reject Euron. He perceives waves and wind as a divine response. Their divine origin is his interpretation, not independently confirmed.
4. Dawn reveals a broad gathering including common shipworkers and fishermen, warriors, captains, thralls, salt wives, women and children. The claimed kings' champions and voters occupy the hill while most others watch from below.
5. Aeron begins the assembly by proclaiming Balon's death and recalling the Old Way. His kingsmoot is meant to legitimate a successor while excluding a godless candidate.
6. Aeron hopes impatient Euron will claim first and exhaust interest, but Euron waits. This is a concrete tactical defeat for Aeron's expectations before any horn appears.
7. **Gylbert Farwynd of Lonely Light** presents first, accompanied by presumed sons. Lonely Light is described as eight days' sail northwest of Great Wyk. Rumors that Farwynds can skinchange into marine mammals are community hearsay, not confirmed abilities.
8. Gylbert promises a distant land beyond the Sunset Sea where winter, scarcity and death cannot touch the people. His proposed ten thousand ships invoke Nymeria, but the destination's existence is unverified and the claim does not win meaningful support.
9. Gylbert offers sealskins, walrus ivory, whalebone ornaments and other prizes. Some spectators take gifts, yet most captains withhold endorsement; gifts are not equivalent to votes.
10. **Erik Ironmaker**, an elderly former warrior, claims to offer strength, wisdom and many heirs. His age and martial notoriety are not contested, but Asha publicly challenges him to stand, and he cannot.
11. Asha's intervention abruptly collapses Erik's momentum, demonstrating how public competence tests can override genealogical or reputational arguments. Euron laughs but has not yet made his own claim.
12. **Dunstan Drumm** challenges the monopoly of Greyjoy blood, arguing Vickon Greyjoy was made lord rather than king after the black line ended. This is a direct constitutional argument within ironborn tradition.
13. Drumm bears **Red Rain**, an actual Valyrian-steel sword, reportedly taken from an armored knight by Hilmar Drumm using a wooden cudgel. The origin account is family tradition, while the sword's presence is witnessed.
14. Andrik the Unsmiling and Drumm's sons champion his candidacy. Drumm's long family recitation and modest gifts fail to sustain early audience interest.
15. Aeron cues **Victarion**, praises his fearlessness and performs a public saltwater blessing. Victarion has three champions: Ralf the Limper, Red Ralf Stonehouse and Nute the Barber.
16. Victarion offers continuity with Balon's war rather than a different policy. He claims practical naval victories and says the ironborn can expect more of Balon's achievements. His guarded chests from AFFC 18 contain substantial plunder that helps win shouts.
17. **Asha interrupts Victarion** and acknowledges his martial strengths while ridiculing his ability and stressing Euron's seniority and her own status as Balon's surviving child.
18. Asha's champions are **Qarl the Maid, Tristifer Botley and Ser Harras Harlaw**, bearing Nightfall. Their identities provide tangible information about her existing coalition.
19. Asha directly confronts the human cost of Balon's rebellions, including bereaved families, ruined settlements and defeat. Her analogizing Robb Stark's tactical victories with strategic defeat develops the same argument she made privately to Victarion.
20. **Asha stages her own chests as political theater**: stones for the Stony Shore, pinecones for Deepwood and turnips for Winterfell. This contrasts the apparent spoils of inland occupation with what those conquests actually yield.
21. She names Harmund Sharp's son Harrag as one of those lost at Winterfell and asks whether the fathers present would sacrifice more sons for such gains. This is a specific casualty appeal rather than abstract antiwar sentiment.
22. Asha promises peace, a durable foothold at Sea Dragon Point and the Stony Shore, and northern allies against the Iron Throne. These are **proposed outcomes**, not an already secured pact with the north.
23. **Rodrik the Reader and Baelor Blacktyde publicly endorse Asha**. She draws Tristifer, Harlaws, some Goodbrothers and others beyond the small faction Aeron expected. Nevertheless many remain silent or vocally support Victarion.
24. The Asha–Victarion shouting becomes contentious, with jostling and objects thrown. Aeron attributes the discord to the Storm God, a theological reading of political disagreement.
25. An extraordinarily loud black horn abruptly silences the gathering. The instrument has a twisted shape, is taller than the man holding it, and carries bands of red gold and dark steel marked with Valyrian glyphs.
26. **The glyphs appear to blaze as the horn sounds**. The noise produces distress and physical alarm, while the blower suffers visible burns and bleeding. These are observed phenomena, not merely Euron's assertion.
27. The blower is a heavily built member of Euron's retinue, with bird-of-prey tattoo and ornaments. At the end he nearly collapses and smoke rises from the instrument. Whether the display actually binds dragons is not demonstrated here.
28. **Euron climbs beneath Nagga's ribs with all attention on him.** Aeron feels watched by Euron's hidden eye, a subjective impression; the visible eyepatch is not itself proof of a magical second sight.
29. Euron frames his eligibility through Greyjoy descent, his unbeaten record, extensive travel, and a proposed scope of conquest that far exceeds Balon's northern campaign or Asha's negotiated foothold.
30. Euron promises wealthy southern targets and essentially all of Westeros. He borrows Drowned God rhetoric despite Aeron's religious objections. For an instant Aeron himself is attracted by the ambition, demonstrating its persuasive force.
31. Asha objects that the islands cannot hold the North, much less Westeros. Euron invokes Aegon the Conqueror and claims that his horn will supply the missing dragon power.
32. Euron **claims** he retrieved the horn from ruined Valyria, that its Valyrian enchantments can command dragons, and that three living dragons can be found. The existence of dragons is consistent with the wider series; the instrument's origin and command mechanism remain unverified.
33. Euron's followers scatter enormous wealth, and prominent captains including Hotho Harlaw, Gorold Goodbrother and Erik Ironmaker take up his acclamation. Aeron hears a mass cry naming Euron king.
34. Aeron's attempt to use the kingsmoot against Euron has failed. His closing interior experience is religious silence and the same hinge-sound trauma, tying a public political defeat to a private dread.
