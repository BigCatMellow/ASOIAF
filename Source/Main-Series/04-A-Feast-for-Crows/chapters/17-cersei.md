# AFFC 17 — Cersei: council appointments, debt suspension, false Stark and covert northern intervention

- Status: FIRST-PASS COMPLETE; independent challenge and cross-POV validation open.
- Primary source: [AFFC 17 Cersei](../text/018-cersei.md), read in full 2026-10-18.

## Close reading and chronology
1. Three bounty hunters deliver the severed head of an innocent dwarf whom they mistook for Tyrion. It is the third offered head; the previous one belonged to an unattractive child. Cersei acknowledges the victim's innocence but chooses not to punish the hunters, fearing that would discourage further searches.
2. Qyburn's informers search Oldtown, Gulltown, Dorne and the Free Cities. Cersei correctly cautions that Tyrion might still be hidden in King's Landing. Her recurring dream of Maggy's prophecy intensifies the obsession.
3. Qyburn reports that Gregor Clegane's skull has been cleaned and prepared for delivery to Doran Martell. Cersei intends Balon Swann to carry it to Sunspear on another, undisclosed errand. The severed head and its provenance should be audited against later evidence, not assumed to resolve every question about Gregor's remains.
4. Cersei has appointed Qyburn to Varys's former intelligence role and considers his network interchangeable because informants seek payment. The operating competence of the new network remains untested.
5. Boros Blount now serves as Tommen's food taster, a position Jaime arranged and that Boros finds humiliating.
6. The council has been purged of Tyrell allies and Kevan's supporters. Cersei styles Orton Merryweather justiciar, Gyles Rosby lord treasurer, Aurane Waters grand admiral, Qyburn intelligence chief and Harys Swyft Hand.
7. Swyft's appointment is deliberately coercive: he is Kevan's father-in-law, so Cersei views him as an indirect hostage constraining her uncle. This is a completed appointment, correcting AFFC 16's mere speculation.
8. Cersei sidelines young Tommen from affairs of state while using his royal seal. Her contempt for Jaime's disability and absent opinion also serves to reduce independent scrutiny.
9. Pycelle reports continued unrest at Sunspear despite Doran's arrest of the Sand Snakes. Sending Balon with the supposed Mountain head is framed as fulfillment of the crown's promised justice.
10. Approximately two thousand sparrows are said to be in the capital with more arriving. The Faith's choice of High Septon is unsettled: Torbert, Raynard, Ollidor and Luceon are mentioned as contenders, with accounts of campaigning among the Most Devout.
11. Qyburn treats the previous High Septon's death as unremarkable. His complacency and Cersei's expectation of a malleable successor should not be conflated with proof of how the septon died.
12. Cersei wants the next High Septon to condemn Tyrion and largely dismisses the sparrows as the Faith's problem if they do not openly advocate treason.
13. Cersei notices the young admiral's resemblance to Rhaegar and reads attraction into his behavior. Her recollection of being treated differently when disguised as Jaime as a child supplies an unusually concrete basis for her sense of gendered injustice.
14. Gyles Rosby has an increasingly alarming cough, including blood in his sputum. The monarchy's financial administration therefore depends on a visibly ill officeholder.
15. Six Vale magnates, including Bronze Yohn Royce and Lady Waynwood, have signed a declaration challenging Littlefinger's regency; forces gather below the Eyrie. Petyr seems unconcerned and asks the crown for Robert's old tapestries.
16. The council chooses non-intervention in the Vale except requiring that Petyr not be harmed. Cersei undervalues the strategic importance of the Vale and the meaning of Petyr's apparently trivial request.
17. Aurane reports that fewer than twelve royal vessels survived the Blackwater and proposes ten new dromonds. A possible ironborn alliance is debated but Cersei refuses, while her councillors demonstrate confusion about who rules the Iron Islands.
18. Rosby says the royal treasury cannot meet existing demands. Cersei orders repayment to the Faith and the Iron Bank of Braavos deferred until after the war, directing the withheld funds to shipbuilding.
19. Pycelle warns that the Iron Bank will enforce its claims; Cersei dismisses the risk because Braavos is distant. The deferral is an actual royal policy, not merely a suggestion, although neither the bank's future response nor the completed ships is observed.
20. Qyburn says popular preachers accuse the crown of complicity in the Red Wedding. He proposes letting a future Frey ruler punish some relatives to deflect blame; Cersei approves the idea as a hypothetical convenience, not a completed purge.
21. Aurane reports the Golden Company has broken its contract with Myr. Dockside rumor says Stannis hired it; Qyburn's Myrish sailors instead claim the company heads toward Volantis. These reports conflict, and no direct contractual explanation is available in this scene.
22. Cersei declares the war virtually won: Storm's End invested, Riverrun besieged, Redwyne's fleet proceeding toward Dragonstone. These are her strategic claims and expectations rather than independent confirmations of victory.
23. Cersei believes Bolton will soon secure the north with northern adherents and Frey support, and that Wyman Manderly is nearly hers through a plan to marry his granddaughters to Freys. Pycelle questions Manderly's allegiance.
24. Wyman demands the return of his captive heir before submission. Cersei admits she does not know if the man survived Gregor's custody and offers substitute vengeance should he be dead.
25. Cersei reports that Wyman forwarded Stannis's messages and imprisoned Stannis's envoy Davos. She has ordered Davos killed as proof of loyalty; this chapter does NOT show the execution taking place.
26. Cersei knows the 'Arya Stark' planned to marry Ramsay Bolton is a steward's child supplied by Littlefinger, not Ned's daughter. This is direct access to a central northern identity conspiracy.
27. Cersei still regards Sansa as alive and dangerous, wrongly accusing her of a role in Joffrey's death. Her obsession with Sansa contrasts with her willingness to manufacture a different Stark daughter.
28. Cersei recalls threatening Robert when he proposed bringing a daughter of his to court, and remembers his violent response. This is relevant firsthand interior history of the royal household, not evidence that she acted against the child.
29. Jon Snow has written to claim Watch neutrality and request assistance; Cersei instead interprets his accommodations to Stannis as treason. Pycelle acknowledges the Watch's traditional neutrality.
30. Councillors consider embargoing Watch recruits or redirecting convicts as oarsmen. Qyburn proposes sending one hundred purported recruits actually tasked with removing Jon from command; Cersei explicitly approves the covert plan.
31. Aurane reports eastern sailors talking of dragons. Cersei laughs off the warning rather than investigating, another instance of potentially consequential intelligence being screened by prejudice.
32. Taena tells Cersei that Horas and Hobber Redwyne are devoted to Margaery and fantasize about joining her Kingsguard. Cersei immediately sees a possible means of scandal against Margaery; Taena's reported story is not proof of misconduct.
33. Cersei privately recruits Osney Kettleblack to create compromising evidence against Margaery, intending his exposure to lead to punishment and removal from court. Her aim is to engineer a pretext, not uncover a demonstrated affair.
34. Cersei ties that scheme to the approved Wall plot: Osney would be sent north and given men to eliminate Jon, with a pardon and reward promised afterward. He verbally agrees; neither stage has happened.
35. Cersei plans for Osmund to catch his brother with Margaery, expecting his Kingsguard standing to protect the family from suspicion. This is her design rather than an observed event.
36. She instructs Taena to seed the notion of a secret admirer for Margaery while making the name seem discovered reluctantly. This is a distinct, completed act of disinformation through a likely double agent.
37. Cersei tries to draw information from Taena about a former lover, who is described in an anecdote as a Myrish seafarer. Whether the anecdote is truthful and whether the relationship is current remain uncertain.
38. Cersei ends the chapter dreaming of possessing Tyrion's head, extending the opening's fixation on erroneous bounty victims.

## Evidence boundaries and priority hypotheses
- Confirmed decisions: Qyburn/Swyft/Aurane and other council appointments, suspension of Iron Bank and Faith repayments, ten-dromond construction order, covert Jon mission approved in principle, Osney recruited for Margaery deception.
- Reported but not observed: Gregor skull identity, Davos execution, Golden Company destination, presumed Wyman allegiance, Redwyne arrival, sparrow count and new High Septon results.
- Strong insider fact: Cersei knows the Ramsay bride is not Arya. Audit the chain of Littlefinger's false identity and later northern responses.
- Most consequential causal risk: the Crown simultaneously alienates an influential foreign creditor, attacks the Faith's financial interests, neglects mass religious mobilization and diverts maritime intelligence resources.
- Competing POVs: Arianne's Golden Company interpretation versus Cersei's Myrish crew report; Brienne's Vale inference versus Crown non-intervention; Jon's Watch neutrality claim versus Cersei's lethal interpretation.
