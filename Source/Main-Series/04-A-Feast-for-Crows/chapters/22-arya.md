# AFFC 22 — Arya: the price of being No One, Needle, and Cat of the Canals

- Status: FIRST PASS COMPLETE; independent challenge open.
- Primary source: [AFFC 22 Arya](../text/023-arya.md), fully read 2026-10-18.

## Chronological close reading

1. Arya continues her private names prayer: Gregor, Dunsen, Raff, Ilyn Payne, Meryn Trant and Cersei, while intending to identify Freys later. The kindly man identifies this private vengeance as incompatible with serving the Many-Faced God.
2. The kindly man says deciding who dies belongs to the deity, not a servant. This is a stated institutional ethic and may be in tension with the practical economics of later killings.
3. House members include the kindly man, waif, three acolytes, two elderly servants and cook Umma. Their names are concealed or unused; the kindly man alone speaks the Common Tongue to Arya initially.
4. Arya repeats 'no one' but privately enumerates her Stark identity, family and Nymeria. Her various aliases never displaced Arya of Winterfell in her inner self.
5. A blind junior acolyte senses extinguished candles by scent and local warmth, training Arya to notice evidence other than sight; this ability is demonstrated without proving supernatural sight.
6. Temple worshipers use thirty divine images including Weeping Woman, Lion of Night, Hooded Wayfarer, Bakkalon, Moon-Pale Maiden, Merling King and the Stranger. Patterns of offerings vary by occupation and social class.
7. Some worshipers drink from the black cup and die peacefully; many visitors do not. The kindly man says the god has many faces and that death is release from unbearable suffering.
8. The temple has three underground levels. The deepest sanctum is forbidden to Arya. Upper vaults contain weapons, armor, clothing and goods left by the dead; Arya imagines additional treasure chambers without seeing them.
9. Servant work includes preparing food, cleaning, checking alcoves for corpses, sorting possessions and counting money. Arya learns useful Braavosi food vocabulary in Umma's kitchen.
10. The kindly man describes candles whose smells summon home memories. Arya internally remembers Winterfell in unusually extensive sensory detail but conceals the reaction; he identifies her denial as a lie.
11. The kindly man repeatedly offers Arya a genuine choice to leave, while emphasizing that departure means she cannot return. Remaining entails unconditional obedience, not a place to shelter simply because she is orphaned.
12. Corpses are collected from temple alcoves, washed and stripped. Their bodies descend to the priests-only sanctum. Arya does not know what happens there and briefly fears the meat she eats might be human; the kindly man says it is pork.
13. Arya retains clothes and gifts from the Titan's Daughter, money, dagger, boots, belt, and above all Needle in her cell. She still practices swordplay by candlelight, revealing continuity with Syrio's teaching.
14. The waif notices sword practice; the kindly man demands Arya surrender the belongings as evidence of her old identity. He says previous aliases were costumes rather than transformations.
15. The kindly man explains that Jaqen's iron coin purchased passage to the temple but not induction. He describes the price of becoming a Faceless Man as the total surrender of personal self.
16. He tempts or tests her with other possible lives, including service, marriage, passage back to Westeros and courtesan houses. These are verbal alternatives, not choices Arya takes.
17. His claim that women seldom serve because bringing life and bringing death are incompatible is the priest's ideological position; the waif and other women's actual service complicates a universal rule.
18. Arya discards her sailor gifts, clothes, money, dagger and belt in the Braavosi canal; she stops at Needle because it embodies Jon, Sansa, the Starks and Winterfell rather than a mere weapon.
19. Arya hides Needle beneath a removable stone on the steps toward the temple and counts the steps so she can recover it later. This is a concrete retained secret and physical retrieval point.
20. The kindly man seems to know she withheld something, but the narrative does not establish how much he knows or whether he knows the hiding spot.
21. The kindly man reports the Faceless Men's origins predate Braavos and lie among forced laborers in the volcanic mines beneath Valyria's Fourteen Flames. This is a priestly institutional origin story, not independent documentary history.
22. He describes oppressive mining conditions, mass mortality, slave revolts, and firewyrms that supposedly burrow through rock and breathe fire. Wyrm size and pre-dragon antiquity are traditions within his account.
23. Competing origin traditions claim the first Faceless Man was an enslaved man, freeholder's son or sympathetic overseer; he explicitly says the original person's identity is unknown.
24. The founding revelation is that slaves who prayed to separate national gods were all asking for deliverance through the same death-giver. The first Faceless Man killed an especially suffering slave and later, the priest intimates, targeted masters too.
25. The transition from mercy to the killing of masters is left intentionally incomplete in the priest's narrative; the larger Valyrian historical implications remain hypothesis rather than proof of responsibility for the Doom.
26. He teaches Arya that face-reading rests on observable details such as eyes, jaw, mouth and shoulders and need not require magic. Claims that physical signals invariably reveal lying remain the teacher's asserted method.
27. The waif becomes Arya's Braavosi tutor, while Arya teaches her Common Tongue. The waif learns faster; the practical difficulty of language acquisition is portrayed as harder for Arya than sword drills.
28. Their reciprocal truth-and-lies game develops Arya's skills at expression and detection. Early on the waif reliably identifies Arya's lies while Arya guesses poorly.
29. The waif tells Arya she is thirty-six years old, despite looking like a child. The kindly man confirms this age and attributes her youthful, stunted appearance to poisons rather than a supernatural transformation.
30. The waif adds a stone flagon's contents to the black pool after evening prayers. Arya associates this with the priest's explanation of poisons; the exact mixture and its function are not chemically established.
31. Other recurring temple visitors have recognizably changing appearances: a handsome man's hair color and nose vary, alongside a fat man and pale-eyed severe man, plus less-frequent figures. Their faces may be manufactured identities but the chapter does not independently establish technique.
32. The kindly man directs Arya to serve visiting men silently and refrain from listening. She hears Braavosi but understands little; this illustrates information discipline as well as her limited grasp of the language.
33. While standing still she dreams she is a wolf leading a pack through moonlit woods. The chapter depicts the experience as a dream; potential warging significance requires cross-book comparison.
34. The kindly man refuses to recognize Jaqen H'ghar's name. His denial neither proves he is uninformed nor identifies any visitor as Jaqen.
35. He distinguishes difficult glamors from ordinary mummer's tricks, requiring Arya first to gain control of facial muscles. She practices in a Myrish mirror twice daily.
36. Arya is assigned corpse preparation and connects the temple's voluntary deaths with Old Nan's stories of elderly northern people disappearing to hunt during cruel winters. The analogy concerns cultural attitudes to death and scarce resources.
37. After several moon cycles of training, the kindly man judges her Braavosi poor but functional and orders immersion outside the temple.
38. He places her with Brusco, a fishmonger with a bad back, to push a barrow and sell shellfish around the Drowned Town's wharves.
39. Because Salty is known to the Titan's Daughter crew, Arya is assigned a distinct identity: Cat, a King's Landing orphan whose galley-oarmaster father died after taking her overseas.
40. Arya spontaneously names the fictional ship 'Nymeria,' embedding the direwolf's name in her supposed replacement self.
41. She departs carrying a long iron knife and wearing humble garments; outside, she is lost in unfamiliar Braavosi canals but visibly joyful and continues repeating her private list and 'valar morghulis.'
42. End state: Arya has become a novice and then begins field immersion as Cat; Needle is hidden and recoverable; her revenge vows survive despite the institution's demands for non-attachment.

## Series-wide research priorities

- **Names versus No One:** Arya's narrative preserves personal identity by itemizing her family, holding Needle, naming the new ship Nymeria, and chanting revenge names after beginning her novice assignment.
- **Religion and assassination:** The kindly man's claim that individuals cannot select victims must be reconciled with subsequent contracts and their economic/social rules. It is doctrine, not yet proved practice.
- **Volcanic and dragon lore:** Fourteen Flames, slaves, and burrowing firewyrms are concrete parts of the order's origin account; avoid inferring knowledge of Doom causation without a link in source.
- **Sensory training:** Blind candle servant, scent cues, facial muscle drills and lie game anticipate changes in Arya's perception and methods.
- **House of Black and White's resources:** Weapons, clothing, collected coins and postmortem facilities support identity operations, but no magical body-changing method is directly exhibited here.
- **Northern death customs:** Old Nan's winter hunting account parallels the House's consent-based death practices without being an identical institution.
- **Logistics:** A specific hidden stair stone and fishmonger assignment are material future breadcrumbs.

**Receipt:** Entire source chapter read. Later training outcomes are not retroactively imported.
