## Plot and evidence syntheses

### The Dragonstone victory has an unverified human price

Aurane returns on the royal dromond **Sweet Cersei** and says **Dragonstone has fallen**. The victory enables Cersei to expect Redwyne's main fleet to sail toward an increasingly threatened Reach. Aurane claims a costly **frontal assault initiated by Loras** rather than patient siege mining, leaving around **one thousand attackers dead or dying** and **Loras gravely wounded** (thigh/shoulder quarrels, broken ribs, boiling oil). His assessment that Loras will die is **not** a witnessed death, and no independent Dragonstone commander, healer, participant or account checks every battlefield detail here. Earlier AFFC 32 recorded **Loras volunteering** and being granted the command, while Margaery protested; this chapter records the **interested later report** and its immediate use by Cersei to hurt the younger queen. Margaery recognizes precisely that **dying does not equal dead**. Loras's actual condition, location of body and future must be independently established.

The ironborn's reported raids against the **Arbor, Mander and potentially Whispering Sound/Oldtown** extend the tactical consequences of their AFFC 29 seizure of the Shields. A report of about a dozen Redwyne home-defender warships lost does not establish the main fleet's arrival or victory. Cersei assumes release of the Redwyne force after the supposed Dragonstone capture solves the southern problem, ignoring travel, casualty and logistics delays.

### Cersei's enemies are institutions as much as individuals

Her bounty for Tyrion has produced a **seventh severed dwarf head** that is plainly not his, identifiable by mismatched eyes, age, teeth and artificial nose removal. The killer's claim that eyes were replaced and the teeth stolen is self-exculpatory; the murdered victim's identity is **unknown**. Cersei sends the Tyroshi claimant to **Qyburn** and menaces three other unverified informers. Personal vengeance encourages killings or desecration of other dwarfs without locating Tyrion.

The Iron Bank now calls in **merchants' private debts and denies fresh loans**, beyond the Crown's own suspended repayment. Cersei proposes a fantasy **Golden Bank of Lannisport** but authorizes nothing. Meanwhile a visibly organized **Warrior's Sons** escort, including **Lancel**, accompanies Septon Raynard; Qyburn counts near a hundred sworn knights. Their distinctive rainbow swords and crystals and the High Sparrow's campaign against **the Street of Silk** show that the Faith is not a passive partner bound to Cersei's agreements. The queen defends prostitution mainly as a source of taxation and sailors' commerce, a material challenge to the new religious military authority. **Lancel's appearance now confirms more than his AFFC 30 stated intention**, even while the final legal annulment of his Darry marriage remains unshown.

**Gyles Rosby** has advanced lung symptoms and Pycelle provides **sweetsleep** for his cough, explicitly linking that named drug to Arya's AFFC 34 education and Lord Robert Arryn's AFFC 23 treatment; this neither establishes that sweetsleep killed him nor proves murder. Cersei suspects every rival or physician while missing the danger of her own decisions. **Tommen** protests Cersei's threat to mutilate Margaery's tongue and asserts his legal authority; Cersei commands his **whipping boy Pate** be beaten by the king and threatens Pate with removal of his tongue should Tommen refuse. Tommen's later subdued demeanor is observed; the complete beating **is not shown**.

### Maggy the Frog: the full prophecy and the Westerling genealogy

In her dream, Cersei relives the childhood meeting with **Jeyne Farman and Melara Hetherspoon**. Maggy says Cersei will wed a *king*, be displaced by another younger queen, have **three** children while her husband has **sixteen**, and see golden crowns/shrouds before being strangled by a **valonqar**. Melara is warned she will die unmarried, with death very near; Cersei says she later **fell down a well**. None of this establishes *who caused the fall*. Cersei's dream includes changes from the remembered night and a blanket around her physical neck when she awakens choking; the actual strangler **is not identified**. Cersei's recurring attribution to **Tyrion** is an **interpretation**, and Maggy does not name him in the recalled utterance.

**Major independently cross-checked clue:** In [ASOS 19 Tyrion](../../03-A-Storm-of-Swords/chapters/19-tyrion.md), Kevan describes **Sybell Spicer's grandmother** as a woman brought from the East by a **saffron-and-pepper merchant**, known locally as a **maegi** who provided cures and potions. In AFFC 36 Cersei says Maggy was an eastern merchant's wife, mother of an ennobled petty lord, visited for such services in Lannisport, with nickname derived from **maegi**. These two independently given origin stories are unusually specific and mutually supportive: **Maggy is strongly identifiable as Sybell's grandmother, and therefore is Jeyne Westerling's great-grandmother**. This connects **Cersei's foretelling** to the **Westerling/Spicer family implicated in Robb Stark's marriage and its political exploitation**. It does **not** prove inherited powers, magic used on Robb, Sybell's complicity in administering any potion, or Maggy having predicted Robb's marriage. ASOS 19 also records Tywin's suggestive satisfaction with Westerling incentives, but neither passage establishes a conspiracy method.

Cersei asks Pycelle whether prophecy should be studied, then seeks Qyburn's more encouraging counsel. Qyburn categorizes bloodmagic as potent and asserts the future can be changed, without demonstrating anything. **Her practical response is to engineer grounds to execute Margaery**: she reasons direct murder would attract suspicion, so a manufactured sex/treason case involving Margaery's own male attendants or guards would better compel even Mace Tyrell. At chapter end she asks Osmund whether **Osney can defeat Ser Boros Blount**, but this is a **capability inquiry, not a fight, accusation or completed plan fully spelled out**. The Osney plot's initial objective in AFFC 17/24—to create *false* evidence of Margaery's adultery—remains crucial.

### Falyse's fate is more concrete, but not complete

AFFC 32 ended with Qyburn taking Falyse Stokeworth. Here he states she is **alive**, has suffered **severe lasting damage**, is not capable of feeding herself, and cannot reclaim Stokeworth. Cersei has no direct examination and Qyburn discloses no procedure, but he admits the human cost of his discoveries. Meanwhile **Lady Tanda reportedly died**, and Lollys was proclaimed ruling Lady Stokeworth with **Bronn** as her lord. Do not mark Falyse dead; do not assume her consciousness, cognition or precise mutilations. This particular sequence demonstrates a political threat neutralized by *the queen's own commissioned torture* rather than any loyalty from her former Stokeworth allies.

## Independent cross-POV tests and future falsifiers

1. **AFFC 32 → 36:** Loras volunteered to storm Dragonstone and Cersei had political reasons to hope he died. Aurane's triumphant yet casualty-heavy report needs independent confirmation, including **witnessed Loras medical care** and true battle toll.
2. **AFFC 29 → 36:** Ironborn were intentionally positioned to strike the Arbor, Mander and Oldtown; reports of spreading raids are credible strategic consequences, but each alleged attack is sourced as court intelligence, not proof that Euron personally commanded every raiding ship.
3. **AFFC 30/33 → 36:** Lancel's renunciation is no longer just prospective: **he is physically among the armed Warrior's Sons**. Genna and Jaime's fear of armed Faith has become operational. Track High Septon's ability to defy summons and control city trade.
4. **ASOS 19 Tyrion → AFFC 36:** The merchant/eastern maegi grandmother explicitly connects **Sybell Spicer and Jeyne Westerling** to **Maggy the Frog**. Follow Westering–Spicer arrangements and Tywin's possible interest in Robb's marriage, **without presuming a love-potion conspiracy**.
5. **AFFC 17/24/28/32 → 36:** The planned Osney–Margaery scandal has not been demonstrated true, despite the queen's growing plan to turn it into a capital charge. Is Osney meant to fight Boros, serve as a false witness, or both? The exact mechanics require later on-page revelation.
6. **AFFC 24/32 → 36:** Falyse now alive but disabled after Qyburn custody, with Tanda dead and Bronn/Lollys in control. Track custody and later status, and keep Rosby's ward claim separate.
7. **AFFC 34 / AFFC 23 → 36:** Sweetsleep appears again in Pycelle's Rosby care. The waif reports dose dependence, yet the precise quantities given to Rosby or Robert Arryn are unknown.
8. **Maggy and Melara:** Seek non-dream account or admissions about Melara's well death. The prophecy and Cersei's emotional memory permit suspicion but not conviction.
9. **Iron Bank consequences:** Check the difference between credit contraction and immediate insolvency and test any newly chartered Golden Bank before counting it in royal funds.
10. **Tommen–Pate:** Separate stated disciplinary order, resistance and inferred outcome, and monitor Tommen's trust in Margaery and resentment of Cersei.
11. **Pycelle chronology:** Corroborate his 84 years, election at 42 under Aegon, and predecessors from Citadel records before treating these as exact exhaustive histories.
12. **The valonqar:** Treat “little brother” as an open relational description with multiple candidates. Cersei's sight of dream-Tyrion does not prove Tyrion will kill her, or that her attempts to prevent prophecy won't contribute causally.
