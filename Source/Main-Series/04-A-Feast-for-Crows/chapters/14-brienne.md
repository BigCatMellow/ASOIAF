## Plot, sequence and evidence

1. **Brienne and Podrick ride past landscapes she crossed with Jaime and Cleos in ASOS.** She cannot securely identify the exact walls, stream and ambush locations now that seasons and vegetation have changed. Her memory is emotionally vivid but geographically imprecise.
2. **Podrick has no reliable parental protector.** His father died in the Greyjoy Rebellion, his mother left when he was four, and cousin Cedric treated him as a servant before dying in Tywin's campaign.
3. **Ser Lorimer the Belly next took Podrick into the baggage train and taught him theft rather than arms.** Lorimer stole a ham from Tywin's stores and was hanged; Podrick escaped the same punishment after Kevan intervened on account of his Payne name.
4. **Kevan later placed Podrick with Tyrion.** Tyrion sent him to the Red Keep's master-at-arms, but Ser Aron Santagar's death in the bread riots interrupted his sword training.
5. **Brienne starts training Podrick with practice swords.** He is quick of hand, brave and attentive, but underfed, weak and largely untaught. Her assessment avoids equating speech difficulty with stupidity.
6. **Podrick willingly accepts blisters and bruises to remain with her.** Their relationship begins to resemble an apprenticeship even though she insists neither is fully entitled to the rank the other gives them.
7. **The two pass an impoverished couple hauling a cart by hand after losing an ox.** The farmer blames 'wolves' for stealing it and assaulting his daughter; these are his allegations about soldiers, not eyewitness proof about the individual attackers.
8. **The farmer prefers Tarly's brutal order to Mooton's earlier passivity.** This is ordinary smallfolk evaluation of safety amid devastation, not approval of every particular judgment Tarly delivers.
9. **Brienne sees patrols and ruined or converted inns on the road.** Tarly's military control has reduced open banditry but has not restored normal civic life.
10. **A patrol reports the Hound crossed the Trident with a hundred outlaws.** This is secondhand, and the name 'Hound' can now refer to someone other than Sandor after the helm's transfer. Do not treat the rumor as a direct Sandor sighting.
11. **The farmer brings eggs for the wedding of Mooton's daughter and Randyll's son Dickon.** The betrothal/wedding will materially connect the formerly wavering Riverlands house to the Reach commander currently occupying Maidenpool.
12. **Brienne recalls an earlier betrothal to a Caron child who died of illness.** She imagines an alternate conventional life of marriage and motherhood, with both grief and relief.
13. **Maidenpool is being rebuilt by Tarly's soldiers.** Gates, homes, a sept, harbor trade and other infrastructure are visibly recovering under coerced military labor.
14. **The town flies Tommen's royal banner alongside Tarly's sigil, while Mooton's salmon is limited to his castle.** The banner distribution visibly reflects the hierarchy of actual power.
15. **Gate guards confiscate the farmer's eggs at an inadequate price and attempt to seize his wife.** The coercion is directly observed and contradicts any uncomplicated account of Tarly's troops as restorers of justice.
16. **Brienne draws steel to stop the assault despite being surrounded by halberds.** Her protective reflex is independent of whether intervening is tactically advantageous.
17. **Ser Hyle Hunt intervenes, threatening the men with Tarly's punishment of rapists and thieves.** He saves the couple in this encounter while simultaneously mocking Brienne cruelly.
18. **Hyle's presence reactivates Brienne's earlier trauma from Renly's camp.** His mockery is not simply ordinary banter, as the later flashback explains.
19. **Hyle orders Brienne brought before Randyll Tarly.** Brienne has the signed royal letter Jaime supplied; it protects her from arbitrary detention but does not secure Randyll's respect.
20. **Tarly holds court at the fishmarket beside gallows displaying corpses.** Spectators attend in hopes of watching hangings, a public theater of coercive law.
21. **He orders seven fingers removed from a boy who stole objects left by fleeing septons.** The disproportion is justified by the religious character of the theft.
22. **He fines a baker for adding sawdust to flour, with lashes substituting for unpaid stags.** The disposition depends on capacity to pay, making economic inequality part of punishment.
23. **He orders a sex worker punished with a lye washing and imprisonment after soldiers accuse her of infecting them.** No independent medical examination or proof of blame is shown.
24. **He assesses a stabbing/dice-cheating case by threatening to inspect the dice.** The archer's hesitation supports cheating; Tarly punishes both parties, illustrating forensic cleverness alongside harsh sentences.
25. **Brienne remembers Jaime's mutilation while watching the condemned thief.** Her ethical attention follows the bodily cost of 'justice' rather than merely the formal offense.
26. **Tarly says Brienne did not kill Renly but allowed him to die.** The charge ignores the shadow assassination and places a gendered impossible burden on a witness who could not stop it.
27. **Tarly threatens to send Brienne home and blames her for being a woman in armor.** Even after reading Tommen's letter, he warns that she will have earned rape if attacked. These are his judgments, not narrative approval.
28. **The letter lets Brienne name Sansa as her real target before Tarly.** She is no longer relying exclusively on the fictitious missing-sister cover with someone who already knows her identity.
29. **Brienne blurts out the Vale hypothesis again.** Tarly replies that Lysa is dead, a singer has been blamed, and Littlefinger holds the Eyrie under pressure from Vale lords. This is partially accurate political news filtered through public cover-story information.
30. **Brienne now dismisses the Vale as a likely refuge because she assumes Lysa's death removes it.** The reader knows Petyr continues to shelter Sansa as Alayne; this is a second diversion produced by accurate but incomplete news.
31. **Hyle admits the 'courting' of Brienne in Renly's camp was a wager.** Young knights paid gold to compete for who could take her virginity; the wager encouraged escalating humiliation and possibly sexual assault.
32. **The early participants were Edmund Ambrose, Ben Bushy and Hyle.** Others joined as interest spread, including Farrow, Beesbury, Mullendore and others who appeared to offer gifts or genuine friendship.
33. **Hyle had cultivated Brienne's trust through books, horses and a sparring session.** The cruelty lay in making the gestures she most valued into parts of a contest.
34. **Young Dickon Tarly reported the wager to Randyll, who stopped it.** He recognized that some participants might use force.
35. **Randyll nevertheless blamed Brienne for encouraging them by joining the war host.** His prevention of rape coexists with contempt for the threatened woman and a stated ideology confining women to childbirth.
36. **The incident helps explain Brienne's vigilance among knights.** It is evidence of betrayal by a social institution whose ideals she genuinely wants to uphold.
37. **Hyle says several gamblers died or were maimed in the Blackwater.** Brienne briefly wants to rejoice, then remembers individual human details and the 'knights of summer' transformed by war.
38. **Brienne refuses Hyle's offer to escort her to the Stinking Goose.** She remains tactically wary of someone who betrayed her trust, despite his help at the gate.
39. **The Stinking Goose is a low waterfront cellar, not an ordinary respectable inn.** Brienne pays its keeper a copper star to identify Nimble Dick when he arrives.
40. **Nimble Dick Crabb admits knowing a frightened 'fool' who sought sea passage for three.** He says the man frequented the dockside tavern and grew alarmed when men bearing Tarly's huntsman appeared.
41. **Dick reports 'two girls' but immediately admits he never actually saw them.** This is an inference from the fool's request for passage for three, not a witnessed pair of sisters.
42. **Dick says he referred the fool to a supposed secret landing place in Crackclaw Point.** He then admits ships had not used the location for about thirty years; the guidance was knowingly deceptive.
43. **Brienne pays escalating amounts for details.** Dick spins Robert's silver stag and demands gold, displaying both practiced opportunism and awareness of her desperation.
44. **The destination is the Whispers, an old Crabb stronghold on Crackclaw Point.** Dick claims descent from legendary Ser Clarence Crabb, who supposedly kept living severed heads advised by a woods witch.
45. **The Clarence Crabb story is local folklore.** Neither the giant knight, undead talking heads nor witch's resurrection are verified by Dick's boast.
46. **Dick offers to guide Brienne to the Whispers for a conditional reward.** They agree on six gold dragons if her sister is found and two if only the fool is found; zero if nothing is found.
47. **Brienne distrusts Dick but chooses to follow the lead.** She considers using the threat of steel to compel him, then pays instead and explicitly warns him not to deceive her.
48. **End state:** Brienne has Podrick as an apprentice/companion, new information about Lysa's death, an uncertain smuggler-cove lead at the Whispers and a financially interested guide. Neither Sansa nor Dontos has been sighted.

## Cross-POV significance

- **Two accurate leads combine into a wrong conclusion:** Brienne correctly considered the Vale, then learns Lysa died and assumes Sansa cannot be there—while readers know Petyr remains her protector.
- **Tarly is not simply order or brutality:** he curbs predation and rebuilds infrastructure, yet his own guards prey on civilians and his judgments can be grossly disproportionate.
- **Podrick's poverty and lack of training matter to later danger:** bravery is not armor; Brienne starts supplying the competence he has never been given.
- **The Hound rumor must be checked against ownership of the helm:** the name can circulate separately from Sandor.
- **Dick's information is contaminated:** a boast about tricking someone turns into a paid guide contract; no firsthand Sansa/second-girl sighting exists.
