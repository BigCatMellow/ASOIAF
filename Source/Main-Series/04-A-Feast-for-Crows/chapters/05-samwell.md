# AFFC 5 — Samwell: incomplete histories of the Others, Jon's political tightrope, Aemon's removal and the road to Oldtown

- Status: `FIRST PASS COMPLETE — INDEPENDENT CHALLENGE OPEN`

## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Sam is conducting sustained primary-source research on the Others in Castle Black's underground library.** He has been reading through the night, neglecting sleep, meals and archery practice. This establishes him as the Watch's most active researcher but also shows the work is incomplete and physically unsustainable.
2. **The library itself is deteriorating.** Mice have gnawed bindings, loose pages are separated from their books, dust fills the vaults and some ancient volumes crumble when handled. Missing evidence can therefore result from ordinary material loss rather than deliberate suppression.
3. **Sam finds a hidden cell and multiple chests of detached pages.** The archive is physically disordered enough that relevant records may remain undiscovered even after extensive searching.
4. **The oldest surviving histories are not contemporary Long Night records.** Sam says the earliest histories he has found were written after the Andal arrival, while the First Men left mainly runic inscriptions. Accounts of the Dawn Age, Age of Heroes and Long Night were therefore written down by later septons, often thousands of years after the supposed events.
5. **Sam explicitly questions traditional chronology.** The old stories include rulers living for centuries and knights appearing before knighthood should have existed. This is a direct warning against treating legendary-era dates and titles as literal.
6. **The Watch's claimed succession number may be inflated or incomplete.** Jon is officially called the nine-hundred-and-ninety-eighth Lord Commander, but Sam's oldest list contains only **674 commanders**. The discrepancy is verified; its cause is unknown.
7. **Osric Stark is one historical precedent for a child Lord Commander.** Sam reports Osric was chosen at ten and served sixty years, leaving Jon only the fifth-youngest commander Sam has found so far. Because the underlying annals are incomplete, this is not a definitive all-time ranking.
8. **Sam finds a recurring association between the Others and cold/night.** Stories disagree on causality: the Others may come with cold and darkness, or cold and darkness may accompany them. The source does not resolve which causes which.
9. **Some old accounts say the Others animate or ride dead animals.** Sam lists dead horses, bears, direwolves and mammoths; his own encounter with an Other riding a dead horse gives one part of this tradition contemporary corroboration.
10. **The old stories also mention giant ice spiders.** Sam does not know what these are and supplies no firsthand confirmation. They remain legendary reports rather than verified creatures.
11. **Burning the dead is presented as necessary after battles with the Others.** Sam's research says fallen men can rise as their thralls. This is consistent with the Watch's witnessed wights and is more strongly supported than many other legendary details.
12. **Dragonglass remains the best verified anti-Other weapon in Sam's evidence.** His own killing of an Other with an obsidian dagger is firsthand experience, while the records say the children of the forest once supplied the Night's Watch with **one hundred obsidian daggers each year** during the Age of Heroes.
13. **The annual obsidian tribute is potentially important but unexplained.** It suggests an old institutional relationship among the children, the Watch and dragonglass, but the chapter does not say why the gifts began, how long they continued or whether the recipients understood their anti-Other use.
14. **One Long Night account names 'dragonsteel' as effective against the Others.** Jon asks whether this means **Valyrian steel**, and Sam says that was his first thought. The equivalence is **an inference by Jon and Sam**, not a confirmed identification.
15. **The same chapter undermines overconfidence in the dragonsteel clue.** Sam has just established that legendary histories were recorded much later and contain anachronisms. Any attempt to equate dragonsteel with Valyrian steel must therefore survive both textual-corruption and chronology objections.
16. **Sam has not found who the Others are, where they come from or what they want.** Jon asks those exact strategic questions; Sam can only request more research time. No motive or origin theory is established here.
17. **Jon says there is no more time for the research.** The Watch's immediate survival needs force him to remove Sam from the very archive most likely to contain additional evidence. This creates a genuine knowledge-versus-capacity tradeoff.
18. **Sam briefly wonders about dragon eggs at the Wall and Dragonstone.** He speculates whether Silverwing could have left an egg at Castle Black or Stannis might possess one from Dragonstone, then recalls failed Targaryen hatching attempts. These are Sam's thoughts, not evidence that either egg exists.
19. **Jon is rebuilding the Wall's switchback stair and ordering daily archery practice for the entire garrison.** Cooks and stewards train alongside fighting men because Jon believes the Watch overvalued swords when it had far more knights. These are concrete preparedness reforms.
20. **The reforms are unpopular with some brothers.** Sam hears complaints that Mormont did not work the builders as hard. Operational necessity and internal morale are already in tension.
21. **Pyp and Grenn perceive Jon as increasingly distant.** They complain that command duties leave him little time for old friends, even though he still trains frequently in the yard. This is social evidence of leadership isolation, not proof that Jon has rejected them.
22. **Jon's constant sword practice comes from insecurity rather than vanity.** He previously told Sam that Longclaw is better than its wielder and that Qhorin could easily have killed him. He is trying to become worthy of the weapon and office.
23. **Sam secretly knows Bran is alive beyond the Wall with Coldhands.** When Jon speaks of Bran as dead, Sam nearly corrects him but keeps his sworn promise. This is a major information asymmetry inside Jon's closest circle.
24. **Jon and Aemon draft a 'paper shield' to King Tommen.** The letter insists the Watch serves the realm rather than Stannis, while acknowledging that Stannis provides help against threats beyond the Wall.
25. **Jon initially doubts the letter will matter; Sam persuades him to send it.** Sam argues that the Lannisters may fear the political shame of allowing Stannis alone to defend the realm. Jon signs, seals and orders the letter sent by raven to King's Landing.
26. **The letter discussion exposes a major communications lag.** Jon and Sam still calculate how **Lord Tywin** may respond, while the reader has already witnessed Tywin's death in AFFC 3. Their strategy is rational relative to their information but partly obsolete before the message leaves Castle Black.
27. **Jon sees the Watch trapped between rival kings.** He has given Stannis food, shelter, the Nightfort and permission to settle free folk in the Gift, while insisting the Watch has not joined his rebellion. Jon expects Stannis to want more and the Lannisters to judge even this assistance excessive.
28. **Jon personally wants the Lannisters destroyed but still acts through the Watch's neutrality.** The distinction between private desire and institutional duty remains active rather than resolved.
29. **Stannis's northern recruitment is weak at this moment.** Ravens have been sent to northern lords asking them to declare for him, but Sam says only the Karhold bird has returned. Jon therefore talks of 'having the Karstarks' and hopes for White Harbor while Sam remains skeptical.
30. **That Karstark confidence is based on limited information.** This chapter proves receipt/reply, not the reliability of the Karstark commitment or the motives behind it.
31. **Jon expects Mance Rayder to die but prefers a beheading to burning.** He regards Mance as a former brother whose life properly falls under Night's Watch justice and doubts Melisandre's reported plan to use a fiery execution for sorcery.
32. **Jon rejects Mance's supposed king's blood while still hedging against Melisandre's magic.** He argues that Mance never had true royal blood, yet later removes Aemon partly because Melisandre might value Aemon's genuine Targaryen ancestry. Jon is skeptical, not dismissive of risk.
33. **Aemon's identity is therefore a strategic liability at Castle Black.** Jon explicitly says Stannis knows Aemon is a Targaryen and that the old maester is safer away from Melisandre despite the dangers of travel.
34. **Jon orders Sam to travel to Oldtown and forge a maester's chain.** His long-term goal is to make Sam the Watch's next maester because Aemon is failing, Maester Mullin at the Shadow Tower is more fighter than scholar, and Maester Harmune at Eastwatch is frequently drunk.
35. **The maester plan directly reactivates Sam's trauma from Randyll Tarly.** Sam remembers being chained by his father for three days and nights after raising the prospect of becoming a maester; a chain around his throat cut the skin and sometimes choked him. His terror of a maester's chain is therefore not mere academic reluctance.
36. **Jon deliberately overrides Randyll's authority.** He tells Sam that a sworn brother no longer has a father in the relevant sense and that his life belongs to the Watch. The order forces Sam to choose institutional identity over paternal conditioning.
37. **Jon also forbids Sam from calling himself a craven.** He cannot command Sam to feel brave, but he commands him to stop defining himself by fear. This is an imposed identity challenge, not evidence Sam's fear disappears.
38. **Aemon supports Jon's decision and supplies a Targaryen parallel.** He says his own father objected to a life of service, while his grandfather sent him to the Citadel because too many possible Targaryen claimants could be dangerous. The lesson is about service and dynastic risk, not a prediction of Sam's future.
39. **The planned route is concrete.** Sam, Aemon and Gilly go first to Eastwatch under ranger escort; Dareon is to join them; the *Blackbird* will take them to Braavos; from there they must arrange passage to Oldtown.
40. **Dareon has an additional recruitment mission.** Jon hopes the singer's songs can win men for the Watch in the south. The party is therefore carrying institutional outreach as well as passengers.
41. **Gilly's distress creates an unresolved infant-identity problem in Sam's POV.** She leaves Jon crying, distinguishes her own quiet baby from Dalla's crying son, and later uses strained language about 'the other' child and Dalla's 'little prince.' Sam does not understand what has happened; this chapter should not be used by itself as a full explanation of the arrangement.
42. **Jon wants Gilly and a child removed from the Wall while another nursing infant remains.** He says a new wet nurse will be needed. The practical milk problem is explicit even though Sam lacks the full reason for the arrangement.
43. **Gilly rejects the courtly identity others put on her.** At departure she insists she is Craster's wife, Craster's daughter and a mother, not a lady. Her self-definition centers survival and motherhood rather than rank.
44. **Aemon sends a marked copy of Colloquo Votar's *Jade Compendium* back to Jon.** He says a passage may interest Jon, but the content of that marked passage is not read in this chapter. It is a live follow-up clue, not evidence for any specific theory yet.
45. **Aemon also carries a chest of rare old books he believes the Citadel may lack.** Sam could locate only about one quarter of those Aemon wanted. The journey therefore transfers part—but explicitly not all—of Castle Black's unusual knowledge south.
46. **Aemon's physical weakness is already severe.** Wind nearly knocks him over and Sam fears a sea voyage may kill him. Jon knowingly accepts this travel risk because he judges remaining at the Wall more dangerous.
47. **Sam leaves Castle Black reluctantly despite once hating it.** His attachment to the Watch, Gilly, Aemon, the books and his old friends now conflicts with his fear of Oldtown and Horn Hill.
48. **Jon's farewell links Sam and Gilly through unrecognized courage.** He tells Sam that Gilly has more courage than she knows, then says the same is true of Sam. This is character judgment from Jon, not proof that either feels fearless.
49. **Mormont's raven repeats charged words during the conversation.** It says variants of 'blood,' 'obey' and later joins other ravens repeating Jon's surname. The words are observed behavior, but this chapter does not establish supernatural foreknowledge rather than learned mimicry.
50. **End state:** Sam departs Castle Black for Eastwatch with Aemon, Gilly, an infant, rare books and a ranger escort. His Others research is unfinished; Jon has sent an appeal to Tommen, is balancing Stannis against the crown, and has intentionally moved two people who could matter to Melisandre's blood magic away from the Wall.

## Cross-POV implications

- **Wall intelligence is fragmented:** Jon does not know Bran lives, Castle Black does not know Tywin is already dead, and Sam cannot finish identifying the Others before being sent south.
- **Oldtown now becomes a strategic knowledge junction:** Sam is carrying firsthand Other experience, Aemon's rare books and a mandate to become a maester, while the Citadel may possess records Castle Black lacks.
- **Jon's magical-risk policy is asymmetric but rational under uncertainty:** he dismisses Mance's royal blood yet moves Aemon because genuine Targaryen ancestry creates a downside too large to ignore.
- **The dragonsteel hypothesis is promising precisely because it is weakly sourced:** it deserves later corroboration against Valyrian-steel history, Long Night chronology and actual combat—not promotion to fact.
- **The infant arrangement is a deliberate knowledge trap for Sam and potentially for Melisandre:** the chapter supplies visible distress and contradictory labels while withholding the full explanation from its POV.
