## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Asha returns to Ten Towers and immediately sees that too few captains answered her cause.** The half-empty benches make her succession problem material rather than theoretical.
2. **Her support is concentrated on Harlaw.** Botley, Stonetree, Volmark, Myre and multiple Harlaw branches are present, but most other islands are absent.
3. **Rodrik Harlaw avoids the drunken gathering and remains with his books.** His political identity is explicitly opposed to the dominant ironborn performance of masculinity.
4. **Asha's mother Alannys is physically and psychologically damaged by the loss of her sons.** She still imagines Theon as the ten-year-old hostage who left Pyke.
5. **Asha herself is not actually certain Theon is dead.** She dreads telling her mother but later admits Winterfell yielded scattered remains rather than an identified body.
6. **Asha brings Lady Glover and her children as captives from Deepwood Motte.** She insists they receive warmth and decent quarters rather than a dungeon.
7. **The Glover infant is sick and lacks sufficient milk.** Asha treats the child's survival as strategically and personally important, threatening Three-Tooth if neglect kills the baby.
8. **Deepwood Motte remains central to Asha's mainland plans.** Her treatment of the Glovers is therefore humane relative to ironborn norms but also politically useful.
9. **Ten Towers has long been Asha's emotional home more than Pyke.** Her childhood attachment to Rodrik and his library helps explain why his support matters beyond ships.
10. **Rodrik is reading Archmaester Marwyn's *Book of Lost Books*.** He says Marwyn claims to have recovered three pages of *Signs and Portents*, attributed to the maiden daughter of Aenar Targaryen before the Doom.
11. **That bibliographic claim is tantalizing but indirect.** Rodrik is reading Marwyn's report about purported lost material; the original pages and their contents are not independently authenticated in this chapter.
12. **Rodrik's failing eyesight has led him to order a Myrish lens.** Even the Reader's access to evidence is constrained by physical age.
13. **Alannys believes Balon was murdered.** Rodrik gives the conventional account of the broken rope bridge during a storm and does not claim direct knowledge.
14. **Rodrik confirms the reported timing problem around Euron.** Balon died first and *Silence* returned the day after, or so they have heard; Rodrik calls the timing notable without declaring murder proven.
15. **Asha lacks the fleet concentration needed simply to overthrow Euron.** Her political options therefore depend on the unexpected kingsmoot.
16. **Aeron's kingsmoot summons has drawn captains away from Asha's private gathering.** Religious legitimacy is reorganizing the succession contest.
17. **Rodrik warns that historical kingsmoots were not necessarily peaceful.** He cites Urron Greyiron's massacre of rival claimants and the subsequent long Greyiron rule.
18. **Even ironborn chronology is openly disputed.** Rodrik gives one traditional interval for the last kingsmoot and notes Maester Denestan argues for roughly half as long, echoing Sam's broader warning that ancient dates are unstable.
19. **Rodrik explicitly compares Euron to Urron Greyiron.** This is a historical analogy and threat assessment, not proof Euron plans the same massacre.
20. **Rodrik refuses to attend and urges Asha not to go.** He sees the kingship itself as the trap.
21. **His strategic alternative is strikingly mainland-oriented.** Rather than raid for temporary plunder, he wants the ironborn to choose a claimant in the civil war, provide naval strength and demand durable land in return.
22. **Rodrik still speaks as if Tywin Lannister is an active contender.** The reader knows Tywin is already dead, showing another major information lag far from King's Landing.
23. **Asha agrees that Balon's Old Way was strategically obsolete.** She calls her father brave but a bad lord and recognizes Aegon's conquest changed the conditions that once made reaving sustainable.
24. **She rejects both submission and simple repetition of Balon's policy.** Her metaphor is to steer a third course between rocks and storm, foreshadowing a kingsmoot platform not yet fully stated.
25. **Rodrik says gender custom makes her election unlikely.** No woman has ruled the ironborn, and each captain has an equal voice beneath Nagga's bones regardless of Rodrik's feudal command at home.
26. **Harlaw support cannot simply be transferred as a bloc.** Captains who owe Rodrik fealty in war may vote independently in kingsmoot.
27. **Rodrik offers Asha the Ten Towers rather than the Seastone Chair.** He wants to create a secure future for her outside the Greyjoy succession struggle.
28. **He also expects Ser Harras Harlaw to become Lord of Harlaw after him.** The proposed arrangement separates control of Ten Towers from broader Harlaw lordship and would require Asha to live within that hierarchy.
29. **Asha refuses because she identifies as Greyjoy and intends to contest her father's seat.** This is ambition, dynastic claim and personal identity together.
30. **Theon remains an unresolved succession variable.** Asha saw no identified corpse at Winterfell, and the evidence there suggested northmen may have fought one another.
31. **Rodrik's 'crows over a corpse' metaphor reframes the War of Five Kings as predation on Westeros.** It also supplies the volume's title image without establishing any supernatural meaning.
32. **Tristifer Botley confirms major Euron reprisals.** Sawane Botley was drowned for denying Euron's claim; Harren later died at Moat Cailin; Germund now rules in Euron's interest.
33. **Euron redistributed Botley property to reward early submission.** Lord Wynch, among the first to hail him king, received lands, illustrating patronage as a tool of consolidation.
34. **Tris says *Silence* returned with extraordinary eastern treasure.** Plate, pearls, gems and coin are being used to purchase allies; the treasure's exact origins are not independently established.
35. **Tris lists additional captains gathering to Euron.** The names show that Asha's dismissal of his coalition as socially minor does not eliminate its military danger.
36. **Reports of 'monsters' and 'wizards' from the east remain secondhand.** They should be tracked but not converted into verified supernatural assets.
37. **Asha plans to use her Deepwood victory as a kingsmoot credential.** Her own crew's loyalty is strong, but she knows she must persuade captains beyond it.
38. **Tris proposes marriage and says Alannys consented.** Asha makes clear that maternal approval cannot substitute for her consent.
39. **Asha describes choosing her own sexual partners and using moon tea to prevent pregnancy.** Her autonomy directly conflicts with Tris's fantasy of her as the mother of many sons.
40. **When Tris grabs her arm, Asha puts a blade to his throat.** Her final distinction is political and personal: he may serve her claim, but he does not own her as a prospective husband.
41. **End state:** Asha is going to Old Wyk despite weak numbers and Rodrik's warning, with a more modern strategic outlook than Balon's but without enough committed captains to assume victory.

## Cross-POV implications

- **Euron's political strength is not just fear or charisma:** property redistribution and imported wealth are buying concrete loyalty.
- **The murder question remains properly unresolved:** Aeron, Asha, Alannys and Rodrik all register suspicious timing, but this chapter adds no eyewitness to Balon's fall.
- **Ancient-history uncertainty is now a cross-region pattern:** Sam's Wall records and Rodrik's kingsmoot dating both show traditions with unstable chronology.
- **Asha and Rodrik both reject Balon's strategic model, but differ over risk:** he favors bargaining from naval strength; she still wants the crown as the vehicle for a third course.
- **Theon's missing body matters politically before his survival is reader-confirmed in this book's sequence:** Asha cannot honestly eliminate his claim from evidence available to her.
