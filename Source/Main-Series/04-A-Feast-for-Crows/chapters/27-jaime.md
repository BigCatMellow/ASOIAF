## Chronological close reading

1. **Cersei opens by criticizing Jaime's beard, age, white cloak and missing hand.** Jaime is still sexually drawn to her but cannot stop hearing Tyrion's claims about Lancel, Osmund Kettleblack and Moon Boy. His desire, resentment and unreconciled uncertainty coexist.
2. **Cersei orders Jaime away from Tommen to end the Riverrun resistance and restore Harrenhal.** Jaime points out that she made Daven Warden of the West and questions why she does not trust him. He insists that as Lord Commander of the Kingsguard he belongs with the king.
3. **Jaime invokes his oath to Catelyn Stark not to take up arms against Starks or Tullys.** Cersei dismisses the promise as forced and drunken. His military commission creates a real oath-versus-command conflict; obedience does not by itself resolve it.
4. **Cersei's strategic demands are explicit:** she wants Riverrun, Brynden Tully captive or dead, Harrenhal brought under control and **Wylis Manderly** retrieved if he remains alive. The Harrenhal garrison has not answered ravens, a material intelligence failure.
5. **Jaime identifies Harrenhal's garrison as Gregor Clegane's men**, chosen for their brutality rather than ability to administer justice. Cersei is willing to send him among them and mocks his disability.
6. **Cersei proposes Osmund Kettleblack to command the Kingsguard in Jaime's absence**; Jaime insists Loras Tyrell would be the proper deputy. These are conflicting intentions, not a witnessed formal order appointing either man.
7. **Cersei defends sending Balon Swann to Dorne** as necessary to protect Myrcella. Her grievance over Oberyn championing Tyrion and her distrust of the Dornish help explain why this Kingsguard knight is unavailable in King's Landing.
8. The siblings' argument becomes personal: Cersei strikes Jaime across the face. He refrains from retaliation and leaves, privately acknowledging that different words might have softened her yet finding her presence increasingly enraging.
9. **Jaime regards Cersei's court as weakened**, describing her flatterers and officials contemptuously. He particularly distrusts Qyburn, whose past with the Brave Companions he remembers despite Qyburn's earlier treatment of his wound.
10. **Forty knights and as many squires assemble at the Red Keep**, a mix of westermen and former foes now uncertain allies. Dermot of the Rainwood bears Tommen's standard; **Red Ronnet Connington** the Kingsguard's white banner. A Paege, a Piper and **Josmyn Peckledon** squire for Jaime.
11. Jaime's mounts are a blood-bay palfrey and grey destrier, which **Little Lew Piper** names **Honor** and **Glory**. Jaime usually avoids naming warhorses because of their mortality. Glory bears Lannister crimson, Honor Kingsguard white: visible competing facets of Jaime's identity, without proving an authorially coded future outcome.
12. **Jaime initially refuses to display his new golden hand to the crowd**, preferring that they see his actual stump rather than an illusion of recovered fighting ability. This decision contrasts with later use of the prosthesis.
13. **Jaime brings silent Ser Ilyn Payne beside him** and negotiates for both Payne and **Addam Marbrand** as conditions of agreeing to the mission. Marbrand, a childhood friend, leads the outriders; Payne was Tywin's former man and King's Justice.
14. **Payne lost his tongue because Aerys punished a boast about Tywin's power over the king.** The anecdote links Aerys's arbitrary vengeance, Tywin's household and a witness physically unable to repeat secrets.
15. **Crowds favor Mace Tyrell's earlier triumphant departure**, whereas Jaime's column passes largely indifferent onlookers and religious sparrows. Jaime recognizes that the smallfolk respond to Tyrell roses more warmly than Lannister lions; this is a local political observation, not a census of popular opinion.
16. **Sparrows preach in Cobbler's Square**, gathering hundreds and accusing sinful and allegedly demonic rulers. The Faith's agitation is overt in the capital and contrasts with Cersei's confidence that it can be managed.
17. **The larger host waits outside the city**, including Marbrand's outriders, Steffon Swyft and baggage, Bonifer Hasty's Holy Hundred, Sarsfield's mounted archers, Maester Gulian with four raven cages, and Flement Brax's two hundred heavy horse. The whole force totals fewer than a thousand.
18. **Jaime knows Riverrun is already surrounded by Lannisters and larger Frey forces**, and food is difficult for the besiegers because Brynden deliberately stripped nearby country. Jaime's column is reinforcements and command capacity, not the army that originally began the siege.
19. **Jaime sees the devastated riverlands as the work of his own side as well as its enemies.** He considers that Cersei sends him to complete the destruction Amory Lorch and Gregor began and dislikes the prospect. This self-awareness has not yet stopped him accepting the campaign.
20. **He sends outriders despite the road's relative safety**, explicitly remembering Robb Stark's surprise at Whispering Wood. He also forbids troops to depart without leave, limiting recreational riding that could destroy remaining farms and livestock.
21. **Near King's Landing there are still cultivated fields, orchards, sheep, cattle and moving wagons**, unlike the deeper war zone. The distinction matters to supply geography and prevents a misleading assertion that all of the riverlands are equally barren.
22. **Jaime gives his squires and Payne blackberries found by Lew Piper**, a small human action amid his effort to practice ordinary leadership outside court.
23. **Payne's former office involved dungeon oversight**, but he cannot read, write or speak. The realm had no Lord Confessor for many reigns, and a chief gaoler who bought his post from Littlefinger later died as one of Joffrey's Antler Men. Practical operation fell to undergaolers.
24. **Rennifer Longwaters**, the hunchbacked undergaoler who claims a small amount of dragon blood, had shown Jaime to Payne's chambers. This continues the earlier AFFC 8 dungeon chain involving Longwaters, missing Rugen and Jaime's knowledge that Rugen was Varys.
25. **Payne's quarters are verminous, filthy and strewn with neglected possessions**, yet his executioner's greatsword is meticulously maintained. Jaime infers Payne values killing above his surroundings; the man's silence cannot independently establish all his motives.
26. **Payne accepts Jaime's offer by nodding.** Jaime values that the decision was offered rather than imposed and recognizes that both men, reduced by physical injury, may have a path forward.
27. **At Hayford, Jaime sets sentries despite proximity to the capital**, remembering Stafford Lannister's surprise at Oxcross. He dines with local officials and his officers.
28. **The gilded prosthesis has inlaid mother-of-pearl nails and fingers curved to hook a goblet**, but cannot grip like a living hand. An armorer predicted Jaime would be known as Goldenhand; Jaime expects to remain known as the Kingslayer.
29. **Jaime wears the golden hand to the supper but spills wine**, then angrily rebukes a knight praising it. The ornamental limb cannot conceal its practical shortcomings or the stigma Jaime feels.
30. **Lady Ermesande Hayford is an infant married to the missing Tyrek Lannister** by a political arrangement made before she could walk. Her heraldry incorporates the Hayford green fretty and green pale wavy; her appearance at dinner exposes the transactional use of child marriages.
31. **Tyrek disappeared during King's Landing's riots**, when he was apparently separated from the gold cloaks while mounted. His palfrey was recovered but not his body. Addam Marbrand searched at Tywin's order after Bywater searched, without result.
32. **Strongboar suggests Tyrek could have been taken alive for ransom**. Addam notes no ransom was ever demanded; Jaime tells the table Tyrek is dead and imagines that frightened killers concealed the corpse to escape Tywin's wrath. All remain hypotheses, not verified outcomes.
33. **Privately Jaime entertains a darker alternative: Varys could have abducted Tyrek**, a former squire of Robert who might possess politically useful knowledge. Jaime notes Varys gave no warning of the riots and was absent at Myrcella's departure, but has no witness connecting him to Tyrek's fate.
34. Jaime wants to strike someone as he reflects on Varys and the weight of his false golden hand. Instead he seeks **Payne** and equipment for blunted sword practice.
35. **Jaime straps a shield to his golden right hand and takes a practice sword in his left.** The artificial fingers can hold loosely around shield loops but not make a reliable grip. This is a concrete adaptation, not a restoration of right-handed mastery.
36. **Payne outmatches Jaime repeatedly** in the moonlit practice fight, landing blows on his limb, body and helmet and nearly tearing off his shield. Jaime recognizes the truth of his present skill deficit but welcomes the clearing of his mind.
37. **Jaime commits to practice daily until his left-hand skill improves.** Payne answers with a rasping or clacking laugh; Jaime feels a pang at being laughed at but does not abandon the plan.
38. **The training continues every night for days**—in a stable, cellar, burned barn, shallow-stream island and rainy field. Jaime disguises bruises as amorous mishaps; he thinks Marbrand and other captains probably understand while silent Payne cannot disclose the precise humiliations.
39. **War damage worsens as they ride north**: overgrown fields, empty roads, abandoned crops, abundant wolves. An outrider's dismounted horse is killed by wolves; Ser Bonifer calls them demons in animal skins.
40. **Jaime rejects Bonifer's demon certainty**, mocks the logic of divine punishment for the horse and orders the remains salted as potential food. This is a practical response to a verifiable animal attack, not proof that every anomalous wolf report is ordinary.
41. **At Sow's Horn, Ser Roger Hogg holds his towerhouse with a small garrison and peasants.** His surname prompts Strongboar to investigate a possible Crakehall/boar relation. Genealogical speculation is amusing but unverified.
42. **Hogg says a group he calls 'white star wolves' troubled his holding**, but it was Lorch's men under a manticore shield who slaughtered his sheep and goats and tried to burn him in his thick-walled tower. The description and identity of the earlier 'wolves' should not be conflated with the actual four-legged wolves he reports afterward.
43. **Hogg argues he owed fealty to House Hayford and ultimately King's Landing**, not the riverlords. Lorch ignored that political distinction. Lannister raiding harmed even the king's own subject population.
44. **Actual wolves later consumed livestock Lorch left**, and Hogg says pelts could not replace food. Jaime advises planting and hoping for a final harvest rather than offering a security guarantee.
45. **The host crosses from Crownlands into Riverrun's feudal territory** and finds both Wode brothers' timber-and-earth halls reduced to burned remains. Maester Gulian's map supplies the underlying lordship; neither the fate of the Wodes nor their tenants is learned.
46. **Outlaws occupy a cellar beneath one ruined hall.** Jaime hangs them, including one wearing the remains of a red cloak. He experiences the action as justice and dreams of the epithet Goldenhand the Just. No trial, confirmed crimes or individual guilt findings are narrated, so the moral label is his own.
47. Jaime speculates that **Brienne might have passed toward Riverrun searching for Sansa**; this is a guess. Earlier AFFC Brienne chapters independently locate her elsewhere and show Jaime lacks information about her travels and goals.
48. **Harrenhal's five damaged black towers** appear beyond the lake. Petyr Baelish formally holds the lordship but is absent; Jaime has been delegated to stabilize its actual military occupation.
49. **The Mountain seized Harrenhal from the Bloody Mummers** and left followers occupying it. Jaime recognizes that soldiers trained for terror are ill-suited to peacekeeping.
50. The closed gates open after three blasts of **the Horn of Herrock**, an actual signal instrument with black twisted shape and old-gold bands; no magical power or relation to Sam's cracked horn is shown.
51. **Shitmouth** and other Gregor men greet Jaime with coarse familiarity. Jaime briefly uses a threatened spear punishment to force an answer about command but does not carry it out.
52. **The garrison says Polliver had become castellan but Sandor Clegane killed him, the Tickler and a Sarsfield squire** at the crossroads inn. The information comes through an innkeeper and prostitutes, not these men's eyes.
53. **Reader-level correction from ASOS 74 (Arya):** Sandor killed Polliver, but **Arya killed the Tickler and the young squire**. Sandor was accompanied by Arya disguised as a peasant boy, not an unidentified male companion. The hearsay is substantially grounded but collapses different perpetrators into the Hound.
54. **Rafford, called Raff, wears Vargo Hoat's elaborate chain of mixed coins.** He is bold enough to speak for the garrison. His name, Gregor association and the earlier AFFC/ACOK references make him an important potential match for **Arya's named enemy Raff the Sweetling**; correlate the continuity rather than inventing a different Raff by default.
55. **No pursuit of Sandor followed Polliver's death.** The men say they feared the Hound and deferred the response to Gregor or other supposed superiors. Their willingness to terrorize peasants contrasts with reluctance to confront a feared fighter.
56. **Jaime demands to inspect prisoners and Vargo Hoat's head.** The head is recognizable from the long braided beard, although ears, lips, nose and eyes have been mutilated or consumed.
57. **Gregor's men describe systematic mutilation of the living Hoat**—sequential removal of extremities—and forcing both Hoat and prisoners to eat the remains. This is **CHARACTER CLAIM**, but the disfigured head directly supports severe abuse. Jaime is appalled by the retaliatory horror even though Hoat ordered his hand severed.
58. **Jaime recognizes an apparent historical repetition** in memories of Lady Lothston's reported cannibal feasts at Harrenhal. The old lore is not direct proof of the legend; its relevance is the castle's recurring association with cruelty.
59. **Jaime gives Hoat's head to Peck to discard in the lake** and announces that **Ser Bonifer Hasty** will hold Harrenhal for the crown until Petyr comes. Gregor's men may apply to remain with Bonifer or accompany Jaime to Riverrun.
60. **Gregor's men demand the rich rewards their master promised**, rejecting Bonifer's offer of one hide of farmland immediately, more upon marriage, and another on fatherhood. Gold and agrarian resettlement are competing incentives; Jaime sends claims against Gregor's promise to Cersei.
61. **Jaime finds Ser Wylis Manderly alive among the captives.** He also finds several highborn northmen captured at the Trident fords and surviving household personnel, including **Ben Blackthumb**, the cook who opened Harrenhal's postern, and **Pia**.
62. **Pia has suffered severe violence**: broken nose and many missing teeth, and she weeps upon seeing Jaime. Her condition reflects Gregor's punishment for speaking in his presence, not an unexplained change of appearance.
63. **The highborn captives are grimy, wounded and psychologically crushed but were fed**, unlike many less-valued captives. Jaime fears that they unknowingly ate Hoat's remains; the text does not give each captive's knowledge or precise meal history.
64. **Jaime orders Wylis escorted to Maidenpool and sent by ship to White Harbor**. Wylis breaks down emotionally. This is a release and transportation instruction, not confirmation of embarkation, arrival or political allegiance of Wyman Manderly.
65. **Jaime commands fires in the hall and food for his men, excluding goat.** This follows the revelation of the horrific feeding and shows he has absorbed its moral impact.
66. **Bonifer rejects all of Gregor's followers and Pia on moral grounds.** Jaime accepts the soldiers onto his moving force and separately takes Pia, proposing that she could wash his clothes. His partial protection of Pia sits alongside a readiness to expend violent soldiers as disposable shock troops at Riverrun.
67. **Bonifer's nominal 'Holy Hundred' is actually eighty-six**, fourteen lost at the Blackwater. His disciplined, pious unit is small for a castle as vast as Harrenhal. The precise nickname is an institutional tradition, not current strength.
68. **Jaime tests Bonifer's fitness for command**. Hasty has local neutrality, sober conduct and disciplined riders but uncertain battlefield distinction. Jaime suspects the appointment may come via Orton Merryweather's connections, while admitting the apparently naive choice might work.
69. **Bonifer cites the Seven-Pointed Star as protection against spirits, wights and revenants**; Jaime counsels material armor as well as faith. This is Bonifer's doctrinal claim, not a tested universal rule against undead enemies.
70. **Bonifer proposes cooperation with Lancel at Darry and Randyll Tarly at Maidenpool**, supported by Maester Gulian and ravens. The political-security network is an intention whose success depends on effective communication and policing.
71. **Jaime wants surviving Brave Companions apprehended** and asks to be told of them, naming Zollo, Shagwell, Rorge, Urswyck and others. Reader knowledge from AFFC 20/25 establishes Shagwell already dead and places Rorge toward Saltpans; Jaime's list is outdated.
72. **Bonifer distinguishes repentance from punishment**: religious forgiveness may be possible but serious crimes still warrant the block. His willingness to kill Sandor and bring Beric's movement to heel complicates a simple 'gentle piety' assessment.
73. **Jaime wants Beric Dondarrion brought alive for a public execution**, but leaves the fate of Thoros, the Myrish priest, largely to Bonifer. He does not know the complete Brotherhood/Stoneheart changes available to readers from the ASOS epilogue.
74. **Jaime approaches the bear pit for another practice session** after seeing Strongboar sparring with Flement Brax. Instead of Payne he finds Red Ronnet Connington examining the bones and remains of the bear Jaime had fought to save Brienne.
75. **Ronnet repeats the exaggerated rumor that Brienne fought the bear naked**. Jaime corrects him: the Mummers dressed her in pink silk with a tournament sword. The corrupted story shows how spectacles change in retelling.
76. **Ronnet reveals that he was once betrothed to Brienne, the second of three proposed matches**. He gives the crude account of his father's advice, his visit to Tarth and his humiliating rejection of her.
77. **Ronnet identifies Jon Connington as a cousin of his father, not an uncle**. Jaime remembers Jon's loyalty to Rhaegar, his appointment as Hand after Owen Merryweather's failure, dismissal after the Battle of the Bells and reported exile/death. **Jaime believes Jon died abroad**; he has not witnessed that death.
78. **The Connington family's division in Robert's Rebellion matters**: Jaime recalls Ronnet's father supported the rebels, receiving Griffin's Roost, but the Crown retained the gold and reassigned other Connington lands. Kinship, land, hereditary claim and opposite political loyalties remain separate facts.
79. **Ronnet describes giving Brienne a rose as an insult**, while Brienne's earlier AFFC 20 POV documents lasting shame from this rejection. Jaime did not previously know about this betrothal or her childhood wounds.
80. **Jaime strikes Ronnet across the mouth with his golden hand** when he insults Brienne again. The impact sends Ronnet and his lantern down the bear-pit steps, spilling burning oil. This is the prosthesis's first consequential use as an effective striking instrument in this scene rather than a fake replacement sword hand.
81. **Jaime demands that Ronnet refer to Brienne by her name and treat her as a highborn lady**. Ronnet complies under duress and then pointedly uses her mocking epithet. The chapter ends at this tense interruption, not a reconciliation or a formal punishment.
82. **End state:** Jaime leads a mixed force toward Riverrun; Bonifer is assigned Harrenhal; Wylis's release is ordered; Tyrek remains missing; Gregor's remaining men follow Jaime; Jaime is retraining secretly, and his attachment to Brienne is publicly expressed through his defense of her dignity.

## Cross-chapter comparisons

- **ASOS 74 (Arya) ↔ AFFC 20, 25 (Brienne) ↔ AFFC 27:** the original crossroads fight distinguishes Sandor killing Polliver from Arya killing Tickler and the squire. Harrenhal gossip assigns all deaths to Sandor; Brienne hears other Hound stories via intermediaries. A distinct **Saltpans atrocity report** cannot be assigned automatically to Sandor.
- **ACOK 26, 38 (Arya) ↔ AFFC 22 (Arya) ↔ AFFC 27:** Raff the Sweetling is an Arya revenge-list name and an associate of Gregor. Rafford/Raff is now active with Jaime's garrison; **Ilyn Payne**, another Arya list name, is Jaime's silent training partner. The apparently separate Braavos and riverlands plots retain live adversaries without proving future encounters.
- **AFFC 20 (Brienne) ↔ AFFC 27:** Brienne remembers Ronnet's treatment and rejects roses as humiliation. Jaime now hears Ronnet repeat the conduct and does something visible to stop the insult; neither POV knows all of the other's thoughts.
- **AFFC 8, 16, 24 (Jaime/Cersei) ↔ AFFC 27:** Jaime's knowledge of Varys/Rugen distinguishes Varys as a plausible manipulator, but does not independently establish a Tyrek plot. Cersei's concern about Wylis and lack of information from Harrenhal is now corrected at Jaime's location, not yet at the court.
- **AFFC 17, 24 (Cersei) ↔ AFFC 27:** royal demands about Wyman Manderly, return of Wylis and alleged Davos execution must be separated. Jaime's order to release Wylis neither confirms northern submission nor resolves Davos's identity.
- **AFFC 20, 25 (Brienne) ↔ AFFC 27:** Jaime still speaks of Shagwell as a fugitive, while Brienne already killed him. Jaime is equally unaware of Rorge's reported Saltpans route and Stoneheart-related distortions.
- **ASOS 75/77–79 and AFFC 8/16 (Jaime/Jon aftermath):** Jaime's current command is an oath conflict and political test for someone who values the Kingsguard White Book and his vow to Catelyn. His military goals and the means he is prepared to use require scrutiny, not automatic absolution.
- **AFFC 17, 24 (Cersei) and AFFC 25 (Brienne):** the sparrows' street presence, popular preference for Tyrells, Bonifer's hope of Faith-led restoration and Meribald's account of broken men show distinct religious responses to war—agitation, disciplined punishment and pastoral rehabilitation.
- **Later Connington research (NOT current Jaime knowledge):** track the divergent rebellion loyalties of Jon Connington and Ronnet's branch and test Jaime's claim about Jon's death before treating it as canon. This scene supplies a family and landholding nexus, not any verified return from exile.

## Open causal questions / disconfirming tests

1. **Tyrek:** Can independent witnesses place him at a location after the riot, establish a ransom demand, or reveal prior Varys planning? Absence of a corpse is insufficient to prove abduction; the missing witness may be merely a riot casualty.
2. **Kingsguard vows:** Can Jaime carry out a Riverrun surrender without attacking the Tullys or dishonoring Catelyn's promise? His belief that this is possible must be tested against what he actually does.
3. **Raff and Ilyn:** Verify name/physical/personnel continuity and watch for Arya's knowledge lag; proximity to Jaime does not guarantee Arya a confrontation.
4. **Hound/Saltpans:** Keep a source-by-source ledger of who saw burned skin, which Hound helmet or horse, and when; the earlier crossroads account does not validate later atrocity allegations.
5. **Wylis:** Track escort, boat, actual arrival and Wyman's independent strategy. Court reports about Davos need separate independent evidence.
6. **Harrenhal governance:** Test whether Bonifer's disciplined eighty-six can hold a huge cursed-in-reputation castle without Gregor's garrison, and whether sectarian doctrine helps or hinders local cooperation.
7. **Broken men versus punishment:** Compare the material conditions Jaime witnesses and perpetuates with Meribald's explanation; hanging outlaws and rehiring Gregor's men cannot be treated as settled justice without a victim-centered analysis.
8. **Connington:** Source-check reported exile and death, and analyze how rebel/loyalist branches acquired or lost lands; the account in Jaime's head is not eyewitness proof.
9. **Brienne:** Follow the practical consequences of Jaime's striking Ronnet, while keeping the defense of her name separate from a fulfilled vow, romance, or sustained moral transformation.
10. **Retold stories:** Track the false naked-bear detail and wrongly attributed crossroads killings as demonstrations of rumor distortion, including erroneous changes within otherwise true accounts.
11. **Wolves:** Distinguish Hogg's 'white star wolves', ordinary predators near Sow's Horn and the gigantic Trident pack rumored to Meribald; do not leap to a single cause.

**Completion receipt:** The chapter opens with Cersei dispatching Jaime and closes with Jaime striking Red Ronnet at Harrenhal's bear pit. The full original chapter has been read, and key earlier primary-source chapter notes cross-checked. No later outcome is imported as Jaime's knowledge.
