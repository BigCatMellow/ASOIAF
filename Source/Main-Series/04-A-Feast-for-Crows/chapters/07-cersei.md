## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Tywin lies in state at the Great Sept while cold rain falls on King's Landing.** The funeral is a public political performance as well as family mourning.
2. **Cersei tightly manages Tommen's behavior.** She prevents him from riding, corrects his posture and crown, and repeatedly insists a king must not cry in public.
3. **She measures Tommen against Joffrey rather than against Tommen's own temperament.** Joffrey's hardness is remembered as kingly strength despite the instability and cruelty the reader has witnessed.
4. **The public does not display spontaneous love for Tywin.** Cersei notices subdued crowds and interprets their duty-bound attendance through her father's prestige.
5. **The sitting High Septon reached office with Tyrion's help and Tywin's crown.** Cersei treats these associations as possible evidence he knows dangerous secrets, but no such knowledge is demonstrated here.
6. **Cersei has advertised a major reward for Tyrion.** She offers lordship for his head and has ravens sent widely, extending the manhunt beyond King's Landing.
7. **Tywin's corpse already smells and appears to wear a slight smile.** Physical decay is directly observed; any theory that the odor proves prior poisoning must compete with ordinary decomposition and preparation failure.
8. **Cersei blames Pycelle for the body's condition.** Her reaction fits her broader tendency to assign personal fault quickly when events embarrass her.
9. **She privately believes she can exceed Tywin's accomplishments.** The thought is an important measure of her confidence at the moment she begins dismantling parts of his political settlement.
10. **Lancel appears radically changed.** He is gaunt, prematurely white-haired and intensely religious after his wounds, and he is preparing to take Darry through marriage to a Frey widow.
11. **Lancel says the High Septon believes the Mother spared him so he could atone.** This is the High Septon's reported spiritual interpretation, not medical evidence.
12. **Cersei fears Lancel may confess their sexual relationship and Robert's drugged wine.** Her internal alarm strongly corroborates that these are real shared secrets rather than rumors invented by Stannis.
13. **She responds to Lancel's religious language with pressure for silence.** Rather than addressing guilt or risk openly, she tries to preserve secrecy.
14. **Cersei studies Margaery's companions for weaknesses she can exploit.** She is already treating the new queen's social circle as an intelligence target.
15. **Taena Merryweather stands out because she is ambitious and useful.** Taena says she has sent word across the Narrow Sea to watch for Tyrion; Cersei begins drawing her closer.
16. **Mace Tyrell expects his uncle Garth to become master of coin under an arrangement he attributes to Tywin.** Cersei rejects that expectation and names Gyles Rosby instead.
17. **The appointment publicly undercuts Mace.** Cersei values demonstrating that she, not Tywin's prior bargains or the Tyrell alliance, controls offices.
18. **Lady Olenna openly remarks on the odor of Tywin's corpse.** Cersei answers politely while privately imagining Olenna's death. No assassination plan is yet enacted.
19. **Tommen reports that Margaery has spoken of taking him to Highgarden after their wedding.** Cersei refuses the idea, revealing that even ordinary marital integration feels to her like loss of control.
20. **Gyles Rosby is physically ill while becoming master of coin.** His severe cough and blood make his durability doubtful even as Cersei selects him as a supposedly manageable official.
21. **Cersei wants the records to imply Rosby was already part of the council.** Her instruction to backdate or regularize the appearance of appointment shows willingness to manipulate administrative chronology for legitimacy.
22. **Rosby wants to remove Littlefinger's financial appointees.** Cersei is receptive and assumes Petyr will eventually lose the Vale and return dependent on her favor.
23. **That model of Littlefinger's weakness is unverified.** The reader knows Petyr has established substantial control in the Vale, making Cersei's forecast a useful example of decision-making from stale or incomplete intelligence.
24. **Qyburn searches Rugen's former cell and finds a concealed old gold coin.** The coin bears imagery associated with an ancient Gardener king of the Reach.
25. **The coin is real physical evidence but its provenance is unknown.** It could have belonged to Rugen, been hidden by someone else, been planted or be unrelated to the escape.
26. **Qyburn initially presents the find as evidence that the cell contained hidden valuables.** Cersei rapidly transforms it into a Tyrell conspiracy theory because the coin is associated with the Reach.
27. **The Gardener coin therefore reveals more about Cersei's inference process than about Tyrell guilt at this stage.** No payer, recipient, transaction or link to Tyrion's escape is established.
28. **Qyburn gives the clearest medical account yet of Gregor Clegane's decline.** He identifies the poison as manticore venom altered to act slowly, while also mentioning the possibility of sorcery as speculation.
29. **Gregor's symptoms remain severe and prolonged.** Darkened veins, infected-looking fluids, a large wound, constant screaming and heavy milk-of-the-poppy use are directly reported by Qyburn.
30. **Gregor's longstanding headaches and opiate use predate Oberyn's wound.** Those details are potentially relevant to character and health history but do not explain the new systemic decline.
31. **Qyburn says the Citadel stripped his chain because he experimented on living people to understand death.** This is his admission and institutional account from his perspective.
32. **He also invokes Marwyn's contempt for conservative archmaesters.** The remark is evidence of a relationship or shared criticism, not proof of a coordinated Citadel conspiracy.
33. **Cersei authorizes Qyburn to use Gregor as he wishes in the black cells.** She provides secrecy, access and support while requiring Gregor's head for Dorne after death.
34. **This is a significant expansion of Qyburn's power.** Cersei prefers an investigator she sees as useful and personally loyal despite knowing he was expelled for prohibited human experimentation.
35. **Cersei's Gardener-coin theory hardens almost immediately into presumed fact.** Ambiguous evidence becomes political certainty because it confirms her prior distrust of the Tyrells.
36. **Kevan refuses Cersei's offer to become Hand on her terms.** He will serve only if she surrenders the regency and returns to Casterly Rock.
37. **Kevan says this course aligns with Tywin's own intentions.** He reports Tywin meant Cersei to leave court and perhaps remarry; the reader cannot independently verify every unexecuted detail, but Kevan was Tywin's closest political lieutenant.
38. **Kevan judges Cersei as a poor ruler and mother.** He points to Joffrey and the need for Tommen to be separated from her influence.
39. **Cersei responds by throwing wine at him.** The confrontation breaks the expectation that Kevan will simply inherit Tywin's subordinate role.
40. **Kevan has independent material power.** He can support hundreds of knights and potentially hire additional men, so his refusal is more than symbolic family dissent.
41. **Kevan proposes Mathis Rowan or Randyll Tarly as Hand.** His reasoning is to give powerful Reach lords a stake distinct from Mace Tyrell's personal control.
42. **Cersei interprets even this balancing proposal as evidence Kevan has been bought by the Tyrells.** Again, suspicion substitutes for demonstrated motive.
43. **Kevan indicates he understands Tommen's true paternity.** His final remark that Tommen has his mother and, he thinks, his father strongly implies knowledge of Jaime and Cersei's incest.
44. **That implication explains why Cersei cannot simply dominate him through family authority.** Kevan possesses both independent power and dangerous knowledge.
45. **End state:** Cersei has isolated Kevan, humiliated Mace, elevated Rosby and Qyburn, strengthened her own suspicion of the Tyrells and failed to secure an experienced Lannister Hand.

## Cross-POV implications

- **Cersei is beginning to create the threats she fears:** humiliating Mace and rejecting Kevan weaken the coalition that protected Tommen after Tywin's death.
- **The Gardener coin is a high-value epistemic test:** future evidence should establish provenance before it is used to validate Tyrell involvement in Tyrion's escape.
- **Qyburn rises because Cersei rewards usefulness over institutional constraint:** that decision gives a disgraced experimenter access to Gregor and royal secrecy.
- **Kevan is not another Tywin proxy:** his conditions, military resources and knowledge make him an autonomous political actor.
- **Lancel's atonement creates a leak risk rooted in conscience rather than espionage:** Cersei has no good way to control a man who now values confession over advancement.
