## Key plot and interpretive findings

### The hangings: salt, not crabapples, and no proven hangmen

Scores of corpses hang along the approach, first among trees and then from constructed gibbets. Every corpse Brienne inspects bears a **noose and salt in its mouth**. Heraldry and clothing suggest leftovers from multiple armies. Hyle says these are the **Saltpans raiders**, while Brienne guesses their executioners may be the **Brotherhood Without Banners**, and her earlier information includes both Beric and Lady Stoneheart. **Neither the identities of all victims nor the hangmen are independently verified**. These corpses have a different symbolic marker from Jaime's **crabapple-stuffed hanged foragers** in AFFC 33, and both differ from a simple inference that *all* hangings in the riverlands are Stoneheart's.

### The crossroads inn and the rust-red dragon

Meribald's history of the property follows a politically revealing chain: **Old Inn → Two Crowns (Jaehaerys and his queen) → Bellringer Inn → Clanking Dragon (three-headed black iron sign forged by Long Jon Heddle) → River Inn → crossroads inn**. During the **Blackfyre rebellion**, a loyalist Darry lord allegedly destroyed the iron dragon sign for looking like the pretender's black dragon. A separated head was said to wash onto the **Quiet Isle** and rust to **red**. This offers unusually explicit, materially grounded *black dragon becoming red* imagery; the sign head is a **reported artifact, not examined in this chapter**, and the association with a possible disguised Blackfyre claimant is **a motif to test, not proof of any person's ancestry**. Historical river movement stranded the former waterfront wing and ferry. The same inn witnessed **Arya and Sandor's ASOS 74 battle**; embedded blood remains.

**Masha Heddle** gave Meribald food and shelter before she was hanged by Lannisters; later an unnamed lord reportedly killed her innkeeping nephew. Two nieces now attempt to keep the inn open, while **Willow, age ~10**, directs a shelter filled with children who lost their parents. They demand silver and need Meribald's donkey provisions, but shelter strangers despite risks. Sparrows sometimes lead children there, a possible **grassroots Faith charity network**; there is no proof the High Sparrow personally commands these transfers. The orphan refuge's vulnerability gives the battle a concrete purpose beyond Brienne's hunt.

### Gendry and the false Arya lead

Brienne mistakes **Gendry** momentarily for Renly because of his black hair and blue eyes. Gendry differs in age, size and outlook, resembling **Robert's** powerful physique, and says he was born in King's Landing, his mother is dead, and his father unknown. The reader has **AGOT 27 Eddard's separate identification of Gendry as probable bastard of Robert**. Brienne reaches a strong *inferential* version of that conclusion, not a witnessed confession by Robert or an independently documented birth. Gendry is **not merely pretending to be knighted**: ASOS 39 Arya directly showed **Beric Dondarrion knighting him** to defend the weak, explaining his firm insistence here that he is a knight and helping account for his devotion to the **Lord of Light**.

Brienne also wonders if **Willow is Arya Stark**, an inference based on age, plainness, brown hair/eyes and her command of the children. **AFFC 34 Cat of the Canals establishes the real Arya in Braavos**, refuting this particular suspicion for the reader, though Brienne has not learned that. The three highways offer live choices—north to Winterfell, west to Riverrun, east to the Vale, or south back to Jaime and her father on Tarth—but no route is decided before the attack. The separation from Meribald and Hyle is **planned for dawn, not accomplished**.

### Rorge, the stolen helm and why Brienne survives the first duel

The Elder Brother in **AFFC 31** claimed Sandor died and someone stole the helm left atop his cairn. **AFFC 20** placed **Rorge heading toward Saltpans**. Here a rider with **the recognizably stolen hound-head helmet** is **the man Shagwell said lost his nose and genitals**; Brienne remembers that conversation, and the rider is surrounded by surviving violent outlaws including **Biter**. **The cumulative identification of this man as Rorge is very strong**; it is not evidence that *Sandor* personally ravaged Saltpans, and the Elder Brother's death/burial testimony about Sandor remains separate. The mechanism/time when Rorge took the helmet from the cairn is **not personally observed**.

The raiders arrive seven strong; one offers to leave if given horses and food, but their helmeted leader immediately threatens sexual violence against **Willow** and Brienne. Brienne consciously chooses to defend the child knowing she cannot fight seven directly. She draws Rorge into an **individual duel** with a deliberately provocative taunt. Armed with Jaime's **Oathkeeper**, and remembering **Ser Goodwin's training**, she uses mud, rain, pace and his pride to exhaust him; after a shoulder graze she drives Oathkeeper through his body and kills him. Her utterance about **sapphires** points back to Jaime's sapphires bluff to Vargo Hoat and to the weapon's moral and material provenance. **The Hound impersonator's death is directly observed. Sandor's is not.**

**Biter then attacks immediately**. He knocks Brienne down, strangles her, smashes her head, pulls her hair, breaks her forearm, and bites off flesh from her face. She slashes his belly with a concealed dagger but cannot regain control. She hears fighting nearby and **guesses** Hyle has joined; neither the battle's outcome nor an actual rescue is shown. In the final seconds she sees a pointed, unusually elongated tongue like a sword while fainting. **Her impaired perceptions cannot independently establish whether this is a literal tongue, a blade, a rescue or delirium**. A reader must resume in a future POV rather than silently resolving the cliffhanger.

### Agency, gender, and the two kinds of vows

Willow controls entry and food allocation despite being a child; Hyle ridicules vulnerable children, proposes marriage to Brienne explicitly to gain **Tarth**, admits an existing daughter he fails to parent, and suggests entering Brienne's bed against her stated wishes. Brienne refuses his sexual advances clearly and directs her care toward the hungry Gendry. She likewise refuses to flee when Willow is threatened even though it may kill her. The vow to Catelyn and Jaime, and the ordinary obligation to protect a small child, drive the same act. Her fight is **a material defense of innocents**, not proof her entire quest has succeeded.

## Cross-source questions and testable theories

1. **ASOS 74 Arya ↔ AFFC 37:** Arya and Sandor killed the Tickler, Polliver and the young squire at the same inn. Compare each death and the floor's blood traces; note the Red Keep/Riverrun travelers now passing unknowingly through the same violence.
2. **AGOT 27 Eddard / ASOS 39 Arya ↔ AFFC 37:** Ned identifies Gendry's strong Baratheon resemblance; Beric formally knights him. Brienne's discovery matters more than mere coincidence: a suspected royal bastard is **working at a pro-smallfolk refuge with a Brotherhood connection**. Do not assume he knows his bloodline or is being saved as dynastic claimant.
3. **AFFC 20 and 31 Brienne ↔ AFFC 37:** Rorge's Saltpans route and stolen helm culminate in his direct confrontation with Brienne. The identification **shatters apparent evidence against Sandor** but does **not** independently confirm the Quiet Isle gravedigger identity.
4. **AFFC 33 Jaime ↔ AFFC 37:** Different mouth offerings (**crabapples / salt**) demand a subgroup or event-by-event execution investigation. Jaime's Stoneheart-related ignorance does not transfer to Brienne, who still only guesses Beric.
5. **AFFC 25 Brienne ↔ AFFC 37:** Hyle's reported hunt for the Hound becomes physically urgent; the dangerous figures are *someone using the stolen helmet* and Biter, not the Sandor Arya left wounded by the Trident.
6. **Blackfyre sign motif:** Correlate the black three-headed iron dragon, Blackfyre political seizure, red-rusted washed-up head, and any later dragon-banner changes. A motif alone cannot prove Aegon ancestry, a dragon's color or the sign's continued custody on Quiet Isle.
7. **Faith charity:** Identify who gathers orphaned children and whether the sparrows' routes reflect central High Sparrow's order, Meribald's independent network or other charitable initiatives.
8. **Willow's identity:** The true Arya is in Braavos; Willow's biological relations to Jeyne, Masha or other inn residents still require independent direct identification. Avoid conflating this Jeyne with Jeyne Westerling.
9. **Rorge's last combatants:** Count riders and wounds; determine separately Biter's fate, which outlaws fought Hyle/Gendry and what happened to Dog, Podrick, Meribald and children.
10. **Brienne's fate:** Read the next reliable on-page continuation before asserting survival, death, resurrection, tongue bite or emergency treatment. Preserve the **extreme facial and forearm injuries** in future continuity.
