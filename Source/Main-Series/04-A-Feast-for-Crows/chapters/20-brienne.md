# AFFC 20 — Brienne: Crackclaw Point, the Whispers and the price of mistrust

- Status: FIRST-PASS COMPLETE — independent challenge and cross-POV follow-up open.
- Primary source: entire [AFFC 20 Brienne](../text/021-brienne.md), read 2026-10-18.

## Chronological close reading

1. Brienne, Podrick and Nimble Dick follow the coast northeast from Maidenpool; inns vanish as the road becomes increasingly isolated.
2. Brienne refuses to share a room with Dick and asks Podrick to watch him while she sleeps, but explicitly instructs the boy to alert her rather than attack.
3. Dick searches Brienne's saddlebags for promised gold and gets flour on his fingers. She had concealed the real gold on her person and padded a decoy purse with flour before leaving Duskendale. This proves Dick opportunistically searched, not that he plotted murder.
4. Dick describes Crackclaw Point's distinctive local autonomy, inherited First Men ancestry, bog defenses, rivalries, and efforts by Darklyns, Mootons and Celtigars to assert overlordship. These are local historical claims, some embellished.
5. Brienne recounts the legend of Galladon of Morne and the Just Maid, a supposedly divinely gifted sword used only three times because using it against ordinary opponents would be unfair. The dragon-slaying is legend, not witnessed history.
6. Dick contrasts Galladon with fantastic tales of Clarence Crabb, Crackbones and a dragon. These are regional heroic narratives, not evidence for living animated heads or dragons.
7. Dick says Visenya accepted Crackclaw submission directly to the Targaryens, bypassing neighboring lords, and claims Crackclaw houses fought for Rhaegar at the Trident. He identifies old Targaryen loyalties; verify against other historical testimony before promoting the precise roster as fact.
8. At a fishing village Brienne and Podrick secure the hayloft and withdraw the ladder, leaving Dick below; she recognizes his hunger but remains distrustful.
9. Brienne remembers Septa Roelle insisting apparent compliments were merely attempts to curry favor with Selwyn Tarth. The lesson reinforced her later humiliation by Hyle Hunt's wager.
10. Brienne recalls deliberately defeating participants in the wager during the Bitterbridge melee, then Ronnet Connington and Loras Tyrell. The memory links her rejection of roses, her allegiance to Renly and her later relationship with Jaime.
11. Cold rain, failing firewood and dwindling shelter exhaust the party. Dick describes grotesque amphibious creatures called squishers, allegedly predators of children. No creature is witnessed, and the story is explicitly told to frighten Podrick.
12. Brienne's reflections on Renly and Catelyn are dominated by perceived failures to protect them. She both wishes Jaime were present and accepts that his place is with the king.
13. The road terminates beneath Dyre Den, House Brune's cliffside seat. A steep concealed path leads to its walls; Dick appears familiar with the household and Brienne notices a missing badge on his tunic, leading her to suspect desertion.
14. Podrick spots a distant rider following their route. Brienne suspects Dick, but neither can identify the rider at this point. Rather than confront him at Dyre Den, the party enters the forest.
15. The interior forests are navigationally dangerous: indistinguishable pines, bogs, caves, ruined structures, and low visibility. One seeming path nearly swallows their horses. Dick's local route knowledge is valuable even though his honesty is doubtful.
16. Brienne sleeps only about four hours per night while Podrick keeps watch. Fatigue and her anticipation of a trap impair her judgment.
17. Brienne remembers Ser Goodwin testing her willingness to kill by making her slaughter livestock. His Stepstones anecdote warns that hesitation after defeating an opponent can be fatal. Brienne prays she will not flinch if forced into actual battle.
18. The Whispers is real: a derelict triangular coastal castle with a collapsed wall, a blocked main portcullis, overgrown ruins, and a cave below a fallen beacon tower. The old smugglers' access is visibly unusable.
19. The seemingly disembodied whispers have a direct physical explanation: sea water booms and reverberates through passages below the cliffs. Dick continues claiming they are the legendary living heads.
20. A partly cleared bramble near the postern and evidence of a recent fire show that people have occupied the supposedly deserted ruin. Their identities remain unknown until the ambush.
21. Dick anxiously acknowledges he deceived the 'fool' about the abandoned smuggler landing and fears the travelers might retaliate. His earlier informant story is thus partly confirmed as an actual deception, not evidence Sansa visited.
22. Brienne arms Dick with her ordinary longsword and draws Oathkeeper for herself, simultaneously reducing his vulnerability and making herself tactically ready. She posts Podrick outside to watch the horses.
23. Three surviving Brave Companions—Pyg, Timeon, and Shagwell—appear from hiding. Brienne recognizes them from Harrenhal; Shagwell is the apparent 'fool' at the Whispers, and the earlier reported fool was not Dontos.
24. Shagwell attacks Dick immediately, destroying his knee and killing him. The ambushers identify Dick as the man who sold them the old map. This overturns Brienne's theory that Dick was steering her into a jointly organized ambush.
25. Timeon reports the Brave Companions scattered after leaving Harrenhal: Urswyck's group headed toward Oldtown and Rorge went toward Saltpans. These are interested witnesses' accounts and need cross-checking.
26. Timeon attributes Vargo Hoat's illness to the bite Jaime gave him and recounts Gregor's brutal killing of Hoat. Much is secondhand, but it clarifies the consequences of Jaime's resistance and the group's dissolution.
27. Timeon reports an encounter with Beric's men and says they heard Arya Stark was traveling with the Hound toward Riverrun. A later report from the crossroads inn confirmed a girl accompanied the Hound. This is a composite report—relevant to Arya's previous route, not direct proof of her present location.
28. Brienne thinks the girl may have been bound for her relatives at Riverrun. She has not established whether the report concerns Arya or Sansa; the speaker's 'Stark girl' does not mean Sansa.
29. The three assailants attempt to force Brienne toward the cliff and threaten her. She attacks Pyg first and kills him with Oathkeeper despite receiving an injury.
30. Brienne defeats Timeon despite a shoulder wound; his account of the Brotherhood and Hound does not exempt him from his own aggression. Oathkeeper's sharpness is material in the fight.
31. Podrick climbs the ruined wall and hurls stones at Shagwell, distracting him twice. His intervention decisively assists Brienne, though she had left him on horse guard for safety.
32. Shagwell pleads to surrender; Brienne orders him to dig Dick's grave beneath the young weirwood. The burial reflects Dick's local belonging and Brienne's belated recognition of his humanity.
33. While Brienne lowers Dick into the grave, Shagwell attacks with a rock. Brienne kills him using a concealed dagger but repeatedly demands that he laugh, then recognizes her own sobbing. This is a visible moment of emotional excess and distress after combat, not simply clean professional violence.
34. Brienne puts two gold dragons into Dick's grave, fulfilling the reward for finding the supposed fool although the result was tragic. She apologizes for not trusting him; his attempt at theft remains real, but her assumption that he planned her murder was wrong.
35. Hyle Hunt reveals himself as the following rider. He says Randyll Tarly ordered him to trail Brienne and recover Sansa if she found the girl, while sparing Brienne. This identifies one surveillance mechanism but does not prove any other trackers absent.
36. Hyle helps bury Dick after sarcastically mocking the gold in the grave. Brienne resolves to seek the Hound based on Timeon's information; that choice still rests on partially reliable and time-lagged reports.
37. End state: Dick, Pyg, Timeon and Shagwell are dead; Brienne has survived and used Oathkeeper; Podrick has directly assisted her; Hyle has rejoined; her revised Stark-girl lead is temporally stale and may identify Arya rather than Sansa.

## Research implications

- **AFFC 14 corrected:** Dick *did* direct a purported fool to an abandoned landing, but that fool was Shagwell rather than Dontos; his report of two girls never involved an eyewitness sighting. The supposed Sansa trail has failed.
- **Hound identity:** Timeon's report of a real encounter between the Hound and a Stark girl describes Arya's earlier movements. Rorge's Saltpans direction is particularly important when auditing accusations made against Sandor.
- **Trust as a theme:** Brienne is right to guard her money against Dick and right to anticipate danger at the ruin; she is wrong that he knowingly orchestrated the danger. Her apology and two-dragon burial are not proof he was honest about everything.
- **Honor versus vengeance:** Galladon's unused enchanted blade frames Brienne's lethal use of Oathkeeper. Her killing of the armed attackers is survival; her emotional state with Shagwell complicates a simple heroic account.
- **Young weirwood:** Tree and burial are directly observed; any role as witness or mystical observer is a hypothesis, not demonstrated.
- **Podrick:** His courage and awareness save Brienne at a key point despite her instruction to remain with the horses; avoid describing him as an expert swordsman.
