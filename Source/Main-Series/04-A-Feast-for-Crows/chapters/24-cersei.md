# AFFC 24 — Cersei: the Braavosi creditor, Tommen's lessons, Qyburn and Bronn

- **Status:** FIRST-PASS COMPLETE. Independent challenge and subsequent-source checking remain open.
- **Source:** complete [AFFC 24 — Cersei](../text/025-cersei.md), read 2026-10-18.

## Sequential close reading

1. Tommen asks to sit on the Iron Throne and attend meetings; Margaery has told him that kings should learn governance. Cersei refuses because she intends to govern until his majority and views instruction as Tyrell encroachment.
2. Cersei threatens to have Tommen's whipping boy Pate hurt unless he tells her Margaery prompted him. This coercion is part of a pattern of suppressing the child's autonomy.
3. Cersei substitutes memorizing names of kings and Hands for attending councils. Her recollection of Robert leaving government to Jon Arryn underlines the irony that she denies her son practical experience.
4. Gyles Rosby's persistent coughing accompanies treasury business; despite severe illness, he remains the financial gatekeeper and later struggles with a Braavosi creditor.
5. Aurane Waters says the first three of the commissioned dromonds are nearing completion and requests more funds for elaborate finishings; Cersei approves. This is construction progress, not a completed or operational fleet.
6. At council Pycelle favors proven survivors of the Blackwater for ship captains; Aurane favors younger candidates. Cersei backs Aurane and dismisses survivorship as a sign of failure. Experience, patronage and fleet competency are at issue.
7. Merchant guild representatives complain of sparrows occupying city streets and squares. Cersei considers using the gold cloaks; she still frames the growing religious movement as public disorder.
8. Pycelle reports that Wyman Manderly has displayed a supposed Davos head and severed hands above White Harbor, with Frey witnesses identifying a recognizable maimed hand. The report confirms claims and apparent displays; Cersei never sees the victim or independently verifies that Davos died.
9. Cersei responds by ordering Wyman's son returned and anticipates White Harbor's loyalty. Neither the hostage's actual delivery nor Wyman's internal allegiance is shown.
10. Cersei predicts Bolton forces will clear Moat Cailin, then Torrhen's Square and Deepwood Motte, and win the rest of Ned's bannermen. This is a southern forecast, not observed northern military success.
11. Storm's End remains under Mace Tyrell's siege; two dozen mangonels achieve little against the walls. Cersei derides Mace as inactive despite his ongoing investment.
12. Noho Dimittis, envoy of the Iron Bank, has held six unproductive meetings with Gyles and has been delayed by Cersei for a fortnight. She ends the audience without paying or negotiating, deepening the concrete consequences of AFFC 17's debt suspension.
13. Cersei occupies a separate gilded chair at the base of the Iron Throne, recognizing that only king or Hand may sit on the actual throne; her formal regent status is distinct from the sovereignty she claims in private.
14. Cersei presses Osmund Kettleblack because Osney has not completed her scheme to compromise Margaery. Osney has been installed as Tommen's sworn shield to facilitate proximity.
15. Osmund describes Margaery's familiarity with Osney—conversation, joking, touching his scars, and archery instruction—but these are Osney's interested reports and do not establish sexual involvement.
16. Margaery is seldom alone: Tommen, rotating sleeping companions, cousins, septa, guards, and court entertainers are nearly always present. Her dense social household is an actual security barrier to Cersei's planned trap.
17. The frequent visitors to Margaery include Redwyne twins, Ser Tallad, singers, and Jalabhar Xho. Osmund says Jalabhar is teaching Margaery's circle the Summer Tongue; Cersei speculates disparagingly about his motives.
18. Cersei observes Tommen breaking a lance against a quintain while guided by Loras and cheered by Margaery and her attendants. His excitement is genuine and precedes Cersei's intervention.
19. Cersei credits Tommen's jousting potential to Robert and slips: Robert favored melees, whereas Jaime was an excellent jouster. Margaery asks for specific examples, momentarily exposing Cersei's personal confusion between husband and brother.
20. Cersei demands Loras stop training the king, invoking risk from the horse, sandbag and lance. Loras points out the Red Keep has lacked a master-at-arms since Aron Santagar died.
21. Cersei remembers how a squire's attachment can transfer to his mentor and rejects Loras chiefly because it might bond Tommen to a Tyrell. She considers a Dornish master-at-arms because Dornish-Highgarden rivalry could counterbalance Tyrell influence.
22. Qyburn repeats news that the Golden Company's departure from Myr disrupts Free Cities military plans and that revolts and dragons are discussed in reports from Meereen. Cersei dismisses these as irrelevant and confusingly describes the reports as harpies.
23. Qyburn reports Doran has imprisoned Ser Daemon Sand after he demanded the Sand Snakes' release; he also reports Sylva Santagar was unexpectedly married to elderly Lord Eldon Estermont and sent to Greenstone. Cersei does not recognize the linkage to Arianne's failed expedition.
24. Cersei assumes Sylva's rapid marriage may conceal pregnancy, an unsupported insinuation. Qyburn notes both Daemon and Sylva were close to Arianne, indicating the Crown possesses potentially meaningful but fragmentary Dornish intelligence.
25. Memory of Cersei's marriage includes a visit to Greenstone, Robert's involvement with a widowed cousin, and Jaime following Robert at Cersei's request. She links the visit to Joffrey's possible conception but offers no independent timing check.
26. Qyburn reports a popular animal puppet play whose greedy lions consume their subjects and a protesting stag, after which a hatched dragon consumes the lions. This is political satire with explicit dragon imagery, not proof its creators possess inside information about Daenerys.
27. Cersei treats the puppet ending as treason. She orders audiences investigated, wealthy spectators fined half their wealth, poor spectators mutilated, and performers executed, allowing Qyburn to take some performers into his custody.
28. Qyburn notes Senelle is now incapacitated by his handling of her. Cersei briefly remembers her detention and a frightening sound belowground, yet approves giving him further prisoners; the nature of his experiments is not disclosed.
29. Jaime arrives with Tommen, supports a daily jousting lesson, and notes his wall inspection found sound fortifications but corroded Iron Gate hinges and damage to the King's and Mud Gates from Stannis's assault. The stronger strategic threat, he says, is Highgarden's faction already inside the city.
30. Cersei interprets the Gaoler Rugen's hidden old gold coin as Highgarden evidence, despite no demonstrated connection between the Gardener coin's origins and Tyrell conspiracy. This is a central provenance and inference error.
31. Tommen insists on Loras, a white horse, a kitten and avoiding beets; Cersei threatens Pate again, demands obedience and defers the training. Jaime undermines her contention that Osmund is Loras's superior.
32. Cersei privately concludes Jaime must be removed from her inner circle because he contradicts her; Jaime directly calls her judgment foolish. Their rupture is an observed change in alliance, not an announced concrete assassination plan.
33. Cersei blames washerwomen for older gowns no longer fitting and, after Taena advises mercy, deducts the gowns' cost from their wages. The text does not independently establish who caused the fit change.
34. Taena is now an intimate confidante and has urged Cersei to be kind to smallfolk. Cersei compares her friendship with Melara Hetherspoon, remembered as drowned, while treating Melara's ambitions with enduring hostility.
35. At dinner Cersei receives Falyse Stokeworth and Balman Byrch; she learns elderly Tanda broke her hip following a riding accident involving a failed saddle girth. She privately assumes Tanda will soon die; outcome is not established.
36. Falyse reports Lord Gyles's unnamed young ward denied hospitality at Rosby and may seek to keep estate and title; she asserts a remote family inheritance claim. Her suspicions are self-interested and not independently established.
37. Balman reports rough armed men with seven-pointed stars on the road, consistent with the increasingly mobilized sparrows. Cersei reports Septon Ollidor's prospects collapsed after sparrows exposed him visiting a brothel, with Luceon now close to a majority.
38. Balman disavows the choice to name Lollys's son Tyrion. Cersei outwardly forgives the Stokeworths while viewing Bronn as a threat and selecting Balman as a potential instrument.
39. Cersei manipulates Balman and Falyse into suspecting Bronn is secretly harboring Tyrion and raising sellswords for him. They confirm he has recruited four men of doubtful knighthood, but not that he is hiding Tyrion or plotting against Tommen.
40. Cersei seeks Bronn's death in an apparent hunting accident. Balman indicates his willingness, and Cersei promises Lollys could have a different husband afterward. This is a commissioned attempt, not a completed death.
41. Her dinner entails extensive drinks, delays and domestic costs, contrasting the ease with which she commissions violence and resents paying for hospitality.
42. Cersei visits Tommen after the meal and finds three black kittens provided by Margaery. The kittens represent Tommen's own wish fulfilled; Cersei reads even the gift primarily as manipulation.
43. The black kittens trigger Cersei's memory of Rhaegar's daughter and the violent fall of his family, followed by bitter counterfactuals about her own intended marriage to Rhaegar. The book's images of black cats are not proof of magical bad luck.
44. Cersei recalls Tywin privately suggesting her marriage to Rhaegar when she was six or seven and hopes raised again at the Lannisport tourney when she was ten; Rhaegar wore black plate and gold ringmail and performed with a silver-stringed harp.
45. Genna told Cersei a betrothal would be announced at the final feast, but Aerys rejected Tywin's suit, calling him a servant whose daughter could not marry the heir. This establishes an old dynastic humiliation in Cersei's recollection.
46. Cersei's childhood visit to Maggy the Frog, joined by Jeyne and Melara, coincides with the expected Rhaegar betrothal. The prediction challenged her belief she was guaranteed that marriage.
47. Cersei imagines marrying Rhaegar would have prevented his interest in the 'wolf girl' and produced a different royal line. This is retrospective wish fulfillment, not evidence of Rhaegar's motives or an established alternative history.
48. The chapter closes on Cersei's refusal to forgive Robert for Rhaegar's death and her expectation that Bronn will shortly suffer retribution; both reveal the personal origins of political decisions.

## Follow-up tests

- **Davos:** Compare the Manderly/Frey assertion to subsequent Davos POV evidence; never turn a political loyalty performance into independent proof of death.
- **Tommen's education:** Track Loras's influence, Cersei's proposed Dornish counterweight, and the use of Pate's punishment to compel Tommen's obedience.
- **Iron Bank:** Track concrete financial, trade and political responses to the king's default, rather than describing Braavosi retaliation before evidence appears.
- **Cersei's perception:** Three separate information opportunities (Dorne, Slaver's Bay and the puppet-show dragon) are dismissed or misread as she focuses on Margaery and Bronn.
- **Qyburn:** Investigate Senelle, the prison cells and his requests for further prisoners with explicit limits on what this chapter shows.
- **Stokeworth/Bronn:** Test Balman's competence against Bronn's military background; Cersei's promise of replacing Lollys's husband shows her indifference to Bronn's household.
- **Cersei's backstory:** Cross-check Rhaegar's broken marriage arrangement, Aerys–Tywin estrangement and Maggy's prophecy, distinguishing memory, fact and self-justifying hindsight.
