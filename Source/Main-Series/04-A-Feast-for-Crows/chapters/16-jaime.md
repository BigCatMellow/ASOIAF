# AFFC 16 — Jaime: Tywin's funeral, Cersei's council and the Kingsguard's memory

- Status: FIRST-PASS COMPLETE; independent challenge open.
- Primary source: [AFFC 16 Jaime](../text/017-jaime.md), read in full 2026-10-18.

## Complete chapter sequence
1. Tywin's funeral procession exits through the Gate of the Gods. Jaime notes the mismatch between his father's lion identity and the gate choice. Fifty knights, one hundred crossbowmen, three hundred men-at-arms, and western lords accompany the bier.
2. Jaime rides to bid Kevan farewell, not to represent Cersei. Kevan holds his position: he will attend Lancel's wedding at Darry and restore the castle and its lands, rather than serve Cersei as Hand.
3. Jaime tries unsuccessfully to speak privately with Lancel about Tyrion's allegations involving Cersei. Lancel remains constantly near Kevan or septons, and Jaime discounts the accusation despite its hold on him.
4. Kevan reports raiding blamed on Sandor Clegane along the Trident. Jaime recalls the exceptionally destructive attack at Saltpans. This is an attribution circulating among characters, NOT verified proof of Sandor's involvement.
5. Jaime urges Kevan to guard himself and offers other commanders for the outlaw pursuit, wanting Kevan in the Handship. Kevan takes his caution as a possible threat before Jaime explains.
6. Kevan's reference to Jaime's access to Cersei's bedchamber makes Jaime conclude Kevan knows about the twins and their children. Jaime worries his sister might harm Kevan, but no such plan is demonstrated.
7. Jaime taunts Lancel about his approaching wedding. Lancel reacts with piety rather than offering an answer about Jaime's underlying suspicion.
8. The Tyrell army has divided: Garlan takes half back to Highgarden; Mace Tyrell and Mathis Rowan take half to invest Storm's End. Two thousand Lannister veterans await Redwyne shipping to Dragonstone. These are deployments/plans, not captured castles.
9. Remaining western troops return to harvesting and rebuilding. Jaime recalls that Cersei presented Tommen to the departing men effectively, demonstrating political ability when she chooses to exercise it.
10. Watching quintain practice, Jaime recognizes Loras's extraordinary horsemanship and considers the permanent consequences of losing his sword hand for jousting.
11. In Cersei's solar, Pycelle reports that Bronn has named Lollys Stokeworth's newborn son Tyrion. Jaime appreciates the insult; Cersei considers a hostile response, but her intended action is not specified.
12. Jaime notices Cersei drinking more heavily and revisits her reaction to the burning Tower of the Hand. He witnessed tears and intense emotion; he cannot distinguish grief from pleasure.
13. The wildfire spectacle triggers Jaime's firsthand recollection of King Aerys's cruelty toward Queen Rhaella. Jaime and Jon Darry were on duty when the queen called out in distress, but Darry maintained that their protective vows did not allow intervention against the king.
14. Rhaella's attendants later reported signs that she had been injured. Jaime heard their account after her departure; he did not directly inspect those injuries. His recollection exposes a consequential limitation in the Kingsguard's concept of duty.
15. Aerys grew frightened of ordinary weapons even while his own throne repeatedly injured him. Jaime remembers the king's physical neglect and deterioration.
16. Jaime privately warns Cersei against reliance on the Kettleblacks, Qyburn and Taena Merryweather. Cersei concedes Taena may pass messages to Margaery but claims she controls what is disclosed and receives intelligence in return.
17. Cersei argues Taena's ambitions for her son ensure loyalty. She repeats a story from Taena about Olenna paying merchants with underweight pre-Conquest Highgarden coins. Treat the coin practice as reported rather than corroborated.
18. Jaime tries to advocate for Kevan. Cersei dismisses him and expects Roose Bolton, his bastard, and two thousand Freys under Hosteen and Aenys to resolve threats in the north. Jaime notes Moat Cailin still obstructs Bolton.
19. Cersei mentions Orton Merryweather and pyromancer Hallyne as possible Hands. Jaime remembers Owen Merryweather's ineffective tenure and the last pyromancer Hand killed under Aerys.
20. Cersei considers Aurane Waters for master of ships, citing his age and maritime experience. Jaime recommends Paxter Redwyne because of his much larger demonstrated command; Cersei rejects a Tyrell-aligned councillor.
21. Jaime notes Qyburn previously served Vargo Hoat and was expelled from the Citadel. Cersei privileges compliance and usefulness over professional reputation.
22. Jaime judges Cersei intelligent but impatient and short on judgment. This is his personal evaluation, informed by close acquaintance and increasingly adversarial feelings.
23. Cersei reproaches Jaime for refusing the Handship despite previously breaking his oath to kill Aerys. Jaime refuses to subordinate his Kingsguard post to her and she ejects him angrily.
24. In White Sword Tower, Jaime studies the White Book and compliments Loras's riding. He challenges the younger knight's focus on famous exemplars and sensational scandals.
25. Jaime names overlooked long-serving or capable Kingsguard, including Long Tom Costayne, Donnel of Duskendale, Addison Hill, Michael Mertyns, Jeffory Norcross and Red Robert Flowers. Some were bastards who rose to command.
26. Rolland Darklyn was younger than Jaime when admitted, received the white cloak in battle and fell soon after while protecting his king. Loras's dismissal reveals a narrow interpretation of ability.
27. Jaime points to Criston Cole's White Book entry to close the chapter. Arianne invoked Cole in AFFC 13 for a different persuasive purpose, making the shared Kingmaker example a deliberate cross-POV test.

## Research boundaries
- Kevan's incest knowledge: strong direct-dialogue implication plus Jaime's conclusion, not a Kevan confession.
- Hound at Saltpans: secondhand attribution; helmet and identity must be separated.
- Bronn naming the child: letter reported by Pycelle; Cersei's retaliation remains unspecified.
- Taena's ultimate loyalties and Olenna's old coins: Cersei's accounts, not independently established.
- Cersei's proposed Hands and naval master: suggestions and intentions, not appointments.
- Aerys's conduct: Jaime's direct experience of the queen's distress; subsequent reports of injuries are secondhand.
- Cersei versus Aerys: a parallel perceived by Jaime, not literal identity or guaranteed future catastrophe.
- Criston Cole: history used differently by Arianne and Jaime; analogy does not predict a repeated Dance.

## Synthesis
Jaime recognizes Cersei's tactical gift but fears the destructive impulses he associates with Aerys; she prizes allegiance over qualification and treats family critics as enemies. Their breach deepens as western forces disperse and the capital faces multiple campaigns. Jaime's White Book conversation with Loras offers a competing vision of Kingsguard honor built on historical complexity, service, and judgment rather than only splendid reputations.
