## Chronological and causal sequence

1. **The chapter opens with a violent dream, not an actual prophecy enactment.** Cersei imagines herself on the Iron Throne above supplicant courtiers; Tyrion appears, mocks her as **she realizes she is naked**, and the throne's metal edges progressively tear into her. Her humiliation and bodily terror are **dream content**, not a documented waking assault or independently authenticated future event.
2. **A group of armed men wakes Cersei in her bedchamber.** Her maid **Senelle**, **Jocelyn Swyft**, **Osmund Kettleblack**, **Boros Blount** and Lannister guards are present. Cersei initially mistakes Osmund for Jaime and imagines she may still be dreaming, demonstrating shock before full knowledge of the discovery.
3. **The first report concerns Tywin on the privy with a crossbow wound.** Jaime sent the Kingsguard to fetch Cersei; **Osmund** reports the guards were at their posts and a **secret door behind the Hand's hearth** had been found. Jaime himself has gone down the passage to investigate. This connects directly to Tyrion's climb in ASOS 77.
4. **Cersei immediately prioritizes Tommen's physical protection.** Osmund says **Ser Loras Tyrell** is guarding the sleeping boy; Jaime has also sent **twelve men** to check him. Cersei dislikes Loras's duty because she regards his Tyrell loyalty as dangerous, **not because the chapter proves he is harming Tommen**.
5. **She dispatches Boros to inspect Tyrion's black cell.** She simultaneously entertains Stannis and Tyrell conspiracy hypotheses despite no scene evidence either caused Tywin's death. The reader knows **Tyrion killed Tywin**; Cersei's leap among rival suspects is evidence about her decision process.
6. **Tywin's fall instantly changes her self-image.** Crossing Maegor's Holdfast bridge, Cersei imagines controlling **Casterly Rock** and being the only suitable Lannister successor. She sees Tywin's death as catastrophic to royal security and potentially liberating her from his marriage commands. These are **her aspirations**, not a formal regency/ownership adjudication.
7. **Tywin was found by Lum.** Osmund says the guardsman discovered him while seeking the privy. **Lum and Lester** were earlier heard conversing by Tyrion through a hidden passage. This cross-POV overlap adds consistency to the murder aftermath.
8. **Cersei reaches the Tower of the Hand and issues restricted-access orders.** She bars further entrance without permission. Guards including **Red Lester, Puckens, Shortear and Hoke the Horseleg** are named in the ensuing room sequence.
9. **Kevan is present mourning beside Tywin's body.** Cersei sees him attempting prayer and losing composure. She is angry no one first called the queen. Her observations expose both family grief and sensitivity to court rank.
10. **The hidden door behind the hearth is directly visible.** The aperture is **small enough that an ordinary-sized man would have to crawl**; the tunnel below has iron rungs and barred lateral openings. Its architecture is confirmed by two independent POVs (Tyrion's successful entry and Cersei's discovery).
11. **Tywin's body unmistakably confirms the patricide aftermath.** A crossbow quarrel entered low in his abdomen, near the groin, lodging to the fletching; blood stains the body and bedding. Servants moved him from the privy to the bed. The death is **verified**, not just Cersei's fear or a false report.
12. **The corpse smells and contradicts Cersei's idealized patriarch.** Cersei commands an appropriate noble funeral, mourning bells, washing and clothing in gold and crimson; she compares her conduct with what she imagines Tywin would have wanted. Her refusal to examine disgrace closely is a **character response**, not evidence the murder was less sordid than shown.
13. **Cersei recalls Tywin's father Tytos dying at his mistress's quarters**, and imagines Tywin never wept. This is **family story and Cersei's conjecture**, not a witnessed episode of Tywin's internal life.
14. **Grand Maester Pycelle leaves to summon the silent sisters.** Cersei condemns his absence and orders other medical assistance.
15. **Qyburn, formerly stripped of his Citadel chain, offers his services.** He reminds Cersei that he treated **Jaime's stump and saved his arm**. She orders him to remove the crossbow quarrel and prepare Tywin for burial, threatening punishment if he fails. **His actual long-term appointment or powers are not yet formalized**.
16. **Shae's corpse is discovered in the same bedchamber.** The woman is naked, with darkened strangulation face and a **chain of linked golden hands embedded tightly around her throat**. Cersei initially does not recognize her. The reader saw Tyrion deliberately strangle Shae in the preceding novel.
17. **Cersei refuses the evidence of Tywin's sexual hypocrisy.** She insists Tywin never took lovers after Joanna, recounts how he humiliated his father's mistress, and accepts **Qyburn's convenient suggestion** that Tywin was merely questioning Shae about missing Sansa. **That account is offered to preserve Cersei's belief**, not independently supported as the reason Shae lay nude in Tywin's bed.
18. **She orders a deliberate cover-up of Shae's presence.** **Osney, Osfryd and Osmund Kettleblack** are told no one may speak of her; they bundle Shae in Tywin's bloodied blankets. Cersei threatens to cut out any witness's tongue and orders the corpse taken through the **secret shaft**, not carried openly through the yard.
19. **The golden chain itself is to be recovered intact.** Cersei explicitly directs her Kettleblack agents not to scratch the gold. This is important material custody evidence, connecting Tywin's political badge to Tyrion's improvised weapon and the earlier ominous song.
20. **Cersei recalls bargaining with Shae before the trial by combat.** Shae demanded **jewels, a house in the city and a knight to marry**, which Cersei had apparently suggested. Cersei withheld rewards unless Shae identified Sansa's location; Shae insisted she had no knowledge and left crying. This directly adds a **financial/coercive incentive** for Shae's prosecution testimony, without proving every claim Shae made about her threats.
21. **Jaime returns from exploring the lower tunnels.** He reports an intersection of **about half a dozen passages blocked by iron gates** and lacks the needed keys. **He does not disclose his role in Tyrion's release to Cersei**, and Cersei does not know his actual complicity.
22. **Cersei asks Jaime to become Hand.** She believes twins should replace Tywin together; Jaime refuses, mocks the physical pun of a Hand without a hand, and says he pities **Tommen and the kingdoms** if she rules. **His refusal is real**, not a concession to her proposal.
23. **Cersei slaps Jaime.** She is offended by his public refusal and loss of her imagined authority over him. He responds by asking for decency beside their dead father. Their estrangement is an active political risk.
24. **Cersei privately settles on Kevan as preferred Hand.** She values him as tireless, prudent and—critically—**obedient**, expecting he will serve as an instrument of her decisions. **No appointment occurs in this chapter**.
25. **She strongly distrusts the Tyrell bloc.** Cersei fears **Mace, Redwyne, Rowan and Loras**, and resents the planned Tommen–Margaery marriage. She suspects the Tyrells might benefit from Tywin's death, but gives no witness or plot instrument linking them to the murder.
26. **Cersei recalls Jon Arryn's inquiry into her and Jaime and Ned's continuation.** She thinks the Hand's office itself brought repeated threats and asserts Robert's death came sooner than she originally intended because Ned investigated. This is an **important self-revelation**, but it is not a full confession of each instrument used.
27. **Varys's absence becomes conspicuous.** Cersei notes that he normally appears immediately at every Red Keep crisis, but is not present now; she orders **Meryn Trant** to bring him in. **He assisted Tyrion's escape**, though Cersei imagines a separate alliance with Stannis.
28. **Boros confirms Tyrion has vanished from the black cells.** His cell stands empty, a turnkey called **Rugen** is missing, and other guards were found sleeping. **The coincidence may implicate a disguised or false-name agent**, but the chapter does not explicitly reveal Rugen's identity or actual whereabouts.
29. **Cersei orders the sleeping guards killed.** When Boros asks how long they should remain asleep, she says they must **sleep permanently**. This is an actual order for extrajudicial killing; **whether and how Boros executes it is not shown**.
30. **Her inner thoughts invoke the 'valonqar' prophecy.** Cersei fears a younger-brother figure will choke her, and overlays a remembered prophecy onto visions of Tyrion's fingers at her throat. **This establishes Cersei's belief and remembered wording fragment, not a verified referent or actual future murder.** The exact previous encounter with the prophetic woman is not fully narrated here.
31. **End state:** Tywin and Shae are dead, their shared room is discovered, the secret way is known and Tyrion is missing. Cersei is acting as queen regent and imagining command over the Rock, but increasingly trusts only her own interpretations. **Kevan is not yet Hand; Varys is missing; the guard executions remain an order rather than verified completed deaths.**

## Theory tests

- Separate **actual murder** from **Cersei's chain of hypotheses**. Cersei's political overreaction can shape events even when she guesses the original plot incorrectly.
- Investigate **Rugen's identity** from later direct records rather than treating the missing gaoler alone as proof.
- Test claims about Tywin's celibacy against his actual bedchamber, not Cersei's desire to preserve reputation.
- The **golden hands chain**, once public symbol of Handship, becomes Shae's murder weapon; the symbolism does not require literal supernatural prediction.
- Keep the **valonqar referent undecided**. This chapter establishes Cersei's fear of Tyrion and a prophetic word, not proof that he fulfills it.
