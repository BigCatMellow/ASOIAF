## Chronological observation and consequential choices

1. **Aeron is performing actual near-death drowning rites on Great Wyk** when riders bring Balon's death news. The fourth initiate, **Emmond**, resists violently; Aeron's four drowned men hold him under until he ceases movement and bubbling. Norjen and Rus perform manual revival efforts before Aeron repeatedly gives mouth-to-mouth breaths; **Emmond coughs out water and recovers**. This establishes an observed resuscitation ritual with a physical mechanism and risk, not proof every drowned priest is literally resurrected by a god.
2. **Aeron rejects mere infant immersion.** The Sparr and his son **Steffarion** consider their infant/childhood rites sufficient, but Aeron insists a meaningful death and recovery occurred. His near-lethal requirements create internal tensions within the Drowned God's worship.
3. **Drowned followers receive symbolic equipment and obligation.** Emmond is given a **sea-colored roughspun robe and driftwood cudgel** after the ritual, then punched and kissed by the other drowned men. Aeron's own sealskin clout, uncut black hair and seaweed-beard mark distinctive religious practice.
4. **News arrives through named local messengers.** **The Sparr, Steffarion Sparr and Gormond Goodbrother**, wearing the Goodbrother black-and-gold warhorn badge, have received a raven from Pyke to Lord Gorold; Aeron initially confuses Gormond with brothers **Gran and Greydon**.
5. **Balon's death is reported, not witnessed by Aeron.** According to the news, **Balon fell from a bridge linking Pyke's towers during a storm**. Aeron instantly attributes the death to **the Storm God**. The storm and fall are reports; *divine attack, accidental fall or murder* are not proven by this chapter.
6. **Balon was recently alive, aged and apparently not acutely ill.** Aeron had seen him less than a moon earlier after he returned from **Stony Shore raiding**. Balon's hair had whitened and back stooped, but Aeron saw nothing he recognized as immediate fatal sickness.
7. **Balon's death psychologically knocks out one of Aeron's 'two pillars'.** The remaining pillar is his faith. His fear and grief are concrete, but his feeling of divine certainty is internal rather than verification of prophecies.
8. **Aeron commandeers Gormond's stronger horse to reach Hammerhorn.** He orders his followers to assemble at **Pebbleton under Lord Merlyn's tower** and travels inland over the **Hardstone Hills**, noting Great Wyk's mines and isolated people who may never see the sea. This explains the local split between coastal piety and inland practice.
9. **Quellon Greyjoy's nine sons are specifically named.** First wife **Stonetree** bore **Harlon, Quenton, Donel**; second wife **Sunderly** bore **Balon, Euron, Victarion, Urrigon (Urri), Aeron**; third wife, **a Piper of Pinkmaiden**, bore **Robin**. Several died young, leaving four adult surviving brothers. **This family tree matters directly to kingship claims and rumor reliability.**
10. **Harlon died of progressive greyscale**, recalled as an isolated child with stone-like lips and tongue. Aeron remembers infant brothers little. These are **his childhood recollections**, not current disease reports.
11. **Balon's youth included daring and early reaving.** Aeron remembers Balon scaling Flint Cliffs aged ten, sailing with **Dagmer Cleftjaw** at fifteen and captaining a ship at seventeen; he praises Balon despite admitting his contempt for young Aeron. This is hero-worship and prior history, not proof Balon was a good king.
12. **Gorold Goodbrother's hall contains Maester Murenmure**, whom Aeron orders out; Lord Goodbrother refuses. The maester explicitly relays the news of **Euron Crow's Eye's return and self-installation on the Seastone Chair**.
13. **Euron arrived the day after Balon died.** **Gorold** says Euron sailed into **Lordsport** immediately after Balon's fall and now sends ravens to captains and kings to demand homage. The close timing warrants an **assassination hypothesis**, but there is no witness to Euron at the bridge or direct proof he caused Balon's fall.
14. **Euron had been exiled by Balon two years before and threatened with death if he returned.** Aeron's surprise suggests he truly thought Euron half a world away; how he navigated back and learned of Balon's death is **unexplained**.
15. **The proper successor is contested by separate rules.** Maester Murenmure says **Theon** should inherit, or **Asha** if Theon is dead; Aeron rejects mainland inheritance conventions and female succession, denounces Euron as godless, and favors **Victarion** for religious and military reasons. This distinguishes **legal arguments, religious claims, and control of force**.
16. **Balon reportedly favored Asha.** Aeron remembers Balon privately discussing Theon's weakness and saying he hoped Theon was dead so he would not obstruct Asha. That is **Aeron's recollection of a private conversation**, not a signed succession document.
17. **Theon is still not known alive or dead on page.** Aeron calls him a weak captive who disgraced himself at Winterfell, but an empty claim that he is dead should never be treated as verified.
18. **The Iron Fleet is associated with Victarion**, explicitly distinguished from Euron's immediate control of Pyke. Aeron judges Victarion duty-bound to his older brother despite mutual hostility; this creates a practical difficulty in urging his candidacy.
19. **Euron has Sawane Botley drowned for arguing Theon's claim.** Lord Goodbrother relays the execution; Aeron replies that drowning avoids spilling ironborn blood. This exposes a ritualistic evasion of the anti-kin-blood norm rather than a genuine avoidance of political violence.
20. **Aeron advises silence rather than homage to Euron.** The choice delays commitment and creates time to mobilize religious authority. Neither direct rebellion nor acceptance has yet occurred at Hammerhorn.
21. **Aeron's trauma around Urri is precise and self-incriminating.** Playing the **finger dance** aged about fourteen, **Aeron's thrown axe cut off half of Urrigon's hand**. Their father's Piper wife entrusted Urri to a maester who tried to sew fingers back; his hand mortified and an attempted amputation came too late. Aeron blames maesters and resents the widow, but acknowledges his **own accident initiated the injuries**.
22. **Balon retaliated brutally for Urri's death.** Balon cut three of the maester's fingers off and ordered the Piper wife to reattach them; the maester died after treatments. Quellon's third wife died in childbirth. Aeron admits being glad, showing profound guilt and displacement.
23. **Aeron recalls a former reckless drinking identity.** He once sang, joked, juggled, bet on urination feats and named his longship **Golden Storm** after a dare. This establishes that his current austerity is **a later transformation**, not a lifelong personality.
24. **His ship was sunk in Balon's first rebellion by Stannis's war galley *Fury* off Fair Isle**; he survived, was captured by fishermen and imprisoned at **Casterly Rock** through the rebellion. He attributes survival and later religious rebirth to the Drowned God, but the battle and captivity are historical events independent of that explanation.
25. **Memories of Euron trigger involuntary fear.** A sound like **a squealing rusty hinge and a door opening** repeatedly unsettles Aeron even as he insists no mortal can frighten him. This is a striking psychological signal of **unrevealed past experience with Euron**, not on-page confirmation of exactly what happened behind the door.
26. **Aeron believes Victarion's estrangement from Euron concerns 'the woman' who died.** The dead woman's identity, whether Euron harmed her and the event's full chronology remain **UNKNOWN within AFFC 1**.
27. **He preaches Balon's death as Storm God's violence across Great Wyk.** Villagers follow, and he performs more drownings; by Pebbleton about **a dozen new followers** and roughly **forty drowned men** are assembled. This is a **real organizational mobilization** using theological language.
28. **Aeron's night swim yields a religious decision.** He thinks of **Nagga's hill on Old Wyk**, the **Grey King's Hall's bones**, and the ancient right of captains to select a king. He experiences certainty but **no external divine voice is heard by an independent witness**.
29. **Lord Meldred Merlyn confirms an Asha counter-summons.** A raven directs captains to **Ten Towers, Harlaw**, at the request of **the Reader, Rodrik Harlaw**, who claims Balon intended Asha's succession. Euron directs them to Pyke. **Two competing summonses precede Aeron's third**.
30. **Aeron calls a kingsmoot on Nagga's hill.** He cites legendary elections of **Urras Ironfoot, Sylas Flatnose, Harrag Hoare and the Old Kraken** and calls captains and lords to **Old Wyk** when the moon has waned and returned. The public call is **actually made and enthusiastically endorsed**, but **no kingsmoot has yet met or selected a king**.
31. **Aeron does not seek kingship himself.** The crowd calls for Damphair to reign; he explains that the god gave him a **prophet's voice**, not a warrior's axe. His practical objective is preventing Euron's unchallenged accession, with Victarion his preferred alternative.
32. **End state:** Balon is dead **by report**, Euron occupies the Seastone Chair **by report**, Asha is moving via Harlaw, Victarion controls the fleet and Aeron has independently summoned the islands to an ancient **elective kingsmoot**. Ironborn civil conflict is threatened but no succession vote is yet held.

## Prediction tests

- Verify Balon's bridge fall from non-Aeron POVs before claiming assassination, divine murder or a magical curse.
- Compare actual **captain support, fleet command and hereditary legitimacy** rather than presuming Aeron's religious authority wins a ballot.
- Follow Euron's unexplained return, his treatment of Botley, and Aeron's response to trauma as separate signals.
- Ask whether the revived kingsmoot can enforce its result or will simply give a contested claimant legitimacy.
