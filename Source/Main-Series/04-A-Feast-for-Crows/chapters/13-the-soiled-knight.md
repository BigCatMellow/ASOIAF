## Plot, sequence and material details

1. **Arys Oakheart travels disguised through Sunspear's shadow city to visit Arianne Martell.** He conceals his Kingsguard white cloak beneath layered Dornish robes, including turquoise stripes and golden suns, and carries a sword. This is a knowingly secret meeting, not an official audience.
2. **Violence against people from King's Landing is already happening.** Arys remembers a fruit trader from the capital murdered in the shadow city about a fortnight earlier. Dornish anger after Oberyn's death is therefore more than rhetoric.
3. **Arys carries inherited anti-Dornish memories from Old Oak.** Tapestries celebrate Oakheart victories and depict his relatives slain by Dornish opponents. His actual service now requires protecting a Lannister princess in a region his family historically regarded as hostile.
4. **Arys experiences ordinary public hostility as a security threat.** Shopkeepers, taverners and children have mistreated or harassed him; he previously drew his sword to disperse boys throwing stones. His suspicions that vendors cheat or spit in drinks are not independently verified.
5. **Doran's detention of the Sand Snakes has somewhat quieted the streets.** This is evidence that the arrest order in AFFC 2 was carried out in at least relevant part; it has not eliminated broader resentment.
6. **Myrcella and Trystane play cyvasse in the palace.** Arys notes the pieces have varied capabilities, boards are configured afresh and Myrcella has begun winning more often. The game came to Planky Town by a Volantene ship and spread through Greenblood communities. The children are both adept and apparently fond of one another.
7. **Myrcella is not yet eleven and Trystane is thirteen.** Their affection is reported/observed through Arys's perspective; the political marriage remains prospective.
8. **Myrcella has four Lannister household guards posted at two doors, plus maids, Septa Eglantine and Trystane's protector Ser Gascoyne.** Arys judges her secure but is the individual officer leaving his station for a secret meeting.
9. **Doran has told Arys he plans to move Myrcella and her party to the Water Gardens.** His stated reason is safety from assassins and agitators at the more permeable Sunspear; he also asks Arys not to inform King's Landing of the destination.
10. **Doran invokes the murder of Elia's daughter Rhaenys as precedent for politically instrumental violence against a child.** He explains that people may kill Myrcella not out of hatred for her but to force his hand or make him seem complicit.
11. **Arys interprets Doran's trembling hand as fear.** Gout and weakness are already established by AFFC 2, so the physiological observation alone does not prove that Arys correctly diagnosed Doran's psychological state.
12. **Doran says the Sand Snake arrests displaced rather than removed the threat.** He fears would-be conspirators may now operate secretly; his assertion is a ruler's threat assessment, not a demonstrated organization in this scene.
13. **The Water Gardens plan is ambiguous to Arys.** He accepts Doran's protective explanation while recognizing that restricting communication with King's Landing could expose his royal charge to decisions made without the queen's knowledge.
14. **Arianne's clandestine room contains a Nymeria-and-ships tapestry and a snake-shaped ornament.** These are visible Rhoynish/Martell identity markers, not evidence of a specific prophecy.
15. **Arys and Arianne are already lovers; this meeting includes another sexual encounter.** His internal struggle is immediate: he sees his desire as a violation of the Kingsguard vows he has tried to embody.
16. **Arianne says she has taken moon tea.** It is her reported birth-control measure, not independently witnessed ingestion or evidence of pregnancy.
17. **Arys has been celibate since taking the white until Arianne.** He has repeatedly resolved to end the relationship but failed. His guilt is sincere even when he fails to act on it.
18. **Arianne disputes the equation of sexual conduct with honor.** She argues that a white cloak does not define the whole person and that defending cruel kings may dishonor a knight more than having a lover.
19. **Kingsguard precedents are contested rather than uniform.** Arys cites Terrence Toyne and Lucamore Strong as examples of destructive oathbreaking, while Arianne cites Prince Lewyn Martell's alleged paramour and challenges the legend of Aemon the Dragonknight.
20. **Arys rejects the allegation that Aemon the Dragonknight fathered Queen Naerys's child.** He attributes it to Aegon the Unworthy's political lies; that is his historical judgment, not a new witness account.
21. **Arianne claims her great-uncle Lewyn had a long-standing paramour despite an unblemished White Book entry.** Her family testimony underscores how official Kingsguard records can omit private lives; it does not establish that all such rumors are true.
22. **Arys remembers obeying Joffrey's orders to strike Sansa Stark.** He is ashamed, yet still assumes Tyrion poisoned Joffrey. His moral awakening coexists with an incorrect understanding of that death.
23. **Arys recognizes Myrcella as braver, brighter and more politically composed than Tommen.** This is firsthand comparative judgment from their former guard; comparative suitability does not settle the legal succession.
24. **Arianne makes that comparison the pivot from intimacy to politics.** She asserts Myrcella's seniority should place her ahead of her younger brother, invoking Dornish inheritance custom against the crown's prevailing male preference.
25. **Arys replies that Dornish custom does not govern the Iron Throne.** This mirrors Doran's distinction in AFFC 2 and shows the legal objection is known to both sides.
26. **Arianne invokes the Dance of the Dragons and Ser Criston Cole.** She says a Kingsguard knight once helped disinherit a designated princess and asks Arys to reverse that historical wrong by championing Myrcella.
27. **Arys knows competing interpretations of Criston's motives.** Some call him ambitious, others a defender of Andal custom, others a spurned lover. The chapter expressly does not validate any single romantic or ideological explanation.
28. **Arianne recasts Doran's Water Gardens plan as secret imprisonment.** She predicts Hotah will prevent Myrcella leaving, whereas Doran told Arys it was protection. Neither future restriction nor unrestricted freedom has been observed yet.
29. **Arianne raises the arrests of the younger Sand Snakes Loreza and Dorea.** She says the children are six and eight and cannot meaningfully have fomented war. The father's generalized detention order now has identifiable human consequences.
30. **Arianne fears she may herself be imprisoned.** She presents Tyene's captivity and her own belief that Doran prefers Quentyn as signs that his command apparatus could be turned on her.
31. **Arianne describes her exclusion from actual government.** While publicly left at Sunspear, she says Manfrey, Ricasso, tax officials, magistrates and Maester Myles handled administration and Oberyn exercised overriding authority; she was assigned court entertainment.
32. **She reports Doran used to summon Oberyn far more frequently than her.** This supports the experience of marginalization without alone proving an intention to remove her legal inheritance.
33. **The strongest evidence for Arianne's fear is a letter she says she personally saw at fourteen.** Doran reportedly wrote Quentyn that he would someday sit in Doran's place and rule Dorne. That remembered text is consequential character testimony; this chapter does not show the actual letter.
34. **Arianne says Quentyn secretly crossed the Narrow Sea disguised as a merchant.** She attributes this to unnamed friends, contradicting the public assertion he is with Yronwood's host in the Boneway. At this stage the location is her intelligence report, not a verified departure in Arys's POV.
35. **She connects Quentyn's alleged mission to the Golden Company's broken Myr contract.** Her argument rests on the company's reputation for keeping contracts and its Blackfyre exile tradition. The temporal and motivational connection to Quentyn is her inference.
36. **Arianne specifically accuses Anders Yronwood of aspiring to make Quentyn Dorne's ruler.** Yronwood ancestors' Blackfyre loyalties are offered as historical context, not proof of Anders's present conspiracy.
37. **Arianne invokes House Toland's ouroboros-like dragon device to argue history repeats.** The dragon eating its tail is an observed/reported heraldic symbol used rhetorically, not evidence of fate.
38. **Arys supplies a serious ordinary alternative.** Doran's letter might have been encouragement to a young son rather than an operative legal succession order, and mercenary contracts can end for reasons unrelated to Dornish princes.
39. **Arianne's account mixes real injury with unproved explanatory leaps.** Her exclusion from administration and reported letter deserve investigation even if her Golden Company/Yronwood theory fails.
40. **Arianne offers Arys a legal/emotional escape hatch.** She suggests Myrcella as queen could release him from Kingsguard constraints and allow them to marry, citing Joffrey's dismissal of Barristan as precedent. Whether Myrcella would choose this is not known.
41. **Her appeals combine Myrcella's welfare, women's inheritance, Arianne's safety and the promise of a shared future.** These motives may all matter, but their combination is also a deliberate persuasion strategy tailored to Arys's vulnerabilities.
42. **Arys yields and vows his sword, life and honor to Myrcella's claim and to Arianne.** This is a completed private commitment to intervene in succession, not yet a completed crown, flight or public rebellion.
43. **Arianne's final request is specifically for Myrcella.** The promised political undertaking ends the chapter with a new obligation whose detailed execution is still to come.
44. **End state:** Myrcella remains at Sunspear, Doran intends a transfer to the Water Gardens, Arianne is organizing an alternative succession claim, and Arys has privately agreed to help her despite his Kingsguard oath and doubts about the law.

## Cross-POV implications and falsification tests

- **Doran versus Arianne:** Compare Arianne's remembered letter and loss of governing responsibilities with Doran's later explanation. Do not dismiss her fear as entirely imaginary, or adopt her Golden Company theory on the strength of family hurt.
- **Myrcella's agency:** Her intelligence and fondness for Trystane are witnessed; this chapter contains no independent statement that she wants to claim Tommen's crown or release Arys from his vows.
- **Arys's reliability:** He directly witnesses his own repeated betrayal of celibacy, admits complicity in harming Sansa, and wants the legal objections resolved. He is not a detached judge of either Doran's safety plan or Arianne's intentions.
- **The royal child's security:** Four household guards and a sworn Kingsguard are physically real safeguards, but Arys's affair creates a direct route through the security perimeter.
- **Golden Company theory test:** Seek a verified Quentyn itinerary, an actual company contract decision and message/meeting evidence before connecting them.
- **Foreshadowing boundary:** Criston Cole's analogy provides a frame for Arys's decision, not proof he will recreate the Dance's exact sequence.
