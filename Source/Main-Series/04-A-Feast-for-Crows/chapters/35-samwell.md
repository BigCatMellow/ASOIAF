## Five major causal and theoretical knots

### Aemon's death ends a real chain of unique firsthand institutional knowledge

Aemon dies at **102**, before reaching Oldtown, despite the determination the dragon reports briefly rekindled. His funeral occurs at sea with **Sam, Gilly, Kojja Mo and Xhondo** present; the Summer Islanders translate the Watch words and share rum. The captain will not permit a pyre aboard, so Aemon's remains stay inside a **blackbelly-rum cask** pending arrival. This is an on-page death and preserved body, **not** a resurrection or cremation.

Aemon's earlier position as maester, Night's Watch counselor, brother of Aegon V ('Egg') and once-possible king made him an irreplaceable bridge between royal history, prophecies and anti-Other evidence. Sam blames Jon for the journey, but **Gilly correctly notes the competing threat**: remaining at Castle Black might have exposed Aemon's Targaryen blood to Melisandre's sacrificial interests. This is an argument about risk, not evidence Melisandre had actually ordered Aemon burned.

### Promised prince: translation, competing candidates, and false light

Aemon's sequence of interpretation is explicit: first **Rhaegar**, with Rhaegar's Summerhall birth associated with smoke and tears; later **Rhaegar's son Aegon**, following a claimed comet during conception; finally **Daenerys**, because the language's gender assumptions may be wrong and living dragons have returned. He cites **Septon Barth** on dragons changing sex and uses that analogy to challenge a thousand years of prophecy readers. No independent Valyrian manuscript, dragon-sex observation or divine designation appears in this chapter; **Daenerys is Aemon's newly strengthened hypothesis**.

Aemon traces **Stannis Baratheon's Targaryen descent via Rhaelle, Aegon V's daughter and paternal grandmother of Steffon Baratheon's sons**, but rejects Melisandre's Stannis reading. He says the apparent sword is **light without heat**, a glamor rather than the expected true weapon. This is a direct and specific character critique, not yet a fully tested magical account. His remaining cryptic statements—**an unlit glass candle, unhatched eggs, the dragon with three heads and 'the sphinx is the riddle'**—are preserved for future Oldtown/Citadel connections without pre-assigning an identity or secret society.

### Knowledge transmission: books sold, a chain saved, one broken horn retained

For passage out of Braavos, Sam surrendered the Watch's **rare volumes that Aemon expected the Citadel to lack**. Quhuru Mo knows the maesters may pay well for them and plans to sell them in Oldtown; the books are **not necessarily destroyed or permanently lost**, but their custody changed and access is now commercial. Sam refused to surrender Aemon's maester's chain. He also retains the **old cracked horn found by Jon Snow at the Fist of the First Men**. This is a direct object-custody bridge to northern lore and a worthy **Horn of Winter** hypothesis lead; no horn blast, magical effect, inscription or origin proof occurs here. The captain also requires Sam's **manual labor for four passengers**, revealing why Sam's blisters and fatigue are so severe.

### Baby swap and a genuine wording anomaly in the primary text

Gilly explicitly confirms that **Dalla's son is aboard**, and her own son remained at the Wall in the child's place. Aemon rocked Dalla's child just before dying. Gilly chooses prospective names **Aemon Battleborn** and **Aemon Steelsong**, referencing his birth during the siege of the Wall, but states that the name will wait **until he is two**. Child identity, naming *proposal*, actual baptism and future parentage records should be distinguished.

**Verbatim-level issue to audit without rewriting the source:** Sam thinks of the infant as simultaneously **Mance Rayder's son and Craster's grandson**. This wording appears in both the repository transcription and the uploaded original EPUB (`text/part0302.html`). Yet AFFC 15 identifies the child as **Dalla and Mance's son**, while **Gilly's own son—related to Craster—is at Castle Black**. The text supplies no genealogy linking Dalla or Mance by blood to Craster. Possible explanations include a slips-of-memory/editing error, a **social/adoptive** sense in which Craster's daughter Gilly now cares for the child, or an otherwise unrevealed relation. The adjacent contrast about lacking **Sam's blood** makes a purely adoptive explanation less straightforward. **Do not assert a secret biological relation as canon**; classify this as **UNRESOLVED PRIMARY-SOURCE CONTRADICTION**, with independent birth/parentage verification required.

### Sam, Gilly and a competing religious ethic

Sam and Gilly drink repeatedly after the funeral and have a mutual sexual encounter. Gilly says she is now his wife; Sam is painfully aware he vowed to take no wife and father no child, and his feeling of violation is personal/institutional rather than an order from Jon to separate. In a moment of shame he considers drowning himself but makes **no attempt**; the next day he avoids her until **Kojja Mo** confronts him. Kojja presents love and sexuality as an appropriate way to honor the dead and insists that shame in harmless affection is peculiar to Westerosi norms. She also touches Sam without asking; his retreat and verbal refusal should not be flattened into total acceptance of her sexual ethics.

Their final exchange matters: **Sam does not abandon the Watch**, and Gilly does not deny the oath's existence. She invokes the heart trees where he swore but points out there are none at sea. This could be a rhetorical loophole, an attempt at consolation or a larger theme about gods and geography; **no institutional annulment or marriage ceremony is demonstrated**. Gilly's losses and her specific trust in the capable Summer Islander women explain why she needs companionship without reducing her to Sam's romantic accessory.

## Cross-book source comparisons

- **AFFC 5, 15, 26 Samwell → AFFC 35:** The command to move Aemon and Sam to Oldtown arose from the need for a new maester and to shield royal-blood Aemon from Melisandre. AFFC 15 confirms the infant substitution, and AFFC 26 establishes Aemon's Braavosi decline, his hopes of information about dragons, the failed *Lady Ushanora* passage and Xhondo's rescue of Sam. AFFC 35 completes the Aemon death trajectory, gives the actual book/chain/horn transfer inventory, and turns Sam into the **sole present courier** of Aemon's last arguments.
- **AFFC 34 Cat of the Canals:** Arya killed the deserter Dareon; Sam doesn't mention or learn of that killing in this chapter. Aemon's speculation about meeting the dead Dareon in the afterlife would not show independent knowledge of a homicide. Keep timelines and separate POV access distinct. A Volantene mate's earlier **'Saan' pirate report near Stepstones** should not be merged with the pirates Kojja repelled absent ship identification or corroboration.
- **ACOK Daenerys prophetic signs:** Aemon's focus on salt, smoke, red star, and three-headed dragon can be compared with Dany's own visions, eggs and dreams. These are **candidate associations**, not proof her own myth or Aemon's reading is exhaustively correct.
- **Stannis and Melisandre:** Aemon recognizes Stannis's real Targaryen ancestry and still questions the alleged sword. Compare with the original Chapter of Cressen and Melisandre displays, avoiding the fallacy that royal blood proves savior status.
- **Oldtown/Citadel agenda:** The missing Aemon intermediary, merchant-retained rare books, ambiguous sphinx clue and appeal for a maester to Daenerys converge on Sam's forthcoming arrival. The chapter does not yet show an archmaester agreeing, glass candle burning, or Citadel dragon mission.
- **Horn found at the Fist:** The broken old horn remains *with Sam* after an expensive ship passage even as swords and books are traded. This unusual object continuity may matter to **Horn of Winter** theories. No blow/activation is given, and it must not be equated automatically with any specific wildling horn.
- **Naming and false identities:** The possible future Aemon Steelsong is **Mance's son biologically per earlier testimony**, while Gilly's boy stays at the Wall in his place. The original source's unexplained Craster-grandson phrase is **an audit target**, not a new confirmed dynastic link.
- **Summer Island funerary practice:** The crew's shared rum and intimacy provide an honest competing conception of loyalty, love and honoring death, not a universal cultural endorsement of all intimate contact or an automatic invalidation of Watch vows.
