# AFFC 15 — Samwell: the Blackbird, Bloodraven's arrival, Gilly's grief and the infant exchange

## Plot and decision spine

1. The Watch galley **Blackbird** leaves Eastwatch carrying Sam, Gilly, an infant, Aemon and Dareon. Sam experiences severe seasickness; the apparent escape from the Wall is physically and psychologically difficult.
2. Sam's sea terror is rooted in Randyll Tarly throwing him into Horn Hill's pond to teach swimming. **Hyle Hunt rescued young Sam**, creating a concrete cross-POV link to Brienne's contemporaneous Maidenpool chapters.
3. Sam imagines optimistic separate futures for Gilly at Horn Hill, Aemon at the Citadel and Dareon recruiting. The assumptions are Sam's hopes, not guaranteed outcomes; he expects only his own Citadel role to be miserable.
4. **Dareon says his conviction for rape was unjust**, a claim Sam reports but cannot verify. Jon designated him to replace Yoren as recruiter, singing for new Watch men in southern courts.
5. Gilly responds to Sam's imagined pleasures of Braavos with exhaustion and withdrawal. Sam initially misidentifies **grief** as fear of the sea, ignorance or discomfort.
6. Blackbird is the Watch's largest galley; Cotter Pyke chose her for rough seas rather than the faster fighting galleys **Storm Crow** and **Talon**. The initial ten days coast through the Bay of Seals in sight of land.
7. Gilly stays in the dark cabin with the crying, vomiting, frequently soiling infant, whose discomfort is directly visible and adds to crew hostility.
8. Dareon entertains the crew with ballads and bawdy songs; the oarsmen recognize him. His genuine musical ability contrasts with his harsh behavior toward Sam and Gilly.
9. **Aemon remembers his journey to the Wall aboard the Golden Dragon**, aged thirty-five, sent by his brother **Aegon V ('Egg')**, with **Ser Duncan** as escort. The party included prisoners Egg released from dungeons to accompany Aemon.
10. **Brynden Rivers (Bloodraven)** was among those exiles and later became Lord Commander of the Night's Watch. This is unusually specific, high-value Aemon testimony linking royal succession, Watch command and the figure involved in Bran's far-northern story.
11. Aemon recalls having declined an opportunity to help Egg govern; his account emphasizes service over royal involvement rather than romanticizing the Wall as exile alone.
12. Sam remembers an earlier sea trip to the Arbor aboard **Paxter Redwyne's Arbor Queen**. Horas and Hobber Redwyne humiliated him; one had a kitchen girl in armor defeat him before onlookers.
13. Sam later learned Randyll intended to leave him on the Arbor as a page/cupbearer, potentially marrying him to Redwyne's daughter if he impressed the family. This was another effort to change or displace the heir Randyll rejected.
14. Sam longs to see his mother and siblings again but still fears any chance of encountering Randyll. These opposing desires explain why taking Gilly to Horn Hill feels safer in his imagination than visiting himself.
15. Blackbird sails east toward **Skagos**, described in Sam's books and songs as mountainous, dangerous to shipping and populated by 'stoneborn' who ride shaggy unicorns. Stories of cannibalism and the destruction of Skane are **reported lore**, not firsthand verification.
16. The island reportedly rebelled a century earlier and cost the Lord of Winterfell his life. The exact old chronology and accounts should be checked independently before treating folklore as conclusive.
17. Dangerous currents, reefs and storm conditions around Skagos are directly supported by an observed galley wreck and dead crew on the shore. The captain deliberately steers clear.
18. Aemon remains exposed to cold rain on deck, saying it feels like tears. Sam later finds him asleep, disoriented and addressing him as **Egg**; the maester is startlingly light when Sam carries him below.
19. Sam and Gilly cooperate in warming Aemon. His age and frailty make the planned passage to Oldtown a genuine survival risk, not just an inconvenient itinerary.
20. Severe storms blur together in Sam's memory. A sail tears loose, passengers cannot eat, the captain pushes his rowers, and a brief eight-day period of calm is followed by worse weather.
21. **Dareon increasingly drinks firewine** and responds to Gilly's distress with contempt and threats. His behavior sharply undermines Sam's initial assumption that Dareon will make an easy transition to respected recruiter.
22. Some rowers explicitly blame Gilly's incestuous origins and her infant for the storms and discuss throwing them overboard. Their superstition turns the only wildling passengers into potential targets.
23. Sam does not confront the physically powerful crew openly but sharpens his knife and accompanies Gilly when she leaves the cabin. This is deliberate protection despite fear, not the absence of courage.
24. Sam finally asks Aemon whether Gilly can be medicated for fear. **Aemon distinguishes grief from fear** and says she is grieving her own infant.
25. Aemon reveals the central withheld fact: **Gilly is nursing Dalla's son, not the child she bore**. Gilly loved and fed both, but left her own baby behind unwillingly.
26. Aemon infers Jon used threats and promises to enforce the exchange. He is certain Gilly did not choose freely; the **exact words of Jon's bargain are not witnessed in this POV**.
27. Sam independently understands the strategic rationale: Jon sent the supposed royal child away from **Melisandre's interest in royal blood**, leaving Gilly's child in the position of apparent 'little prince' at the Wall.
28. The substitution transfers mortal risk to an infant considered socially unimportant. Sam recognizes that no one but Gilly may defend a boy described as Craster's offspring and given no name here.
29. **Aemon differentiates Jon the friend from Lord Snow the commander.** His point is that power imposes choices with no innocent outcome; describing a choice as less grievous does not make the coercion disappear.
30. Sam's previous role helping Jon gain command now implicates him emotionally: his vote-making, Gilly's rescue and service at the Wall have led to a decision that harms her.
31. At the end of a brief calm Sam sees storm clouds and lightning ahead, rejecting Dareon's hope that the worst is over. The final weather is physically real while its moral resonance follows Sam's grief.
32. End state: the vessel has **not yet reached Braavos**; Aemon is frail, Gilly's own child is at the Wall, Dalla's child is aboard, Sam now knows the exchange, Dareon's conduct is deteriorating and the journey remains dangerous.

## Cross-book research significance

- **Infant identity correction:** Reconcile AFFC 5's deliberately incomplete Sam POV with this chapter: Dalla's son travels south, while Gilly's own child remains at Castle Black, vulnerable to being mistaken for Mance's son. Later Jon POV should be used to test the precise coercive method.
- **Bloodraven chronology:** Aemon locates Brynden Rivers in the contingent sent to the Wall with him by Egg. This deserves connection to Bran/Three-Eyed-Crow evidence but does not by itself prove every disputed identification or purpose.
- **Hyle and Randyll:** Hyle rescued child Sam from his father's dangerous swimming 'lesson' while he later participated in Brienne's humiliation. Preserve conflicting behaviors instead of assigning uncomplicated moral labels.
- **The Watch's discrimination and care:** Gilly is endangered by men allegedly defending the realm. Sam's protective acts must be evaluated alongside his fear and his initial inability to perceive her grief.
- **Skagos:** Distinguish geography and seafaring danger from dehumanizing folklore of cannibals, particularly for future Rickon-location research.
- **Dareon:** His beautiful music and attractive public persona are real; they offer no guarantee of empathy or fidelity to Watch vows.
