# AFFC 23 — Alayne: the Lords Declarant and Littlefinger's staged confrontation

- Status: FIRST-PASS COMPLETE; subsequent Vale developments and independent source challenge open.
- Source: full [AFFC 23 Alayne](../text/024-alayne.md), read 2026-10-18.

## Chronological close reading

1. Alayne wakes in the Maiden's Tower, now housed in apartments larger than those given her under Lysa. She sees the Eyrie's height, the iced Alyssa's Tears and the enemy camps below; winter and the high castle's isolation frame the siege.
2. Young Lord Gilwood Hunter joined the six Lords Declarant two days earlier. Nestor Royce holds the Gates of the Moon with fewer than three hundred men, whereas each of the six opposing lords reportedly brought about a thousand.
3. The six are Benedar Belmore, Symond Templeton, Horton Redfort, Anya Waynwood, Gilwood Hunter and Bronze Yohn Royce. They swore at Runestone after Lysa's death to defend Robert Arryn, the Vale and one another; their declaration condemns unnamed 'misrule' and 'evil counselors.'
4. Alayne wears Lysa's borrowed and altered gowns, briefly choosing Tully colors. She repeatedly darkens her naturally auburn hair with a Tyroshi wash; her red roots return and her supply will eventually run out, a practical identity vulnerability.
5. The Eyrie has unusually few resident people, no horses or kennels, no combat yard and a now-frozen waterfall. The quiet and emptiness both preserve and endanger Robert's isolated household.
6. The Eyrie's granaries contain enough oats, corn and barley for approximately a year. Fresh food depends on Mya Stone bringing supplies up from below; Belmore has stopped deliveries until Robert is sent down. This amounts to a selective blockade rather than complete starvation.
7. Robert wants eggs and bacon which the castle lacks and repeatedly refuses porridge. He reports hearing Marillion sing at night even though Petyr has said the singer died by stepping into the sky. Neither Alayne nor Robert has witnessed Marillion's corpse.
8. Robert says Petyr refused his demand to remove Marillion's tongue. Alayne reasons a tongue would be needed for a confession. The singer's purported death and continuing songs are therefore unresolved from her viewpoint rather than proof of supernatural haunting.
9. Petyr says eight men are ascending for negotiations under safe conduct, led by Nestor and accompanied by Lyn Corbray; the actual party will include the six declarants, Nestor and Lyn.
10. Petyr explains Lyn's deadly reputation and feud with his older brother Lyonel. Lyn earned Lady Forlorn after leading a charge at the Trident, killing Lewyn Martell after Lewyn was already badly wounded, while Lyonel inherited the lordship, castle and other wealth.
11. Robert's fit escalates after his refused breakfast; he strikes Colemon with a bowl, overturns food and seizes. Colemon reports increasingly frequent and severe spells since Lysa's death.
12. Colemon says he already employs bleeding, dreamwine and milk of the poppy. Petyr complains Robert sleeps twelve hours daily and proposes a small quantity of sweetsleep before the audience; Colemon cautiously agrees to try only a small amount infrequently. The precise dose and consequence are not shown.
13. Petyr demands a kiss as a purported father. Alayne supplies only a brief cheek kiss and steps away. His dissatisfaction underscores the disparity between the fabricated parent-child role and his actual conduct.
14. Petyr instructs Alayne to provide warming hospitality and rejects a gown in House Tully's colors; he also warns against House Arryn's blue and cream, displaying control over public appearances.
15. Sansa fears Bronze Yohn recognizes her because he visited Winterfell when Waymar traveled north to join the Watch and saw her again at the Hand's tournament. Petyr claims a little girl with auburn hair will not be recognized as a taller brown-haired Alayne because observers see what they expect.
16. Petyr plans to negotiate in his solar, not the High Hall, so the Lords Declarant will not see him occupying Arryn symbols of sovereignty.
17. When Alayne suggests surrendering Robert and withdrawing, Petyr stresses that the lords control much but not all the Vale; he claims Grafton, Lynderly and Lyonel Corbray support him, though they cannot match the six militarily.
18. Petyr explains that he holds Harrenhal by title but not in practical security and calls the castle expensive, defensively difficult and reputedly cursed. His references to past deaths and owners are historical examples, not direct evidence of a supernatural curse.
19. Petyr thanks Cersei for promised tapestries, independently linking to AFFC 17 Cersei's request from Baelish. He says that if Cersei discovers Sansa he might remove Cersei from the political game first, unless Cersei ruins herself; this is his strategic assertion, not a proven capability.
20. Petyr warns that even the humblest participants in politics have wills of their own and may refuse planned moves. This is an explicit statement of his method and an implicit admission that schemes are fallible.
21. Sansa invokes guest right because she prepares bread and cheese and knows the Freys violated hospitality in killing her mother and brother; she nevertheless trusts Bronze Yohn to respect the prohibition.
22. Alayne arranges six seats opposite two, organizes hot wine, bread, white cheese, a fire and beeswax candles, and chooses a modest brown gown rather than her aunt's sumptuous jewels. This demonstrates her growing competence as a political host and public role performance.
23. The final ascent requires visitors to climb a steep chimney or use a swaying supply basket. The older Redfort, Waynwood and Belmore are hauled up, providing a practical demonstration of the Eyrie's defensive advantages and access constraints.
24. Alayne studies heraldry to recognize all the lords. Her account singles out Hunter's age, taste for wine and rumors that he hastened the death of his father Eon; this latter allegation is hearsay at this point.
25. Lyn's three ravens holding red hearts mark his Corbray identity. Alayne watches his dangerous manner and Lady Forlorn, described as dark smoke-grey steel; the chapter does not explicitly classify the sword's metal.
26. Bronze Yohn pauses on seeing Alayne and asks whether he knows her. Nestor immediately explains that she is Petyr's natural daughter. This is a genuine near-recognition risk, though Royce does not identify Sansa.
27. Sansa stumbles over Alayne's age, first beginning to say four before settling on fourteen. Her true age is thirteen in this chapter; maintaining a distinct biography imposes cognitive risk.
28. Hunter and Lyn make inappropriate remarks toward Alayne. Lady Waynwood rebukes them, showing both her decency and Lyn's willingness to intimidate nominal allies.
29. The company passes murder holes and a portcullis into Petyr's solar, where Nestor selects a chair beside Petyr and Lyn stays by the hearth rather than among the six; these spatial choices are visible before the later revelation of clandestine cooperation.
30. Petyr pretends willingness to sign the declaration, forcing the six to clarify that they seek his removal and Robert's transfer rather than merely cleaner government.
31. Royce proposes fostering Robert at Runestone; the other lords defend the plan with specific tutors, companions and the more experienced Maester Helliweg, and say other seats could also host him.
32. Petyr counterproposes sons of Grafton and Lynderly as wards and asks Waynwood to send Harrold Hardyng to serve near Robert. Waynwood calls him a thief; Bronze Yohn claims Harrold should join Robert at Runestone. Harrold's potential dynastic importance is a follow-up hypothesis, not explained outright here.
33. The delegation argues Lysa as Jon Arryn's widow could serve as regent but Petyr has neither Arryn blood nor an independent claim to rule. Petyr cites Lysa's designation of him as Lord Protector and frames the question as the child's custody.
34. Nestor defends Petyr on the ground that the Vale once sought Lysa's marriage and would have accepted another husband as Lord Protector. Bronze Yohn insists Robert belongs to the Vale, making this an internal House Royce split.
35. Templeton cites each lord's thousand men and the option of raising more. Petyr does not match their forces but relies on hostage custody, mountain terrain, legitimacy arguments and the cost of open war.
36. Lyn suddenly draws Lady Forlorn and challenges Petyr. Bronze Yohn and the other Lords Declarant rebuke him for threatening an unarmed host under safe conduct, invoking their status as guests and distancing themselves from Frey-like violations.
37. Petyr uses Lyn's display as evidence that the delegation violated safe conduct. He says he could arrest them but will instead negotiate, converting their moral appeal into a liability and shifting attention from his contested legitimacy to their recklessness.
38. Petyr asks for the blockade lifted and a single year to govern with Nestor's help, promising to step down if he cannot improve conditions; he additionally offers a pardon and no reprisals. The delegation acquiesces rather than initiating battle.
39. Bronze Yohn explicitly remains suspicious and leaves early. Other delegates stay for a makeshift feast at which Robert, now dressed in Arryn colors, is briefly present and socially compliant.
40. Sansa suspects Petyr deliberately staged the outcome and asks what he will accomplish within a year. He predicts the deaths of elderly rivals, further intrigue in House Hunter, bribing Belmore, befriending Templeton and isolating Yohn.
41. Petyr names Harlan Hunter as likely killer of Gilwood and asserts Harlan arranged Eon's death; these are unproved accusations by a character with an interest in factional division.
42. Sansa guesses Lyn is secretly in Petyr's pay. Petyr confirms he will reward Lyn for publicly acting as an unyielding enemy with money, access to boys and promises; the chapter establishes Lyn's staged opposition, not an independent uncorroborated suspicion.
43. End state: Petyr retains the Lord Protectorship for one year, food passage is to resume, Alayne's concealed Stark identity has narrowly survived Yohn's attention, Robert remains dependent on dangerous medical and political caretakers, and Lyn Corbray's apparent antagonism is exposed as paid theater.

## Priority cross-book links and hypotheses

- **AFFC 17 Cersei:** Crown chooses not to intervene in the Vale, and Petyr requests Robert's tapestries; read Crown and Alayne POVs together to establish what each actor thinks is happening.
- **AGOT / Waymar Royce:** Sansa's fear of Yohn's recognition is grounded in Winterfell and King's Landing encounters; her age slip and returning auburn roots are independent failure points.
- **Hostage versus guardianship:** Lords Declarant want Robert educated at Runestone. Petyr insists custody and stepfather status give him authority; Nestor Royce unexpectedly supplies legitimacy.
- **Sweetleep risk:** Petyr's casual insistence contrasts with Colemon's visible concern and Robert's worsening condition. Do not conclude intentional poisoning or medical effects without later evidence.
- **Corbray controlled opposition:** Public fury is not genuine opposition. This potentially contaminates future reports of plots involving Lyn and changes the reading of apparently hostile political testimony.
- **Vale inheritance:** Petyr's eagerness to bring Harrold Hardyng near Robert is a priority for verification against genealogical and legal facts from later chapters.
- **Political pedagogy:** Alayne organizes hospitality, clothing, heraldry, seating and wine while Petyr models coalition splitting, staged threats, and time-buying. Distinguish growing practical skill from consent to his intimate demands.
- **Marillion:** Do not close his fate based only on Petyr's statement when Robert continues to report hearing him.
- **Winter position:** The Eyrie can store grain but cannot independently replenish fresh food; the strategic value of the waycastles and lower valley increases with worsening weather.

**Reading receipt:** Entire primary chapter read. No later Alayne, Royce, Hunter, Corbray, or Robert outcomes retroactively asserted here.
