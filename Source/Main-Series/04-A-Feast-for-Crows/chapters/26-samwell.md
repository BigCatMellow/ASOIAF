## Chronological close reading

1. The chapter opens at evening in an upper room of a Braavosi inn. **Sam waits anxiously for Dareon**, who promised to return with food and wine before dusk but has not appeared. The party is stranded and destitute rather than proceeding to Oldtown.
2. Mist rises from the city's canals. Sam observes nightfall and expects the shuttered city to belong increasingly to bravos and courtesans. The night setting makes a timid, armed foreigner particularly vulnerable.
3. **Gilly's eyes are swollen from weeping**, her hair neglected and her manner withdrawn. She stays near an unlit hearth, an image of persistent need for warmth despite the room's lack of fuel.
4. Gilly identifies Dareon's incentive more accurately than Sam's excuses: the singer prefers cheerful drinking company to the sick, hungry, grieving people depending on him.
5. Dareon is courting the courtesan called **Moonshadow**, who kissed him when he sang by the Moon Pool. He talks about writing a song for her rather than using his talents to acquire passage money or fulfill Watch duties.
6. **Jon's intended recruitment project is failing in practice**: Dareon was to publicize the Wall and attract men to the Watch, but composes love songs about beautiful women. His real talent as singer is established; how he uses it is the issue.
7. Dareon has complained that Gilly's sobbing and the child's cries disrupt his sleep and has expressed a wish to strike her. His cruelty predates the final confrontation in this chapter.
8. **Sam understands Gilly's grief is for her own son left at the Wall**, yet still cannot bring himself to ask which infant she nurses. Aemon has already inferred Jon ordered the exchange; the reader can separately link this to AFFC 15, while Sam's refusal to ask preserves his internal uncertainty.
9. Sam angrily blames Jon for the child's removal. When Sam previously asked when Jon became emotionally hard, Aemon answered that it happened when Sam helped make Jon Lord Commander. This is Aemon's moral interpretation of office and responsibility, not evidence that Jon lacks empathy.
10. The **Titan sounds nightfall** loudly enough to wake the infant, which wakes Aemon. The chapter places Braavosi landmarks, the Watch's exile, and bodily frailty in the same scene.
11. Aemon first calls for **Egg**, then needs Sam to identify himself and remind him they are in Braavos. He now loses the thread of conversation and confuses time, contrasting with his formerly reliable mind at Castle Black.
12. **Aemon is one hundred and two**. His skin is clammy, his breathing weak and wheezing; Sam describes disorientation and exhaustion. Illness and age are directly depicted; its precise medical cause is not independently diagnosed.
13. The room has no firewood. Sam regrets spending their last free silver on a healer instead of heat, but the healer from the **House of the Red Hands** judged the old man beyond curative treatment.
14. That healer, wearing a red-and-white striped robe, provided **half a flask of dreamwine** as palliation and claimed death was in Aemon's lungs. This is medical testimony, not proof of a supernatural death sentence or malicious treatment.
15. **Their autumn crossing was punishing.** After Skagos, northern and southern storms alternated, lightning and prolonged black rains struck, the vessel iced over, and the captain lowered and lashed down the mast to complete part of the crossing under oars.
16. Once ashore, Sam, Gilly, Dareon and the infant recovered their appetite, but **Aemon never did**. The differential recovery supports the inference that his decline is more severe than ordinary seasickness.
17. At first the party possessed a **substantial purse**, but Dareon arranged an oversized bed in the inn; the proprietor charged for its capacity of eight. They later paid extra for a hearth without understanding the cost of supplying fuel.
18. **Aemon insists on going to the docks** to find out about dragons. Sam wants him to recover first and promises a future ship to Oldtown, but the immediate financial and physical obstacles render Sam's optimism precarious.
19. Aemon dreams he and **Egg** are young again in Oldtown with Egg's large knight, at an inn known for very strong cider. Reader-level context identifies Egg as Aegon V and the knight with the remembered Duncan; the dream does not make them present.
20. Aemon's urgency is specifically about **reports of living dragons**. Dareon brought sailor gossip while drunk and cannot recall the details. Aemon admits singers invent things but urges Sam to identify any underlying truth.
21. Sam fears his limited High Valyrian and poorer Braavosi, revealing a concrete linguistic barrier to investigating a cosmopolitan port. Aemon speaks more languages but cannot safely make the visit.
22. Sam argues the old man can wait until he is stronger. Aemon counters that he will **never see Oldtown again**, recognizing an end Sam refuses to accept.
23. Aemon recalls his **father's succession** as unexpected and relates the father's view that the throne was punishment for the blow that killed his brother. This is family memory and interpretation, to reconcile with Targaryen succession histories, not an eyewitness account of the older incident.
24. Aemon also voices **fear of death and uncertainty about the afterlife** despite decades of religious service. His courage and wisdom coexist with human fear.
25. In response to Sam asking what he remembers, Aemon says dragons were his family's glory and grief. Sam correctly points out the last living dragon died before Aemon's birth; **Aemon's ensuing dragon 'memories' are dreams, not sight of historical living dragons**.
26. The dreams include a **bleeding red star**, dragon shadows crossing snow, leathery wings and hot breath. Aemon says other Targaryen brothers had dragon dreams that killed them. This familial claim and its implied causal relationship require independent checking.
27. Aemon says the world approaches forgotten prophecies and possible wonders or terrors, then supplies his **own rival explanation**: he might merely be feverish and dying. The text explicitly prevents automatic prophetic certainty.
28. Aemon regrets leaving the Wall, contrasting destructive fire with preserving cold. **This is his late-life inference** from deteriorating after traveling south, not proof the Wall has a medically or magically protective field.
29. Aemon asks Sam to undertake a final act of courage: find **any sailor with firsthand knowledge of dragons**. Sam agrees and simultaneously plans to find Dareon and bring back food, wine and wood.
30. **Gilly remains in the locked room with Aemon and the infant**. Before leaving, Sam retrieves his swordbelt and the **old cracked horn Jon gave him**. The horn's continued physical presence matters; this scene neither sounds it nor attributes power to it.
31. Sam goes out by the street entrance to avoid the disgruntled proprietor. Thick fog and canal edges have already nearly caused him to fall into the water; his outward route is hazardous before any confrontation.
32. Sam remembers romantic childhood fantasies of seeing the Titan and water dancers, but now wishes only to leave Braavos. **Expectations of the city contrast with the hunger, language isolation and medical crisis actually experienced.**
33. He follows side streets toward **Ragman's Harbor**, tugging at a swordbelt too loose for him and flinching at the city's numerous cats. He first imagines Dareon might be injured or murdered, showing he has not yet accepted abandonment.
34. Sam searches several places Dareon has played: **Inn of the Green Eel, Black Bargeman, Moroggo's, Foghouse, Nabbo's Bridge winesink, Outcast Inn, House of Seven Lamps and the Cattery**. None yields the singer; language barriers frustrate inquiries to boatmen.
35. By the Cattery's red lantern, two richly dressed, armed **bravos** accost Sam. Their demand escalates from mockery of his body and clothes to asking for his cloak; Sam avoids touching his sword because it could signal a challenge.
36. The chapter notes an **inversion of Westerosi class costume**: Braavosi bravos dress ostentatiously while the city's wealthy and powerful often wear discreet dark colors. Sam's black Watch attire and sword are misread in this context.
37. A barrow girl pushing seaweed, whose identity the reader can connect to **Arya's 'Cat of the Canals' assignment in AFFC 22**, intervenes. She recognizes his Night's Watch affiliation, tells him of another Watch singer at the **Happy Port**, and instructs him on how to avoid dueling over courtesans' reputations.
38. The bravos try to take Sam's cloak. **Cat warns that yielding once invites further extortion**; she draws a slender knife with her left hand and the men depart. Their names are given as **Terro and Orbelo**. She displays street judgment and the capacity to threaten violence without proving an actual fight.
39. Cat offers Sam her remaining baked clams, which he eats, and explains that wearing a sword at night exposes him to challenges. Her help is practical rather than a ritual or supernatural intervention.
40. Asked who she is, Cat answers **'No one'** before giving her current name. This aligns with the House of Black and White's demand to abandon self, but her speech and choices may still preserve the old Arya.
41. Cat gives Sam a story: her father served as oarmaster on a vessel named **Nymeria**, a bravo killed him in a dispute about her mother's beauty, and **Brusco** took her in. **This is an identity-cover claim**, not independently verified family history. The named ship evokes her direwolf but cannot prove a real vessel or ship connection.
42. **Sam states he and his companions purchased passage aboard the Lady Ushanora, but that ship has already sailed.** The captain had waited three days, personally seen Aemon's condition, refused additional delay, and retained the payment for the finest cabin despite the travelers' inability to use it.
43. The departure establishes their practical trap: **their passage money is gone and no ship is booked**, while the captain insists he must honor tide and cargo schedules. Sam privately imagines where he might be by now, but those are speculative travel estimates.
44. Cat explains that the **Sailor's Wife** at the Happy Port accepts clients only after a ritual marriage. Sam is horrified at the apparent breach of a sworn Watch vow. Cat offers to lead him; he already knows the Mummer's Ship landmark and rushes off.
45. At the **Happy Port**, Sam encounters an affectionate one-eyed woman called **Yna**, dancing women and a **red priest**, plus sailors of different origins. The brothel is also a crossroads for maritime news; the priest's presence alone proves no magical activity.
46. Dareon openly calls Sam his brother, is drinking and has a naked woman on his lap wearing **his black cloak**. The Sailor's Wife's marriage is **for a night**, as Dareon understands it, but the actual offense most decisively established is his abandonment of companions and intention to leave the Watch.
47. Sam demands food and wine promised to their sick party and invokes the Watch oath. Dareon mocks Sam's killing of an Other and suggests he sleep with Gilly, demonstrating contempt for Sam's obligations and the woman's suffering.
48. Sam says Aemon seeks information about dragons, red stars and shadows. Dareon refuses to assist on his 'wedding night' and states that **Westeros—and, implicitly, its Watch discipline—no longer applies to him in Braavos**.
49. **Dareon explicitly declares himself finished with the black**, retrieves his cloak from the woman, throws it at Sam for Aemon's warmth and forecasts a prosperous life dressed in velvet and furs. The explicit declaration is stronger evidence of desertion than the temporary 'marriage' itself.
50. **Sam attacks Dareon with his fists**, knocking him over a table and hitting his mouth, belly, shoulders and nose. For once anger overrides Sam's fear; this does not make his action legally sanctioned or end the vows dispute.
51. Someone pulls Sam off and strikes him; he is then hurled into a canal. Sam **cannot swim effectively**, panics, swallows brackish water and becomes entangled in the ill-fitting swordbelt. His childhood trauma around drowning and Hyle Hunt's earlier rescue are relevant AFFC 15 connections.
52. A large **Summer Islander** leaps in and pulls Sam out. Sam revives on the cobbles as the rescuer expels swallowed water; the man identifies himself as **Xhondo**, laments his ruined feathered cloak and finds the attack on the singer amusing.
53. **Xhondo identifies himself as a mate on the Cinnamon Wind and says he knows about dragons**. This is the chapter's closing reversal: the last person Sam expected to encounter can offer the information Aemon requested.
54. **Reader-level cross-book identification:** In **ACOK 27 (Daenerys)**, Quhuru Mo captained the *Cinnamon Wind* and personally saw Daenerys's living dragons in Qarth. Sam does not yet know that history and Xhondo does not explicitly claim to have seen the dragons himself. His link is unusually concrete but still requires testimony.

## Cross-book and cross-POV junctions

- **`AFFC 5, 15 (Samwell)` → this chapter:** Jon sends Sam and Aemon away; the infant exchange, Gilly's grief, Dareon's worsening behavior and Aemon's cold exposure now have material consequences. Jon's recruitment-by-song plan has failed without his knowing.
- **`ASOS 75 (Samwell)` → `AFFC 15` → this chapter:** Dalla's infant and Gilly's infant were both fed by Gilly at Castle Black, later exchanged under Jon's orders. This chapter demonstrates the continuing trauma rather than introducing a third child or fresh proof of a different swap.
- **`AFFC 6, 22 (Arya)` → this chapter:** Arya arrived in Braavos, hid Needle instead of surrendering her identity, and entered Brusco's household as Cat. Sam does not recognize her. Cat's alleged Nymeria ship, lost father and threatened vengeance cannot be lifted into canon.
- **`ACOK 27 (Daenerys)` → this chapter:** Quhuru Mo's *Cinnamon Wind* left Qarth having learned of actual dragons; its mate Xhondo now meets Sam by chance in Braavos. This independently supports **a plausible dragon-information route** between Daenerys and Aemon, without proving what Xhondo personally witnessed or what Sam will report.
- **`AFFC 25 (Brienne)` and `AFFC 15 (Samwell)`:** In Sam's childhood **Hyle Hunt** rescued him from drowning; Sam nearly drowns again here. The physically credible difference is that Xhondo can rescue him in time, not that Sam has suddenly learned to swim.
- **`AFFC 5 (Samwell)`:** The search for dragons is not a replacement for the Watch's original Others research but potentially intersects it: dragonsteel, actual fire-breathing dragons, dragon dreams and dead men's return are separate evidentiary questions.
- **`AFFC Prologue (Pate)`:** Oldtown and Citadel archives await Sam, but the ship's loss, Aemon's illness and Braavosi information bottlenecks make reaching them contingent rather than inevitable.
- **Potential later cross-check (not imported as current-POV knowledge):** Follow Cat's intelligence on Dareon's desertion and the Happy Port in the later Arya POV; the scene establishes motives but not his eventual punishment here.

## Consequences and next investigation tests

1. **Urgent:** Identify what Xhondo and Quhuru actually know, the dates and ports of their voyage, and whether Aemon survives to receive any account of the dragons. Do not confuse a trader's firsthand sighting with secondhand dock gossip.
2. **Urgent:** Track Aemon's chronology, physical decline and stated regret at leaving the Wall, using a medical/environmental reading alongside the more speculative cold-preservation theory.
3. **High:** Compare Aemon's red star and shadows-on-snow dreams with `AGOT` comet sightings and `ACOK` Daenerys dragon visions, but preserve the uncertainty Aemon himself voices.
4. **High:** Track Dareon's broken vow and Sam's assault separately; no execution, killing or later judgment occurs in this chapter. Check what the Faceless Men's training ultimately asks of Cat regarding strangers and oathbreakers.
5. **High:** Track the loss of the *Lady Ushanora* payment, the missing wood, the healer's palliative treatment and Gilly's ability to move safely within Braavos as the real cost of a stalled Watch mission.
6. **Medium:** Follow the **old cracked horn** as an object physically transferred from Jon to Sam; reject identification with any named magical horn without a distinctive description or independent test.
7. **Medium:** Reconcile Aemon's family memory about his father's fratricidal blow and the deaths of brothers with other sources, keeping retrospective guilt and the literal blow distinct from supernatural explanations.
8. **Medium:** Map *Cinnamon Wind*, *Lady Ushanora*, the two Braavosi harbors and Sam's itinerary; the names are logistical evidence, not permission to invent exact dates.
