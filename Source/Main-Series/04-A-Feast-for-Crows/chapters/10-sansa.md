## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Marillion's songs carry through the Eyrie from his sky cell.** Sansa cannot escape songs about betrayal, murder, hanging and grief, making the cover-up a constant sensory presence rather than a settled event.
2. **Petyr says he gave Marillion his word and reached an agreement with him.** He also says Mord can be persuasive, making coercion part of the arrangement even before the singer is produced as a witness.
3. **Sansa fears Lord Nestor Royce will see through the false account of Lysa's death.** Her concern is not abstract guilt; she knows Marillion could tell the truth.
4. **Petyr's answer is explicitly transactional.** He says their lies will profit Nestor, demonstrating that he plans to make belief materially advantageous.
5. **Sansa still thinks of herself internally as Eddard and Catelyn's daughter.** The Alayne identity is an externally necessary cover, not yet a fully internalized self.
6. **She is traumatized and sleep-deprived after Lysa's death.** Fear therefore affects how readily she accepts Petyr's reassuring framing.
7. **Petyr teaches that some lies can be acts of love or protection.** Sansa extends this into a broader survival ethic because lies did help keep her alive in King's Landing.
8. **Sansa realizes Petyr also serves comforting lies to her.** This is a major epistemic step: she recognizes manipulation while still choosing dependence because she sees no safe alternative.
9. **She distinguishes 'Petyr' from 'Littlefinger' in her mind.** One is protector and quasi-family figure; the other is the calculating courtier who did little for her during earlier abuse.
10. **Her belief that she has nowhere else to go is partly based on false or stale information.** She thinks Bran and Rickon dead, Tyrion executed and other relatives inaccessible.
11. **Sansa now knows that Lysa murdered Jon Arryn.** She also knows Lysa's death followed the attempt to murder Sansa and Petyr's intervention. This makes Sansa a holder of highly dangerous Vale and crown-level information.
12. **Sweetrobin repeatedly seeks Sansa's bed.** She has Lothor Brune lock him in because he nuzzles at her breasts and may wet the bed during shaking fits; the behavior is intrusive even if Robert is a sick, dependent child.
13. **Sansa comforts Robert with another deliberate 'kindly' lie about Petyr loving Lysa.** Her use of deception is becoming active and situational rather than purely defensive.
14. **Before Nestor, Robert repeats the prepared claim that Marillion killed Lysa.** He relies on what Sansa and Petyr have told him rather than eyewitness knowledge of the push.
15. **Sansa gives the false testimony successfully.** A genuine tear and visible fear make the account more persuasive because the emotions are real even though the accusation is false.
16. **Robert suffers a severe shaking fit during the hearing.** He loses bodily control, falls from the throne and must be carried out, demonstrating the seriousness of his condition.
17. **Petyr orders Robert returned to bed and bled.** The treatment reflects current medical practice in the setting; its efficacy is not established here.
18. **Marillion appears bandaged over the eyes and gloved.** Rumors say Mord removed fingers; Sansa cannot verify that through the gloves.
19. **Marillion gives the agreed confession.** He claims jealous love and rage after hearing Lysa carried Petyr's child. Because Petyr previously described their agreement and Mord's persuasion, this confession is not reliable evidence that events occurred as stated.
20. **Nestor and his companions are predisposed to hate Marillion.** They recall mockery, arrogance and Lysa's favoritism, creating strong confirmation bias in favor of his guilt.
21. **Petyr performs self-blame as part of the story.** He says he caused Lysa's death by insisting she send Marillion away, prompting the Royces to reassure him.
22. **Nestor reports that Bronze Yohn Royce and allied Vale lords intend to remove Petyr as Lord Protector.** Their potential military strength vastly exceeds Petyr's small garrison.
23. **Petyr already knows much of the coalition's composition.** His information includes Waynwood, Templeton, Redfort, Belmore, Hunter and Corbray connections.
24. **Petyr gives Nestor a hereditary grant of the Gates of the Moon.** Historically the office had not been hereditary and the castle remained an Arryn possession.
25. **The parchment carries the Arryn seal but not Lysa's signature.** Petyr signed it himself as Lord Protector after her death.
26. **Nestor wants to believe Lysa valued him.** Petyr converts old slights into a flattering story that makes the irregular grant feel like overdue justice.
27. **The grant ties Nestor's new status directly to Petyr's legitimacy.** If Petyr is removed, the authority behind the grant becomes challengeable.
28. **Sansa independently works out this leverage.** Petyr praises her for seeing why he signed it himself, providing direct evidence that she is learning political mechanics rather than merely memorizing scripts.
29. **Petyr explains that honor can be bypassed through family interest.** He believes Nestor would reject an explicit bribe but will accept advantage framed as his son's and house's rightful due.
30. **Sansa feels pride at solving the scheme even while remaining morally uneasy.** Political competence and discomfort are developing together.
31. **Petyr insists Sansa must be Alayne even in private.** His practical reason is accidental eavesdropping; identity discipline is becoming a security protocol.
32. **Oswell and Lothor Brune know who Sansa really is.** Petyr says each is controlled partly by surveillance and long service rather than blind trust.
33. **Petyr uses reciprocal watching among his own agents.** Brune watches Kettleblack and Kettleblack watches Brune, illustrating that his system assumes even loyal servants may fail.
34. **Petyr invokes his old warning to Ned to trust no one.** Sansa is being taught the exact political lesson her father did not adopt.
35. **Sansa associates hidden identity failure with blood on her hands.** Petyr invokes Marillion and other deaths to make constant performance feel morally necessary.
36. **Sweetrobin later asks whether Sansa is his mother now.** She answers with another kindly lie and accepts a maternal role inside the Alayne identity.
37. **End state:** Petyr has secured Nestor's immediate interest, Marillion has publicly taken the blame, and Sansa has moved from frightened participant toward a student who can identify the structure of Petyr's leverage.

## Cross-POV implications

- **Sansa's survival logic now includes knowingly false testimony.** This is not evidence she has become Petyr; she understands the moral cost and remains frightened.
- **Petyr's power is contractual and informational rather than military.** Nestor's grant is designed so a stronger lord has something to lose if Petyr falls.
- **The Marillion confession is structurally contaminated evidence.** Later characters who rely on it are relying on a coerced narrative built by the actual killer.
- **Sansa's false family information is again decisive:** like Arya, she believes key Stark options are gone, narrowing her perceived alternatives to the manipulator currently sheltering her.
- **Alayne is beginning to acquire real relationships and duties.** Sweetrobin's dependence makes the alias more than a name even while Sansa's internal Stark identity survives.
