## Chronology and contextual observations

1. **Cersei accepts Tommen's immediate marriage to Margaery because the throne is insecure.** She explicitly weighs Stannis at Dragonstone/Storm's End, Riverrun's resistance and ironborn attacks against her hatred of the match.
2. **Her anger is displaced onto servants such as Jocelyn Swyft.** This continues the pattern of using lower-risk targets when the real political constraint cannot be attacked directly.
3. **A breakfast egg contains a bloody half-formed chick.** The event disgusts Cersei; the chapter assigns no supernatural meaning to it.
4. **Jaime constructs a layered anti-poison security plan for Tommen.** Kitchens are watched, dishes escorted, Boros tastes food and a maester with purges and antidotes remains nearby.
5. **Cersei believes Tyrion will use a different method because he is too cunning to repeat himself.** This is paranoia built on a false central premise: she still treats Tyrion as Joffrey's murderer.
6. **Maggy's prophecy is a private driver of Cersei's fear.** Cersei remembers that only Melara heard the original prophecy with her and that Melara is dead.
7. **Cersei orders Jaime to remain inside Tommen's bedchamber on the wedding night.** Her concern is not just assassination; she wants surveillance of Margaery and control over access to the king.
8. **She fears intimacy between Tommen and the Tyrell household even though consummation is impossible at his age.** Court influence is experienced as bodily and dynastic threat.
9. **The search of the Tower and Red Keep has uncovered many hidden passages but not Tyrion or Varys.** Cersei interprets failure to find them as evidence there must be still more passages.
10. **Some crawlways are too small for adult guards.** Pages and stableboys are used to explore them, showing the physical extremity of the search.
11. **Searchers find a passage toward the black cells, an apparently bottomless stone well, old bones/skulls and sacks of silver from Viserys I's reign.** The Red Keep contains layers of old hidden infrastructure whose original purposes are not explained here.
12. **The search causes casualties.** One boy becomes stuck, another breaks his legs after a fall, and two guards vanish in a side tunnel.
13. **Other guards claim to hear the missing men faintly through stone.** Demolition reveals only earth and rubble; their fate remains unknown.
14. **Cersei still wants to burn the Tower of the Hand partly to smoke out Tyrion.** Jaime correctly notes the tower has already been stripped to a shell and the search found no one.
15. **Cersei imagines moving the court away from King's Landing.** Jaime explains that possession of the Iron Throne in the capital is itself part of Tommen's legitimacy, while retreat to Casterly Rock would make him look like another claimant.
16. **Jaime warns that wildfire may spread beyond control.** Cersei trusts Hallyne's assurances despite the substance's known danger.
17. **Jaime explicitly tells Cersei that the planned spectacle makes her sound like Aerys.** The comparison angers her rather than prompting reconsideration.
18. **Cersei now thinks Jaime has become a stranger rather than her other half.** Their emotional split is becoming an explicit part of her political worldview.
19. **Tommen and Margaery marry in a deliberately smaller ceremony than Joffrey's.** Cost, grief and fear of another catastrophe all shape the modest scale.
20. **Margaery reuses the gown from her wedding to Joffrey.** Cersei interprets her ability to celebrate again as evidence she cared only for the crown.
21. **The king's marriage cloak retains Baratheon symbolism.** The regime still publicly performs Robert's dynasty even while the reader knows Tommen's biological paternity.
22. **Maggy's 'younger and more beautiful' warning attaches itself to Margaery in Cersei's mind.** The prophecy does not name Margaery; Cersei's identification is interpretation.
23. **Mace's toast to 'king and queen' makes Cersei feel displaced.** Redwyne's attempted toast to both queens worsens the insult by calling her the old queen.
24. **Jaime remains intensely vigilant at the feast.** He checks tapestries and patrols rather than relaxing, showing that he takes physical security seriously despite rejecting Cersei's more extreme fears.
25. **A harmless cough from Tommen triggers Cersei's memory of Joffrey dying.** Her panic and private tears demonstrate unresolved grief beneath the political hostility.
26. **Margaery responds to Tommen's cough calmly and affectionately.** No threat is present in the incident despite Cersei's fear.
27. **Taena Merryweather finds Cersei crying and supplies a new intelligence claim.** She says Senelle is secretly reporting Cersei's actions to Margaery through Margaery's cousins.
28. **Taena gives a concrete method for testing the claim.** She names expected meetings in the sept and intermediaries, making the allegation falsifiable rather than pure rumor.
29. **Cersei initially considers that Taena could be lying to manufacture distrust.** This moment of skepticism is important because she often loses it once information confirms her fears.
30. **Taena says her loyalty is to her husband and son rather than Highgarden.** Ambition is an obvious alternative motive for betraying Margaery or inventing the story.
31. **Cersei remembers Taena testified against Tyrion.** That prior alignment increases her willingness to trust Taena even though the trial testimony itself was not a neutral reliability test.
32. **After the report, Cersei decides she must replace the court with 'her own' people.** Suspicion of individuals expands into a broader purge mentality.
33. **Aurane Waters catches Cersei's eye because his Valyrian coloring reminds her of Rhaegar.** Attraction and nostalgia are entering her assessment of a politically ambitious courtier.
34. **Cersei sees Kevan speaking with Garlan and immediately folds it into her Tyrell-conspiracy model.** The Gardener coin continues to contaminate interpretation despite its unproved provenance.
35. **Jaime notices Cersei has had too much wine.** Her judgment during the later spectacle is therefore occurring under emotional stress and intoxication as well as paranoia.
36. **Cersei orders the Tower of the Hand burned publicly after the wedding.** Hallyne's pyromancers place fifty pots of wildfire plus ordinary fuel inside.
37. **The tower ignites in green flame and begins to collapse.** This is a deliberate destruction of royal infrastructure for symbolic purification rather than military necessity.
38. **Cersei experiences the fire as beautiful and cleansing.** She thinks of Joffrey as an infant and tells herself the old Hands and their plots are burning away.
39. **The emotional logic resembles the Aerys behavior Jaime warned about.** The text creates the comparison through Jaime's explicit line and Cersei's ecstatic response; it does not mean their motives or mental states are identical.
40. **Tommen is frightened and fascinated until Margaery reframes the flames as dancing.** Her ability to soothe him contrasts with Cersei's repeated use of fear and shame.
41. **Cersei sends Jaime to escort the newlyweds but remains with Osmund Kettleblack.** Given Tyrion's earlier accusation, the arrangement is psychologically charged for Jaime's storyline, but this scene alone does not establish a sexual encounter.
42. **End state:** Tommen is married, Cersei's fear of Margaery has intensified, Taena has positioned herself as an informer, and the Tower of the Hand is physically destroyed in a wildfire spectacle that Jaime explicitly associates with Aerys.

## Cross-POV implications

- **Cersei's prophecy is functioning as a threat-detection algorithm:** ordinary succession, beauty, intimacy and court popularity become evidence for the foretold rival once she selects Margaery as candidate.
- **Taena's usefulness should not be mistaken for reliability.** Her claim is testable, and her family ambition gives her a clear incentive to tell Cersei what Cersei wants to hear.
- **The Red Keep itself is an unresolved intelligence environment.** Searches reveal real hidden infrastructure, which makes Cersei's fears less absurd in form even when her conclusions about Tyrion are wrong.
- **Jaime and Margaery both regulate Tommen more effectively than Cersei in this chapter.** Jaime through physical security, Margaery through reassurance; Cersei interprets both independence and affection as threats to her control.
- **The Tower fire destroys evidence as well as a symbol.** Even if no fugitive is present, old rooms, possessions and hidden spaces are being deliberately erased.
