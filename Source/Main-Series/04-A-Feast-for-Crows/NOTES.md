## Source-checked oath and aftermath scenes (additional pass)

- **AFFC 37 (Brienne):** Brienne refuses to abandon children threatened at the crossroads inn, is savagely bitten by Biter and later falls into the Brotherhood's custody. Rescue doesn't immunize her from subsequent accusations.
- **AFFC 42:** Thoros describes the moral decline of Beric's followers. Stoneheart interprets the Lannister sword and royal documents as evidence against Brienne, requires her to kill Jaime, and orders Brienne, Podrick and Hyle hanged; the exact word Brienne cries out is not narrated.
- **AFFC 42:** Lem Lemoncloak is operating in Sandor Clegane's Hound helm. This complicates later uses of the name 'the Hound'; no claim about Sandor's physical return is needed.
- **AFFC 44 (Jaime):** Jaime secures a negotiated Riverrun surrender but threatened grave harm to Edmure's family. Contrast later unwillingness to punish innocent villagers at Pennytree (ADWD 48).

## Focused agency audit — Arianne and Sansa (2026-10-15)

- **AFFC 10 (Sansa):** She witnesses Petyr leveraging a sealed hereditary grant and helps sustain a cover story about Lysa. Her understanding is incomplete and she fears being discovered.
- **AFFC 23 (Alayne):** She independently detects that Lyn Corbray's apparent opposition is staged. Petyr confirms paid hostility. He also recommends sweetsleep for Sweetrobin's shaking fits; Colemon hesitates.
- **AFFC 41 (Alayne):** Sansa helps Sweetrobin descend safely. Colemon warns that sweetsleep accumulates; Myranda mentions Jon Snow and Sansa blurts his name, but no Myranda identification is verified. Petyr **proposes** Harry Hardyng as husband after Robert's anticipated death; this is conditional, not an achieved marriage.
- **AFFC 13, 21 (Arys/Arianne):** Arianne's attempt to crown Myrcella is interrupted. **Hotah kills Arys on-page**; Myrcella's injury occurs as Gerold Dayne flees, though the exact strike is not visible in Arianne's narration.
- **AFFC 40 (Arianne):** Doran admits his old letter really contemplated Quentyn inheriting Dorne and reveals the abandoned Viserys betrothal. Arianne has a real grievance; new information alters her interpretation without dissolving distrust.

## AFFC 1–22 sequential close reading (2026-10-18)

- [1 The Prophet](chapters/01-the-prophet.md): **Aeron Damphair** is drowning and reviving **Emmond** on Great Wyk when **Sparr and Gormond Goodbrother** bring news that **Balon fell from a bridge at Pyke during a storm**. This is reported, not Aeron-observed; blaming **the Storm God** is his theological interpretation and accusing an assassin remains hypothetical. The **maester Murenmure** and **Gorold Goodbrother** report that **Euron** arrived at Lordsport **the day after** Balon died, occupied the **Seastone Chair**, summoned captains for homage and **drowned Sawane Botley** for championing Theon's heirship. Aeron prefers **Victarion**, who commands the Iron Fleet, while **Balon's preference for Asha**, **Theon's unknown survival** and mainland inheritance rules conflict with his misogynistic religious conception of kingship. The POV supplies **Quellon's nine-son genealogy**, childhood **Urri/Urrigon's mutilation and death** (Aeron accidentally cut off his hand in the finger dance), **Balon's reprisal against the maester**, Aeron's previous alcoholic personality, and **the Golden Storm's destruction by Stannis's *Fury***. The recurrent **creaking hinge and Euron-associated dread** and reference to **a dead woman behind Victarion–Euron enmity** are important *unexplained* memories, not an account of their precise cause. **Rodrik Harlaw summons Asha's adherents to Ten Towers**, while Aeron sends all captains to **Nagga's hill on Old Wyk** and actually **calls a kingsmoot**; no election has yet occurred.
- [2 The Captain of Guards](chapters/02-the-captain-of-guards.md): From **Areo Hotah's** limited loyal POV, gout-ridden **Doran Martell** watches children at the **Water Gardens** and initially delays reading Oberyn's death letter. **Obara** demands two armies and the sack of **Oldtown**; **Nymeria** proposes killing **Tywin, Jaime, Cersei and Tommen**; **Tyene** wants to crown **Myrcella as elder sibling under Dornish law** to provoke an invading army into defensible passes. Doran notes **Tommen is personally innocent** and that **Dornish succession law does not directly govern the Iron Throne**. He describes having sent Oberyn to assess the court, find allies and investigate Elia's death without provoking Tywin. **Tyene expressly knows the poison Oberyn used on Gregor is slow and agonizing**, strengthening ASOS's clinical evidence without naming the substance. **Caleotte inspects Doran's hand for scratches** after Tyene approaches, but no actual dosing is witnessed. Hotah's history includes being **sold to Norvos's bearded priests**, branding and vows **Serve / Obey / Protect**; his memories of the three **Norvosi bells** anchor that origin. Returning to **Sunspear**, Doran meets genuinely angry crowds and orders **all eight Sand Snakes detained, first Tyene, Nymeria and Obara, without bloodshed**. The order's implementation is **unshown**; nearly adult **Sarella** is away at an unspecified 'game'. **Doran still expects Tywin's promised execution of Gregor**—the reader already knows Tywin died.
- [3 Cersei](chapters/03-cersei.md): Cersei dreams **Tyrion humiliating her on the Iron Throne** and wakes to **Tywin dead**. Jaime had sent guards to protect sleeping **Tommen**, and **Loras** is present as a Kingsguard protector; she nonetheless suspects Stannis and the Tyrells. **Lum** found the privy corpse; the **hidden fireplace access** Tyrion used is exposed. **Kevan** mourns, and the unchained **Qyburn** undertakes preparation of the body after Pycelle summons the silent sisters. **Shae's naked, strangled corpse and the linked golden-hands chain in the Hand's bed** directly contradict Cersei's idealization of Tywin, so she welcomes Qyburn's suggestion that Tywin was merely questioning Shae and **orders the Kettleblacks to conceal the corpse and recover the chain**. Cersei remembers promising Shae **jewels, a house and a knight husband**, while withholding them until she gave information on Sansa. **Jaime refuses to serve as Hand** and Cersei slaps him, then imagines making **Kevan** a compliant official; that appointment is only contemplated. **Varys is missing**; **Boros** reports Tyrion's empty black cell, vanished turnkey **Rugen** and sleeping guards. Cersei **orders those guards killed** (completion not observed), recalls a prophecy involving the **valonqar**, and wrongly assumes various unproved political motives for Tywin's real murder.
- [4 Brienne](chapters/04-brienne.md): **Brienne** follows roads through **Rosby** toward **Duskendale**, questioning locals for thirteen-year-old **Sansa** with an older knight/fool; she misses a **skinny boy on a piebald horse** who is also later reported traveling by night. **Sansa is actually under the 'Alayne' cover in the Vale** by reader knowledge, and the supposed Arya headed north is counterfeit. Brienne shares camp with impoverished hedge knights **Ser Creighton Longbough** (grand Blackwater boasts unverified) and **Ser Illifer the Penniless**, who initially suspects Brienne because she bears **Harrenhal's old Lothston bat shield**, then accepts her oath denying Renly's murder. Memories of **Renly dancing with her on Tarth** and fear of sexual assault clarify her commitment and vigilance. A **sparrow** procession of around sixty commoners with a **cart of murdered holy people's bones**, a **star-carved poor fellow** and **barefoot septon with crystal** calls for knights to defend the Faith from war atrocities; the crowd menaces nonparticipants, and the septon restrains it. This is **actual organized religious agitation**, not yet an identified future church leadership. Merchant **Hibald** employs **Ser Shadrich the Mad Mouse**, who openly recognizes the Dontos lead and claims **Varys offers a bounty for Sansa**. At **Old Stone Bridge** inn Brienne pays for lodging with Jaime's money and carries **Tommen's signed assistance letter**, keeps **Oathkeeper (reworked *Ice*)** hidden in her bedroll, prays for guidance and **departs during the night** rather than stay near the rival searcher. **No verified sighting of Sansa occurs**.
- [5 Samwell](chapters/05-samwell.md): **Sam** is exhausting himself in Castle Black's disordered archive researching the **Others**. His most important methodological discovery is that the supposed ancient record is itself unstable: the earliest histories he finds were written after the Andals arrived, legendary chronology contains obvious anachronisms, and the oldest Lord Commander list has only **674 names** despite Jon's official number of 998. The old accounts nevertheless preserve several useful leads: the **children of the forest once gave the Watch one hundred obsidian daggers a year**; fallen men must be burned; Others are associated with cold, night and reanimated animals; and one Long Night account says **dragonsteel** could kill them. **Jon and Sam infer this might mean Valyrian steel, but the chapter does not confirm that equation**, and the same unreliable-transmission problem applies. Sam has still found **no answer** to who the Others are, where they come from or what they want. Meanwhile Jon signs Aemon's diplomatic **'paper shield' to Tommen**, even though he and Sam still strategize around **Tywin as a living actor after the reader has seen Tywin die**. Only the **Karhold raven** has answered Stannis's northern summons, making Jon's belief that Stannis 'has the Karstarks' notably under-verified. Jon rejects Mance's supposed king's blood yet removes **Aemon Targaryen** because Melisandre might use genuine royal blood, sends **Sam to Oldtown to become the Watch's future maester**, and sends **Aemon, Gilly and an infant** south as well. Sam's maester assignment directly reactivates the trauma of **Randyll chaining him for three days and nights**, while Jon insists the Watch now outranks paternal authority and orders Sam to stop calling himself a craven. **Gilly's distress and contradictory infant language are visible to the reader but not understood by Sam**, so the exact arrangement should be cross-checked rather than reconstructed from this POV alone. Aemon leaves Jon a **marked passage in the *Jade Compendium*** and carries rare books toward the Citadel; Sam finds only about one in four volumes Aemon wanted.

- [6 Arya](chapters/06-arya.md): **Arya reaches Braavos only because the *Titan's Daughter* refused her preferred destination, Eastwatch**, continuing a pattern in which every attempted route ends somewhere else. She believes **Jon is her only surviving close family member**, so her entry into an identity-erasing institution is built on major false information about the Stark family. Braavosi sailors give her the temporary name **Salty**, while **Needle** remains her strongest physical link to Jon and Winterfell. Denyo describes Braavos's Moonsinger origins and many tolerated gods; those are civic/religious accounts rather than independently verified ancient history. The **House of Black and White** recognizes Jaqen's **iron coin and phrase** as credentials, contains a black pool used by suffering people who then die peacefully, and presents Arya with a face-changing fear test whose mechanism is still unexplained. The priest rejects her succession of aliases and finally tells her there is no place there for **Arya Stark** as such. She has entered the House, but **she has not yet become 'no one'**, abandoned Needle, lost Stark memories or stopped wanting personally chosen deaths.
- [7 Cersei](chapters/07-cersei.md): At **Tywin's wake**, Cersei tightly disciplines eight-year-old **Tommen**, compares him unfavorably with Joffrey and begins dismantling parts of Tywin's coalition. **Lancel** returns gaunt, white-haired and religious; Cersei's private fear that he may confess both their affair and **Robert's drugged wine** strongly corroborates those shared secrets. She humiliates **Mace Tyrell** by naming sickly **Gyles Rosby** master of coin instead of the expected Garth, recruits ambitious **Taena Merryweather**, and elevates **Qyburn** despite his admitted living-subject experiments. Qyburn finds an old **Gardener gold coin** hidden in Rugen's cell; the object is real, but Cersei immediately turns its Reach association into certainty of a Tyrell conspiracy without proving provenance. Qyburn identifies **slow manticore venom** as Gregor's poison and receives authority to continue secret work on him. **Kevan** refuses to be Cersei's compliant Hand, demands she surrender the regency, proposes Rowan or Tarly instead, and strongly implies he knows **Jaime is Tommen's father**. Cersei ends the chapter with less experienced family support and more dependence on personally chosen clients.
- [8 Jaime](chapters/08-jaime.md): Jaime's vigil establishes that **he forced Varys at dagger-point to arrange Tyrion's escape**; Varys did not originate that rescue, and Jaime did not know Tyrion would kill Tywin. His search of the Red Keep finds old **Targaryen tunnels** but no Varys. A Rhaegar memory confirms the prince intended to **call a council and make unspecified changes after the Trident**, but does not prove any particular deposition or succession plan. Crucially, Jaime already knows **Rugen was Varys**, so objects later found in Rugen's cell—including the Gardener coin—cannot safely be read as straightforward evidence of a Reach paymaster. He also confirms that Cersei's order to kill the drugged turnkeys was carried out even though **Jaime knows they were innocent of Tyrion's escape**, making her evidence suppression self-defeating. Cersei asks Jaime to become Hand; **he refuses** and begins asserting independent Kingsguard and political authority. Tommen breaks down at Tywin's decaying corpse and says he used to **'go away inside' when Joffrey...**, strongly suggesting prior abuse without specifying its form. Jaime then proposes a pragmatic compromise: allow **Tommen and Margaery to marry**, then send **Mace to take Storm's End**. Cersei tells him he sounds like Tywin, even as Jaime is trying to redefine himself away from his father's and sister's models.


- [9 Brienne](chapters/09-brienne.md): In **Duskendale**, Brienne learns that Dontos was the sole Hollard spared after the Defiance because **Barristan** interceded, but he never returned and had no local base. Her own reasoning gets strikingly close to the truth when she identifies **the Eyrie as Sansa's easiest plausible family refuge**, yet a more concrete but weaker report diverts her toward **Maidenpool**: a holy brother recalls a ragged fool seeking passage for **three**, and names **Nimble Dick** as someone who later bragged of 'fooling a fool.' Brienne also has Jaime's shield repainted with a **green tree and falling star** remembered from an old shield in her father's armory; the original owner's identity remains an open provenance clue. Her dream of Renly's death now substitutes **Jaime** for Renly, while Duskendale smallfolk still publicly dismiss Jaime as an oathbreaker. The recurring piebald-horse follower is finally exposed as **Podrick Payne**, Tyrion's abandoned squire, who has been following Brienne because he hopes Sansa may lead him back to Tyrion.
- [10 Sansa](chapters/10-sansa.md): Petyr's cover story for Lysa's death becomes an explicit exercise in **coerced evidence and profitable lies**. He tells Sansa that Mord persuaded **Marillion**, and the singer appears bandaged, gloved and ready to confess to killing Lysa from jealous love; because the confession was arranged, it is not reliable event history. Sansa knowingly repeats the lie to **Nestor Royce**, while also recognizing that Petyr serves comforting lies to her. She now knows the far more dangerous truth that **Lysa murdered Jon Arryn**. Nestor reveals that **Bronze Yohn and allied Vale lords mean to remove Petyr**, but Petyr neutralizes the immediate threat by granting Nestor hereditary possession of the **Gates of the Moon** under Petyr's own signature as Lord Protector. Sansa independently deduces the real mechanism: remove Petyr and the legal foundation of Nestor's new prize becomes vulnerable. This is clear evidence of growing political comprehension, even as Petyr insists she must be **Alayne even in private**.
- [11 The Kraken's Daughter](chapters/11-the-krakens-daughter.md): **Asha's support is too thin for a direct seizure of Pyke**, concentrated mainly on Harlaw, while Aeron's kingsmoot has drawn captains to Old Wyk. **Rodrik the Reader** is studying Marwyn's *Book of Lost Books*, including Marwyn's claim to have recovered three pages of the pre-Doom *Signs and Portents*; that remains a secondhand bibliographic claim. Rodrik and Asha both register **Euron's suspicious day-after-Balon timing** without proving murder. Rodrik also notes that even the date of the last kingsmoot is disputed by roughly a factor of two, reinforcing the broader warning about ancient chronology. His strategic alternative to Balon's Old Way is to bargain the iron fleet to a mainland claimant for **durable land**, though he is already working with stale news when he speaks of **Tywin as living**. Asha agrees Balon was brave but a bad lord and proposes a still-unstated **third course**. Tristifer Botley reports that Euron drowned Sawane, redistributed lands and is **buying allies with eastern treasure**. Asha, meanwhile, has no identified body for Theon and refuses both Rodrik's safer inheritance and Tris's marriage proposal.
- [12 Cersei](chapters/12-cersei.md): Cersei permits **Tommen and Margaery's wedding** because Stannis, Riverrun and the ironborn still make the Tyrell alliance necessary, but Maggy's prophecy increasingly causes her to interpret Margaery as the unnamed **younger and more beautiful** rival. Jaime establishes serious anti-poison security while Cersei remains convinced Tyrion is hiding in the Red Keep. The search has found genuine mysteries—many secret passages, an apparently bottomless well, old bones and silver, and **two guards who vanish in a tunnel**—which gives her uncertainty a real substrate without validating her Tyrion conclusions. **Taena Merryweather** claims Cersei's maid Senelle spies for Margaery through her cousins and gives a testable meeting pattern; Cersei briefly considers deception before embracing the threat. After the wedding Cersei orders **fifty pots of wildfire** used to burn the Tower of the Hand despite Jaime's warning that wildfire is dangerous and that the spectacle makes her **sound like Aerys**. She experiences the fire as beautiful and cleansing, while Margaery calms Tommen by reframing the flames as dancing.

- [13 The Soiled Knight](chapters/13-the-soiled-knight.md): **Arys Oakheart** hides his white cloak while pursuing his secret relationship with **Arianne Martell**, repeatedly breaking the celibacy he considers central to Kingsguard identity. He remembers shame at obeying Joffrey's orders to strike Sansa, complicating his conception of honor. **Doran** says he intends to transfer Myrcella to the Water Gardens to protect her from politically useful assassination and asks Arys to keep her whereabouts from King's Landing; **Arianne** reinterprets the same move as imprisonment. Arys sees real guards and attendants protecting Myrcella and notes her unusually advanced **cyvasse** skills. Arianne's grievances have substantial roots: she says Oberyn and officials actually ruled while she hosted feasts, and she remembers seeing Doran's handwritten promise that **Quentyn would one day rule Dorne**. Her account that Quentyn secretly crossed the sea and that **Anders Yronwood and the Golden Company** are working to disinherit her remains **reported/inferred rather than verified by Arys**. She uses the analogy of **Criston Cole**, the Dance, female seniority, imprisoned young Sand Snakes, her relationship with Arys and a promise that a crowned Myrcella might free him to marry her. **Arys ultimately swears privately to defend Myrcella's claim**, and Arianne ends by asking him to obtain the child. Neither a coronation nor an escape has happened here.
- [14 Brienne](chapters/14-brienne.md): **Brienne** begins genuinely training **Podrick**, whose early guardians neglected his education and whose loyalty to Tyrion survived being abandoned. On the road to **Maidenpool**, a dispossessed farmer praises Randyll Tarly's suppression of outlaws, but soldiers at the town gate then extort the couple's eggs and try to seize the farmer's wife. Brienne intervenes at swordpoint; **Hyle Hunt** stops the guards while reviving the humiliation he caused her in Renly's camp. Tarly's public court mixes effective inquiry with savage punishments, and the chapter finally reveals the **wager among knights to seduce or violate Brienne**. Young Dickon exposed it; Randyll stopped it, then blamed Brienne herself for wearing mail among men. Tarly confirms **Lysa's death** and says a singer was blamed, causing Brienne to **discard her almost-correct Vale hypothesis** even though Sansa remains there under Petyr's protection. At the **Stinking Goose**, **Nimble Dick Crabb** says he sent a frightened fool seeking ship passage for three to a deserted smuggler landing at **the Whispers** on Crackclaw Point. He **never saw either supposed girl** and admits the landing has gone unused for thirty years. Brienne conditionally hires him despite knowing he tricked the original traveler. The report of **the Hound with one hundred outlaws** is secondhand and must not be assumed to identify Sandor personally.
- [15 Samwell](chapters/15-samwell.md): **Blackbird's** difficult passage toward Braavos exposes Sam's lifelong terror of the sea, rooted in **Randyll throwing him into Horn Hill's pond**; notably, **Hyle Hunt rescued Sam** in that incident. Sam hopes Gilly can live safely at Horn Hill and Aemon can rest at the Citadel, while **Dareon** sings beautifully but becomes increasingly callous, drinks heavily and claims his old rape conviction was unjust. **Aemon remembers traveling north aged thirty-five aboard the *Golden Dragon*, sent by Egg and escorted by Ser Duncan; Brynden Rivers (Bloodraven) was among the prisoners traveling with them and later became Lord Commander**. The vessel crosses dangerous waters around **Skagos**; its unicorn/cannibal traditions remain secondhand folklore, though a wrecked galley confirms serious navigational hazards. Storms imperil everyone, Aemon becomes dangerously frail and confused, and Watch sailors blame Gilly and her infant for misfortune. **Aemon explicitly identifies the child aboard as Dalla's son, not Gilly's**: Gilly's own baby remains at the Wall under the substituted identity. He infers Jon forced the exchange through threats and promises; this POV does not reproduce Jon's precise bargain. **Sam realizes Jon was trying to remove the supposed royal infant from Melisandre's sacrificial reach by transferring that risk to Gilly's son.** Aemon frames the choice as a lord commander's lesser evil, while Sam recognizes Gilly's suffering and his own indirect responsibility. The chapter ends before arrival in Braavos, with further storms approaching.
- [16 Jaime](chapters/16-jaime.md): Tywin's funeral procession departs while Kevan refuses to serve as Hand and prepares to rebuild Darry. Jaime recognizes Kevan's knowledge of a sensitive royal family secret and worries about Cersei's response, without evidence of a plot against Kevan. The Hound reports remain identity-uncertain. Tyrell forces head toward Storm's End while westermen await transport to Dragonstone. Bronn names Lollys's newborn boy Tyrion. Jaime distrusts Cersei's reliance on Taena, Qyburn, and inexperienced or factionally selected councillors. His memories of the Aerys court sharpen his doubts about blindly obeying authority. Jaime and Cersei quarrel. Later Jaime teaches Loras about the White Book and concludes with Criston Cole, a notable link to Arianne's argument in chapter 13.
- [17 Cersei](chapters/17-cersei.md): Cersei's bounty has led to **multiple innocent mistaken-identity deaths**. Qyburn prepares the purported **Gregor Clegane skull** for Doran, and **Balon Swann** is assigned an unrevealed additional task. **Harys Swyft is now Hand**, appointed largely to pressure Kevan through family ties; Qyburn, Aurane Waters and other loyalists take council seats. **Two thousand sparrows** are reported in the capital, while candidates for High Septon contest the vacancy. The **Lords Declarant** challenge Littlefinger in the Vale; the crown chooses non-intervention, notwithstanding Petyr's odd request for **Robert's old tapestries**. Aurane proposes **ten new dromonds**, financed by Cersei's completed decision to **suspend repayments to the Iron Bank and the Faith**; Pycelle warns of repercussions. Golden Company movements remain contradictory rumors (**Stannis** or **Volantis**), cross-checking Arianne's speculation. Cersei orders **Davos's execution** at White Harbor but does not witness the outcome, and explicitly knows the **Ramsay bride called Arya is a steward's child provided by Littlefinger**. Cersei approves Qyburn's plan to send apparent Night's Watch recruits to remove **Jon Snow**. She recruits **Osney Kettleblack** to compromise **Margaery** and then go north as part of the Jon plot; neither objective has occurred. She instructs **Taena** to plant the secret-admirer rumor, while dismissing dockside reports of **dragons**. All completed appointments and policy decisions are distinct from their uncertain consequences.
- [18 The Iron Captain](chapters/18-the-iron-captain.md): Victarion brings most of the Iron Fleet to Nagga's Cradle after leaving Ralf Kenning to command Moat Cailin.
