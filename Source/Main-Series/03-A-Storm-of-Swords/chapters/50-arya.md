## Sequential observations and causal mechanism

1. **Arya and Sandor approach within an hour of the Green Fork.** Their immediate transport is now **a muddy two-wheeled wagon drawn by two old draught horses**, carrying **four casks of salted pork and one of pickled pigs' feet**. **Stranger** follows tied behind, giving an enormous tactical clue that an 'ordinary farmer' actually possesses an exceptional warhorse.
2. **Sandor's disguise rests on class invisibility.** He wears cheap green roughspun, soot-grey hood and farmer's clothes **over concealed mail/leather**; Arya looks like a farm boy. The pair have abandoned the visual identifiers of the Hound, particularly his dog-faced helm. He does not transform his face; he hides it under the cowl and avoids eye contact.
3. **The wagon and stores were stolen at swordpoint.** A chance-met farmer gave up cart, horses, clothing and boots unwillingly, under explicit bodily threats. **Sandor's prospective delivery of Arya to family does not excuse his predation upon a civilian.**
4. **An outrider challenge reveals a minor heraldic lead.** The patrol consists of a knight and two squires, wearing a **black pitchfork on a gold bend/bar sinister against russet**; Arya does not know the device and therefore resists revealing her identity. Sandor later identifies the knight as **Ser Donnel Haigh**, once his tournament/mêlée opponent. She had imagined men bearing Stark, Umber or Glover arms and cannot safely assume the pitchfork patrol is friendly.
5. **Sandor uses a deliberate House Whent lie.** He says the salt pork is for the wedding and **Old Lady Whent** sent Stranger as a horse gift for Edmure. The patrol knight dismisses the story with contempt for an elderly woman's plan to reclaim Harrenhal; the exact status or consent of House Whent is not verified. **No Whent representative arranged this delivery in the text.**
6. **Arya notices the class-based blind spot.** Donnel scrutinizes Stranger closely but barely looks at the supposed farmer and child. Sandor says knights see the horse and don't see the peasant. This explains how an extremely recognizable knight can approach a major gathering undetected, and ties to the series' class epistemology.
7. **Sandor forcibly cuts Arya's hair.** A couple of days earlier he chopped it unevenly to disguise her as a boy. She worries that Robb or Catelyn may not recognize her, an extension of the identity and belonging fears present since Harrenhal.
8. **Arya remembers a terrible unrecallable dream.** On the night before, she experienced a frightening dream without accessible content and now feels increasing dread. **The sensation is narrated; no external magical source or precise foreknowledge is demonstrated.**
9. **She fears Roose Bolton more than anonymous northerners.** She remembers escaping Bolton's Harrenhal custody by slitting a guard's throat and wonders whether his men reported it or can identify her. Her distrust is grounded in experience rather than hindsight concerning a later massacre.
10. **The wedding feast is underway when they arrive.** They hear drums, horns and pipes before seeing the Twins. Arya notes clashing musical selections at the two castles. Sandor infers the wedding is over but feasting continues. This is **wedding-night timing**, not proof that the ceremony and bedding have both ended.
11. **The approaching camp is vast and vulnerable.** Three giant feast tents stand adjacent, while Robb's personal camp is on higher ground away from the walls. The Green Fork overflows banks; some camp tents are submerged. Wet banners become unreadable in darkness, making heraldic identification unreliable.
12. **The wagon hedge is a weak perimeter.** A line of carts protects the camp, but the sentries admit a cartload of food after questioning. The guard sergeant wears a **pale pink cloak with red teardrop-like spots**, and his men show the **Bolton flayed man**. Sandor is not identified by this more careful patrol, though the sergeant refuses access to the castle.
13. **A second cover story is only partly accepted.** The Bolton sergeant says the wedding feast is half over and **the castle is closed to ordinary deliveries**. He allows them to unload by the soldiers' feast tents and directs them to **Sedgekins**. **Clegane ignores the named destination**, showing his tactical goal is Robb personally, not legitimate provisioning.
14. **Smallwood's six-acorn pavilion is an important recognition cue.** Arya identifies **Lord Smallwood's tent**, remembering Lady Smallwood's hospitality at Acorn Hall; it proves the camps contain some of the people she knows by household symbols, though she sees no one there she can ask for assistance.
15. **Armed archers are already preparing bows at night.** Arya watches **four men stringing longbows** under a tree. She notices they are not her father's men; the narration gives no explicit sinister reason in this chapter, but at a wine-soaked wedding camp this is a **specific potential prepositioning cue**.
16. **The soldiers are deliberately being supplied drink.** Three feast tents are packed with northern soldiers and camp followers, with repeated toasts and abundant mead, ale and wine. Arya recognizes mountain and northern accents, Umber/Karstark identifiers, and earlier house emblems. **These are genuine Stark-aligned soldiers, not only a staged false camp.**
17. **Arya learns Robb has a new queen without recognizing the name.** She overhears a toast to **Robb and Queen Jeyne** but does not know who Jeyne is, an unambiguous **knowledge lag** relative to Catelyn and the reader.
18. **The soundscape matters across POVs.** Competing tunes, excessive drums, the swollen river and the immense crowd drown out normal auditory cues. The later **Catelyn wedding hall** and **Arya exterior** chapters need synchronization before one attributes a single song to every position at every moment.
19. **Sandor intends to reach Robb and Catelyn in the castle.** When Arya proposes stopping at the soldiers' tents, he refuses and says her **brother**, not Sedgekins, is the target. He still has an economic incentive to deliver her; this is **declared destination**, not an accomplished safe reunion.
20. **End state:** Arya and Sandor are inside the outer camp near the soldiers' feast tents but **have not reached the castle or found the Starks**. Many apparent details of impending military attack are visible to the reader but not interpreted as a plot by Arya in this chapter.

## Theories and tests

- **Why disguise as smallfolk?** Visible failure by Haigh supports a class-blindness explanation far stronger than any hypothesis of magical concealment.
- **How much can Arya infer?** She correctly mistrusts Roose due to Harrenhal but does not know the planned violence; neither she nor Sandor witnesses the inner Frey council.
- **Preparations for massacre?** Hidden food wagons, camp separation, drinks and archers can contribute, but the chapter does not yet show a Frey firing a shot. Compare the next two POV chapters for firm attribution.
- **An imminent family reunion:** Distance shrinks to a castle gate, but armed layers and gate control prevent immediate access. Do not summarize this chapter as Arya reaching her mother.
