## Narrative sequence and the politics of information

1. **INVITATION AND CONDITIONAL TRUST:** Sansa receives a seemingly gracious invitation to supper from **Margaery Tyrell**, the woman selected to replace her as Joffrey's bride. The invitation appears in **Margaery's own writing**, but Sansa fears Joffrey may be using it to stage another humiliation. She accepts because she cannot safely refuse the future queen. The scene occurs after the betrothal switch in [ACOK 65 Sansa](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/65-sansa.md), not after Joffrey and Margaery's marriage.
2. **TYRELL POPULARITY HAS A PUBLIC FACE:** Sansa has witnessed Margaery arriving through cheering King's Landing crowds with Joffrey, wearing green and **autumn flowers**, followed by a rose-carved gilded carriage carrying her mother and grandmother. Sansa contrasts this adoration with the same city's **riot against her** ([ACOK 41](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/41-tyrion.md)). Different public receptions need not reflect the women's personal virtue; food aid and alliance politics documented in [ASOS 4 Tyrion](04-tyrion.md) form a plausible causal background.
3. **DONTOS'S ESCAPE STILL PENDING:** Sansa remembers Dontos's promise to extract her **on the night of Joffrey's wedding**; the mysterious sponsor, ship and practical route remain unshown. She keeps **Sandor Clegane's stained white cloak in a cedar chest beneath her summer silks**, a direct **continuity check of the physical garment** from [ACOK 62 Sansa](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/62-sansa.md). She reflects that his fear was of fire, not all fighting.
4. **LORAS AS ESCORT, NOT CONFIRMED SUITOR:** **Ser Loras Tyrell** arrives as a Kingsguard escort, handsome and courteous. Sansa is entranced and remembers the **single red rose** he gave her at the Hand's tourney while other girls received white roses. He responds politely but **does not recall her or the specific rose**. This establishes the difference between Sansa's sincere memory of a uniquely meaningful romantic signal and Loras's more routine tourney courtesy.
5. **LORAS'S ROBAR CONFESSION:** When Sansa mentions **Ser Robar Royce**, Loras says plainly **he killed Robar at Storm's End** following Renly's death, with tension and sorrow rather than boasting. This is an important first-person confession about violence in the chaotic aftermath of Renly's shadow assassination; the circumstances and any personal responsibility of Robar for Renly's death **are not established here**. Loras's warmth toward Sansa sharply cools after this topic.
6. **GARLAN'S MARTIAL METHOD:** Sansa sees **Garlan Tyrell training simultaneously against three fighters**, sometimes four, because he believes real battle rarely offers honorable single combat. Loras tells her Garlan is **the better swordsman**, while Loras is better with a **lance**. This specific ability comparison is **a participating brother's assessment backed by observed training**, not a guarantee of either man's future battle outcomes.
7. **OLENNA'S GUARDS AND COURT GEOGRAPHY:** Two indistinguishably handsome, seven-foot guards **Erryk and Arryk**, whom Olenna calls **Left and Right**, guard the **Maidenvault** where Mace's party lodges. Their similarity and local protection serve as a practical security detail, not proof of hidden identities. The Tyrell entourage inhabits crowded Red Keep chambers near the royal sept, not an isolated Highgarden court.
8. **FAMILY AND GUEST STRUCTURE:** Sansa meets **Lady Alerie** (Mace's wife), cousins **Megga, Alla and Elinor**, Lady **Janna Fossoway**, Lady **Leonette** (Garlan's wife), **Septa Nysterica**, **Lady Graceford** (pregnant), child **Lady Bulwer**, **Meredyth Crane** and another **Merryweather noblewoman**. These are actual women surrounding Margaery and Olenna, important for a later **ladies-in-waiting and court-influence network**, although no unified secret plot is demonstrated for every attendee.
9. **OLENNA'S PRAGMATIC HISTORY:** Olenna says she **warned against Mace crowning Renly**, arguing Robert's sons and Renly's older brother outranked him. She dismisses the Tyrell descent from **Garth Greenhand** as widely shared, recognizes their **steward origin**, and notes rival **Florent** claimants' complaints about the title to Highgarden. These are a politically knowledgeable participant's **claims and opinions**, not an omniscient genealogical judgment of every house. Her candor about Mace's desire for a royal grandson is not proof she can unilaterally cancel his intended marriage.
10. **OLENNA'S SEEMINGLY TRIVIAL BRIEFING:** She says **Varys informed her that Sansa loves lemon cakes** and uses humor to keep the meal informal. This is directly revealing: Tyrell intelligence is already drawing on the **royal eunuch's information**, while Olenna is aware of or suspicious of **palace spying**. The lemon-cake knowledge can be ordinary surveillance, not necessarily evidence of an elaborate poison operation.
11. **THE STATED INTELLIGENCE PURPOSE:** Olenna abruptly asks Sansa for **the truth about Joffrey**, distinguishing comeliness and ceremonial courage from **kindness, intelligence, gentleness and the likelihood of abusing Margaery**. Sansa tries to protect herself with empty praise. Olenna invokes **Aerion Brightfire** as a historical example of an attractive but monstrous prince. The questions are not a generic social visit: they are direct **risk assessment of a forthcoming dynastic marriage**.
12. **COVER NOISE AGAINST EAVESDROPPING:** When Sansa is frightened that others or **Varys** might hear, Olenna tells her immense jester **Butterbumps** to perform an extended, **loud 'Bear and the Maiden Fair'** and orders him louder still while they speak. Olenna explicitly references the Red Keep's **walls with ears** and spiders in Highgarden. Thus the song is a **deliberate acoustic cover** for sensitive intelligence gathering, not incidental feast entertainment.
13. **SANSA'S DIRECT TESTIMONY:** Sansa eventually says **Joffrey is cruel and monstrous**, recounting **his lie about the butcher's boy, Lady's death, Ned's execution despite a promise of mercy, display of Ned's severed head, and Kingsguard beatings when displeased**. Most central actions have been independently narrated in AGOT/ACOK; her present testimony is **truthful about abuse**, though her claims remain socially perilous if relayed back to Joffrey. She also implicates **Cersei**.
14. **OLENNA AND MARGAERY'S REACTION:** Olenna and Margaery **exchange a look** and acknowledge Sansa's report. Yet Olenna tells her **the wedding will proceed**, because Mace remains determined that Margaery be queen. This is not proof that Olenna approves Joffrey's violence or that she has already decided to kill him. It confirms the family **knows credible damaging testimony** and does not cancel the marriage at this time.
15. **PROTECTION AS A PROPOSAL:** Margaery invites Sansa to **Highgarden**, describing flowers, singing, hawking and the Mander's pleasure boats, and says she may remain there. Sansa fears **Cersei will not permit departure**, but Olenna contends the Lannisters **cannot maintain Joffrey's throne without the Reach** and will have to accept Mace's request. This is the Tyrells' **negotiating assessment**, not yet a successful transfer of Sansa from royal custody.
16. **THE 'TRUE PURPOSE' DISCLOSED:** Olenna says the intention is **to marry Sansa to her grandson Willas Tyrell, heir to Highgarden**, after she comes south. Sansa mistakenly assumes **Loras**, revealing how her romantic interpretation of the escort and tourney flower has misled her. Olenna corrects her bluntly: **Kingsguard Loras cannot marry**, an important institutional continuity point linking Tyrion's similar argument that **Jaime legally renounced inheritance** ([ASOS 4](04-tyrion.md)).
17. **WILLAS AS A DISTINCT CANDIDATE:** Margaery and Olenna describe **Willas as older than Sansa**, amiable, a reader and star-drawer who has **never been knighted** due to an injured leg from an early **tourney horse fall**. Olenna blames **Oberyn Martell** and a maester for his disability. **The initial injury and causal blame are family statements**, not a firsthand account from Willas or Oberyn, and should be tested before asserting malicious sabotage.
18. **DYNASTIC MOTIVE, NOT JUST RESCUE:** A marriage between Sansa, who is widely believed to be one of the only surviving **Stark heirs**, and **Willas, Highgarden's heir**, could give House Tyrell future leverage on **Winterfell and the North**. This is a strong **political inference from marriage and inheritance**, **not an explicit admission by Olenna or Margaery of a plan to seize the North**. Their warmth may be genuine, instrumental, or both.
19. **ENDING BOUNDARY:** Sansa indicates **willingness to marry Willas** and visit Highgarden after an initial mistaken enthusiasm for Loras, but the chapter shows **no Cersei consent, betrothal deed, journey, meeting with Willas or marriage**. Sansa's captivity, Dontos's alternative escape plan, and the first-hand warnings about Joffrey remain unresolved.

## Theory testing and cross-book handoffs

- **Olenna's knowledge and future Joffrey danger:** She has directly solicited alarming testimony, arranged privacy, and refused to cancel the match. These facts give her **motive for protective action**, not an observed murder plan; avoid retroactively labeling the entire dinner an assassination conference.
- **The dark-stone hairnet and competing exits:** Dontos's gift remains in Sansa's possession from `ACOK 65`; this chapter has **no handling or testing of the stones**. A Tyrell marriage proposal and a secret Dontos wedding-night extraction now compete for her future.
- **Willas and the North:** A warm invitation can simultaneously offer relief and acquire future dynastic advantage. Determine **who else knows Sansa remains heir-eligible, whether Tywin sees the risk, and whether the marriage ever becomes formally negotiated**.
- **Loras/Robar:** Direct testimony that Loras killed Robar is unexpectedly valuable for reconstructing post-Renly deaths and mistaken blame for the shadow murder. It does **not** make Robar a killer of Renly.
- **Court counterintelligence:** Olenna's performative deafness and deliberate deafening song suggest a sophisticated political actor capable of cultivating secrets even in monitored environments, but the full reach of Varys's surveillance remains unknown.
