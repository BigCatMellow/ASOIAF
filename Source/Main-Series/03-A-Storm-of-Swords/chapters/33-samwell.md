## Causal and chronological close reading

1. **Survivors reach Craster's Keep in catastrophic condition.** Sam tends wounded ranger **Bannen** as **Gilly gives birth upstairs**. Bannen repeatedly complains of cold, cannot swallow broth, and ultimately dies. A mangled foot was amputated **nine days earlier** by Kedge Whiteye after infection. Without a maester and with medical stores lost on the Fist, care is inadequate. Other injured men include feverish **Fornio** and **Ser Byam**, whose shoulder seeps pus.
2. **The Watch is starving while their host keeps limited provisions.** Craster gives shelter, fire, some hot wine and thin onion broth, but appears to reserve better meat for his household. Men suspect a hidden larder of hams, oats and preserves. **Only some private food is seen; the dimensions of any hidden cache are not verified.**
3. **Watch losses have a specific numerical trajectory.** Approximately **sixty** men fought free from the Fist, **forty-four** reached Craster's house, **three** subsequently died, leaving **forty-one living before Bannen dies**, of whom **nine** are too injured to fight. After his death, the exact number still alive requires accounting for later mutiny killings. **Do not confuse the 300 departing Fist brothers with 300 current survivors.**
4. **Host and guest status frustrates protection.** Sam recalls Craster beating a young wife while black brothers stood aside, including Ronnel Harclay's maxim that a host rules his own roof. Sam believes the Watch vows should cover women beyond the Wall as much as Westerosi women. His fears about Gilly's son reflect previous warnings ([ACOK 23 Jon](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/23-jon.md)).
5. **Gilly's child is confirmed male on page.** Sam hears the birth, hopes for a daughter, then **Craster announces that he has a son**. Craster refuses Sam's tentative suggestion that the Watch might take the infant and threatens him. This is a living infant at birth, not proof of his ultimate fate or a witnessed transfer to the Others.
6. **Craster's supernatural protection is asserted, not demonstrated.** There have been no wight or Other attacks during the stay. Craster boasts that his religious practices ensure safety, and says he formerly told **Mance Rayder** that the 'white cold' requires piety, not fires and swords. An ordinary alternative is that the thaw or other circumstances stalled pursuit; Sam does not see Craster bargaining with an Other here.
7. **The physical defenses are poor.** Earthen ramparts and firepits surround the muddy hall. Some horses too weak to travel are slaughtered, men gather fuel for nighttime fires, and **only 22 mounts** remain, of which Ronnel Harclay doubts half can reach the Wall. The rain and thaw are a fragile reprieve.
8. **'Slayer' divides Sam's comrades.** Archers including Sweet Donnel and Alan of Rosby mock him, while **Grenn** argues the title is literally deserved and that courage involves acting while afraid. This captures competing witnesses to Sam's deed rather than allowing later reputational legend to erase his private self-doubt.
9. **Ulmer's prior outlaw career preserves historical names.** He boasts that as a **Kingswood Brotherhood** archer he wounded **Ser Gerold Hightower, the White Bull**, and kissed a Dornish princess; he names Fletcher Dick, Simon Toyne, Smiling Knight, Wenda the White Fawn and other members. **The precision of names is valuable; the boast is self-report.**
10. **Obsidian weapons are inventoried precisely.** From Jon's buried cache at the Fist, the survivors now possess **Mormont's dagger**, the dagger Sam entrusted to Grenn, **nineteen obsidian-headed arrows**, and **one hardwood spear with an obsidian point**. Ulmer receives **four arrows**; five others get **three each**: Muttering Bill, Garth Greyfeather, Ronnel Harclay, Sweet Donnel Hill and Alan of Rosby. The rest of Jon's cache is still at/near the Fist, not magically replenished.
11. **Sam insists on an essential threat distinction.** His dagger visibly destroyed an **Other** in [ASOS 18 Samwell](18-samwell.md). He expressly says the Watch **does not know** that dragonglass will similarly destroy **wights**. Grenn assumes it may; hundreds of prior fire arrows slowed but did not end the Fist attack. **Do not collapse Others and animated corpses into a single vulnerability.**
12. **Mormont realizes the Watch forgot its purpose.** He says a seven-hundred-foot Wall makes little sense solely against wildling raiders and identifies the real enemy as something other than people; he wants every brother armed with obsidian once supplies can be obtained. **He is reconsidering institutional history**, not showing proof of its founders' every intention.
13. **Mormont considers the children of the forest as a source and dismisses them.** Sam knows they once used dragonglass; Mormont asserts they are extinct through First Men/Andal conquest. This is **character belief**; his claim of total extinction is not omniscient proof.
14. **The raven repeats meaningful-sounding words.** Mormont's bird cries variants of snow, son, corn, ended, now and wish at apparently relevant moments. These are directly observed **vocalizations**, not independent proof of prophecy, warging or informed comprehension.
15. **Bannen's death and burning embody material scarcity.** After Giant covers the body, the Watch burns him to prevent an undead return and divides his belongings among survivors. Mormont honors him as from **White Harbor**; Sam involuntarily finds the smell of burnt flesh like roasted meat and vomits. The body is consumed by fire, not witnessed as a wight.
16. **The men plan to leave at first light.** Craster grudgingly offers horsemeat, beer and bread as a farewell feed; he controls a chair of his own while the watchmen crowd benches and the women do all serving. Just **two loaves** trigger anger among starving men. A sword/axe gift matters: Craster holds the **steel axe Mormont presented** as guest gift.
17. **Clubfoot Karl openly challenges Mormont's authority.** Karl, Dirk and multiple Garths accuse Craster of withholding food. The raven chants corn; Mormont orders silence. Karl seems about to subside when Craster himself escalates by wielding the gifted axe and evicting complainants.
18. **Craster is killed by a named mutineer.** During the escalation **Dirk** seizes Craster's hair and cuts his throat, dropping him across the already injured Ser Byam. Mormont denounces murder by a guest as a profound violation of hearth law. **Craster's crimes do not make the actual killing an authorized trial or legitimate Watch order.**
19. **Mormont is stabbed by Ollo Lophand.** **Garth of Greenaway** blocks Mormont and **Ollo Lophand** restrains him; when the Lord Commander reaches for a dagger, Ollo plunges a knife into his belly. The Watch command is destroyed **by surviving brothers**, not by an Other, wildling army or Lannister plot.
20. **The mutiny becomes sexual and factional predation.** Afterward Sam sees some rebels eating, **Ollo sexually assaults a crying woman**, and reports of men climbing into the loft. He remembers **Garth of Greenaway killing Garth of Oldtown**, and **Rolley of Sisterton** dying after falling from the ladder. The killings' full order and remaining headcount are not securely reconstructed from Sam's shock.
21. **Grenn and others flee.** Grenn, Giant and Dolorous Edd try to rouse Sam and escape, but he remains frozen with the dying Mormont. Their **departure is witnessed in Sam's recollection**, not proof any escaped man has reached Castle Black.
22. **Mormont gives a two-part dying charge.** He orders Sam to reach the Wall and tell of **the Fist, the wildlings, dragonglass and the mutiny**, then asks that his estranged son **Jorah Mormont** take the black and be forgiven. The **request** is verified; Jorah's knowledge, willingness and actual reconciliation are entirely open.
23. **Jeor Mormont dies in Sam's lap.** Three women arrive to urge Sam to leave. Sam sees Mormont's eyes no longer moving and closes them. This is a directly narrated death, resolving the uncertainty in [ASOS 32 Tyrion](32-tyrion.md)'s earlier Marsh correspondence that the Lord Commander might be dead.
24. **Gilly's elderly allies support her escape.** **Ferny** and another older woman (identified by the latter as Gilly's mother, with **Nella** separately named as the one who predicted a son) tell Sam horses remain and the murderous crows will return. They urge him to take **Gilly and her newborn**. Their plan is **not yet an executed journey** by chapter end.
25. **A striking causal claim about Craster's sons closes the chapter.** The women say Gilly's son's **brothers**, Craster's earlier sons, will come with the 'white cold' to claim him. This is **CHARACTER CLAIM** from people who know Craster's household; no Other appears in this final scene. A plausible reading is supernatural kinship or transfer, but its biology, identities and timing are **UNKNOWN**.
26. **End position.** Craster and Jeor are dead. The Watch has splintered at the Keep; Sam has an urgent instruction to flee and a promise to Gilly, but no demonstrated safe route, completed escape or delivery of intelligence.

## Theory and disconfirmation tests

- **Craster's sons as Others:** Strongly suggested by the women and repeated 'white cold' terminology; no son is followed from offering to transformation. Test later first-person White Walker evidence, sex/age, and religious language.
- **Why no attack at the Keep?** Possible offerings, weather, strategic delay, or protection by walls/fires. Craster's confidence is not an objective pact certificate.
- **Raven significance:** Contextual repetition might be ordinary trained mimicry; independent evidence of a controller or foresight would be needed for stronger attribution.
- **Sam's 'cowardice' versus agency:** He has already killed an Other and now faces a fresh choice to save Gilly. His bodily terror and consequential agency can coexist.
- **The Watch's collapse:** Watchmen killed their host and commander under scarcity, exposing political and ethical fragility. The Watch as an institution is not identical with these mutineers.
