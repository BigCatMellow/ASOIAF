## Chronological scene reconstruction and evidentiary boundaries

1. **The royal wedding is validly solemnized before the feast.** In Baelor's Great Sept, Joffrey and **Margaery Tyrell** exchange vows, blessings and cloaks before the High Septon. Mace removes his daughter's green Tyrell maiden cloak; Tommen hands Joffrey the inherited Lannister bride cloak; **Joffrey and Margaery are declared married**. This is a completed ceremonial union; **no consummation occurs before his death in this chapter**.
2. **Tywin has supplied the Faith with an extravagant crown.** The High Septon's crystal/spun-gold crown doubles the height of the mob-destroyed former crown. Glittering rays and excessive weight are noticed by Tyrion. The opulence contrasts the earlier sacking of religious authority, not proof a supernatural manifestation occurs.
3. **Tyrion privately identifies Joffrey as his leading catspaw suspect.** Remembering Joffrey's remark in [ASOS 59 Sansa](59-sansa.md) that he knew Valyrian steel, Tyrion constructs a scenario: **Joffrey hired a disposable camp follower** to murder unconscious Bran and **took a plain Valyrian dagger from Robert's baggage**, ignorant of its connection to Littlefinger. **CRUCIAL: this is Tyrion's unverified inference, not a witnessed confession, recovered transaction or established fact of Joffrey's guilt.**
4. **Tyrion mentally excludes Jaime and Cersei.** He reasons Jaime would act directly and Cersei would avoid a traceable knife; his memories include Joffrey's cruel talk at Winterfell about killing wolves. These are **probabilistic behavioral arguments**, not disproof of other perpetrators or proof of the specific Joffrey procurement route.
5. **The bride is initially popular with the city.** Tyrion sees King’s Landing spectators cheering Margaery, partly because Tyrell food is helping a starved capital. He points out to himself **Mace previously blockaded the roseroad**, contributing to that hunger. Public acclaim does not prove unanimous affection for Joffrey.
6. **The procession and seating are detailed.** **Loras and Meryn** escort; **Tommen** scatters roses; **Cersei with Mace**, **Alerie with Tywin**, **Olenna with Kevan** and **Garlan with Leonette** follow. At the feast Tyrion and Sansa are placed **well to the right of the royal dais beside Garlan and Leonette**, not in its immediate center.
7. **Tyrion attempts another overture to Sansa.** He proposes a future trip to **Casterly Rock, Golden Gallery, Lion's Mouth and Hall of Heroes**, even later mentioning Braavos and Oldtown. Sansa responds with formal obedience but no enthusiasm. Her grief and lack of choice account for reticence; **Tyrion's longing for gratitude is not a debt she owes**.
8. **The broader Lannister war effort is advancing but not finished.** Tyrion reports **Gregor has seized Harrenhal and ruby ford**, **Seagard surrendered to Black Walder Frey**, **Randyll Tarly holds Maidenpool and Duskendale**, **Daven Lannister and Forley Prester** march from the Golden Tooth toward Riverrun, **Ryman Frey brings 2,000 spears**, and **Paxter Redwyne's fleet plans to sail around Dorne to menace Stannis**. Distinguish completed captures/reported surrenders from the **future Redwyne fleet operation**.
9. **Lancel is gravely changed by his wounds.** At the feast Sansa warmly praises the weak, white-haired Lancel, needing **Kevan to support him**, and speaks politely to numerous guests. Her diplomatic competence is directly observed by Tyrion despite emotional withdrawal at home.
10. **Shae styles Sansa's wedding hair with a jewel-studded net.** Tyrion sees **a silver hairnet with dark purple gemstones**, and Shae begs to serve at the feast to see the pigeons fly from the pie. **Tyrion refuses access because the servers are chosen and the hall is crowded**. This is a separate, exact prop/custody event relevant to the later crime.
11. **Olenna Tyrell physically touches Sansa's hairnet before entry.** The Queen of Thorns reaches up to **straighten loose strands and rearrange the net**, and later touches the hair again while speaking of sorrow and returning to Highgarden. **This is direct witnessed opportunity to alter, remove or inspect something**. The chapter does **not expressly say she removed a gemstone, handled poison or conspired with any accomplice**.
12. **Margaery's redressing and public iconography assert Tyrell rule.** She arrives with Joffrey on a white charger, wearing revealing pale green, a small gold crown and loose hair. The throne room is hung in **Baratheon, Lannister and Tyrell** colors, emphasizing multiple dynastic interests in the new monarchy.
13. **The huge wedding chalice is actively used by the couple.** Joffrey's cupbearer pours **a whole flagon of dark Arbor red** into the tall, two-handled, seven-faced golden chalice gifted by **Mace Tyrell**. **Joffrey and Margaery share it early**, followed by many rounds of feasting and drinking. **Sharing wine at one point does not establish identical exposure at a later point**.
14. **The extravagant seventy-seven courses contrast urban hardship.** Tyrion sees an endless succession of dishes, sweets and imported delicacies and thinks of **hungry children who would kill for a radish**. This is a powerful political economy clue; no actual famine relief is carried out inside the banquet scene.
15. **Royal entertainment distorts political history.** **Hamish** sings a version of Renly's return at Blackwater as a ghost serving the king, contrary to Tyrion's knowledge; **Galyeon of Cuy** later performs a seventy-seven-verse account crediting Joffrey and Cersei instead of Tyrion's battlefield measures. **Garlan Tyrell** privately confirms Tyrion's chain and wildfire contributions and the tactical role of his wildlings against Stannis's scouts. The songs are **court-sponsored narratives**, not military history.
16. **Several performers and tracks are named.** **Collio Quaynis** sings of the Dance of Dragons and the Doom of Valyria; 'Rains of Castamere' recurs repeatedly; pyromancers display staged beasts of living flame. This is **entertainment spectacle**, not evidence the wedding fire creatures were independent dragons or creatures.
17. **Even this guarded banquet suffers unrelated violence.** A **Rowan knight stabs a Dornishman** while guests drink; gold cloaks arrest one and send the other for treatment by **Maester Ballabar**. Court faction tension continues beneath the ceremony.
18. **Joffrey's two dwarf jousters mock the War of Five Kings and Tyrion.** Two performers in wooden armor ride **a dog and a sow**, representing **stag and wolf**. Tyrion initially resolves to reward them but punish the organizer; **the sponsor is not named directly in this entertainment sequence**.
19. **Joffrey's humiliation of Tyrion escalates.** He challenges Tyrion to ride the pig; Tyrion retorts that Joffrey should ride the dog, winning laughter and enraging him. Joffrey then **empties the chalice of red wine over Tyrion's head**, orders him to retrieve it, **refill it three-quarters full and kneel as cupbearer**. **Garlan quietly objects** and physically helps Tyrion stand.
20. **Joffrey drinks from the refilled cup during the humiliation.** After Tyrion fills and offers the golden cup, **Joffrey drinks deeply and sets it on the table**. This gives the cup a specific timeline and establishes Tyrion visibly handled it under orders; **handling is not evidence he introduced poison**.
21. **The great wedding pie enters and Ser Ilyn's sword appears.** Joffrey borrows **Ilyn Payne's six-foot silver-bright greatsword**, with runes and a **dragonglass skull on its pommel**, to cut the **live-dove wedding pie** with Margaery. The blade **resembles Ice in length and width but is a much brighter shade**. **Sansa recognizes her father's sword is missing** and asks what happened to it; Tyrion regrets not returning it to Robb. **This supports the split/reworking of Ice into new swords, but detailed forging is not narrated here**.
22. **Tyrion and Sansa attempt to leave before Joffrey collapses.** Their reason is Sansa's pallor and Tyrion's drenched clothing; Joffrey **refuses permission** and orders his forced cupbearer back. Tyrion is therefore visibly near the king at the fatal moment, while **Sansa's precise route after the commotion begins becomes UNKNOWN**.
23. **Joffrey consumes pie, then wine, just before acute symptoms.** After Joffrey again takes the cup from Tyrion and drinks, he **grabs some of Tyrion's hot pigeon pie**, eats a mouthful or more, calls it dry, **drinks wine again**, and develops rapidly worsening **coughing, difficulty breathing and inability to swallow**. The immediate oral sequence is witnessed; **it does not by itself discriminate poison in wine versus pie, food obstruction or another agent**.
24. **The symptoms are catastrophic and fatal.** Joffrey's face reddens then darkens, he coughs up wine, claws his throat, produces a thin obstructed wheeze, loses airflow and dies as Cersei holds him. **His death is unambiguous**, but **the precise cause is not medically or chemically identified in this chapter**.
25. **Bystanders give conflicting interpretations.** **Margaery** first calls it choking; **Alerie** says he choked on pie and assures her daughter he caused it himself. **Cersei insists he was poisoned**, accusing Tyrion and Sansa. These are **immediate family and witness claims**, not authenticated toxicology. The wedding's political stakes make the difference consequential.
26. **The cup's final handling becomes an evidentiary complication.** During the chaos Joffrey drops the chalice and wine spills; **Tyrion retrieves it**, notices perhaps **half an inch of deep-purple wine remaining**, and **pours the residue onto the floor**. This destroys or contaminates potential material evidence regardless of motive. The chapter does **not state he had previously tampered with the wine**.
27. **Sansa disappears from Tyrion's sight during the collapse.** He turns to look for her but cannot locate her among the guests. **Her absence is observed**, but the **means, helpers and destination of any departure** require a later Sansa POV; this scene alone does not prove flight or homicide participation.
28. **Cersei's command is unequivocal.** After Joffrey's death the High Septon begins prayers, Cersei orders Kingsguard to **arrest Tyrion and his wife as the killers**, and **Loras** asks for clarification. **A formal accusation and arrest order are completed**; adjudication, confessions, physical poisons and the perpetrators' identities remain unresolved.
29. **The character's susceptibility to confirmation bias matters.** Tyrion earlier concluded Joffrey arranged Bran's assassination and now despises his nephew; his calm as the boy dies may look suspicious to others but proves neither foreknowledge nor murder. Cersei's ready identification of Tyrion reflects established antagonism, not an evidentiary chain.
30. **End state:** **Joffrey is dead on his wedding day**, Margaery widowed before any consummation is shown, Tyrion publicly implicated by Cersei after forced handling of the royal cup, and Sansa missing from Tyrion's view. A poison theory is plausible and loudly asserted, but **no compound, poisoner or complete delivery chain is demonstrated by this POV alone**.

## Serious competing interpretations and theory tests

- **Poisoned wine versus pie or choking:** Court witnesses attribute the event differently. Establish the wine-pour/refill/pie chronology and who could access each container; do not label it one substance merely from purple gemstones or rapid respiratory distress.
- **Olenna and hairnet:** A very pointed opportunity exists, but **the chapter does not narrate a missing stone**. Follow the earlier source of the hairnet, any later examination and the identities of servers/handlers.
- **Joffrey and Bran's catspaw:** Tyrion's hypothesis is an important reconstruction but **not external proof**; it must survive alternatives for dagger ownership and testimony.
- **Tyrion's supposed guilt:** Forced cupbearing and residual wine disposal create suspicion, but no scene shows him introducing a toxin. He has threatened Joffrey, and that can prejudice accusers.
- **Sansa's disappearance:** She is missing at a moment of great disorder, but neither her fate nor knowledge is revealed here. Do not treat her absence as proof of complicity.
- **A marriage succession reset:** Joffrey's death changes the monarchy immediately; the consequences for **Margaery, Tommen, the Faith and Lannister alliances** have not yet unfolded at this reading boundary.
