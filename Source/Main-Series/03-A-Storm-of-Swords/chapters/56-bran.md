## Narrative, ancient history, physical mechanisms and ethical consequences

1. **Bran knows Robb and Grey Wind may have suffered disaster.** He fears acknowledging a **dream shared in some sense with Summer** in which Robb and Grey Wind were lost, though he **does not recount its full contents to the Reeds**. The reader already witnessed the Red Wedding; this is **Bran's reaction to an incompletely disclosed dream**, not proof he observed Robb's exact killing through a wolf.
2. **The party reaches the Nightfort but its normal gate is unusable.** **Bran, Meera, Jojen, Hodor and Summer** find a vast ruin. The **portcullis is lowered, the lifting chains removed**, and the ordinary tunnel blocked with stones and ice. The physical blockage directly rebuts the simplest version of Jojen's belief that an abandoned Watch gate would be open.
3. **The Nightfort is steeped in unverified but specific history.** Bran recalls Old Nan's stories about the **Night's King, Rat Cook, seventy-nine sentinels, Danny Flint, King Sherrit, the apprentice boys, blind Symeon Star-Eyes and Mad Axe**. **Narrating a legend inside the book does not verify every monstrous element**; retain specific locations/actors for later corroboration.
4. **Jojen insists his green dream designated this place.** Despite sealed access he refuses to choose another castle. **His conviction is direct testimony**; whether his dream revealed a method of passage or only an eventual destination needs close checking.
5. **The Queenscrown confrontation is clarified by Bran.** In **Summer's skin** he recognized a man he thought was **Jon**, mauled **three or more** wildlings, and was shot in the **hind leg** by an arrow; intense pain forced Bran from the wolf. This first-person account substantiates the identification of the grey direwolf in [ASOS 41 Jon](41-jon.md), and the psychological cost to Bran is direct.
6. **Summer survives with an actual wound.** After the raiders move away, the wolf returns limping; **Meera pulls out the broken arrow** and treats the wound with plant juices. The visible limp slowly improves; **Bran's pain when attempting to skinchange before Summer's return** suggests animal injury affects shared consciousness.
7. **Thenns nearly locate Queenscrown's underwater causeway.** Three free folk wade partway, but the path bends and **two almost drown**; Styr shouts in a tongue the Reeds do not understand. This independently confirms the physical defense previously mapped by [ASOS 40 Bran](40-bran.md).
8. **Other abandoned castle gates are presumed sealed.** Bran recites **Deep Lake, Queensgate and Icemark** as nearby alternatives, asserts only **Castle Black, Eastwatch and Shadow Tower** have working Watch passages. His knowledge concerns **normal gates**, later rendered importantly incomplete by the Black Gate.
9. **Meera climbs surviving ice-hewn Nightfort steps.** She climbs carefully over ancient, rounded steps cut into the Wall itself, becoming nearly impassable toward the crest. The chapter says **Nightfort is unique in retaining directly carved ice steps**; newer castles use wood, stone or embankments. The Wall melts/refreezes at the surface while its core stays rigid.
10. **The top of the Wall does not solve passage for the group.** Meera surveys the **haunted forest, snow patches, lake and a circling eagle**, but the northern side is a smooth near-vertical drop. She might descend with specialized gear, not **Bran, Hodor and Jojen**. The journey still needs another physical route.
11. **Alysanne built a replacement castle with her own resources.** Bran remembers the **Nightfort** was once the Watch's first and largest castle, already expensive and mostly unoccupied; **Good Queen Alysanne's jewels and Old King Jaehaerys's support** financed **Deep Lake** about seven miles east. These details are **learned history, not direct proof of the queen's every act**.
12. **The ruin retains a precise footprint.** Bran and Jojen see abandoned **bell tower, rookery, library, wine casks, a large dungeon capable of holding ~500, bathhouse, armory, forge, collapsed hall and thick rat-inhabited passages**. The apparently supernatural noises remain explainable through wind, rats and ruined construction **until an actual arrival is observed**.
13. **The seventy-nine sentinels legend includes loving kinship and punitive oathkeeping.** Supposed deserters were entombed alive facing north in the Wall; a lord **Ryswell** returned his son and later joined the Watch to stand with him. This is **Bran's telling of Old Nan's account**, not independently confirmed archaeology.
14. **The Night's King tale contains unusually specific allegations.** He is said to be **the Watch's thirteenth commander**, loved a cold pale blue-eyed woman who became his queen, ruled 13 years using enchantment, **sacrificed to the Others**, and was defeated by a **Stark of Winterfell allied with Joramun**. Competing house identities include **Bolton, Skagosi Magnar, Umber, Flint, Norrey, Woodfoot or Stark**. **Old Nan insists Stark, perhaps Brandon, brother of the victorious Stark**, but historical erasure and conflicting traditions prevent treating one genealogy as a proven fact.
15. **The group's kitchen shelter has a faceless living weirwood.** Its pale roots/trunk have broken through the stone floor beside a vast **12-foot-wide well** with spiral descending stairs; the tree leans through a hole in the domed roof. Its lack of carved face is explicitly noted. The presence of tree and well matters for subsequent magic, but **no tree speech is yet proven**.
16. **The Rat Cook story highlights hospitality, not simply cannibalism.** Bran recalls a cook serving an Andal king his own son in a pie; **Old Nan says the gods punished him for killing a guest beneath his roof**, not for murder alone. **As placed immediately after the Red Wedding, this is a powerful thematic and possible future-consequence frame**, but neither the rat transformation nor supernatural punishment of the Freys is witnessed here.
17. **The well's acoustics generate a false monster identification.** Hodor tosses stone down the shaft; eventually water responds with an unusually low sound. Later approaching steps and whimpers seem gigantic and demonic because the shaft amplifies echoes. These noises are ultimately **Sam and Gilly**, not the Rat Cook, Mad Axe or an unobserved 'thing in the night'.
18. **Bran deliberately skinchanges Hodor again.** Believing Meera endangered, he reaches for Hodor rather than distant Summer; entry feels **ill-fitting**, and he perceives **Hodor frightened and pushing him away**. Bran nonetheless takes control, stands in **Hodor's powerful legs**, takes a step and lifts his sword. This is **first-person evidence of resisted, nonconsensual human control**, more extensive than the half-heartbeat in [ASOS 40 Bran](40-bran.md).
19. **The episode shows limits and bodily consequences.** Bran tastes **vomit in Hodor's throat**, experiences the power of walking, then flees from Hodor when a screaming figure rises from the well, leaving Hodor distressed and shouting. The chapter shows **Hodor's internal resistance**, not an automatically compliant empty vessel.
20. **Meera catches Samwell, not a monster.** **Sam** emerges carrying news from **Craster's Keep**; Meera nets him and lightly **prods his mail, not skin**, before the party recognizes Night's Watch clothing. **Gilly and her newborn** follow and their cries account for the eerie sound. This is a directly witnessed **Bran–Sam encounter**.
21. **The rescuer is explicitly named Coldhands by Gilly and Sam.** Gilly says **Coldhands** was not his real name but their designation based on his cold, black hands. He rescued them with **ravens and a giant elk** and deliberately sent Sam **to find someone specific** at the Nightfort—not Sam himself. This resolves the anonymous rider's nickname from [ASOS 46 Samwell](46-samwell.md), but **not his true identity, origin or allegiances**.
22. **Coldhands anticipates Bran's company.** Sam says Coldhands promised **people would be present in the Nightfort** and that he was looking for 'the one', whom Jojen identifies as **Bran**. This is an apparently accurate prediction or intelligence from a source **unseen by Bran**, not proof who instructed Coldhands.
23. **The Wall has a functional hidden Black Gate.** Sam says there is a passage below the Nightfort **older than the contemporary gate arrangement**, known as **the Black Gate**, which **only a sworn Night's Watch brother** can open by vows. This is **verified in action later in the chapter**; Jojen's dream about a passage is now materially corroborated.
24. **The Wall blocks Coldhands for reasons he attributes to spells.** Sam explains Coldhands **cannot cross** because powerful ancient magic is woven into the Wall. This is **Sam's report of Coldhands's explanation**, supported by his observed refusal to accompany them through, but the precise spell and what classes of beings it excludes are **not directly tested**.
25. **Coldhands is not established as an Other or a normal Watch survivor.** Sam describes him as pale as a wight, wearing black, capable of speech and planning, and with hands colder than living skin; wights have blue eyes and do not converse like him. **No identification with Benjen Stark or a named ancient person is present on page**.
26. **Sam learns Bran is alive and swears secrecy.** Sam recognizes him as Jon's supposedly dead brother. **Jojen initially says the boy is dead**, then Bran directly pleads not to tell anyone. Sam and Gilly agree. This creates a **specific future intelligence channel** between Bran and Jon that could be deliberately concealed.
27. **The two parties exchange Jon's situation and wolf evidence.** Bran reports Summer saw Jon flee wildlings on horseback and likely return to Castle Black. Sam had believed Jon missing on Qhorin's ranging. Summer licks Sam's hand after Sam says he knew **Ghost**, strengthening the reliability of their encounter and direct sensory continuity.
28. **Bran elects to cross north; Gilly remains behind temporarily.** Bran chooses the three-eyed-crow search over joining Jon at Castle Black; Meera and Jojen accompany him, Summer proceeds willingly, and Hodor carries Bran. **Sam promises to come back for Gilly and her infant**, who wait by the kitchen fire. No reunion of Jon and Bran occurs.
29. **The Black Gate is physically extraordinary and oath-responsive.** Far down the spiral well is a **white, softly glowing weirwood door with an elderly face**, despite its misleading name. Its **white blind eyes open**; it asks Sam who he is; **Sam recites the Night's Watch oath** and is admitted as the mouth opens into a passage. The doorkeeper is not conjecture—it **speaks and opens directly on page**.
30. **A drop resembling a tear falls upon Bran.** As Hodor passes the door, its lip touches Bran's head and deposits **warm, salty water**. The description invites a weirwood **tear/blood metaphor**, but does **not establish the door is literally alive, contains a specific person's mind, or recognizes Bran by lineage**.
31. **End state:** **Bran, Meera, Jojen, Hodor and Summer are passing north under the Wall**, guided through the Black Gate by Sam; Gilly and infant wait above, and Coldhands is said to be waiting on the far side. The current text does **not yet show their arrival at the three-eyed crow**.

## Major competing interpretations

- **Wall as magical filter:** The operating, vow-responsive gate plus Coldhands's inability to pass strongly support a real magical boundary. Still determine whether it bars **wights, Others, Coldhands's particular state or every supernatural being**, rather than overgeneralizing.
- **Who is Coldhands?** He communicates, rides an elk and manages ravens, while appearing deathlike. An undead guardian or skinchanger agent is plausible, but **Benjen identity is not demonstrated**.
- **Old Nan accuracy:** The Black Gate validates one ancient Watch mystery, and her local warnings include accurate undercurrents, yet does **not automatically prove Night's King was Stark** or every Nightfort horror was literal.
- **Bran and Hodor:** Safety motive does not remove the fact Hodor actively resists being possessed. Ongoing skinchanging ethics need to track this from the first controlled entry.
- **Rat Cook's hospitality rule:** Its placement after the Red Wedding makes a future retribution hypothesis strong as a narrative test, but the book has not yet shown any supernatural punishment for the Freys.
- **Robb's will and alive Stark children:** Bran's survival, chosen secrecy and uncompleted journey create a major future conflict for people relying on false death reports.
