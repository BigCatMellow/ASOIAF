## Chronological developments and causal record

1. **Sansa remains frightened in an unconsummated marriage.** Tyrion's joke about bad peas and mutton causes her repeated apologies; he privately realizes she assumes any displeasure is hers to fix. She asks permission for solitary evening devotions in the **godswood**, also visiting the sept regularly. Tyrion offers to accompany her but she refuses. **Her prayers' contents are not narrated in this POV**.
2. **Dornish–Reach conflict has already killed and injured people.** After Oberyn's arrival, a Flea Bottom brawl leaves **one Tyrell man-at-arms dead** and **two of Gargalen's men scalded**; **Olenna Tyrell** insults **Ellaria Sand** in the Red Keep yard. Tyrion lodges Oberyn in a far cornerfort but cannot fully separate factions.
3. **Littlefinger's treasury books resist reconciliation.** Tyrion finds businesses and apparent investments hard to interpret and notes **Antler Men condemned after the Blackwater had crown loans outstanding**. Their deaths may impair collection from heirs. This is evidence of debt recovery and recordkeeping risks, **not an audited demonstration of a particular Littlefinger theft**.
4. **Walder's raven uses coded marriage language.** Tywin shows Tyrion a message with a **Frey twin-tower seal**, congratulating Roslin's 'trout' and two 'wolf pelts'. Tyrion decodes **Edmure captured, Robb and Catelyn killed**, a reading corroborated directly by [ASOS 51 Catelyn](51-catelyn.md) for the deaths and by Tywin's later explanation for Edmure's hostage status. **The coded letter is not a transcript of what every Frey saw.**
5. **Tywin refuses to announce the war ended.** He knows some riverlords still command castles. **Brynden Blackfish** holds Riverrun with Edmure hostage; **Jason Mallister** and **Tytos Blackwood** may resist, while **Jonos Bracken** could be induced to switch allegiance. Tywin plans generous terms to submissions, reserving one exception for Harrenhal.
6. **Gregor is ordered to destroy the Brave Companions.** Tywin says he has **commanded Gregor Clegane to put Harrenhal to the sword**. This is an order and target, **not proof Gregor has taken the castle at chapter end**. Littlefinger expects to benefit from the grant without fighting, but his Vale arrival remains uncertain.
7. **Joffrey wants to torment Sansa using Robb's head.** He demands Robb's severed head be brought to the wedding banquet to force Sansa to kiss it, and Tyrion calls him a monster. This is a **proposed cruelty**, not witnessed execution of Joffrey's spectacle. Kevan points out Sansa is now the king's aunt by marriage.
8. **Tywin rejects Joffrey's vengeance-first policy.** He advises sparing kneeling enemies and allowing submission, comparing Joffrey's insistence he is king with **Aerys's**. Joffrey counters that **Robert** won battles while Tywin hid at Casterly Rock. Tywin orders Joffrey removed and offered **dreamwine**, overriding the boy's protest. This shows **control of the royal household by the Hand**, not a formal deposition.
9. **Cersei describes childhood cruelty to a cat.** She recalls **Robert knocking out two baby teeth** after Joffrey's unnamed misdeed with a cat, and her threat to kill Robert if he struck their son again. The chapter does **not describe exactly what Joffrey did to the animal**; do not fill in details from later evidence under this chapter alone.
10. **Tywin thinks Joffrey might still be corrected.** He calls the boy willfully stupid rather than merely headstrong and considers disciplining him. Tyrion privately calls him **Aerys the Third**. This is Tyrion's judgment and Tywin's stated intent, not a completed reform.
11. **Tywin admits advance, compartmentalized Red Wedding participation.** When Tyrion asks when Tywin and Walder began plotting, Tywin says **those with assigned roles were told only what they needed**, that his objective was to kill a dangerous adversary cheaply, and that Tyrion and Cersei were excluded. This is **direct on-page evidence of Tywin's foreknowledge and strategic involvement**, not only inference from the Rains of Castamere.
12. **He explains the killing order as an economical war measure.** Tywin says Robb was too vigilant in the field; an arrow at Edmure's wedding was planned. He claims **Walder's roof makes Walder responsible for the breach of guest right**. His comparison of a dozen diners against ten thousand battlefield casualties is **moral rationalization**, and the documented Red Wedding outside the hall includes far more than a dozen possible victims.
13. **Political rewards are specified rather than assumed.** **Emmon Frey** is promised **Riverrun when Brynden yields**; **Lancel and Daven Lannister** must marry Frey girls; **Joy Hill** is to marry a Frey natural son when older. **Roose Bolton** will become **Warden of the North**, and receive 'Arya Stark' to marry to his bastard son. **Future appointments and marriages are Tywin's arrangements, not all completed events.**
14. **The announced Arya is a critical identity anomaly.** Tyrion knows his past city search failed and believes Arya dead; Tywin suggests **Littlefinger** may have found her, while the reader has directly seen real **Arya at the Twins outside the wedding** in [ASOS 52 Arya](52-arya.md). Tywin gives **no identification procedure, face-to-face verification or custody chain** for the girl to be sent to the Boltons. This is strong **identity substitution or false-claim lead**, not a confirmed name of any impostor.
15. **Tywin plans rival northern claims to compete.** He envisions **Bolton using his purported Arya marriage to subdue the North and ironborn** while Tyrion fathers a child with **Sansa**, eventually providing a Lannister-aligned heir. This is explicitly **dynastic rivalry managed by the crown**, contingent on future conquest and pregnancies.
16. **Tywin continues to coerce Tyrion to consummate.** The final exchange demands Tyrion take Sansa's virginity even after Robb and Catelyn's murder, prompting Tyrion to ask bitterly when Sansa might be receptive to bearing his child once told the truth. **The marriage remains unconsummated during this chapter**.
17. **Oberyn's demand is independently strategic.** Tywin says **Doran is cautious**, Oberyn impatient; he confirms **Oberyn once tried to mobilize Dorne for Viserys** after the Rebellion, interrupted by Jon Arryn's visit with **Prince Lewyn Martell's bones**. This is **Tywin testimony** about diplomacy, not an exhaustive record of Doran's private plans.
18. **Tywin plans an explicitly false or at least knowingly unsupported scapegoat story.** He instructs Tyrion to tell Oberyn **Ser Amory Lorch killed Elia and her children**, although he then distinguishes **Rhaenys's body brought by Lorch** from **Elia and baby Aegon found a floor below**, and Tyrion understands **Gregor Clegane** is the central accused attacker. The instruction is a **politically convenient unified accusation against a dead man who cannot rebut it**, not a proven historical reconstruction.
19. **Tywin personally establishes the children as intended political fatalities.** He says it was necessary to demonstrate loyalty to Robert and that **Rhaegar's children had to die** for the new regime's stability; he presented the bodies to Robert to make allegiance irreversible. **This establishes his strategic purpose and approval of the child killings**, though the exact explicit words of every original soldier's order are not reproduced.
20. **He disavows intentional harm to Elia, but accepts negligent command responsibility.** Tywin claims Elia should have been spared, that he never ordered her raped or killed, and that he may not have mentioned her at all. **This is the accused patron's self-interested denial**, not exculpatory independent evidence. He admits delegating to Gregor without restraint and describes his own need to protect **Jaime from Aerys** and preempt Ned's army.
21. **Tywin describes Rhaenys's horrific murder.** He says Lorch killed the toddler with many knife thrusts after she fought and screamed, and muses about quiet killing instead. The detail shows **calculating indifference to murdered children** even as he rhetorically criticizes excessive violence. His age estimate is uncertain.
22. **End state:** News of Robb and Catelyn's deaths has reached King's Landing; Tywin is confirmed strategically involved and distributing rewards; an unverified 'Arya' is destined for Bolton; Tyrion's forced dynastic marriage remains tense. **None of the proposed northerly regime or Gregor's new Harrenhal orders is executed in the scene.**

## Theory tests / disconfirmation

- **Red Wedding logistics:** Tywin's role is now a **direct admission**. Still separate what Tywin authorized, what Walder orchestrated and what specific soldiers chose; his 'dozen at dinner' minimization fails against Arya's burning feast tents.
- **Stark inheritance:** Neither the source of 'Arya' nor Tyrion/Sansa consummation is verified. Crowning a Bolton–Arya marriage and a future Tyrion–Sansa child is a **future competitive claim structure**, not a settled North.
- **Elia conspiracy:** Tywin candidly wanted the children dead but denies ordering Elia's death. Assess guilt by policy design, selected agents and duty to control, not an unsupported quote of a missing murder order.
- **Littlefinger and financial ledgers:** Difficult accounting and the proposed acquisition of Arya are suspicions. Actual fraudulent instructions and her alleged transfer route must be traced.
