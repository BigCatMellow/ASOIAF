## Narrative sequence, agency and consequences

1. **The dead hostage boys enter Riverrun's hall.** The murdered squires are **Willem Lannister** (son of Kevan) and **Tion Frey** (Lady Genna's son). Their bodies are laid beneath the dais, one apparently surprised in sleep and the other visibly wounded while resisting. Their youth and helplessness directly contradict any claim they died honorably in battle.
2. **Grey Wind is heard, not consulted.** Half a castle away the wolf howls as the corpses enter. Catelyn interprets his distress as sensing the death and disaster through walls and rain. **VERIFIED:** the howling; **INFERRED:** precise awareness of blood or danger.
3. **Rickard Karstark and accomplices are produced.** Eight were implicated in the killing party; the Greatjon reports two killed during arrest and a third dying, with five bound prisoners brought before Robb. Karstark's men also killed **Delp and Elwood**, two Tully guards, to reach the tower.
4. **Karstark asserts vendetta rather than denies killing.** He argues Jaime killed his sons Torrhen and Eddard at Whispering Wood, so any Lannister/Frey kin are permissible substitutes. Robb draws the moral distinction: **unarmed surrendered captives are not combatants**, and the boys did not kill the Karstark sons.
5. **Catelyn accepts causal guilt but not legal agency.** She privately believes that releasing Jaime precipitated these murders, and fears retaliatory danger to Sansa. Robb correctly identifies **Rickard's chosen violence** as the direct act. One decision's consequences do not erase the killer's responsibility.
6. **The Greatjon nearly kills Rickard on the spot.** He strikes Karstark for insulting Robb and threatens him with a spear; Robb stops him. The king insists on pronouncing judgment rather than allowing another uncontrolled reprisal.
7. **Robb orders the seven other perpetrators hanged.** He refuses a lookout's plea that he did not wield a weapon: the man admits knowing the plan and hearing the victims' cries. Robb orders him hanged last so he must watch the others die. This is a severe, possibly disproportionate application of collective culpability, not merely a generic show of clemency.
8. **The Karstark cavalry has deserted.** Brynden reports Karstark's fighters left secretly at night while servants and wounded kept campfires lit. Roughly **three hundred riders with twice as many horses** scattered. They are **hunting Jaime** because Rickard promised his unmarried daughter's hand as a reward to whoever brings him Jaime's head.
9. **The bounty weaponizes marriage as command.** The Karstark daughter's agency is not asked or shown; her hand is offered to **highborn or lowborn** on delivery of Jaime's head. It creates a mobile, motivated threat to Jaime/Brienne independent of formal royal orders. Her identity and consent are not established here.
10. **The political cost is immediate.** Robb has already lost Frey support and expects little from Lysa. The loss of Karhold's mounted strength weakens him further. Brynden predicts no coherent new Karstark field force, merely dispersal. **Do not double-count these same riders as still under Robb's command.**
11. **Edmure proposes concealment.** He wants to suppress news of the killings and perhaps keep Rickard captive to hold his son loyal. Robb and Brynden reject secrecy: the deserters already carry the story, and Robb believes he owes the boys' fathers truth and justice. Concealment would not reverse the deaths.
12. **The absent heir is identified.** Karstark's eldest surviving son **Harrion** was captured at the Green Fork and is associated with Harrenhal. Robb anticipates hostility from him if Rickard is executed. Brynden warns that succession might create a reason for Harrion privately to welcome his father's death; Robb observes his own followers might still require public vengeance. **Neither heir's present custody nor actual response is checked in this chapter**.
13. **Northern kinship raises the moral cost.** Rickard recalls service beside Ned and Robb and common First Men ancestry, while Catelyn recalls that the Karstarks descend from **Karlon Stark** of Winterfell, their name deriving from **Karl's Hold**. His assertion that executing him will make Robb a cursed **kinslayer** is a **character's moral/religious accusation**, not proof the gods curse the act.
14. **The Vale's inactivity constrains Robb's strategy.** Robb says he has repeatedly requested at minimum **passage through the Bloody Gate and shipping from Gulltown**, allowing a landing at White Harbor to outflank Moat Cailin. Lysa has given no useful response. Catelyn predicts that even neutral transit will be refused. Compare [ASOS 19 Tyrion](19-tyrion.md): the Lannisters are concurrently authorizing Littlefinger's Vale marriage mission.
15. **Catelyn's diagnosis of Lysa is interpretive.** She says Lysa has always hidden from difficulty and will let no army through. Brynden agrees. Their forecast may be well-informed, but it is not Lysa's own POV or a signed policy.
16. **The king understands the trap of honor.** Robb acknowledges he owes the Karstark house deep military debts and knows punishing Rickard can cost still more soldiers. He can foresee no gratitude from Lannister or Frey survivors, but refuses to treat sleeping captive children as wartime targets.
17. **Decision at the crown.** Robb places his bronze-and-iron crown back on his head and decides Rickard **must die at dawn**. Edmure's hostage alternative is expressly considered and rejected. This is an ethically coherent but strategically expensive choice, not a sign Robb did not appreciate the tradeoff.
18. **Robb personally executes Rickard.** In Riverrun's godswood before the heart tree, he takes the poleaxe himself, pronounces guilt for murder and treason and kills Rickard with a blow; it takes **three blows to sever the head completely**. Robb afterwards is shaking, bloodied and silent before the heart tree. **The execution is verified on page.**
19. **A retaliatory/retrieval mission is limited.** Brynden takes **one hundred men** in pursuit of the scattered Karstarks, hoping not to hang many. Success or the men's return is not reported here.
20. **Bolton must be informed, but dispatch is not witnessed.** Robb notes **Karstark infantry attached to Roose Bolton** may react to news of their lord's execution, and says Bolton must be warned. This is a crucial **intended communication**, not proof any warning arrived or what Bolton would do.
21. **Hoster is dying.** Maester Vyman says Lord Hoster's strength is failing; Catelyn sits at his bedside. The contrast between a father whom death cannot spare and a son facing severe political choices is Catelyn's reflection, not a literal instruction to surrender the North.
22. **Robb's immediate psychological aftermath.** Jeyne tells Catelyn he has not eaten, still wears bloodied damp clothes, writes a **letter then burns it**, and studies maps without answering her. The letter's **contents, addressee and strategic significance are UNKNOWN**. His anguish is directly reported by Jeyne, not privately witnessed by Catelyn throughout the day.
23. **Jeyne asks how to care for her husband.** She suggests a headsman might have spared Robb pain. Catelyn explains Ned's principle that the man ordering death should face its cost and counsels patience with northern solitude. This is substantive transmission of cultural practice between a southern-born wife and the Stark household.
24. **The heir question becomes explicit.** Catelyn urges Jeyne to give Robb an heir. Jeyne says she and Robb have frequent intercourse, she hopes for twins named **Eddard and Brandon**, and she prays regularly. **No pregnancy is observed or diagnosed**.
25. **An exceptionally testable herbal detail.** Jeyne says her mother **Sybell Spicer** makes her a **daily posset of herbs, milk and ale**, described to Jeyne as helping conception. This is direct testimony that she drinks the mixture, but the herbs, preparation and actual fertility effects are untested. Read beside [ASOS 19 Tyrion](19-tyrion.md), which records Sybell's **grandmother reputed to have been a maegi and healer**.
26. **The posset is an ambiguity, not a proven plot.** A benign family fertility tonic is a full alternative explanation. A deceptive contraceptive is a **THEORY** requiring ingredients, testimony, timing and evidence of outside coordination. Neither Tyrion's speculation about Westerling incentives nor Sybell's herbal practice alone proves tampering.
27. **Catelyn's trust remains qualified.** She concludes Jeyne appears good-hearted and physically well suited to motherhood. This is Catelyn's appraisal of Jeyne, not an examination of Sybell's intentions or confirmation of fertility.
28. **End state.** Rickard and his immediate accomplices have been executed; the Karstark mounted contingent has deserted; Harrion's stance is unknown; the Vale remains closed in practice, Frey reply absent and the Bolton warning unconfirmed. Robb is traumatized, studying maps, and his heir remains hypothetical.

## Competing explanations and theory tests

- **The Karstark catastrophe:** Robb's execution is not simply 'honor over strategy.' He has a real security problem because Rickard just directed murder inside a prison, and the riders **already left before the execution**. Counterfactual mercy might save some future loyalty but cannot retroactively restore the entire cavalry or undo the bounty. Evaluate Edmure's hostage option with that constraint.
- **The Westerling fertility hypothesis:** Catelyn sees a loving Jeyne; Sybell controls an ingested medicinal mixture. Together with Tywin's surprisingly confident posture toward the marriage in the preceding chapter, this merits a **priority evidence test**, not adoption as fact. Need later independent statements about posset ingredients and Sybell's dealings.
- **Rival royal plans for the Vale:** While Robb cannot secure even neutral military transit, Tywin and Littlefinger plan to secure Lysa's allegiance by marriage. Neither side has yet put forces on the Vale road; chronological reconciliation is needed.
- **Northern kinship:** Robb's relationship to Karstarks is both genealogical and politically charged. 'Kinslayer' is not an automatic disqualifying verdict, nor can a legitimate royal death sentence erase the resentments of an allied clan.
- **Knowledge boundary:** The reader knows Robb's marriage is being used by Tywin for Sansa policy (ASOS 19); Robb and Catelyn have not been shown learning of this King's Landing deliberation.
