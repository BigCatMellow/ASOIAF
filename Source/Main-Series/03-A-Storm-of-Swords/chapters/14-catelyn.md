## Chronological plot and character-agency analysis

1. **RIVERRUN'S TENSE RETURN:** Catelyn, still confined to **Hoster Tully's chambers** for unauthorized release of Jaime, hears the castle hounds react to **Grey Wind's scent** and realizes **Robb has returned**. Her father continues deliriously pleading with **'Tansy'** ([ASOS 2 Catelyn](02-catelyn.md)); the name and exact offense remain unexplained in the present scene.
2. **RYGER'S PURSUIT ENDS WITHOUT JAIME:** **Ser Robin Ryger returns to Riverrun on foot** with his men after their pursuing galley was destroyed; Catelyn learns Jaime escaped. This independently corroborates [ASOS 1 Jaime](01-jaime.md), where **Brienne** sank the galley by dropping a rock from the riverbank. Catelyn is refused access to Ryger and therefore **does not personally learn Brienne's specific technique**.
3. **FREY PROTEST VISIBLE BEFORE ITS CAUSE:** Before Robb's arrival, Catelyn sees **about forty angry mounted men leave Riverrun**, some **trampling a Stark direwolf banner**. She tentatively identifies **Perwyn Frey and Martyn Rivers** but cannot be sure at a distance. Edmure, Vyman and the castellan do not explain the departure. This is **firsthand evidence of a political rupture** with identity and grievance explained later in the chapter.
4. **ROBB PUBLICLY PRAISES EDMURE:** Robb returns to a crowded Great Hall and praises **Edmure's fighting at the Stone Mill** while the Greatjon and riverlords hail him as **King in the North and King of the Trident**. He cautions the war is not finished. The apparent praise is later revealed as **deliberate public political management**, not agreement with Edmure's operational judgment.
5. **ROBB IS A CHANGED BOY-KING:** Catelyn sees Robb, now **sixteen**, with a bronze-and-iron crown, lean face, long auburn hair and stained white cloak. His recent arrow wound at **the Crag** passed through his arm and has healed after fever and care. This is Robb's account, supported by the earlier western raven ([ASOS 2](02-catelyn.md)); it is **not evidence of chronic disabling injury** at this point.
6. **CATELYN'S RELEASE DEFENDED, BUT DIVISIVE:** **Rickard Karstark** accuses Catelyn of depriving him of vengeance against Jaime, killer of his sons **Torrhen and Eddard**. Robb silences the charge of treason within his hearing and forgives her because he understands her love and grief for the Stark children. The **Greatjon and Maege Mormont** sympathize, while **Galbart Glover, Jason Mallister and Jonos Bracken** are cooler. This is **political forgiveness amid genuine internal division**, not unanimous exoneration.
7. **A STRANGE NEW RETINUE:** Beside Robb are members of **House Westerling and House Spicer**: **Sybell Westerling** (wife of Gawen), **Rolph Spicer** (Sybell's brother, former castellan of the Crag, badge with three pepperpots), **Raynald Westerling** (seashell badge), young **Elenya** and **Rollam** (Robb's new squire), and another young woman. These named persons and precise devices are **directly introduced**, important for network and inheritance tracking; their mere presence **does not prove hostile conspiracy**.
8. **REVEAL OF A COMPLETED MARRIAGE:** Robb introduces **Jeyne Westerling as his wife and queen**. Catelyn accepts her into the family in public despite recognizing instantly that **Robb had pledged to marry a Frey**. This is a **marriage already performed**, not merely a crush or a rumored betrothal. Jeyne vows to be loyal, and Catelyn sees genuine fear and youth in her.
9. **POLITICAL FAMILY COST:** Robb admits **Lord Gawen Westerling is Tywin's vassal and a prisoner at Seagard**, captured in the Whispering Wood; Robb means to release him but fears the marriage has placed the Westerlings at risk of Lannister retaliation. Gawen **did not consent to his daughter's match**. This is an important **split between a captive house head and a daughter/maternal kin cooperating with Stark occupiers**.
10. **FREY ALLIANCE LOST, QUANTIFIED:** Robb says the Westerling bride brings only **about fifty fighting men, including twelve knights**, while Walder Frey's original pact delivered **roughly 1,000 cavalry and nearly 3,000 infantry**. The earlier forty riders' dramatic exit becomes intelligible. The disparity is strategic, not merely a breach of etiquette.
11. **THE CRAG'S FALL AND JEYNE'S CARE:** Robb explains **Black Walder and the Smalljon** scaled the Crag's walls while Robb rammed its gate; he took an **arrow wound shortly before Rolph surrendered the castle**. His wound became infected, and **Jeyne nursed him in her bed** until the fever broke. The **Greatjon then delivered apparent news that Bran and Rickon had been killed at Winterfell**, triggering grief and intimacy between Robb and Jeyne. **The boys are actually alive** ([ACOK 69 Bran](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/69-bran.md)); the loss news was sincerely believed by Robb but false in fact.
12. **ROBB MARRIES FOR A PARTICULAR IDEA OF HONOR:** He says he wed Jeyne **the day after they slept together** because he thought leaving her dishonored was unacceptable. That is Robb's declared motive, and his affection is apparent; it does not establish that Jeyne, Sybell or Rolph engineered his injury, fever or sexual encounter. **Marriage as an attempt to avoid dishonor produces an enormous prior oath breach**, a central thematic conflict.
13. **FREY RESPONSE AND ALTERNATIVES REJECTED:** Robb says **Ryman Frey, Black Walder and the rest withdrew**, despite **Olyvar Frey's desire to remain his squire**. Black Walder bitterly suggested Frey women would not object to marrying **Robb after Jeyne's death**; Robb considered killing him but Jeyne asked mercy. He offers possible replacement Frey marriages via **Wendel Manderly or Greatjon's uncles**, but Catelyn explains Walder expected to be **grandfather to a king** and may view lesser alliances as an additional slight. Frey retaliation is **foreseeable, not yet a particular planned atrocity in this chapter**.
14. **ROBB'S IRONBORN REGRET:** Robb regrets sending **Theon Greyjoy** to Pyke against Catelyn's advice, reasoning that if he had retained the hostage Winterfell and his brothers might be safe. Catelyn notes **Balon may have invaded regardless**—the prior rebellion cost Balon two sons and did not cure his ambitions. This is a clear example of **counterfactual disagreement** between characters with incomplete information.
15. **GREY WIND ABSENT FROM THE HALL:** Catelyn realizes Grey Wind is **outside eating mutton**, not accompanying Robb as he once did. Robb says Jeyne fears him and **Sybell is terrified**; Grey Wind has killed men in assaults and is no longer easily restrained. The direwolf's absence is **a real shift in protective proximity**, but not independently proof of a wolf-controlled destiny.
16. **GREY WIND HATES ROLPH SPICER:** Robb specifically says the direwolf **bares teeth whenever Rolph approaches**. Catelyn urges Robb to **assign Rolph a distant, honorable task** and remove him from close access, citing her past correct caution regarding Theon. Robb objects that Rolph is a useful and courageous new follower but agrees **to send him away to ease her worry**, not because he explicitly believes the wolf can identify traitors. **Rolph's betrayal or innocence is still unproved**.
17. **WOLF COUNT CORRECTS THE ADULT:** Catelyn calls the pups five for Ned's five legitimate Stark children; Robb insists they found **six, including Ghost for Jon Snow**. This is a precise source-native correction, making any symbolic five-child formula incomplete. Robb admits he once viewed the wolves as guardians but lost confidence when falsely told Bran and Rickon died despite their wolves' presence.
18. **PRIVATE REVERSAL OF PUBLIC PRAISE:** In Hoster's private audience chamber, **Brynden Blackfish sharply rebukes Edmure** for fighting Tywin at the Red Fork rather than simply **holding Riverrun**. Robb likewise disapproves. Their earlier praise was a conscious **public effort not to shame Edmure before riverlords**, so public commendation cannot always be treated as the lord's private judgment.
19. **UNREPORTED OPERATIONAL PLAN:** Robb and Brynden explain they had stayed in the west to **draw Tywin away** from King's Landing, harass his infantry-heavy army with their more mobile mounted force, then occupy **favorable defensive terrain astride the gold road** and trap him far from Stannis's attack. This is a coherent campaign plan, but **Edmure says plainly it was never communicated to him**. It is not intellectually sound to assign him perfect knowledge of secret strategic intention.
20. **THE KEY DISRUPTION AND ITS CAUSAL BOUNDARY:** Edmure's defense of the **Red Fork crossings** prevented or delayed Tywin's westward march; then messengers from **Bitterbridge** reached Tywin and he turned to join **Matthis Rowan, Randyll Tarly and Mace Tyrell**. A forced march and **river barges from Tumbler's Falls** brought the relief army near King's Landing, striking Stannis from the rear. The sequence strongly supports **Edmure's unintentional obstruction** of Robb's west-trap plan. But **whether Tywin would actually have taken the bait, remained trapped, or been unable to respond under alternative circumstances is counterfactual**.
21. **EDMURE WAS ORDERED TO HOLD, NOT FULLY BRIEFED:** Edmure says he did defend Riverrun and fight well; Robb says he was commanded **only to hold Riverrun**, and Brynden says victory at the fords hurt the larger plan. Their complaint has a military basis but shares **responsibility for unclear mission boundaries and lack of strategic communication**. The exchange should be indexed as a **coordination failure, not a uniquely foolish or fully informed betrayal by Edmure**.
22. **THE FUTURE WAR WORSE:** Robb predicts that once **Joffrey and Margaery wed**, the Tyrell-Lannister host will fight him, and the alienated Freys may become opponents as well. Catelyn demands he **retake Winterfell and defend the North**, although her belief that Theon killed Bran and Rickon is still false and she has not received the evidence that **Ramsay burned the castle** ([ACOK 66/69](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/66-theon.md)).
23. **MOAT CAILIN IS THE STRATEGIC BLOCKER:** **Edmure** notes the Ironborn hold **Moat Cailin** and the coast, blocking an obvious march from the Riverlands to the North; Frey hostility could trap Robb with enemies ahead and behind. Robb judges renewed **Frey support essential** for any viable chance.
24. **A PERSONAL MEANS OF REPAIR:** When Robb lists **gold, lands, honors and apologies** as possible amends to Walder, Catelyn insists what the Freys need is **a particular person**, implicitly proposing a fresh high-status marriage bargaining counter. The chapter stops before **the identity, contract, consent or result of any substitute marriage** is laid out. This is a prospective **new betrothal negotiation**, not a marriage already concluded.

## Ranked theory and causal tests

- **Westerling/Spicer marriage conspiracy:** The timing of wound, fever, grief and marriage after a Lannister-vassal castle fell merits review; **Grey Wind's hostility toward Rolph and Sybell's fear** strengthen suspicion. But the chapter **does not prove Jeyne deliberately seduced Robb, Rolph worked for Tywin, or Sybell arranged a drug**. Treat each mechanism as a separate question.
- **Frey fracture:** Freys visibly abandon the cause, Black Walder voices disturbing widow talk, and nearly 4,000 pledged soldiers leave; the marriage insult is direct and severe. The **future form of retaliation remains unknown in this source**.
- **Edmure blame:** The central operational puzzle is **why Robb and Brynden did not explain the strategic aim to their commander**. Edmure was instructed to hold Riverrun, which he interpreted expansively. Robb's intended west trap is a credible account, but its alternative success cannot be demonstrated.
- **Grey Wind's truth-detection:** Direwolves across the books react powerfully to people. This particular hostility may foreshadow danger from Rolph, but **growling alone is not adjudication or proof of a treasonous act**.
- **False deaths compound real losses:** Robb's sincere grief over Bran and Rickon helps precipitate a **real political rupture**. The princes survive, but a false report has destructive downstream effects—matching the project's focus on false public knowledge.
