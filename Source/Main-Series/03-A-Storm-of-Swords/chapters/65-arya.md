## Chronological observations and causal spine

1. **The apparent killing of Arya in ASOS 52 is resolved: she lives.** Her head is painful but the lump is shrinking. Sandor explicitly states he struck her with **the flat of his axe**, not the edge. It was violent incapacitation to prevent entering the Twins; neither rescue intent nor the absence of an open wound makes her experience harmless.
2. **Arya experiences profound grief and psychological numbness.** Each morning she feels an emptiness where family belonged, sometimes wishes not to wake, and withdraws from ordinary conversation. She believes the Starks dead and lacks trustworthy confirmation of several siblings' actual fate.
3. **Wolf dreams are recurring and specific.** Almost nightly she experiences leading an enormous wolf pack, outpacing horses and lions, sharing meat and shelter with wolf brothers and sisters. She describes the felt experience as stronger, freer and more belonging than waking life. A **Nymeria skinchanging interpretation is highly supported by the sensory specificity**, but the chapter does not explicitly identify an observing external narrator controlling the wolf.
4. **Sandor and Arya now have two horses.** His black charger **Stranger** accompanies a riderless sorrel palfrey Arya names **Craven**, found wandering the morning after the massacre. Arya can ride separately; she considers escape but has nowhere safe to go.
5. **Sandor no longer binds her at night.** Although he forces her to rise and insults her, he sometimes acts indifferent to her departure. She contemplates killing him with his axe or outriding him on Craven, yet does neither because **safe destinations have collapsed**.
6. **Arya considers Riverrun, Acorn Hall, Sharna's inn and the Brotherhood.** She imagines Anguy teaching her archery, Gendry's company and an outlaw life. She rejects these hopes because most companions were willing to trade her for money or departed; that is **her wounded interpretation**, not direct proof none valued her.
7. **Sandor says Frey riders are hunting surviving northmen.** They avoid roads, settlements and attention, hiding when Frey twin-tower banners appear. This is **his tactical reading**, corroborated by the broader massacre but not a count of all pursuers.
8. **A wounded Piper archer supplies ground-level evidence of betrayal.** The man bears **Marq Piper's pink dancing maiden badge** and says he drank peaceably with a Bolton man with the **flayed man badge**, exchanging toasts to Edmure and Roslin, before that man smashed his shoulder with a mace. His wound is infected and malodorous; his story is **first-person testimony from a victim**, not a battle-wide casualty census.
9. **The wounded man requests 'mercy'.** Sandor offers water and death; Arya fetches water in the Hound's iron dog-head helm, which leaks through its eyeholes but holds enough at the base. The man drinks and repeats he would have preferred wine. Sandor then **stabs him through the heart**, intentionally ending his suffering at the dying man's request.
10. **The mercy killing doubles as a lesson in killing.** Sandor shows Arya where the heart lies; she views it alongside other forms of killing rather than accepting him as a moral teacher. He then **robs the corpse** of **two silver stags, approximately thirty coppers, a pink-stoned dagger and a kettle helm**. Arya takes the dagger and helmet; **a mercy act coexists with opportunistic theft**.
11. **The Vale ransom becomes Sandor's next plan.** Once the rains ease and he determines they have ridden east into the **foothills of the Mountains of the Moon**, he proposes bringing Arya to her aunt **Lysa Arryn at the Eyrie** by the high road and Bloody Gate. Neither aunt nor ransom is contacted at this stage.
12. **Arya resists abandoning hope Catelyn was captured alive.** Since they have not personally seen her murdered, she imagines her mother in Frey custody and asks to return to rescue her. This is a credible information gap for Arya, though the reader directly witnessed Catelyn's throat cut.
13. **An unusually detailed wolf-body dream follows the conversation.** Arya smells the unmistakable scent of her mother beneath mud, reeds and rotting corpses at a **green, corpse-strewn river**, while leading a wolf pack feeding on battlefield dead. She enters water, seizes a **pale female body by its arm**, drags it ashore and wards away wolves that might eat it. **The cold body has a cut throat**.
14. **The wolf's response is specific: leave the body uneaten and urge it to rise.** The wolf seems to wish the corpse would get up, feed and run with them. This does not show **revival**. Mounted men with **black, yellow and pink banners or clothing** approach from downwind; the wolf disperses her pack and flees, leaving the corpse on shore. **Their identities, who touches the body next, and any magic are not narrated**.
15. **Arya wakes convinced she saw Catelyn dead.** She tells Sandor she now knows her mother is dead **because of her dream**. The claim is strengthened for the reader by Catelyn's death POV and the detailed retrieval imagery, but **the dream's external verification and any resurrection are not yet shown**.
16. **A mountain village briefly hires Sandor.** They are building a **timber palisade**. Sandor agrees to chop and haul logs for food, shelter, coin and ale; work lasts roughly **a fortnight**. Arya sleeps more securely but dislikes domestic routines and being treated as a small girl.
17. **The village provides a grim small-scale view of trauma.** An elder's daughter of Arya's age clings to a stuffed soldier doll. Arya destroys the doll and throws it into a brook, cruelly reproducing the violence she has seen. The act is **direct evidence of her behavior**, not merely an adult's view that she is dangerous.
18. **The village elder says Vale mountain clans now possess steel.** He reports raids by **Timett One-Eye's Burned Men, Gunthor son of Gurn's Stone Crows, Milk Snakes and Sons of the Mist**; clans have **mail and good swords** and control stretches of the high road. This may reflect Tyrion's earlier arming of mountain men, but the source here is **local testimony** rather than direct account of every raid.
19. **The village excludes Sandor after he finishes the palisade.** The elder knows him from markets, worries winter shortages, and says his reputation brings blood. Sandor receives coppers and exchanges the **Twins axe** for a **rusty, old sword** he sharpens nightly. The exclusion is security reasoning as well as prejudice; Arya notes his eagerness to maintain a killing edge despite apparent aversion to battle.
20. **Sandor abandons the Eyrie road.** With snow in the passes, clan raids and no welcome in the village, he turns back toward **Riverrun** to seek a ransom from **Brynden Blackfish**. Arya doubts Brynden would recognize her or pay Sandor. **No meeting with the Blackfish has happened**.
21. **Arya proposes the Wall as the only reliable family refuge.** She remembers **Jon**, imagines his welcome, and proposes traveling to him. Sandor rejects the route as too dangerous through Frey lines, the Neck, ironborn holdings and northern armies. The northbound journey is **not attempted**.
22. **Sandor invokes his own brother.** His last response says he has a brother too, implicitly **Gregor**, as Arya searches for someone who will want her. This preserves their bitter sibling relationship without establishing an actual encounter.
23. **End state:** Arya lives, carries a new dagger, has dreamed a detailed and potentially supernatural **retrieval of Catelyn's corpse**, and remains with Sandor. The Vale plan has collapsed; Sandor now aims for Riverrun while Arya wants Jon at the Wall.

## Key theories / falsifiable checks

- **Nymeria and Catelyn:** The imagery is extraordinarily specific and coincides with known death/dumping reports, but Arya's chapter is still a **dream-subjective POV**. A future independent account of the body being found by riverside outlaws would substantially strengthen its literal interpretation. Do not prematurely report Catelyn restored to life.
- **Red Wedding participant identification:** Piper's injured eyewitness can support involvement by an individual Bolton soldier; it cannot identify command origin or participation of every Bolton retainer.
- **Sandor's changed priorities:** Mercy killing and saving Arya coexist with threats, robbery and willingness to ransom her. Treat a moral redemption arc as a test, not a completed transformation.
- **Arya's identity:** Her cruelty to the doll and repeated adoption of 'no one' prefigure erasure and self-protection but do not negate continuing grief, empathy for her mother and attachment to Jon.
