## Sequential primary-text record

1. **Sam, Gilly and her unnamed newborn fled the Craster mutiny.** They are exhausted, hungry and lost in wildling territory. Sam **walks** while Gilly and baby ride the last garron because the other horse **died three days after departure**. He suffers cracked boots, blisters beneath callus, numb hands and likely frost damage. They have not yet reached the Wall.
2. **He mistakes an unfamiliar weirwood village for Whitetree.** A large-faced white tree stands among empty sod-roofed hovels, a longhall, well and sheepfold. Sam hopes this is mapped Whitetree but recognizes **the face and size differ**, so he cannot locate himself confidently. **A weirwood does not uniquely identify Whitetree**.
3. **The population has left for Mance.** Houses are abandoned with ashes and stale straw. Sam attributes desertion to wildlings joining Mance Rayder's migrating host; he does not question remaining residents because none is present.
4. **Sam prays to the old gods despite uncertainty.** He says his father worshipped the Seven, but Sam took Watch vows before the old gods; he asks the weirwood for guidance while admitting he no longer knows which gods to believe in. **Prayer is witnessed, successful supernatural response not yet conclusively demonstrated**.
5. **Direction is only approximate.** Sam uses daylight sun position or **the Ice Dragon's tail** to navigate south. The Wall's dimensions make encountering it plausible, but he cannot establish east/west position or reach **Castle Black's gate**; he fears he overshot around lakes and river fords. **Do not replace uncertainty with a map pin**.
6. **Gilly is more capable at survival logistics in several ways.** She makes a fire and binds Sam's accidentally cut hand; Sam has failed at hunting and fishing. Their old-women-supplied food from Craster's has dwindled to hard garlic-flavored sausages. This undercuts a simplistic 'Sam rescued helpless Gilly' reading.
7. **The baby remains unnamed by local custom.** Gilly says naming a child before age **two** is unlucky because so many die. This is **her culturally situated explanation**, not proof every free-folk mother follows it or that the infant will die.
8. **Castle Black represents an imagined refuge, not known access.** Sam describes its warmth, venison stew, hot bread, singing, and company. Gilly tentatively asks permission to stand by the fire, illustrating her exclusion from ordinary hospitality. The Watch's actual reception of mother and infant remains **UNKNOWN**.
9. **The religious lullaby excludes the Stranger.** Sam sings a childhood **Song of the Seven** focusing on Father, Mother, Warrior, Crone, Smith and Maiden, then says no one sings about the death-aspect **Stranger**. Gilly notices the omission and asks why a religion with seven has only six named in the song. **Do not reproduce the song lyrics in research notes**; its structure and thematic function suffice.
10. **Randyll Tarly's abusive parenting appears in a specific flashback.** Sam recalls singing to baby **Dickon**, with Randyll denouncing his mother's 'soft songs' and ordering Sam away from his brother. This reinforces the self-esteem damage behind Sam's reluctance to imagine his father recognizing his killing of an Other.
11. **Gilly offers a relationship Sam doubts he can accept.** Gilly repeatedly says she would be his wife, but Sam invokes Watch celibacy and Tarly status against a wildling match; he likes sharing blankets with her. **No marriage or consummation is shown**, and her childbirth vulnerability must not be romanticized as automatic free consent to every future arrangement.
12. **Sam dreams an ideal reversal of Horn Hill.** In sleep he is lord of his father's castle, his Watch brothers feast in bright clothing, he carves with **Heartsbane**, and Gilly awaits in the sisters' old bedroom. This is **a psychological dream**, not evidence he will inherit Horn Hill or that Heartsbane has been transferred.
13. **Sudden unnatural cold heralds a returning corpse.** Waking by dying embers, Sam sees the garron panicking and a large figure entering: **Small Paul**, whose death was previously witnessed during the Fist retreat. He has a **milk-white face, blue eyes, blackened hands** and a raven ripping dead flesh from his cheek. This is a **wight**, not a living Paul or an icy, armor-clad Other.
14. **Gilly thinks the wight wants her newborn.** She says the baby smells of life and the dead are coming for that life, echoing Craster's women's warning that his older 'brothers' will return. Her explanation is **character belief**, not proven motive of the wight.
15. **Sam draws Mormont's dragonglass dagger.** Grenn still has the first obsidian dagger; Sam took **Jeor's** from Craster's Keep. He reminds Paul of carrying him when alive, but the wight ignores pleas and turns at the horse's noise.
16. **The dagger's actual result is restricted by armor.** Sam stabs at Paul's back repeatedly; the **obsidian blade shatters against the iron mail beneath the cloak**, throwing shards. **This does not establish that obsidian fails when applied directly to a wight's flesh**—the mail prevented penetration. It directly illustrates the material fragility of dragonglass weapons.
17. **Steel fails to reach the flesh too.** Paul's hands choke Sam; Sam's steel dagger strikes mail and spins away. He wrestles Paul down by using his greater body mass but still cannot break the grip, showing wights are clumsy yet extraordinarily strong and insensible.
18. **Fire destroys Small Paul.** Sam grabs a smoldering ember and smashes it into the corpse's mouth, breaking teeth; Paul's face ignites, the **blue eye glow disappears**, and the grip releases. The wight makes no human cries. **This is a witnessed wight vulnerability to actual fire**, substantially stronger evidence than extrapolating from the Other killed in ASOS 18.
19. **More wights have surrounded Gilly outside.** Sam sees **more than a dozen, perhaps a score or more**, including recognizable dead Watch brothers **Lark the Sisterman, Softfoot, Ryles, Chett and perhaps Hake**. They tore open the remaining horse. Identity of **Hake** is explicitly uncertain due to skull damage; other names are Sam's visual recognition.
20. **A speaking raven and entire flock intervene.** A raven repeats sounds like **fair/fear/go** from Sam's shoulder. Hundreds or thousands of ravens already perched in the weirwood then descend **as a coordinated swarm**, tearing at wights' faces and blue eyes but **not attacking Sam**. The behavior is objectively extraordinary in scale and specificity; controller or natural explanation remains unconfirmed.
21. **The flock makes escape possible but does not destroy every wight on page.** The birds keep corpses occupied as Sam pulls Gilly away. Sam has not observed permanent destruction of all surrounding wights; the birds can distract and damage without substituting for fire/obsidian kills.
22. **A hooded figure rides a giant elk.** A rider under the trees wears mottled black/grey and calls Sam **'Brother'**, offering transport. The elk is roughly **ten feet high at the shoulder**, with enormous antlers, and deliberately kneels so passengers can climb. These are observed properties, not ordinary horseback travel.
23. **The rescuer's hand is black, cold and stone-hard.** The rider reaches down; Sam initially thinks it gloved but discovers exposed **black cold fingers**. **Identity and nature UNKNOWN at chapter endpoint**: could indicate an undead, enchanted, or otherwise transformed being, but this chapter does not name him **Coldhands** or prove loyalty to the Others.
24. **End state:** Sam, Gilly and baby have escaped the clustered wights and are mounting an unknown rider's elk. The last horse is dead, Castle Black has not been reached, and the nature of the raven intervention and rider remains unexplained.

## Rivals and tests

- **Does dragonglass work against wights?** This scene still **does not settle that test** because both obsidian and steel strike armor. It **does** confirm fire works on Small Paul's corpse, and highlights obsidian fragility as an important tactical disadvantage.
- **Who directs the ravens?** Animal flock behavior, an unknown old-gods/skinchanger agent and interaction with the elk rider are alternatives. There is no narrated telepathic message that settles it.
- **Is the rider 'good' because he rescues them?** Assistance is on page; identity, motives, vitality and trustworthiness await additional evidence. The black hand cautions against simplistic Night's Watch identification merely from clothing.
- **Does Gilly's baby attract the Others?** She believes the attackers came specifically for him; we see a wight army and raven intervention but no Other, no commanded objective, and no child taken.
