## Chronological source record and causal developments

1. **Davos deliberately uses sunset worship as cover for a clandestine evacuation.** Queen Selyse, Stannis and Melisandre attend a **nightfire** in Dragonstone's yard; Davos and his companions watch for a long ceremony that will occupy the guards and religious faction. Stannis is physically present but Davos notices he does not join every congregational response, suggesting more hesitation than his entourage admits. That is **Davos's interpretation**, not a mind-reading fact.
2. **A group of king's men has been recruited for the extraction.** Named participants include **Ser Andrew Estermont**, **Ser Gerald Gower**, **the Bastard of Nightsong (Ser Rolland Storm)**, **Lewys the Fishwife**, **Omer Blackberry** and **Ser Triston of Tally Hill** among allies. They risk their lives by removing royal blood from a king's custody against his wishes. Their precise assignments differ; not every ally is at every door.
3. **Davos rejects a proposed assassination of Melisandre.** Lewys suggests ambushing her with swords, and Davos refuses, citing **Cressen's failed attempt** and the possibility she foresees threats directed at herself. He says ordinary deception and choosing a moment of distraction may work where assassination would expose everyone. **Her ability to detect all threats is not tested**.
4. **Melisandre explicitly distinguishes fire vision from certainty.** Recalling an earlier discussion, Davos reports her saying flames resemble **shifting writing** requiring years to distinguish **what was, what may be, and what will be**. When he asks why Axell Florent appears to learn so quickly, she gives a dismissive analogy rather than endorsing his real gift. **This is key evidence of Melisandre's own epistemic limits**.
5. **Davos equates political rescue with smuggling.** The operation uses familiar skills: routes, tides, timing, silent movement, reliable colleagues, false authority and a ready vessel. He says he needs **smugglers, not men seeking conspicuous honor**, because being discovered means execution or being burned.
6. **Pylos is knowingly involved.** **Maester Pylos** is giving **Edric Storm an arithmetic lesson** when Davos and Andrew enter; Pylos ends it, directs Edric to put on a plain cloak and advises him to obey the Hand. He stays behind, recognizing his possible punishment. **His participation is deliberate**, not a coerced unconscious delivery of a prisoner.
7. **Edric is unaware of his mortal danger.** He thinks Davos might be taking him to **R'hllor prayers**, declares he follows the Warrior as Robert did, asks whether **Shireen** can accompany him, and wants to say goodbye. He does **not understand he is being saved from ritual killing**.
8. **Davos uses his office deceptively to get Edric moving.** When Edric demands to see Stannis, Davos claims the king orders him away and will be furious if disobeyed; Davos displays his shortened fingers as proof of royal anger. He **privately acknowledges the account is a lie**: Stannis cut the fingers without fury from a rigid sense of justice. This is **lying to protect a child**, not a valid royal order.
9. **The actual evacuation route is described.** Through cellar stairs, a yard and a **postern beneath a carved dragon tail**, the boy reaches a boat with **four oarsmen**. Two guards have been **bound and trussed**, not killed. A Lyseni galley from **Salladhor Saan**, named **Mad Prendos**, waits beyond the point.
10. **Ser Andrew accompanies the child by sea.** Edric leaves Dragonstone with his cousin **Andrew Estermont**; the other men support the release and provide cover. **The chapter shows their departure through the postern but does not depict landfall or identify a final refuge abroad**.
11. **The guards are to be released after the ship sails.** Davos tells **Ser Rolland** to put them in a cellar and free them once Edric is safely underway. This is a deliberate plan to minimize bloodshed while keeping the extraction concealed.
12. **Davos then goes voluntarily to confront Stannis.** He does not flee with the boy. He climbs the Stone Drum to the **Chamber of the Painted Table**, lights a fire, opens windows to look for Mad Prendos, recognizes constellations including the **Ice Dragon's blue north-star eye**, and waits knowing he may be executed.
13. **The king and priestess are still arguing about the third leech target.** As they enter, Stannis says a number of three must be treated carefully; Melisandre says she saw **Joffrey dying and Cersei mourning in the nightfire**. **Davos supplies independent intelligence that Joffrey is actually dead**, but reports the cause as choking or possible poison; the alleged culprit Tyrion is a **received rumor**.
14. **Melisandre treats Joffrey as the third validation.** She calls the fulfillment a sign R'hllor requires **Edric's kingsblood**. Stannis asks how Davos knew of Joffrey and then acknowledges **Tommen will likely be crowned**, frustrating any hope the realm instantly sends for him. **The deaths of three named kings do not prove the leeches caused them**; at least two mundane killing mechanisms have been separately witnessed.
15. **Stannis provides firsthand cat-cruelty testimony about Joffrey.** He recalls Joffrey **cutting open a pregnant kitchen cat** to find its kittens, then showing the result to Robert, who struck him violently. This is a **more specific eyewitness-style account than Cersei's shorter report in [ASOS 53 Tyrion](53-tyrion.md)**. It establishes disturbing childhood brutality without identifying who later arranged the catspaw attack on Bran.
16. **Stannis is tempted by the sacrifice and insists on certainty.** He demands Melisandre **swear on her life** that there is no alternative. She calls him the prophesied prince whose failure would doom the world; she invokes **the red comet** and ancient promised hero language. These are **religious interpretation and coercive stakes**, not a verified identity test.
17. **Davos reveals the operation after it has begun.** He tells Stannis **Edric is aboard a Lyseni galley already out at sea**, explicitly identifying himself as the organizer. **Melisandre appears dismayed**, a striking counterexample to claims her visions disclose every danger to her aims; her private internal experience cannot be read beyond her visible reaction.
18. **Stannis considers the action treason.** He reminds Davos that he raised him from humble origins, and worries Saan could use the boy as leverage for money. Davos asserts Edric is **out of reach**; the chapter cannot independently verify the ship has reached a place no ship could intercept.
19. **Davos's central argument uses the actual terms of his oath.** Stannis required him to give honest counsel, obey and **protect the king's people**. Davos argues **Edric is one of those people** and that preventing his immolation fulfills, rather than violates, the oath. He says the legitimacy of kingship rests on protecting subjects, not sacrificing them for a forecast.
20. **Stannis's dilemma is explicitly quantitative and tragic.** Stannis discusses the need to sacrifice one child if it might save a million from darkness, and Melisandre invokes **Azor Ahai killing Nissa Nissa** as the example of genuine, personally costly sacrifice. Davos insists they are discussing **a specific boy, Shireen's friend and Robert's son**, not a replaceable cow or ritual object. **No literal sacrifice is performed**.
21. **The final political move is the Night's Watch letter.** When Stannis draws **Lightbringer** and threatens Davos with very little time to speak, Davos produces the **black-sealed northern appeal** he encountered in [ASOS 54 Davos](54-davos.md), declares Pylos has been teaching him to read, and **begins reading it aloud to Stannis by the sword's light**. **The precise contents he reads and Stannis's decision about aid are not narrated before this chapter cuts off**.
22. **End state:** Edric has been smuggled onto the galley with Andrew, Davos faces potential punishment, the king has direct access to a previously suppressed Watch letter, and the question of Stannis's northern response remains **OPEN at chapter end**.

## Tests for future chapter review

- **Can a Hand disobey to serve a higher oath?** The text establishes his specific protective justification and serious risk; whether this is legally pardoned has not yet been shown.
- **Leech causal claims:** Three contemporaneous political deaths fit Melisandre's prediction but cannot independently prove the burning of three leeches caused each. Stannis himself challenges the inference.
- **Prophecy's limits:** Melisandre admits fire-shapes are hard to interpret and visibly failed to prevent Edric's escape, making overconfident predictions about the Watch crisis suspect.
- **Northern pivot:** Davos finally transmits the letter, which Alester Florent had suppressed. Do not say **Stannis orders a fleet north yet**; the last line is the start of Davos's reading.
