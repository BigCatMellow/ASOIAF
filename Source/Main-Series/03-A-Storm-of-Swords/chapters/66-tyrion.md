## Chronological evidence and legal-process record

1. **A three-judge court is fixed.** **Tywin Lannister**, **Mace Tyrell** and **Oberyn Martell** judge Tyrion for **regicide and kinslaying**. Kevan claims Tywin appointed the other two to avoid judging only his own son's killing of his grandson. The arrangement is **formal** but does not eliminate political conflicts of interest.
2. **Trial by battle is formally available.** Kevan says Tyrion may demand it, but **Cersei plans to name Gregor Clegane as her champion**. Tyrion sends Podrick to summon Bronn, offering greater gold and reward.
3. **Tyrion is held in a tower and barred from gathering witnesses himself.** Kevan promises to summon those he names, gives paper and ink, and keeps the trial date near. Tyrion's inability to investigate accusations independently is a **procedural disadvantage**, not automatic proof all witnesses lie.
4. **Tyrion initially writes only 'Sansa' as a witness.** He suspects Sansa poisoned Joffrey because she disappeared and had motive, but immediately asks how she could obtain poison or act without accomplices. **His private suspicion is inference, not proof of her guilt**; from Sansa's chapter the reader knows she escaped under Littlefinger's design without a witnessed dose.
5. **Pod remains actively loyal.** He runs on Tyrion's errand and keeps trying to reach Bronn, who delays. Kevan grants him access; unlike Bronn, his presence is **not a contracted political bargain**.
6. **Bronn has a new, immediately valuable match.** Cersei has arranged for the sellsword to marry **Lollys Stokeworth**, pregnant after the King's Landing riots. **Lollys is not the current heir** because her elder married sister **Falyse** stands ahead; Bronn remarks Falyse is childless and could die. His marriage is **upcoming, not yet celebrated in this chapter**.
7. **Bronn refuses to fight Gregor for Tyrion.** He realistically assesses Gregor's strength, reach and speed, envisages exhausting or toppling him, but says one error means death and victory would cost his promised marriage and position. Tyrion cannot offer an equivalent castle and bride. **Bronn's refusal is explicitly transactional, not proof he could not possibly defeat Gregor**.
8. **Tyrion considers alternative champions but cannot fetch them in time.** **Shagga** is hidden in the kingswood and **Timett** likely returned to the Vale; the likely trial date and Pod's limited tracking skill make either impractical.
9. **The trial begins in the throne room.** **Ser Addam Marbrand and a dozen gold cloaks** escort Tyrion, while most Kingsguard are to serve as witnesses. High Septon offers prayers. Tywin directs when Tyrion may speak, and frequently silences him.
10. **The initial questioning records Tyrion's denials.** Tyrion says he did not kill Joffrey; on whether Sansa did it he claims the king choked on pigeon pie. This defense is intentionally sarcastic and provokes damaging reactions. It does **not constitute a clinical choking diagnosis**.
11. **Ser Balon Swann opens with favorable character testimony.** He attests Tyrion fought bravely during the Blackwater and he does not believe Tyrion murdered Joffrey, then confirms Tyrion previously **struck Joffrey during the riot**. His testimony combines real exculpatory opinion with genuinely damaging conduct.
12. **Meryn Trant and Boros Blount present a more incriminating version of past events.** They describe Tyrion striking, kicking and threatening the king and warning about Aerys, leaving out Joffrey's brutal punishment of Sansa. These are **selective accounts of real confrontations**, not demonstrations of poison at the wedding.
13. **All three Kettleblack brothers testify.** **Osney and Osfryd** report threats against Cersei made at an earlier supper; they omit **Alayaya's prior suffering**, which supplied context. **Osmund** claims Joffrey predicted Tyrion intended to seize his throne. Tyrion publicly calls him a liar, but the claim has **no independent utterance witness recorded here**.
14. **Tyrion's interruptions worsen his apparent reliability.** Tywin threatens shackles and gagging after repeated interjections. A hostile process can coexist with Tyrion making strategically damaging responses; the text records both.
15. **The second day supplies actual medical testimony.** **Maesters Ballabar and Frenken** testify they opened Joffrey's corpse and found **no pigeon pie or other food obstruction lodged in his throat**. They diagnose **poisoning**. This is stronger than Tywin's earlier report, though no chemical assay is narrated.
16. **Pycelle inventories stolen substances.** From small jars he names **greycap, nightshade, sweetsleep, demon's dance, blindeye, widow's blood, wolfsbane, basilisk venom and tears of Lys** as medicines or toxins Tyrion previously took from his rooms. **The theft is tied to Tyrion's earlier imprisonment of Pycelle**, not proof any displayed substance was used to kill Joffrey.
17. **Pycelle identifies a different rare toxin for suffocation.** He says the listed substances **would not close a man's breathing**, whereas a rare poison the Citadel calls **'the strangler'** would. Pycelle explicitly acknowledges **this substance was not among the recovered jars**, then accuses Tyrion of using it all. **Important: medical theory names a candidate toxin, but no actual sample or chain of custody is presented.**
18. **Other witnesses testify to distinct observations.** **Lord Redwyne, Lord Celtigar and Ser Flement Brax** report prior threats; servers, entertainers and nobles confirm Tyrion **filled the chalice**; **Lady Merryweather** claims she saw him **drop something into the wine while the pie was cut**; others witnessed him **empty the cup's remaining wine** after Joffrey fell. **Merryweather's alleged administration is contested direct testimony**, not corroborated by Tyrion's firsthand scene.
19. **The prosecution uses a compelling but incomplete pattern.** Tyrion was openly humiliated, visibly bore the chalice, had previously taken other poisons, and emptied remaining wine. Yet **Pycelle cannot show the strangler jar**, and some testimony ignores Tyrion's forced service, Joffrey's access to pie and unrelated guests' movement on the dais.
20. **Tywin proposes a coerced confession bargain.** Kevan offers taking the black instead of execution if Tyrion admits guilt and repents. Tyrion cites the failure of Ned Stark's comparable plea, although Kevan argues Tywin was absent from that decision. **This is an offer, not a bargain Tyrion accepts**.
21. **Kevan's defense of Tywin reveals deep family loyalty.** He recounts **Tytos Lannister's weakness, his mistress wearing Joanna's mother's jewels, Aerys's slights and Tywin's long service as Hand**, framing Tywin as just and necessary. Tyrion notes the unusually fervent tone. It is **Kevan's partisan family history**, not independent moral exoneration of Tywin.
22. **Varys produces extensive written evidence.** On the third day, he reads records allegedly compiled by **his little birds**, recording Tyrion's past threats, Pycelle episode and remarks about replacing Joffrey with Tommen. His sources and full transcription are **not produced for cross-examination on page**; Varys does not actually witness the poisoning.
23. **Oberyn meets Tyrion privately after the third day.** He proposes a political reinterpretation of Cersei's plan: **Oberyn himself** might be suspect given his familiarity with poison and desire to block Tyrell influence, while **Dornish succession law** could favor **Myrcella** over younger Tommen. This is a **possible rival royal claim**, not a crown ceremony or actual Doran order.
24. **Oberyn knows Cersei has hinted at marriage.** He rejects marrying her, despite **Ellaria's interest**, and recounts the story of a Tyrell viceroy in Dorne killed by **scorpions released from a bed canopy**. This is the prince's political history story, not independently documented here.
25. **Oberyn seeks justice for Elia and her children, beyond killing the immediate murderer.** Tyrion says **Amory Lorch** killed Rhaenys and was killed by a bear; **Gregor Clegane** killed Aegon and raped/murdered Elia. Oberyn understands **Tywin as the person who ordered or sponsored the Sack's crimes**; Tyrion denies his father's orders, and the narrative explicitly states he knowingly lies at that moment.
26. **Oberyn offers to act as Tyrion's champion.** He believes the proceedings are politically biased, knows **Gregor Clegane** would oppose the accused, and offers his sword **as champion rather than as judge**. The chapter ends with the offer; **combat, acceptance terms and result are not yet shown**.
27. **End state:** Tyrion's guilt remains unproved by reliable firsthand administration evidence despite mounting testimony; a named poison candidate ('strangler') is available in medical lore, Bronn will not fight, and **Oberyn makes a potentially decisive intervention**, partly motivated by vengeance against Gregor and Tywin.

## Theory stress tests

- **Does the strangler explain Joffrey's symptoms?** Pycelle supports airway poisoning in principle, but **no sample is shown**. Revisit purple hairnet gem, Olenna contact, Littlefinger's account, and both wine/pie ingestion routes without conflating proof of ingredient with proof of administrator.
- **Is Tyrion framed?** His actual actions and inflammatory past threats offer plausible grounds for suspicion; **Lady Merryweather's uncorroborated alleged wine-drop**, omissions of context, and Varys's prepared dossiers support a biased-proceedings theory.
- **Oberyn's motives:** Potential defense of Tyrion serves his vendetta against Gregor and Tywin. It need not imply affectionate commitment to Tyrion or an agreed Dornish succession plan.
- **Dornish Myrcella prospect:** Identify whether the law applies beyond Dorne and whether Doran actually advocates such a claim before elevating Oberyn's hypothetical to an active coup.
