## Chronological plot and causal decisions

1. **VOYAGE FROM QARTH IS REAL:** Daenerys and her small khalasar are now actually at sea aboard **Balerion** (the former **Saduleon**), with trading galleys **Vhagar and Meraxes**. Their ships sail for **Pentos**, nominally returning to the patron **Illyrio Mopatis** after the rescue described in [ACOK 63 Daenerys](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/63-daenerys.md). Most Dothraki and their horses are frightened by open water; Irri and Jhiqui become seasick. Dany herself enjoys sailing and compares it to the freedom she longed for during her childhood flights among Free Cities.
2. **TRADE SHIPS ARE NOT DANY'S OWN PROPERTY:** The three vessels are **Illyrio's**, crewed by his captains and carrying **valuable merchandise** as well as Dany's entourage. **Captain Groleo** is the Pentoshi captain of Balerion, experienced and initially wary of carrying dragons. Any later decision to change their course or liquidate cargo may breach agreements and creates a concrete question of **control over the ships versus the ruler's assertion of command**.
3. **DRAGON FREEDOM AND SMALL FIRES:** Groleo initially demanded the dragons **be caged**, and Dany reluctantly consented but then saw them miserable and ordered them **released**. The crew grew proud of watching them fly; one small shipboard fire was easily put out, and Balerion's rat population appeared to dwindle. These are observed changes in husbandry and behavior; **no completed major shipboard fire or passenger death is shown**.
4. **BODY SIZE VS WINGS:** **Rhaegal** is green with bronze and **Viserion** cream with gold bones/horns; **Drogon**, the black dragon, is a little larger, hunts alone and has killed flying fish with flame. All three are still **about the size of small dogs, light relative to their wings**, and Dany estimates **another year or two before Drogon might be rideable**. The timetable is her hopeful prediction, **not demonstrated growth law or a scheduled first flight**.
5. **ANIMAL FLIGHT AND WATER:** Rhaegal and Viserion spar high above the sea, diving into each other and sometimes splashing into water before flying again, **steam rising off their scales**. Dany first fears they mean to kill each other but learns the contests are **play**. The water contact is directly observed; nothing here proves dragons require no air, cannot drown or could remain underwater indefinitely.
6. **BECALMED AND TOWED:** The fleet has been without wind for **six days and nights, entering a seventh**, requiring the **two galleys of 200 oars each** to tow the much broader sail-powered cog. Overcrowding, horse feed and loaded holds magnify the journey's cost. The return of the **wind later in the chapter** is an ordinary observed weather change, not explicitly linked to dragon magic.
7. **ARSTAN AND THE DRAGONPIT:** **Arstan Whitebeard** cites **Balerion the Black Dread's age of about 200 years**, his great size, and a claim that **dragons grow throughout life if fed and free**. He contrasts the ancient wild/large dragons with descendants confined in the **Dragonpit on Rhaenys's Hill**, which maesters thought stayed smaller under a roof and within walls. This is **historical expertise plus an attributed maester theory**, not a controlled experiment proving captivity directly restricts dragon growth. **Jorah disputes its logic by analogy with humans**.
8. **THE DRAGONPIT IS AN ACTUAL LOCATION:** Arstan describes a formerly immense **domed dragon enclosure with vast iron doors** atop **Rhaenys's Hill**, now ruined. The pits and dragon skulls are tangible points for future King's Landing history. **No surviving beast within the pit is claimed**.
9. **ARSTAN HAD ACCESS TO AERYS'S COURT:** Arstan says he **served in King's Landing during Aerys II**, saw dragon skulls and met the king. He describes Aerys as sometimes pleasant but **very harsh toward people he judged enemies**. He is careful when Dany asks if her father was good and gentle. The hedging constitutes an informed participant warning, **not a categorical confession of all Aerys's crimes or of Arstan's true court office**.
10. **RHAEGAR'S COMPANIONS NAMED:** Arstan knows about Rhaegar's **silver-stringed harp**, says his squires were **Myles Mooton** and then **Richard Lonmouth**, and identifies **Jon Connington** as dear to the prince and **Arthur Dayne** as his oldest friend. These named relationships are unusually precise historical clues. They should be indexed **as Arstan's informed account**, not immediately upgraded to a full provenance record for his claimed identity.
11. **RHAEGAR BECOMES A WARRIOR BECAUSE OF READING:** Arstan says the child Rhaegar was **bookish, solitary and absorbed in scrolls**, until he found **something unspecified in his reading** and then requested sword and armor from **Ser Willem Darry**, stating that it seemed he must become a warrior. The **triggering document, interpretation and prophecy are not disclosed**, making it a high-priority future investigation. **Do not assert it was a definite Azor Ahai prophecy, marriage secret or specific book without source evidence**.
12. **REALISTIC CHALLENGE TO 'PEERLESS':** Arstan cautions Dany that even renowned tourney champions can lose through ground, diet, weather, or a favor tied on a knight's arm. He alludes to **Jorah's Lannisport jousting victory with Lynesse Hightower's favor**, which angers Jorah because his marriage failed. Arstan's objection is to **myths of unvarying martial perfection**, not a statement that Rhaegar was unskilled.
13. **JORA H SUSPECTS A HIDDEN IDENTITY:** Jorah argues Arstan is **too old, frank, educated and competent with his staff** to be a mere attendant to **Strong Belwas**. Dany notes Arstan already saved her from the Qartheen manticore, while Jorah proposes **the rescuer and attacker could have collaborated to win her confidence**. This is a possible deception mechanism **without any direct evidence they actually cooperated with the assassin**; Arstan's growing credibility and Jorah's jealousy must both be assessed.
14. **RISK TO THE DRAGON QUEEN:** Dany privately lists potential enemies: a **Robert-backed poisoned wine attempt**, a **manticore assassin** she attributes to the **Sorrowful Men/Pyat Pree**, **Xaro's designs on her dragons**, hostile Dothraki khalasars, and ambiguous **Quaithe**. Her attribution of the manticore's commissioning to Pyat is now **Dany's belief**, not testimony from the escaped attacker, who was never interrogated.
15. **THE DRAGONFIRE COMMAND 'DRACARYS':** After wind returns and that night, Dany deliberately trains **Drogon** to respond to **dracarys**, a **High Valyrian command meaning dragonfire**. He burns tossed salted pork; when **Jorah repeats the word**, all three dragons turn and **Viserion releases pale gold flame** without further warning. This demonstrates **a learnable acoustic command but imperfect dragon-specific discrimination and genuine bystander danger**. Dany chose a rare-language word to avoid accidental use.
16. **JORAH'S THREE BETRAYAL INTERPRETATION:** Jorah warns Dany the Undying foretold **treasons for blood, gold and love**, interprets **Mirri Maz Duur as the first**, and raises **Illyrio, Arstan and Belwas** as candidates for future treachery. These are **Jorah's hypotheses**, not confirmation that any given individual represents the 'gold' or 'love' betrayal. He may be accurately cautious and personally possessive simultaneously.
17. **DEPENDENCE ON ILLYRIO:** Jorah notes Illyrio **owns the ships, their cargo and many personnel**, and returning straight to Pentos would put Dany **physically in Illyrio's household and power**. The argument establishes a genuine asymmetry; it does not prove Illyrio intends to imprison, sell or murder her. Dany replies that **no ruler can govern while trusting nobody**, a defensible political principle that still requires safeguards.
18. **THE ASTAPOR DIVERSION PROPOSED:** Jorah urges Dany **order Groleo to sail to Astapor in Slaver's Bay** first, secure an army of **Unsullied**, then approach Illyrio from a position of independent force. Dany initially knows only the soft, aging eunuch guards seen in Free Cities; Jorah explains the unsullied as **disciplined infantry trained to fight in ranks**, not the same as household retainers.
19. **THREE THOUSAND OF QOHOR STORY:** Jorah recounts an older battle when **3,000 Unsullied** supposedly resisted **18 Dothraki charges** by a khalasar led by **Temmo**, leaving around **600 Unsullied standing** and inflicting enormous Dothraki losses, with survivors offering cut braids. This is a highly specific **historical account from Jorah**, not a contemporaneous POV or independently verified casualty census. It supports a plausible **spear-and-shield military doctrine** against mounted raiders.
20. **AN OPENLY PROPOSED APPROPRIATION OF CARGO:** Dany has little free cash aside from a gifted crown. Jorah points out Illyrio's holds contain **silk, tiger skins, jade, amber, spices, myrrh and other costly trade goods**, argues that if Illyrio is truly a friend he should not object to Dany spending them on her cause, and frames refusal as evidence of bad faith. Dany correctly identifies **the goods are Illyrio's, not hers**. The plan involves **taking or trading a patron's merchandise without his on-page consent**, an important character-agency and trust test rather than costless funding.
21. **REAL PLAN, NOT COMPLETED NAVIGATION:** Dany decides to **order the captain to set course for Astapor**, but **the chapter ends before a command to Groleo, a change in heading, landing or purchase occurs**. Her willingness marks strategic independence from Illyrio while creating fresh risk of ship resistance and the ethical implications of buying enslaved soldiers.
22. **JORAH'S UNINVITED KISS:** As Dany dresses to find Groleo, Jorah **grabs and kisses her without invitation**, holding her firmly. She thinks he **should not be doing it**, steps away immediately afterward, covers herself and instructs him to use **'Your Grace'**, not her familiar name. Her involuntary bodily response and his insistence he should have kissed her earlier **do not establish consent**. It is a major boundary violation by an older sworn protector who controls some of her safety.
23. **THREE DRAGON RIDERS AS A SELF-SERVING ARGUMENT:** Jorah then proposes that the **three heads of the dragon** refer to **Dany and two husbands** riding the three dragons, citing **Aegon with sister-wives Rhaenys and Visenya**, and implies **he himself** is the ideal husband and trusted rider. The prophecy interpretation is **his proposal immediately after the uninvited kiss**, not on-page confirmation Dany is destined to marry Jorah or that all riders must be spouses.
24. **ENDING MORAL/STRATEGIC BOUNDARY:** Dany has displayed insight into **honest counsel versus controlling suspicion**, established small-scale dragonfire training, learned significant Rhaegar history and committed in words to an Astapor detour. Neither **Arstan's true identity, Illyrio's intentions, the outcome of any Unsullied acquisition, nor Jorah's intimate claim** has been resolved.

## Open theory questions and later verification

- **Rhaegar's pivotal reading:** This is the clearest chapter-level evidence of a **book/scroll influencing Rhaegar's life decision**. Candidate interpretations include a prophecy or family history, but the **actual passage has not been named**. Compare later testimonies rather than importing later theory labels as fact.
- **Dragonpit and growth:** The 'captivity stunts dragons' rule is an **in-world historical/maester hypothesis**, albeit one based on real size differences, not a proven physical necessity. Investigate alternative causes (feeding, war deaths, age, lineage) and how Drogon grows in freedom.
- **Arstan's real background:** His specific Aerys-era firsthand proximity, Rhaegar knowledge and modestly coded criticism form a strong identity question. **Jorah's attack on his credentials does not identify him by itself**.
- **Jorah's motivation:** His advice to avoid complete dependence on Illyrio can be sound and also serve his interests; the uninvited kiss and self-serving prophecy interpretation are evidence **he wants control over intimate and political access to Dany**. Do not retroactively assume all his military information is fabricated.
- **Unsullied ethics:** The chapter moves Dany toward **purchasing enslaved soldiers**, a serious complication for a queen who rejects some forms of slave trade. Track the precise contract, the sellers, agency of purchased people and what she actually does on landing before judging the future act.
