## Causal and chronological close reading

1. **Arya and Sandor reach the gate approach, not the inner hall.** Continuing from [ASOS 50 Arya](50-arya.md), the pork wagon passes the soldiers' feast tents and heads toward the **Twins' portcullis and drawbridge**. Arya sees the drawbridge down and portcullis rising, seemingly offering entry despite a Bolton sergeant's earlier statement that the gate was closed to ordinary deliveries.
2. **Sandor recognizes danger before Arya does.** As the cart wheel begins sinking in mud he suddenly curses, **knocks Arya off the wagon** and tears up a concealed swordbelt under the wagon seat. He is preparing to fight, not merely acting because she disobeys him.
3. **Armored riders sortie from the Twins.** A long column moves out of the gate **four abreast**, carrying torches and **heavy spiked longaxes**. Their approach occurs in darkness and floodwater, accompanied by booming music; unlike earlier patrols, this is an obvious military deployment.
4. **Arya perceives a wolf's howl with exceptional intensity.** A remote howling sound cuts through the river, rain and music and stirs her with grief and anger; she wonders whether she **heard it through her ears at all**. A plausible skinchanging/sensory connection is suggested, but **no explicit change into Nymeria or Grey Wind is narrated**.
5. **The outer soldiers' feasts are attacked systematically.** The middle of **three enormous feast tents collapses**; the other two are brought down and all catch fire amid **incendiary arrows**. Separate fires erupt in barracks and numerous silk pavilions. At least one **barrel of flammable material is launched by a castle engine** and increases the flames. These are directly witnessed military acts, not rumor.
6. **The music synchronizes between castles.** Unlike [ASOS 50 Arya](50-arya.md)'s competing tunes, both castles play the same recognizable melody, **'The Rains of Castamere'**, immediately recognizable to Arya because **Tom Sevenstrings performed it at the brewhouse**. It is consistent with [ASOS 51 Catelyn](51-catelyn.md)'s interior switch of music.
7. **The song's significance is concrete, not merely literary.** A Lannister victory emblem plays while Frey forces kill Stark-aligned men. **This shows coordination between music and assault**, though it does not identify every organizer or prove individual Lannisters were present.
8. **Three Frey riders turn toward Arya's wagon.** Their surcoats carry **the twin towers**. Sandor cuts **Stranger** loose, mounts him, and uses sword and horse to defend himself and Arya. His fighting is a visible intervention even though she remains his captive and he has threatened her repeatedly.
9. **Arya hesitates about whom to attack.** She holds a mud-slick rock without remembering picking it up, aware Sandor murdered Mycah yet now facing Freys attacking them both. The conflict is directly narrated and does not imply she has forgiven the Hound.
10. **One rider attempts to kill Arya.** A Frey knight wearing the towers drives a **longaxe** at her, ignoring her protest that his house is allied to her brother. She throws a rock and strikes his helmet's side, deflecting the charge but not overcoming him.
11. **Arya circles the wagon to avoid being ridden down.** She relies on speed and mud despite illness, invoking Syrio's lessons and earlier throw at Gendry. This is **active self-defense**, not an effortless knightly victory.
12. **Sandor kills the pursuer with a seized axe.** Returning on Stranger, he strikes the attacking Frey in the **back of the head**, destroying his helmet and skull. He appears to have obtained the axe from a prior rider. This is a witnessed **lethal rescue of Arya**, separable from his material ransom interest.
13. **The other two Frey attackers are neutralized.** One lies motionless with broken sword in his throat; one is trapped beneath a dying horse and drowning in shallow water. The chapter does not prove each body has been formally counted as dead by another witness, but their threat has ended.
14. **Sandor puts on his dog-head helm.** He tells Arya to retrieve it from a sack of dried apples concealed behind the pork/pigs' feet. He stops pretending to be a farmer once survival depends on intimidation and combat. The shift from hidden identity to recognizable **steel dog** has direct tactical consequence.
15. **The slaughter is unmistakably beyond the gate.** Arya sees burning tents, people chased down, armed riders, and widespread battle between Frey forces and men she recognized as northmen. What she had interpreted as a joyful gathering is now clearly a **mass coordinated attack on guest soldiers**.
16. **The exterior echoes Catelyn's hall scene but has different sight lines.** Arya hears the song, a remaining slow drum and a wolf, and witnesses attack on common soldiery and tents, but **does not see Robb stabbed, Jinglebell's death or Catelyn's throat cut**. Her view is independent support for the scale and coordination of the Red Wedding, not a replay of the hall from omniscience.
17. **Sandor concludes Robb has been killed.** He states the attackers would not massacre Robb's followers and leave their king alive. This is a **reasonable inference** and corroborated directly for the reader by [ASOS 51 Catelyn](51-catelyn.md), but at the exact moment Arya hears it Sandor has not seen the hall.
18. **Arya refuses to relinquish her chance of family reunion.** Even amid fires, she insists the gate is open and Catelyn and Robb might still be inside; she offers the possibility of saving her mother. Sandor warns she would die if she enters. His willingness to leave and her determination to continue reflect opposed risk tolerances.
19. **The Freys are closing the bridge again.** As Arya runs, the **portcullis descends** and the **drawbridge rises**, making entry physically more difficult. The rain-swollen moat/water is at horse-height in places. These are visible siege defenses being reconfigured under Frey control.
20. **Arya flees Sandor when he pursues.** She briefly runs toward the gate for her mother; seeing **Stranger and Sandor bearing the bloody longaxe** behind her, she ultimately runs for her own survival, explicitly comparing herself to **Mycah fleeing Sandor**. This deepens the trauma and cyclical imagery without proving his ultimate lethal intent.
21. **The chapter ends in an axe blow to the back of Arya's head.** The narrator says **Sandor's axe struck her there**. **CRITICAL: The text does not state whether the blade or blunt side struck her, whether there is an open wound, or whether Arya died.** Treat as **INJURED / CONDITION UNCONFIRMED**, not verified execution.
22. **Sandor's precise motive for striking her is unspoken.** A serious possibility is to prevent her running into certain death at the Twins; another is violent self-preservation and coercion of his captive. **No internal Sandor POV or explicit reassurance resolves the choice inside this chapter**.
23. **End state:** The Twins are in open massacre and its approaches contested. **Robb and Catelyn are already confirmed dead to the reader by the prior POV**, but Arya has not met them and cannot verify their deaths herself. Sandor has intervened against Frey killers and then struck Arya; her survival, capture status and next destination must be established in later narrative.

## Cross-POV theory stress tests

- **The Ghost of High Heart:** The wolf grief, wedding music and mass screaming in [ASOS 43 Arya](43-arya.md) now have directly narrated counterparts. This raises confidence in her imagery's referents; it **does not prove how she saw the future or assign causation**.
- **What killed the Stark army?** Separate killed leaders in the hall from burned/fired-on soldiers outside. Do not count the entire 3,500 marching force as killed without a survivor/roll study.
- **Sandor's choice:** Defending Arya from a Frey and striking her with an axe coexist. They may reflect a protective intervention that she mistakes for murder or brutal coercion; only subsequent text can adjudicate.
- **Arya's own perception of death:** She has just lost a realistic chance to reach family and hears a grief-laden wolf cry. Her subjective state is not a verified vision of her family's actual exact final moments.
