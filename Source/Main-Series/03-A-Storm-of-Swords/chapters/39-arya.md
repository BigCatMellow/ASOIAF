## Sequential and causal reading

1. **The Brotherhood attacks a fortified religious holding at dawn.** A Brave Companion sentry watches from the roof of a septry; **Anguy kills him**, while **Kyle and Notch** kill two guards by the door. The force waits among wood and ditches, lighting fires with arrows and then attacking once its presence is known. **Arya and Gendry are deliberately held back** with Beardless Dick and Mudge, rather than participating as front-line fighters.
2. **The tactical setting is specific.** The site includes a **septry, mill, brewhouse, stables and running water**, amid burned trees and weeds. Mist, crossbow fire and flames complicate sight. Arya observes **bodkin arrowheads puncture mail/plate**, while other shafts are broadheads. This produces an immediate wish to learn archery, not an accomplished new skill.
3. **The defenders are diverse Brave Companions.** Arya sees **Ibbenese with shields**, a Dothraki with arakh and bells, tattooed Volantenes and a Myrish crossbowman. They fight and are cut down, while **two Dothraki escape on horses with Beric's deliberate permission** so they can tell Vargo and Roose at Harrenhal. The raid is a deliberate message and disruption, not an attempt to kill every witness.
4. **The Brotherhood pays real combat costs.** **Watty** falls, **Kyle** is pierced by a spear, and individual casualty counts for all sides are not given. **Lem kills the spearman who struck Kyle**; Beric and Thoros fight with burning swords, and the young squire **Ned** also fights. Arya resents the fact that Ned can fight while she must wait.
5. **Eight brown brothers and Septon Utt are recovered.** **Jack-Be-Lucky, Harwin and Merrit o' Moontown** rescue eight religious captives from the burning building. **Septon Utt**, a Brave Companion wearing chainmail over grey robes, is found hiding by the cellar stairs. He is not one of the eight legitimate brothers.
6. **Utt's crimes are investigated in the outlaws' swift proceedings.** Arya remembers hearing from **Shagwell** at Harrenhal that Utt assaulted and killed boys, praying afterward and sometimes encouraging the Mummers to scourge him. Witnesses describe such victims; Utt weeps and acknowledges sins but shifts agency to personal weakness. **Specific confessions, victims and charges should not be replaced by an assumption that his religious title protects him.**
7. **Beric orders trial, then hangings.** The Brotherhood says to kill the dying mercifully and binds surviving enemies for judgment. Following testimony about theft, torture, sexual assault and murder, **Septon Utt and other captive Mummers are hanged from a tree**. Some resist, and others offer to betray gold or join the Brotherhood. The executions are on page; the evidentiary rigor of these rapid trials is limited.
8. **This justice contrasts the previous Hound trial.** Sandor won combat and was freed despite the direct admission of Mycah's killing in [ASOS 34 Arya](34-arya.md); Utt and the Mummers are convicted through narrative testimony and summarily executed. That inconsistency between modes of justice and procedural ideals should remain visible.
9. **The septry is structurally destroyed.** Fire causes its walls to fail beneath a slate roof. The elder brother reports the original **forty-four brothers** and the house's cattle, beehives, vineyard and apple trees have been devastated through repeated wartime plunder. Now **eight remain**. The site is not reconstituted simply by eliminating its latest abusive occupiers.
10. **The surviving brother confesses divulging hidden gold.** An earlier attacker suspected more treasure, killed religious men one by one, and the survivor eventually revealed where gold was concealed. **Thoros says he should have disclosed it sooner**, challenging false ideals that protecting property merits the deaths of innocents.
11. **They sleep at the brewhouse and honor hospitality.** A young novice objects to Thoros praying to another god. Lem says their rescue should entitle them to religious autonomy; **Beric orders respect for the brothers' house rules**, and Thoros complies. This is a counterexample to soldiers treating a host's roof as permission to dictate worship.
12. **Beric now lives with major bodily and cognitive costs.** Arya observes he **rarely eats**, takes occasional wine, apparently never sleeps normally, remains scarred and wears breastplate/scarf over wounds. He is not restored to ordinary human health despite surviving his latest wound.
13. **Thoros explicitly counts six restorations.** At Beric's prompting he says he has brought him back **six times**, and warns each becomes harder, with another death potentially fatal to both. Beric attributes earlier wounds to **Burton Crakehall's mace**, the **Manticore's hanging at Rushing Falls** after taking a beekeeper couple hostage, and **Gregor Clegane's dirk through his visor**. The most recent **Sandor sword blow** from ASOS 34 is an additional on-page event. Precise classification of the full six deaths is not completely enumerated in a witnessed chronology.
14. **Beric is losing autobiographical memory.** He no longer recalls his castle's location, his intended bride's hair color, who knighted him or favorite foods. He remembers an earlier near-death on grass with a hole in his chest. **Repeated revival correlates with fading memory by his own admission**, but a systematic causal mechanism is not demonstrated.
15. **Thoros describes the first resurrection rite.** Believing Beric already dead, he performed **the last kiss**: filled his mouth with fire and breathed it into the dead man's throat/lungs. Beric unexpectedly shuddered and opened his eyes. This is the **priest's firsthand testimony of a ritual**, distinct from independently observing the actual first performance or proving which deity caused it.
16. **Arya asks about restoring a decapitated person.** She plainly wants Thoros to bring back **Ned Stark** and asks whether a man without a head could be restored even once. Thoros says he has only prayers and offers no such possibility. **There is no restoration of Ned**; the exchange marks the limits of Arya's hopes and our current evidence.
17. **Ransom remains the Brotherhood's strategy.** Beric promises Arya he will aim to return her to **Catelyn**, explaining they need ransom income and bought **grain, seed, horses and weapons** with Sandor's seized gold, including purchases south of the Mander. He mentions possible temporary guardianship with Lady Smallwood or his own house of Blackhaven if ransom fails. **No meeting with Robb or mother takes place yet.**
18. **Arya fears her family may not accept her.** She worries about tangled hair, dirty nails, degraded manners, and earlier killings that she thinks might make her mother disappointed. These are **private fears**, not evidence that Robb or Catelyn actually refuse her return.
19. **Gendry voluntarily asks to join.** He wants to smith for the Brotherhood despite Beric's advice that he might find safer work at Riverrun. He explicitly cites Beric's ideal of **Robert's men as brothers** and the fairness, as he perceives it, of giving the Hound a trial. **No wage**, only food, shelter and a forge, is acceptable to him.
20. **Beric knights Gendry at the brewhouse.** He uses a sword without setting it alight and instructs Gendry to defend those who cannot defend themselves, especially women and children. Gendry accepts and is pronounced **Ser Gendry, knight of the hollow hill**. This is a **completed ritual**, not a future knighthood inferred from his abilities.
21. **Sandor returns seeking his money.** He enters despite guards posted, with his burn bandaged, and demands repayment of the gold confiscated when he was freed. Beric says the Brotherhood gave him a **written promise to pay after the war** and spent the coins on grain and seed. The distinction between an honored promissory note and theft is disputable; Sandor rejects the note entirely.
22. **The Brotherhood debates sanction against Sandor.** Men threaten him, but Beric refuses to kill someone whose life was preserved by the outcome of their own trial. He orders that **if Sandor trails them again, Anguy and Beardless Dick should kill his horse**, not Sandor. This is a direct demonstration of loyalty to judicial rules even when inconvenient.
23. **Sandor's situation is one of near-total social exclusion.** Thoros observes he has lost both his master (Joffrey) and social place and cannot safely return to Lannisters, Robb, or Gregor. His gold could be his final portable security; Beric's confiscation may have material survival consequences. **Thoros's account of his options is inference**, not Sandor's autobiographical confession.
24. **A sleeping guard fails, but survives.** The Hound slipped past **Puddingfoot**, who is later found sleeping and unharmed. His threat to have killed guards is not substantiated. The Brotherhood's command structure has significant local failures even while successfully winning the septry assault.
25. **Anguy's fortune from the Hand's tourney is reported squandered.** He won prize money but spent much on hospitality and women including **Dancy, Jayde and Alayaya**, keeping boots and a dagger. This joins [ASOS 38 Tyrion](38-tyrion.md)'s Chataya references with earlier city details, without assigning conspiracy meaning to ordinary names.
26. **Arya again recites death names and holds Jaqen's coin.** She struggles to recall faces of some distant enemies while Gregor, Sandor, Joffrey and Cersei remain vivid. The remembered dying are personally important, and **her targets' actual fates are not established by the ritual**.
27. **Numerous wolves howl near the brewhouse at night.** Arya hears what she thinks may be dozens or more, but scouting next morning **finds neither wolves nor hounds**. This can support a **Nymeria-pack proximity** hypothesis; no animal is observed there directly through an external witness or Bran-like skinchanging scene.
28. **Gendry apologizes after choosing the Brotherhood.** Arya rejects him outwardly and says she will be ransomed to Riverrun, but the thought that he could have served Robb privately pains her. This depicts friendship and class-framed abandonment, not proof romantic intent.
29. **End state:** The Brotherhood has killed Utt and many Mummers, rescued eight brothers, and continued toward Riverrun. Beric's repeated revival and declining memory are documented; Gendry is a newly knighted recruit; Arya remains an unwilling ransom traveler with Sandor and possible wolves nearby.

## Countercases and theory tests

- **Resurrection and identity:** Repeated revival demonstrably differs from healing an ordinary wound; however whether memory loss is universal, progressive or specific to Beric must be tested elsewhere. 'R'hllor did it' is Thoros's theological inference.
- **Trial integrity:** Beric honors Sandor's judgment but executes others after swift testimony; whether that is consistent practice depends on evidence available for each charge, not mere hatred of Cleganes.
- **Gendry as Baratheon heir:** He chooses his identity as a smith and knight under Beric's Robert-centered cause while his bloodline remains unrecognized within this scene. A political connection is an interpretation, not a disclosed dynastic plan.
- **Wolves:** Heard pack voices are not identification; avoid using this chapter alone as proof Nymeria is near Arya.
- **Ransom and reunion:** Beric promises but earlier escorts died and Arya fears abandonment. The promise does not certify delivery.
