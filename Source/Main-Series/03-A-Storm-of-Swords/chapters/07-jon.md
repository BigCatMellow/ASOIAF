## Chronological source-grounded narrative

1. **ARRIVAL AT THE MILKWATER HOST:** Six days after the Halfhand's death, Jon descends with **Rattleshirt's party, Ygritte, Ghost and other wildlings** toward an enormous camp spread along the **Milkwater**, with **hundreds or thousands of separate fires**. The **blue-grey eagle** is still overhead. Jon wears Night's Watch black among enemies but recalls explicitly he is **infiltrating under Qhorin's order** ([ACOK 68 Jon](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/68-jon.md)), not repudiating that oath.
2. **QHORIN'S EQUIPMENT AS TROPHIES:** Ygritte wears **Qhorin's cloak** and gained his ill-fitting helm from **Longspear Ryk**; **Lenyl** wears his mail, **Ragwyle** his gloves, another bowman his boots, while **Rattleshirt carries his bones and Ebben's head**. These concrete object transfers reinforce the ranger's confirmed death and Jon's survivor guilt. They also show an ad hoc system of distributing defeated enemies' goods rather than a conventional centralized military supply service.
3. **GHOST'S POSITION AND THE DOGS:** Ghost follows or scouts at a distance but remains with Jon; one of Rattleshirt's big hounds attacked him **six days earlier** and fled with a wounded haunch, so others keep their distance. **Wildling dogs' dislike of Ghost** is observed; it is not evidence every free-folk warrior hates Jon.
4. **YGRITTE'S UNDERSTANDING OF FREEDOM:** Ygritte says even a free folk deserter may travel as he chooses, but the rest of the free folk are equally free to **kill him** if they wish. Her practical protection helps Jon reach Mance; **Rattleshirt openly doubts Jon's loyalty** and threatens him. Free-folk liberty includes coercive local violence, not assured autonomy.
5. **JON REAFFIRMS SPY INTENT PRIVATELY:** Jon thinks he will **watch, listen and learn**, then carry knowledge back to the Watch. His primary unresolved task is still to identify **what Mance's people have been digging for** in the Frostfangs and whether they found it. This clear interior intention checks theories portraying him as **already sincerely converted into a free-folk deserter** merely because he travels in the camp.
6. **THE WEEPER AT THE OUTER LINE:** An **eight-person outrider patrol** under the **Weeper**, a fleshy blond man with watery red eyes carrying an unusual curved **scythe**, intercepts the party. The Weeper already knows of other raiders (Rattleshirt, Harma Dogshead, Alfyn Crowkiller). He is a **named independent fighter among Mance's coalition**, not a claim that every group is uniformly commanded or trained.
7. **WILDling TOWN, NOT JUST WAR BAND:** Jon sees **carts, sledges, hide tents, felt shelters, open fires, goats, sheep, oxen, children playing, babies crying and women dancing**, mixed with spear-making, staff practice and **women fletching arrows**. This is direct evidence that Mance leads a **population in migration with families and logistical baggage**, as well as fighters. Calling the entire host a planned invasion army without reference to the civilians misses the causality of food, safety and crowd coordination.
8. **LACK OF CENTRALIZED DEFENSE:** Jon notes the camps run **for miles**, with settlements stopping where they prefer and little collective fortification, no extensive ditches, pits or stakes and only small outrider parties. He expects **Watch discipline could defeat larger numbers** in direct engagement. This is an **experienced ranger trainee's tactical assessment**, not a witnessed battle outcome or proof all the wildlings are militarily incapable.
9. **MANCE'S TENT DESIGNED TO LOOK LIKE KINGSHIP:** The king's largest tent is made of **snow-bear pelts**, crowned by the antlers of a **giant elk**. Two armed guards stand at the entrance and **Ghost must wait outside**. Music and hospitality inside contrast with Rattleshirt's intimidation. The king's central tent is visible, but the coalition has many independent subleaders.
10. **THE FIRST IDENTIFICATION TRAP:** Jon sees a bald, **earless, stern man** studying a map, a gigantic white-bearded man eating hens in **rune-marked arm bands**, a **lute-playing middle-aged man in a patched cloak**, a pregnant woman and a pair of young companions. Jon mistakenly addresses the **earless man** as king; he turns out to be **Styr, Magnar of Thenn**. **Magnar** means lord in the Old Tongue; Jon's mistaken inference about royal appearance is explicitly corrected, and the true king is **the singer**.
11. **COURT IDENTITIES GROUNDED:** The white-bearded raconteur is **Tormund Giantsbane**, who claims multiple extravagant titles including **Horn-blower**, Breaker of Ice and Speaker to Gods; the singer is **Mance Rayder**; **Dalla** is Mance's pregnant partner, her sister **Val** and young **Jarl** also present. Tormund's titles are **his deliberately comic self-presentation**, **not evidence he currently possesses a specific magical horn**. Dalla's pregnancy is visible and directly acknowledged.
12. **MANCE'S KNOWLEDGE OF JON IS EXPLAINED:** Mance calls Jon by name and knows he is Ned's bastard, eventually recounting he has **seen him twice before at Winterfell**. The first visit was as a **Night's Watch escort to Lord Commander Qorgyle** when Jon and Robb planned to dump snow on passing guardsman **Fat Tom**. Jon remembers the incident and Mance kept it secret, providing **independent internal confirmation of one part of the tale**.
13. **AN EVEN MORE STRIKING SECOND VISIT:** Mance says he **secretly crossed the Wall** near **Long Barrow**, carried **a lute and bag of silver**, bought a horse, and joined the **royal procession arriving with Robert** for Ned's appointment as Hand. He sat as an anonymous singer/free rider in the back of Winterfell's hall, taking Ned's bread and watching **the royal children, Jaime, Tyrion, Benjen and Ned's children with wolves**. This is Mance's **detailed self-report** with no separate contemporaneous Jon recognition, but Jon knows the public feast occurred and sees a **real personal memory** partially matched by Mance. His crossing method shows a lone person can cross the Wall despite inability to move an army.
14. **THE BAEL-BARD PARALLEL MADE EXPLICIT:** Jon invokes **Bael the Bard**, whose Winterfell infiltration Ygritte described in [ACOK 51 Jon](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/51-jon.md). Mance directly says the legend **inspired his decision**, while denying he carried off either of Ned's daughters. This is **explicit intertextual recurrence of a legendary disguise tactic**; after [ACOK 69 Bran](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/69-bran.md)'s confirmation of a crypt hiding place, the legend has both an echoed hiding strategy and an admitted bard-infiltration imitation. It **does not certify the entire Bael tale as literal history**.
15. **GUEST RIGHT AT WINTERFELL AND IN THE TENT:** Mance contends Ned would have executed him **if his identity were discovered**, while **having eaten at Ned's table gave him guest protection** in First Men custom. He gestures at the food he has just shared with Jon, promising Jon is **his guest and safe from Mance's hands at least tonight**. This directly establishes **Mance understands and uses guest right**, but the limited promise does **not protect Jon against every wildling or guarantee tomorrow's safety**.
16. **THE CROW INTELLIGENCE TEST:** Mance sends all except Dalla out, asking Jon why five Night's Watch men penetrated the Skirling Pass, whether more followed, and who told them about the wildling concentration. Jon supplies a mixed **lie** ('Mormont sent me to Qhorin to season me') and **truth** (Craster informed them, villages abandoned). Mance already suspects **Craster** and reveals he asked to test Jon's honesty. This shows why **competent interrogators need not trust every apparently true answer**, and that Mance is wary of Watch spies.
17. **MANCE'S DESERTION BACKSTORY:** Mance says a **shadowcat mauled his arm and back and shredded his black cloak** during a ranging. Wildlings took him to a healer's daughter who nursed him and repaired the cloak with **red silk from Asshai**, reportedly salvaged by her grandmother from a shipwreck off the Frozen Shore. Returning to the Shadow Tower, **Ser Denys Mallister** required an unmarked all-black cloak and declared the repaired one fit only for burning. Mance says this rejection of **individual freedom and gifted connection** prompted him to desert the next morning.
18. **CAUSE VS SELF-SERVING STORY:** The **red-silk-patched cloak** is directly visible on Mance in the tent; its exact Asshai provenance, the healer's words and the internal motive for desertion are **his retrospective self-report**. This is not proof that Asshai silk has magical effects, that Mallister personally drove every later act, or that his entire army has gathered because of a single dress-code violation. The cloak is a coherent symbolic account of an institution making loyalty feel incompatible with identity.
19. **JON'S PIVOT TO BELIEVABLE RESENTMENT:** Having learned Mance's reason, Jon uses **his own exclusion from Winterfell's royal high table as Ned's bastard** to claim he deserted because of **status and humiliation**. The social memory is plausibly true; its **presentation as the motive for defecting is the cover story Qhorin ordered Jon to improvise**. Mance notices the bitterness and **holds out a hand, suggesting acceptance**. This is admission to a conversation and provisional trust, **not proof Jon has delivered the diggings intelligence or abandoned his oath**.
20. **END POSITION:** Jon has reached the wildling king and secured enough credibility for future scouting but has **not yet discovered the Frostfang excavations' object, obtained a precise invasion timetable, or proved the eagle's magical operator**. The reader now has a richer model of the migrating population, its loose alliances, Mance's unusually sophisticated knowledge of southern institutions and Jon's own vulnerabilities.

## Competing theories and later tests

- **What the wildlings dug for:** Qhorin explicitly instructed Jon to find out in `ACOK 68`; Mance is cunning and plausibly knows it. The present chapter **still does not identify the object, its purported powers or its status**. Prioritize the next Mance/Jon exchanges without assuming a named magical horn.
- **Free-folk migration vs invasion:** Women, infants, domestic herds and social variety reinforce a whole people on the move, but **spears are being made and arrows fletched**. The best reading must account for both **armed coercion of the Wall and flight toward shelter**, rather than making those mutually exclusive.
- **Mance and the Bael pattern:** His admission that he entered Ned's feast disguised as a singer creates a grounded pattern of **infiltrating through harmless-seeming performance**, including the **guest-right shield**. Do not infer he secreted a child or stole a Stark artifact without direct source.
- **Cloak and Asshai:** Red silk and the symbolic rejection of personal history motivate Mance's story; speculative ideas about **enchanted cloth** are unsupported here. The silk's stated geographic origin is **thirdhand** even within Mance's account.
- **Jon's true loyalty:** His internal mission remains Watch-aligned, and Mance's response is to a plausible **status grievance**. Later sincere attachments may complicate the performance, but **no full turncloak conversion is witnessed here**.
