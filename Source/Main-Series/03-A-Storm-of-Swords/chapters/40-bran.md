## Causal and chronological source intake

1. **The travelers leave the northern mountains for the Gift.** Bran, **Meera, Jojen, Hodor and Summer** encounter the first abandoned village since the foothills. Open fields and low grassland promise easier travel, but make Meera anxious about visibility and pursuit. The location is south of the Wall, **not a confirmed location of the three-eyed crow**.
2. **Bran recalls conflicting histories of Watch land grants.** Maester Luwin taught that a **Brandon** gave the Night's Watch land extending approximately **25 leagues** south of the Wall, later enlarged by **Good Queen Alysanne's** visit on **Silverwing** and the Old King's decree to **50 leagues**. Bran notes historians disagree whether the original Brandon was the Builder. **He is repeating learned history**, not personally verifying ancient events.
3. **The village is abandoned despite fertile soil.** The ruined inn, apple orchard, old holdfast and vegetable plots once supported a settlement; rot and tree growth through an inn's common room indicate long disuse. Bran attributes abandonment to **wildling raids and a weakened Watch**, with people moving into Umber lands or farther south. This is **Bran's explanation from stories**, not a local resident's confirmed migration record.
4. **The lake holdfast's gold crenellations preserve Alysanne memory.** Bran recognizes the small stone tower by painted golden 'crown' merlons and Old Nan's story that **Alysanne slept there**. Material golden paint survives; the named royal visit is **oral memory**.
5. **Jojen foresees or anticipates an oncoming storm.** Under an initially clear sky he insists on seeking shelter; dark western clouds and strengthening wind later confirm worsening weather. Whether he relied on his greendream faculty or ordinary meteorology is **not declared**.
6. **Bran knows a concealed crossing.** He says Old Nan taught that a **three-foot-wide underwater stone causeway** connects shore to island. Meera probes it with her frog spear; its length and turns make the island defensible. **The path is verified on page**, unlike the untested buried ways elsewhere beneath the Wall.
7. **The causeway does not run straight.** It winds around a significant portion of the island and doubles back; from the surface one might assume a direct path between shore and steps, and drown. The Reeds wade chest-deep and Hodor waist-deep, while carrying Bran. Its slippery footing threatens a catastrophic fall for a boy who cannot swim to safety.
8. **The holdfast interior has layered defenses.** A low entry room is blocked by rusted iron gates on stairs both up and down to a vault; a barred **murder hole** lies in the ceiling. **Bran accidentally pulls its weakened grate loose**, making an entry way. His apparent strength is due to **rust and crumbly stone**, not an unexplained burst of bodily power.
9. **Hodor's physical strength remains essential.** He boosts the Reeds up through the hole; they haul Bran, then Hodor stacks rocks to reach it himself. He cannot simply be lifted by the others. Their movements expose how difficult these expeditions are for a party with one disabled child, a frightened caretaker and two young guides.
10. **Five stories and minor features are inventoried.** Narrow cells, iron stairs, arrowslits, upper windows, balconies and a **privy draining to the lake** give the tower tactical utility and vulnerabilities. The warping of the oak entry door means it **cannot be securely shut**, relevant when strangers arrive.
11. **From the roof the Wall is not yet visible.** Bran estimates he remains **about fifty leagues away**. He repeats a list of **nineteen Wall castles**, never more than **seventeen concurrently manned** according to his learned history, and recalls Benjen encouraging his geographical knowledge. These are facts of instruction and Bran's estimate, not a precise measurement from the roof.
12. **The Reeds suggest an abandoned castle crossing.** Jojen proposes entering beyond the Wall through one of the deserted forts; Bran cautions its gate should be **sealed with stone and ice**. Meera suggests opening it. **No castle entrance is found or opened in this chapter**.
13. **Bran would prefer Castle Black.** He hopes to meet **Jon and Benjen** and trusts the Night's Watch's neutrality and links to his father. His assumptions are outdated: Benjen is missing and the reader knows Jon has crossed with raiders and the Watch is struggling. These are perspective asymmetries, not a foolish lack of loyalty.
14. **Jojen sees institutional betrayal as plausible.** He says a single Watchman willing to betray vows could send Bran to the ironmen or Ramsay Bolton, or the Watch might confine him. This is a **risk assessment** based on Bran's value as heir, not proof a named Watchman is plotting to sell him.
15. **A solitary mounted stranger arrives.** Observed from the tower across the lake, a man rides toward the abandoned inn and later lights a fire there as rain comes. **He is not identified** from the distance; his motives are unknown.
16. **The party chooses secrecy over warmth.** Jojen forbids a fire in the island tower to avoid telltale smoke, despite cold and Hodor's enthusiasm. They share **half a cold duck caught by Meera with a net** and pass time with Bran's story of **Brandon the Shipwright**, who sailed into the Sunset Sea. This is learned dynastic lore, not evidence the voyager returned or discovered a new continent.
17. **Thunder frightens Hodor.** As lightning flashes closer, Hodor panics, shouts and grabs his sword; Meera obtains it without a fight, but he grows louder again while Jojen fears nearby strangers will hear. His neurological/cognitive state is **shown through behavior**, not explained by a clinical diagnosis.
18. **The danger multiplies.** A new group of **armed men carrying spears and an axe** approaches the inn and fire. Jojen counts 'many and more' but cannot identify their heraldry or allegiances in lightning. Meera notes the tower's warped door, and Jojen the vulnerability of its rusted gates and missing murder grate.
19. **Bran involuntarily enters Hodor's consciousness.** During another panic Bran reaches for him **as he does for Summer**; Hodor suddenly stills, sits down and scarcely hears thunder. Bran privately knows **he was Hodor for half a heartbeat**, though he tells Meera he did nothing. This is a major **first-person admission of human skinchanging**, qualitatively different from wolf dreams or tentative magical symbolism.
20. **The ethical issue begins immediately.** Hodor did not consent or knowingly offer his mind; Bran's panicked seizure silences a vulnerable person to protect the group. The immediate safety motive is understandable, but **consent, memory, physiological effect and ongoing control remain untested**.
21. **Bran infers the island is safe until morning.** If the strangers lack a boat or knowledge of the winding underwater path, they cannot easily reach the holdfast during a storm. Jojen points out they might remain until dawn. **This is provisional tactical advantage**, not perfect immunity.
22. **Summer's independent perspective enters at the cliffhanger.** Bran deliberately slips into **Summer's skin**, feels his full belly from deer, his aversion to thunder, and smells men, leather, horses and fear near the inn. He sees a figure under a skin hood carrying a stick among others and hears their voices. **No identity of that figure or the armed men is established before the chapter ends.**
23. **The second skinchange verifies a distinct form of access.** In contrast to the brief unconscious seizure of Hodor, Bran chooses to enter Summer's viewpoint to scout. This direct sensory alternate POV is evidence of his ability to gather information but **not proof he can translate every human conversation or infer every person's allegiance**.
24. **End state:** Bran and the Reeds hide in an island tower reached by a verified tortuous causeway; unidentified people occupy the village; Hodor has undergone one involuntary consciousness intrusion; Summer approaches the strangers. **The armed group is not yet identified or engaged in this chapter.**

## Theory and countercase tests

- **Human possession and moral limits:** This establishes that Bran can reach a human mind without informed agreement. Questions include whether he can repeat it, how Hodor experiences it, and whether it causes lasting harm.
- **Old Nan as information source:** Her hidden causeway story is mechanically correct, increasing the credibility of her specialized knowledge in this instance—not proving every legendary story or that all Wall tunnels are real.
- **Unidentified men:** Avoid bringing in next chapter revelations; there is no independent name or sigil inside this chapter. Compare subsequent Jon/Bran chapters before reconciling their positions.
- **Physical Wall crossing:** Sealed abandoned gates are plausible but untested. Do not treat Jojen's suggestion as evidence he knows a working secret passage.
- **Gift depopulation:** Raiding and lack of Watch capacity are Bran's causal interpretation; economic and climate causes may also contribute absent a village witness.
