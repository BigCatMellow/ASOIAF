## Chronological record / power and agency

1. **The unexplained gown's purpose is revealed.** Cersei's maids wash and arrange Sansa's hair, dress her in **ivory samite, silver cloth, grey Myrish lace and expensive slippers**, and add **Joffrey-gifted moonstones**. Sansa hopes her prospective husband Willas will admire it, but Cersei orders a pearl-heavy white cloak with an **embroidered silver direwolf**. Sansa recognizes the **maiden's cloak** and realizes this is a wedding outfit.
2. **The proposed Willas marriage has been intercepted.** Cersei says Joffrey, acting as Sansa's father because she is a royal ward and Robb is condemned as traitor, orders her to wed **Tyrion Lannister immediately**. This directly confirms [ASOS 19 Tyrion](19-tyrion.md)'s secret preemption of the Tyrell match. Sansa's desired future in Highgarden was a plan, not a binding betrothal.
3. **The forced choice is concrete, not free consent.** Cersei threatens to have Kingsguard escort or carry Sansa to the sept and says the marriage and subsequent bedding will occur whether Sansa struggles or not. **Ser Meryn Trant and Ser Osmund Kettleblack** wait outside; a maid blocks her attempted flight. Her later verbal acceptance occurs under intimidation.
4. **Sansa draws courage from her Stark identity.** She remembers Tyrion having protected her when Joffrey arranged earlier public abuse and chooses to walk rather than be carried. This is coping under coercion, **not** proof she now desires the marriage.
5. **Joffrey claims paternal authority and threatens alternatives.** At the sept he declares himself Sansa's father for the ceremony, mocks the thought of marrying her to a servant, and invokes **Ilyn Payne** to terrify her. Sansa rejects the assertion that he could replace Ned emotionally.
6. **Tyrion offers a limited alternative.** He privately apologizes for secrecy and says he could refuse so she would marry his cousin **Lancel** instead. She correctly sees that neither option allows Willas or autonomy. Tyrion's offer changes which Lannister receives her, not the royal decision to exploit her claim.
7. **The witness list exposes deliberate political exclusion.** Sansa notices **no Tyrells present in the sept**, even though they appear at the feast afterward. Observed wedding witnesses include **Dontos, Varys, Addam Marbrand, Philip Foote, Bronn, Jalabhar Xho**, several Kingsguard and other courtiers. The absence is compatible with Tywin's strategy to prevent a Tyrell objection before the ceremony.
8. **Cloak symbolism publicly transfers custodial claims.** Joffrey removes her Stark maiden's cloak; Tyrion is supposed to put a crimson-and-gold lion cloak over her. Joffrey gropes Sansa during the first action. The ritual dramatizes male-controlled transfer of her allegiance, not an emotional renunciation of her family.
9. **Sansa resists a humiliating adjustment.** Because Tyrion is shorter than her, he tugs her dress to ask her to kneel. She refuses in silent protest. Joffrey orders **Dontos** onto hands and knees so Tyrion can stand on his back to place the cloak; the gathering laughs. Sansa later kneels voluntarily for their ritual kiss and regrets humiliating Tyrion, showing selective empathy amid an imposed ceremony.
10. **Marriage is solemnized on page.** The septon pronounces **Tyrion Lannister and Sansa Stark husband and wife**, in sight of gods and assembled witnesses. This is a real public marriage ceremony, though the lack of free choice, consummation and subsequent enforceability remain separate questions.
11. **The Tyrell women's behavior changes at the feast.** Margaery looks sorrowful, Olenna averts her eyes, and Elinor, Alla and Megga avoid acknowledging Sansa. This reverses Sansa's sense of recent friendship. **Their private motives are unshown**: calculation, shame, fear of Lannister punishment and genuine helplessness all remain viable.
12. **A small wedding feast is attended.** Roughly **fifty guests** assemble in the Small Hall. Tyrion drinks heavily, eats little and declines Sansa's suggestion that they lead the first dance, expecting ridicule. Joffrey and Margaery lead; Cersei dances with major Lannister allies and Tywin. Sansa fears a public bedding ritual far more than food or court protocol.
13. **Garlan Tyrell offers real but limited kindness.** Garlan invites Sansa to dance with Tyrion's consent; Tyrion says she may choose. He tells her **Willas** named him 'the Gallant' to shield him from humiliating childhood nicknames and makes her laugh. He notices her fear but argues Tyrion could be a better husband than the admired Loras. This is Garlan's judgment, not certainty or a promise to rescue her.
14. **Joffrey threatens continuing sexual access to Sansa.** During a dance he says marriage to Margaery will not bar him from compelling his uncle to send Sansa to his bed, and threatens Tyrion if he disobeys. This reveals that a completed Lannister marriage **does not eliminate Sansa's vulnerability**. Joffrey's threat is directly spoken; no subsequent assault is shown here.
15. **Tyrion stops the ceremonial bedding with a dangerous threat.** When Joffrey tries to command men to undress the newlyweds publicly, Tyrion angrily threatens the king with mutilation, and drives a dagger into the table. Cersei appeals to Tywin; Tywin forbids the public bedding and forces Tyrion to excuse his words as drunken jest. **This prevents the feast's public ritual**, not the private power of a husband to consummate.
16. **Tyrion performs drunkenness to extract them.** He makes a coarse public remark and removes Sansa from the feast, but says privately he is not yet truly drunk and intends to become so. No one follows, sparing Sansa the stripping she feared.
17. **The marital chamber is in the Tower of the Hand.** Sansa and Tyrion drink **Arbor gold**, both anxious. Tyrion recounts a former marriage to **Tysha**, telling Sansa she would not have known it; he calls her Lady Tysha of a mock House Silverfist. The details of that marriage and its destruction are emotionally important, but this is Tyrion's abbreviated disclosure in Sansa's POV.
18. **Sansa states her age.** She is **nearly thirteen**, not already an adult by modern standards. Tyrion describes her as a child while Tywin previously claimed menarche made her marriageable. Menstruation and ritual marriage **do not establish willingness or readiness**.
19. **Tyrion explicitly admits the forced consummation order.** He tells Sansa his father commanded him to consummate; she obeys the request to undress while trembling. Tyrion desires her but also identifies his own shame and fear. The scene is coercive even though Tyrion later stops.
20. **Sansa's courtesy functions as protective armor.** She repeats lessons from Septa Mordane and gives dutiful formulae rather than honest desire. Tyrion asks for sincerity; she recognizes he may be frightened too but feels pity rather than attraction. **Pity is not consent.**
21. **The physical interaction stops before intercourse.** Tyrion touches her while she lies frozen and frightened. After recognizing her distress, he withdraws, refuses to continue and says he will wait as long as necessary until she knows him and wishes it. **No consummation is narrated.**
22. **Sansa does not promise she will ever consent.** She asks what happens if she never wants him; Tyrion is wounded and bitter, responding with a remark about seeking sex workers instead. **A postponement is not a future agreement**, and his promise should be tested against later behavior rather than treated as guaranteed safety.
23. **The dynastic mechanism remains incomplete.** Tywin has achieved a legal public marriage to a Stark girl and preempted the Tyrells, but his desired **Lannister-descended Winterfell heir** is still hypothetical; lack of consummation could become a legal/political weakness in attempts to validate or contest the match.
24. **End state:** Sansa remains imprisoned in circumstances of a royal ward, publicly married to Tyrion and moved into his private marital chamber. Tyrion has refused to consummate that night. **No flight with Dontos, access to Willas, pregnancy, Tyrell rescue or protective legal settlement occurs.**

## Competing readings and disconfirmation

- **Does Tyrion's refusal solve Sansa's plight?** No. It prevents immediate intercourse but does not undo state coercion, legal alliance or Joffrey's ongoing threats. Counterevidence to idealized rescue: he participates in the wedding and his father controls their strategic circumstances.
- **Did Olenna and Margaery abandon Sansa?** They withdraw contact publicly after their scheme fails. Their political incentives are clear, but whether their affection was false, strategic, real or mixed needs later source evidence.
- **Is the marriage valid for inheritance?** A public religious ceremony occurs, but nonconsummation matters for possible annulment and dynastic effectiveness. Formal legal consequences are **not adjudicated** in this chapter.
- **Tyrion's autonomy:** He complies in marrying Sansa but resists both Joffrey's bedding command and Tywin's demanded consummation. Distinguish his choices from those of his family, without excusing intimidation earlier in the scene.
- **Future Sansa agency:** Her momentary refusal to kneel, protection through manners, and question about permanent refusal show decisions under tight constraint, not passive acceptance.
