## Chronology and tactical causal record

1. **Mole's Town burns as defenders prepare.** From the King's Tower Jon sees smoke from the village; this confirms **an attack or burning there**, not a complete census of who died. His earlier warning allowed **roughly three-quarters of villagers to reach Castle Black**. Refugees are still coming up the wooden stair and winch cage as the expected raiders approach.
2. **Jon is still severely wounded.** He can stand only with **Maester Aemon's padded crutch**, assistance dressing, dreamwine and willow bark, and renewed pain from cauterization. **Donal Noye** permits him to shoot from the King's Tower, explicitly questioning his ability to fight or retreat on one leg. Courage is **not** evidence of recovered health.
3. **Scarecrow sentinels create numerical deception.** Maester Aemon proposes filling unused Watch garments with **straw** and stationing false defenders in towers and windows; Noye deploys them with weapons. Jon counts **six scarecrows and two real comrades** beside him on the King's Tower roof. Their visibility is tactical theater, not actual fighting strength.
4. **Jon's tower companions are particular men.** **Deaf Dick Follard** operates a painstakingly maintained crossbow and lip-reads in good light; **Satin** is a comparatively new Watch brother formerly employed in an Oldtown brothel, frightened but trained enough to work a crossbow. These lived histories resist reducing them to interchangeable 'garrison'.
5. **Benjen's institutional explanation is explicit.** Jon remembers his uncle explaining why Castle Black **has no defensive southern wall**: Watch commanders must not become threatening independent lords, recalling **Runcel Hightower's attempted hereditary command**, **Rodrik Flint's ambitions beyond the Wall**, and war between **Snowgate and Nightfort commanders** ended by Stark execution. This is **Benjen's historical account**, not a formal founding charter; it reveals how neutrality has become a tactical weakness.
6. **The defendable ground is deliberately reduced.** Donal abandons outer residences and forms a **crescent barricade** around the **Wall gate** and base of the great wooden stair, using sacks of grain, logs, cloth, salt meat, casks of nails, barrels and fire-hardened stakes. The objective is **deny access through the Wall**, not preserve every building.
7. **Mole's Town inhabitants become a volunteer militia and support corps.** Noye arms able men with Watch weapons and puts women and children to hauling water, tending fires, preparing food, and serving as **Maester Aemon/Clydas's medical support**. Two women proficient with crossbows defend from stair positions. Not every refugee fights, nor are noncombatants simply abandoned.
8. **Fire is deliberately positioned for a contingency.** Jon hears Noye direct **pitch, oil, lard, leaves, logs and rags** to the **stair's base**, distribute spears and crossbows by landing and lift medical staff/evacuees to the top. The lower stair is **booby-trapped before attackers arrive**, making its later burning a designed defense rather than fortuitous magic.
9. **The Watch's appeals are much more extensive than one raven.** Aemon sends warning messages to **four kings**, Oldtown and Citadel and about **fifty great lords**, including northern Umbers, Boltons, Manderlys, Cerwyns, Karstarks, Mormonts, Ryswell-area families and mountain clans; **two birds per northern recipient**. No force could arrive the same day. The correspondence has a contemporaneous constraint independent of political neglect.
10. **Owen the Oaf misunderstands Robert's status.** He believes Robert alive and coming to rescue them, even after having been told otherwise. Jon refrains from upsetting him. This is a small but revealing instance of the distance between royal events and isolated Watch brothers' knowledge.
11. **A night attack begins with horns.** Satin is terrified enough to wet himself; Jon responds without shaming him and advises controlled shots and cover behind merlons, **not the straw sentinels**, which arrows pierce easily. The fort's defenses depend on training and emotional steadiness, not fantasy invulnerability.
12. **Jon fights people he knows individually.** From above he shoots free-folk raiders with **decorated wicker shields**, distinguishes them from the **Thenns' plain bronze-rimmed shields**, and recognizes **Big Boil** too late after shooting near him; Mully later wounds Big Boil's leg. Jon cannot isolate battle from his previous attachment to the raiders.
13. **Archery and weapon technology shape the battle.** A longbow gives Jon faster shooting than Deaf Dick's crossbow, while a crossbow may penetrate better. The King’s Tower overlooks distant roofs, but darkness and moving targets lead to misses as well as kills. **Specific hits do not establish a total personal body count**.
14. **Deaf Dick is killed while defending the armory.** Seeing raiders on its roof, Dick exposes himself atop a crenel to aim, misses, and is struck by a wildling arrow. He falls around **one hundred feet** and dies. Jon sees a red-haired archer near his body and recognizes **Ygritte nearby**, but **he does not witness Ygritte release the specific fatal shaft**; the scene's assignment of the shooter is therefore unresolved.
15. **Ygritte's presence interrupts Jon's attack.** He brings up his bow at the red-haired woman but cannot make his fingers release before she vanishes. This is a decisive emotional and tactical hesitation, **not a demonstrated fatal shot by either party**.
16. **The Thenns storm the barricade.** Around **fifty** attack in a shield-roof formation up the kingsroad while others converge from gardens and courtyards. They wear **bronze disks and axes or stone/bronze spears**, distinctive from other free folk. More approach the fort simultaneously via buildings and roofs.
17. **Longclaw proves lethally superior to bronze armor.** Attackers break into the King's Tower through a trapdoor. Jon **cleaves through a Thenn's bronze helm and skull with Valyrian steel**, while Satin hits another with a crossbow quarrel. Together they drop **boiling oil through the trap**, preventing immediate overrunning of the tower.
18. **The ground barricade collapses.** Jon witnesses **Rast impaled**, **Young Henly dead**, **Old Henly dying**, **Easy's leg cut**, **Dornish Dilly speared while falling**, and other defenders retreating. **Kegs** is saved by Satin's shot into a pursuing Thenn. Some villagers abandon the fighting first; a few Watchmen hold until overwhelmed.
19. **The gate is nearly undefended.** When the Thenns seize the barricade, the steel gate and tunnel north are exposed. The remaining defenders crowd the **wooden switchback stair** and high landings. This is the moment at which the Watch's actual military purpose is at immediate risk.
20. **Noye launches the prearranged fire defense.** From about the **ninth landing**, he sounds a horn. Jon shoots **fire arrows at the combustible piles below the stair**, deliberately **not at Styr**, while archers on other towers follow. Satin feeds burning torches from the parapet. Noye has soaked **upper stair sections** in oil, trapping attackers between fires.
21. **The defense kills both people and infrastructure.** Fire races up and down the steep stair, where an estimated **sixty or seventy Thenns** had been climbing. Many die burning, falling, or trapped, including about **twenty** huddled as **the lower third breaks away with tons of melting/cracking ice**. Jon last sees **Styr** by the gate before collapse; **Styr's apparent death is strongly implied but not separately documented by finding his body**.
22. **The Wall's involvement is physical as well as symbolic.** Heat softens the ice, chunks fall, and the stair collapses. Jon's thought that the Wall defends itself is **an interpretation compatible with engineering and fire**, not proof a conscious Wall intervened.
23. **The victory devastates Castle Black.** The common hall, stables and segments of stair burn. The Watch retains a gate but suffers severe personnel and infrastructure losses. The immediate attack is defeated; **Mance's larger host north of the Wall remains unaddressed**.
24. **Jon searches specifically for Ygritte.** With Satin supporting his weight after battle, he finds **Quort dead, Stone Thumbs dying**, a bleeding but living **Big Boil**, and others. **Ygritte lies mortally wounded** on snow with an **arrow between her breasts**, identified by Jon as **black-shafted but white duck-fletched**.
25. **The fatal arrow is distinguishable from Jon's arrows.** Earlier he uses **black shafts with grey feathers**. He recognizes the distinction and tells himself it was not his, even as he feels emotional responsibility. **The individual archer is UNKNOWN**; do not falsely attribute the shot to Jon or Satin.
26. **Ygritte dies while speaking to Jon.** She asks whether Castle Black counts as a 'proper castle', recalls their cave, cups his cheek and dies. This is a **directly witnessed death**, unlike his earlier nightmare of her dying. Jon's promises that Aemon could heal her are desperation, not a prognosis.
27. **End state:** Noye's improvised defense has beaten Styr's raiding force at tremendous cost; the Watch gate remains in its hands, but parts of the Castle Black stair and building complex are gone, and **Ygritte is dead**. Jon's severe leg wound and mourning persist; the larger invasion is unresolved.

## Rival readings and tests

- **Failure and triumph:** Castle Black is saved by sacrificing buildings and access stair, not an effortless slaughter. Subsequent logistics for the top of the Wall are seriously degraded.
- **What actually killed Ygritte?** White duck feathers exclude attribution to Jon's grey-feathered arrows but cannot identify a specific other defender. Track individual equipment only where narrated.
- **Was the Wall magic?** The ice breaks under enormous heat, entirely compatible with natural collapse; a metaphor of the Wall's self-defense does not independently prove agency.
- **Civilian roles:** Refugees help defend, supply and heal; don't erase women who shoot crossbows from lower landings, or claim all villagers panicked.
- **Watch institutional weakness:** Neutrality prevented southern walls, just when attackers approached from the south. This is a structural paradox, not evidence that Benjen wanted the Watch to fall.
