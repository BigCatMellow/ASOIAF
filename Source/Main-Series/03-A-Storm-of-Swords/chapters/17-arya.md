## Narrative sequence and causal record

1. **Direction becomes the first conflict.** Three days into the outlaws' journey, rain-swollen streams, road bends and moss lead Arya to suspect the company is traveling **south rather than toward Riverrun**. Gendry reasonably notes roads can bend. Arya has been inattentive on a previous day, so the moss alone is not conclusive; the later **sun direction** confirms they deliberately ride south.
2. **Hot Pie chooses a civilian life.** At Sharna's inn he stays to bake bread, preferring ordinary work and safety to exhausting travel. He asks whether Arya will return after the war. His new name at the inn is simply **Boy**, which he says another child already uses. Arya apologizes for once breaking his nose and refuses his courtly farewell: she remains **Arry** to him.
3. **Harwin reconnects the Stark household.** Arya tells Harwin how she encountered his father Hullen dying at the Red Keep stables when she escaped King's Landing. He reports Hullen expected to die in a stable but not by Lannister violence. Their exchange gives both firsthand personal losses rather than a simple noble-retainer reunion.
4. **Arya edits her testimony.** She tells Harwin about Yoren and her travels but consciously omits the stableboy she stabbed and the Harrenhal guard whose throat she cut. She also withholds Jaqen H'ghar's three deaths. **VERIFIED:** concealment motivated partly by fear of how her father would judge those acts. Harwin does not possess a full account of her killings.
5. **The Faceless Man's material trace survives.** Arya carries Jaqen's iron coin under her belt and remembers his visible face change. She recites her vengeance names at night. The coin is physically present; it does not demonstrate that a Faceless Man is now following her.
6. **The original Beric mission is narrated by a participant.** Harwin says Ned's dispatch originally included **twenty Winterfell men**; only six remain, now scattered. Tywin had sent Gregor's force across the Red Fork to draw Ned into a trap while Tyrion was Catelyn's prisoner. **CHARACTER CLAIM:** Harwin's reconstruction of Tywin's intention, though the ensuing ambush itself is firsthand.
7. **The ambush at Mummer's Ford.** Harwin says Gregor concealed men on both banks. **Raymun Darry, Gladden Wylde and Lothar Mallery** died; Alyn organized surviving riders around Thoros to escape. The group began the engagement roughly **six score strong** and had **no more than two score** by evening. These numbers cover the expedition, not just Ned's Winterfell men.
8. **Beric's first remarkable survival.** Harwin saw a severe lance wound to Beric's chest, the lance drawn out and boiling wine applied. Thoros prayed beside Beric through the night, and Beric survived and eventually rode again. This is witness testimony of survival after grave injury; it **does not by itself establish that Beric died and was resurrected that night**.
9. **The Brotherhood undergoes a change of political purpose.** After Robert and Ned died, Beric's men were declared outlaws even though they had acted under the king's Hand. Harwin says some wanted to yield; Beric instead pledged to protect the ravaged king's people. Commoners, craft workers, clergy, women and children began joining. This is a material shift from Stark royal enforcement to **defense of smallfolk outside rival kings' exclusive claims**.
10. **A functioning support network is observed.** Jack-Be-Lucky sounds **two short and two long horn blasts** at a ruined village, drawing civilians out of concealed cellars and wreckage. They provide food and shelter, and Anguy supplies a goose. The network is physical and local, not evidence of limitless Brotherhood manpower.
11. **Jaime's escape has entered village rumor.** Villagers say searchers passed two days earlier seeking the Kingslayer. Lem initially assumes Jaime remains imprisoned at Riverrun, and other brothers doubt his escape. This **reported intelligence** connects to [ASOS 1 Jaime](01-jaime.md) without proving the travelers know his route.
12. **Religious interpretations diverge among outlaws.** Greenbeard expects Thoros to consult the Lord of Light about Jaime; he jokes that his own beard burns when he peers into flames. No vision is witnessed. Anguy insists Beric gives prisoners **a trial before hanging them**; its fairness must be tested independently of the men's teasing.
13. **A song joins outlaw traditions.** Tom sings about the older Kingswood Brotherhood, which attacked wealthy travelers and threatened women. The song can function as folklore, comparison, or moral warning: **similar titles do not establish identical ethics**.
14. **Arya dreams of Winterfell retreating.** She is mired in mud outside the castle while its walls recede like smoke; gaunt wolves encircle her and the taste of blood returns. This is **VERIFIED dream content**, compatible with exile, anxiety and Nymeria-associated experience, **not a dated prophecy** of Winterfell physically vanishing.
15. **The intended route is revealed.** Arya uses a map and the now-visible sun to show they are headed away from Riverrun. Lem says **no promise to deliver her there was made**; Greenbeard calls her a valuable noble captive and says Beric must decide her destination. Tom expects Beric will return her to Catelyn. The good outcome is a **forecast, not a guarantee**.
16. **Arya tries to reclaim agency.** Concluding she is captive, she bolts between Greenbeard and Jack and rides toward woods and fields on a **fast horse taken from Bolton's Harrenhal stables**. Four outlaws pursue: Anguy, Harwin, Greenbeard and Lem; the latter two fall back. Terrain erases the horse's speed advantage and makes navigation uncertain.
17. **Harwin catches her.** He rides alongside and seizes her bridle. He credits both his own horse experience and Lyanna Stark's earlier riding ability, thus making an unusually specific **Lyanna–Arya comparison** by a former Winterfell stableman's son.
18. **Harwin explicitly refuses dynastic ownership.** He says he no longer serves Ned or Robb but **Beric and the Brotherhood**. Robb has an army and lords, while ordinary people have the Brotherhood. He denies intending Robb harm yet will not release a politically useful captive solely because she is a Stark.
19. **The names of identity mark the danger.** Arya regrets abandoning the anonymity of **Squab, Nan, Weasel and Arry**; recognition as a highborn girl has reduced her mobility. She agrees to ride back without being tied, but only **for now**. No transfer to Catelyn or completed judgment by Beric occurs.

## Interpretive tests / rival readings

- **Beric's ethics:** He articulates civilian protection; meanwhile his men take valuable captives without promising an immediate return. Their practice might be pragmatic ransom/security rather than a betrayal of principle. Test Beric's own words and captive hearings in upcoming Arya chapters.
- **Tywin's original trap:** Harwin is an informed survivor, not a witness to Tywin's private instructions. Cross-check Ned's immobilization, Tywin's deployment and the timing of Tyrion's capture without treating motive as independently proved.
- **Beric's 'resurrection':** The first wound and extraordinary recovery encourage investigation of Thoros's abilities, but they allow a mundane survival interpretation at this point. Do not backdate a confirmed death.
- **Lyanna comparison:** A person who knew Winterfell horses identifies a family likeness in riding; using this as a deterministic arc prediction would outrun the evidence.
- **Knowledge boundary:** Arya knows Harwin's allegiance and her captivity; she does **not** yet know any eventual verdict, nor the secret marriage/court negotiations shown in other POVs.
