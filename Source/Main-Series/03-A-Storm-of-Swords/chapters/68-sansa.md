## Chronological primary-text record

1. **The rescue galley is explicitly named and reaches the Fingers after dangerous storms.** The ship **Merling King** has been blown off course by heavy seas; according to crew news **two sailors went overboard** and one fell from the mast and died. Sansa is seasick, feverish and repeatedly haunted by seeing Joffrey suffocate. The bodies and exact number of sea deaths are **reports heard by Sansa**, not direct witnessing.
2. **Littlefinger has misled Sansa about 'home'.** She expected **White Harbor/Winterfell**, but the galley puts her ashore at **his bleak, unnamed tower on the Fingers**, before sailing east to **Braavos** without her. He says they will remain only around **a fortnight** and plans to marry **Lysa Arryn**. The destination change is an actual betrayal of Sansa's expectations, not a mutually agreed route.
3. **Petyr offers a false and vicious account of Tyrion's first marriage.** He says Tyrion abandoned Tysha and gave her to his father's guards when tired of her; Sansa remembers Tyrion had admitted a former wife. **Petyr's blame-shifting version is character testimony** and misrepresents the coercive family circumstances, underscoring his efforts to isolate Sansa from Tyrion.
4. **The Baelish family's actual poverty is documented.** Petyr has a flint tower of **three principal floors/rooms**, servants sleeping with **one brindled mastiff and several sheepdogs**, a little hall, arrowslits, a broken sword and a shield. He possesses **about 23 sheep**, down from 29, and approximately **a dozen smallfolk families** inland. The island-like headland has **no safe anchorage**. This is physical contrast to his later lordships.
5. **Petyr's grandfather used Titan-of-Braavos heraldry.** On an old shield, **a grey stone head with fiery eyes against light green** is explained by Petyr as the Titan; his **great-grandfather**, a Braavosi sellsword employed by **Lord Corbray**, came to the Vale, and his grandfather took the device when knighted. Petyr later preferred the **mockingbird**. This is **Petyr's family account**, with a tangible shield but no documentary immigration proof.
6. **The local household is named and capable of loyalty but small.** **Grisel**, Petyr's old wet nurse, oversees the household; **Umfred** is steward; **Bryen**, aging captain, and his dogs take most watches; **Kella** tends sheep and has several children. Littlefinger mocks the modest establishment, but it gives him a specific defensible landing and a small number of retainers.
7. **Oswell Kettleblack's full identity is revealed.** At the tower Littlefinger brings the old rower **Oswell** forward, and Sansa recognizes he is **father of Ser Osmund, Osney and Osfryd Kettleblack**. Petyr says he had Oswell send the three brothers to King's Landing when **Bronn was recruiting sellswords**. He deliberately embedded agents into both **Tyrion and Cersei's circles**; their later positions are not accidental.
8. **The Kettleblack penetration involves double and triple service.** Petyr says **Cersei asked Lancel to hire swords**, Tyrion thought Bronn had bought Kettleblack loyalty, and Petyr had secretly prompted their arrival. His picture is well supported by **Oswell's connection** and known service; the exact timing and breadth of each man's knowledge remain dependent on Petyr's account.
9. **Petyr expressly rules the Kettleblacks out of the poisoning task.** He says they are too treacherous to trust, especially Osmund after receiving a white cloak, and warns Sansa to keep her **own hands clean**. This is **participant testimony**, not independently documented exculpation of each Kettleblack.
10. **Sansa receives the identity 'Alayne Stone'.** Petyr fears **Varys's agents** would identify her as Stark, and instructs her to claim she is **his bastard daughter**, named for **his mother Alayne**. An invented story makes her mother a **Braavosi merchant-prince's daughter** who met Petyr at Gulltown, died giving birth, sent Alayne to the Faith, and whose daughter later wrote to Petyr after deciding not to become a septa. **Every biographical element of this cover is knowingly fabricated**, not a real parentage revelation.
11. **The false bastard story uses class etiquette as camouflage.** Petyr notes outsiders are discouraged from querying natural children's origins. He offers religious books to supply believable quotations. Sansa considers pretending to be a dead knight's legitimate daughter, but Petyr rejects the conspicuous documentation trail. This is **operational identity construction**, separate from any actual alteration of her Stark blood.
12. **His use of Sansa as a pupil is explicit.** Eating pomegranate seeds, he tells her politics divides people into **players and pieces**, and knowing what they want allows manipulation. He calls Cersei predictable, in part because she values beauty, birth and wealth. These are **Petyr's interpretive claims**, which benefit his objective of making Sansa dependent on his lessons.
13. **Littlefinger confirms Dontos was not trusted with assassination.** Confronted by Sansa, he says **Dontos's limited assignment was getting her out of the castle and ensuring she wore the silver hairnet**. This materially strengthens the theory that the hairnet was a **planned delivery instrument**, while it is **not an independent chemical test of a missing stone**.
14. **Littlefinger points directly toward Olenna as the woman who manipulated the hairnet.** He invites Sansa to remember someone straightening the net during the wedding. Sansa realizes this means the **Queen of Thorns** and her plan to marry Sansa to **Willas Tyrell**. This is an explicit **insider implication of Olenna's involvement**, now independently consistent with her observed contact in [ASOS 60 Tyrion](60-tyrion.md), but Petyr does not recite an ingredient list, a meeting transcript or the final physical deposition into the chalice.
15. **Petyr recounts an elaborate Tyrell influence operation.** At Highgarden, he openly praised Joffrey while his men circulated **negative tales to Tyrell servants**, so **Olenna** would independently see the danger to **Margaery**. His claim that **Loras was encouraged toward the Kingsguard** through tales and songs about knightly exemplars is **a self-attributed manipulation**, not solely Mace's spontaneity.
16. **The motive for replacing Joffrey is explicitly described.** Olenna knows Joffrey can abuse Margaery and that **Loras** might kill him in retaliation; **Mace** wants a king for his daughter, but that king need not be Joffrey. Petyr predicts **Margaery marrying Tommen**, preserving the Tyrell crown and alliance. **This is a motive and projected dynastic outcome**, not a narrated Olenna confession or completed remarriage.
17. **Sansa has been reduced to a dependent 'safe' identity.** She feels relieved at Joffrey's death and imaginary freedom from Tyrion but recognizes she is now a publicly powerless natural daughter with no visible claim. Littlefinger controls the story, lodging and prospective movements.
18. **Eight days pass before Lysa arrives.** Sansa befriends **Bryen's old blind dog**, watches a **30-foot tidal blowhole**, a seven-pointed-star carving said to mark an early Andal landing, a dry abandoned hermit's cave, and smallfolk huts near a peat bog. **Petyr's tale of a filthy hermit predicting his greatness** is family anecdote, not verification of prophecy.
19. **Lysa arrives with modest armed escort and immediate marriage plans.** She brings **about twenty riders**, including three maids, a dozen knights, a **septon** and a singer **Marillion**. She complains **Yohn Royce, Hunter, Corbray and Nestor Royce** push her to call Vale banners or marry powerful lords, while she insists only Petyr truly loves her. These are **Lysa's political claims**, not corroborated conversations with those lords.
20. **Lysa and Petyr marry within the hour on the Fingers.** Despite Petyr's preference for a more public Vale marriage, she demands an immediate wedding and brings the necessary cleric and provisions. Their vows occur beneath a **sky-blue canopy near sundown**; a feast with quail, venison, boar and mead follows. **This is an actually solemnized marriage**, not merely an announced future betrothal.
21. **Lysa's desire for a new child is direct.** She hopes to conceive another son or daughter, recalling repeated tragic losses and dissatisfaction with her previous marriage to **Jon Arryn**. None of these hopes constitutes an on-page pregnancy.
22. **Marillion sexually harasses and assaults Sansa.** After drinking, the singer catches Sansa in a hall near the old dog, touches her thigh and breast despite her explicit refusal, insults her presumed illegitimacy and threatens to claim Lysa and young Robert's protection. This is **witnessed assault under a power imbalance**, not flirtation or mutual affection.
23. **Lothor Brune intervenes with a blade.** Ordered by Petyr to watch Sansa, **Lothor cuts Marillion** and drives him away; Marillion is not shown slain. The old dog had growled at the singer before being struck. Lothor's real protection here coexists with his role in Dontos's execution earlier.
24. **Sansa's nightmares cross two sets of trauma.** She dreams Joffrey transforming into **Robb** as he dies, and Tyrion's wedding night becoming a threatening encounter with a half-scarred man resembling **Sandor**. These are **dream associations**, not literal historical events or proof she desires coercion.
25. **Petyr privately informs Lysa who Alayne really is.** On the morning after the wedding, Lysa says Sansa resembles Catelyn too strongly and orders her **auburn hair dyed dark** before they arrive at the Eyrie. Lysa knows the real identity, but orders Sansa to conceal it and not call her aunt before outsiders.
26. **Lysa acknowledges keeping the Vale out of war.** She points to its **harvest, mountain defenses and Eyrie's claimed impregnability**, and fears Tywin's anger endangering **Robert Arryn**. Her political isolation has a protect-child rationale as well as possible avoidance of danger to her position.
27. **Lysa provides specific unverified household history.** She complains **Jon Arryn's foul breath, advanced age and reproductive weakness**, claims **five children (three girls, two boys) died before surviving Robert**, and credits Petyr's Gulltown customs revenues with his appointment as **master of coin**. This is her first-person recollection and attribution, not an independent fertility diagnosis or audited financial series.
28. **Lysa repeats the crucial Jon Arryn deathbed phrase.** She says his last words concerned **the seed being strong** and interprets this as a prophecy that her son Robert would be a great man. The reader has seen this phrase used in **Jon Arryn's inquiry into Robert Baratheon's offspring**. Her confident interpretation is **materially mistaken or at least unsupported**, a notable case of witness misreading evidence.
29. **Sansa confirms her marriage to Tyrion was never consummated.** She denies pregnancy and says she is still a maiden; Lysa tests both details and approves, because she considers Tyrion's impending execution likely to make Sansa a widow. **Tyrion has not yet been sentenced or executed**.
30. **Lysa proposes a secret cousin marriage for Sansa.** After Tyrion's expected death, she plans to wed Sansa to **eight-year-old Robert Arryn**, using her Stark claim while publicly retaining the Alayne Stone cover. This is **an explicit plan and potentially contradictory public identities**, not an accomplished betrothal or proof Robert's suitability.
31. **Lysa treats Sansa as a tool for Robert's needs.** She lists the sickly boy's preferences—animal stories, a chicken-fox song, **hopfrog, spin-the-sword and come-into-my-castle**—and instructs Sansa to let him win, read to him and act grateful and obedient. Her assertion that no one else will want Sansa underscores coercion, **not factual proof Sansa has no alternative alliances**.
32. **End state:** Sansa is aboard no ship; she is concealed as **Alayne Stone** at the Fingers and preparing to move to the Vale with **newly married Petyr and Lysa**. Littlefinger's Kettleblack infiltration and indirect attribution to **Olenna** substantially sharpen the Purple Wedding conspiracy model. **Sansa's marriage to Tyrion still legally exists until proof otherwise; the Robert Arryn plan remains unexecuted.**

## Rival hypotheses and next checks

- **Purple Wedding plot:** At this point **Littlefinger self-incriminates** through hairnet, entertainment and Tyrell persuasion narratives, while **Olenna is implicated by his pointed disclosure and eyewitness contact**. An operational poison-conspiracy hypothesis is very strong, but neither the exact chemical makeup of the gemstone nor which vessel received it is demonstrated in a direct administration scene.
- **The Kettleblacks as false allies:** Petyr's independently verifiable connection to Oswell plus prior Tyrion/Cersei service shows a designed intelligence network. Do not assume the sons participated in poison administration when their patron specifically says he excluded them.
- **Misdirected prophecies:** Lysa's interpretation of Jon Arryn's last words shows how a high-confidence witness can misread a true phrase due to personal hopes.
- **Does Sansa gain safety?** The False Alayne identity, forced prospect of marrying a child and Marillion's assault show immediate threats persist even though Joffrey is dead.
- **Lysa's knowledge:** She recognizes and conceals Sansa, while other Vale figures have not been shown informed. Track the confidentiality chain separately.
