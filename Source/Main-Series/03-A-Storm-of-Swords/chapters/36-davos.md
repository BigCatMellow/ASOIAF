## Sequential evidence and decisions

1. **Stannis calls Davos directly out of the dungeon.** Axell Florent and guards take **Davos**, leaving his brother **Alester** behind. Axell orders the torch removed, leaving the prisoner in darkness, reversing Melisandre's earlier sparing of that light in [ASOS 25 Davos](25-davos.md). Axell says Melisandre will attend the king but Stannis asked personally for Davos.
2. **Axell manipulates Davos en route.** At a bridge over Dragonstone's courtyard he says he wants to be Hand, claims that he and **Salladhor Saan** have devised a winning plan, and says Selyse and Saan favor his candidacy. Axell offers Davos another ship to endorse him, while **threatening to push him off the bridge** if he refuses. Their private coercion is on page; whether the queen or Saan endorse every detail is still his interested report.
3. **The Painted Table remains materially significant.** Stannis receives Davos in Dragonstone's **Stone Drum**, amid a large carved map of Westeros representing the continent as one realm without drawn political borders. The court uses ritual fires, four-point windows and the island's sea air. The map is real; the 'one realm' political reading is **Stannis's value judgment**, not proof of rightful monarch.
4. **Stannis appears physically diminished.** Davos observes considerable weight loss, grey in his close-cropped beard, hollow eyes and a crown that now looks too large. He estimates the king appears about a decade older, with **more than two stone** lost since before the Blackwater. This corroborates deterioration but **does not independently prove Melisandre's account that shadow-making drained literal life-fire**.
5. **Stannis states treason's legal penalty.** He asks Davos for the penalty, receives 'death', and explains historical precedents. He calls **Alester's letter** an unauthorized attempt to sell his rights and **Shireen** into a marriage with an incest-born pretender. This confirms **Stannis knows of and condemns the proposed terms**, resolving one unknown from ASOS 25, but **not proving any letter was delivered to Tywin**.
6. **Stannis recounts Robert turning enemies into friends.** His description of Robert converting Lords **Cafferen and Grandison** after Summerhall, then of their subsequent loyal deaths/injuries, is **Stannis's historical recollection**. It contrasts Robert's capacity for forgiveness with Stannis's perceived tendency to inspire fear and betrayal.
7. **The proposed campaign targets Claw Isle, not a Lannister fortress.** Axell advocates striking **Ardrian Celtigar's** island, because the lord submitted after capture at Blackwater. He wants to sack its lightly defended home, burn its castle and kill its people while Celtigar remains in King's Landing. He cites treasure: **Myrish carpets, Volantene glass, fine hawks, an axe of Valyrian steel, a horn said to summon monsters from the deep**, gemstones, wine and more. **Those treasures are Axell's report**, not an audited inventory.
8. **The remaining Stannis force is specified.** Axell estimates **about 1,500 men** on Dragonstone, more than half Florents, with Salladhor Saan's surviving fleet. Stannis says Redwyne's fleet has not yet sailed, making a small coastal raid feasible; the naval assessment is his current strategic judgment.
9. **Davos calls the attack cowardice and cruelty.** He argues the women, children and elderly remaining on Claw Isle had family killed fighting for Stannis at Blackwater; the civilian population cannot be equated with the captured lord's decision to kneel. **Davos's anti-massacre argument is the chapter's decisive moral intervention.**
10. **Axell proposes individual duty despite coerced fealty.** His position is that every retainer should die rather than follow a lord who submits to another king; Davos counters that people vary in courage and have limited choices. The difference between formal feudal allegiance and responsibility to protect dependents is explicit.
11. **Davos challenges Stannis with Aerys.** He asks whether Stannis himself remained loyal to his king when Robert rebelled, risking charges of treason and death from Axell. Stannis orders Axell out rather than silencing Davos and later admits choosing between **brother Robert and King Aerys** was difficult. This directly complicates Stannis's seemingly absolute loyalty doctrine.
12. **Stannis distinguishes duty from personal desire.** He says he does not want the Iron Throne for pleasure; as **Robert's lawful heir** he believes ruling is a duty to his daughter, realm and late brother. He notes Rhaenyra's purported treason, Aerys's cuts and Maegor's fate as precedents, but these are a politically interested claimant's accounts rather than legal adjudication of every historical dispute.
13. **Stannis blames corruption in Robert's settlement.** He alleges Cersei's incest, perhaps murder of Robert, Jon Arryn and Ned, and Robert's failure to dismiss **Varys and Jaime**. He recalls Ned urging Jaime sent to the Wall, and Barristan blaming corruption under Aerys partly on Varys. **Accusations and hearsay are not all independently verified.**
14. **Stannis knows why Davos threatened Melisandre.** He asks openly about the attempted killing, then denies she caused the Blackwater fires and attributes battle disaster to wildfire, the fleet's commander and his own pride in sending her away. He says Melisandre urged mercy toward Davos and summoned him instead of letting Axell burn him. **Stannis's blame is a participant's retrospective assessment**, not proof her presence would have prevented the defeat.
15. **Stannis denies responsibility for Renly's shadow assassination.** He claims Melisandre was with him, merely foresaw Renly's death, and could not have participated. This is **his understanding of events**, conflicting with Davos's firsthand observation of a shadow-birth in the Storm's End episode; the king's testimony does not resolve the mechanics or complicity.
16. **The Edric Storm danger is named.** Stannis reports Robert's bastard **Edric Storm** is ill and has been leeched by **Maester Pylos**, with fever and chills. Edric had apparently been befriended by Davos at Melisandre's urging. Stannis speaks of the boy more as **Robert's blood** than as a named child; Davos notes this uneasily.
17. **The apparent royal gifts to Edric are an administrative illusion.** Stannis says Robert's periodic gifts to Edric were selected and delivered by **Varys**, while Robert laughed upon hearing what he supposedly had given. This is a revealing **court-management account**, but Edric's subjective affection remains genuine.
18. **Stannis explicitly rejects burning Edric at this point.** Melisandre demands the boy to wake a stone dragon, weighing one child's death against future children across the world. Stannis says **the boy is innocent**, notes Shireen likes him and Edric shares family blood, and refuses. **This is a present refusal**, not assurance he will never reconsider.
19. **Claw Isle is rejected as evil.** When Davos returns to the idea, Stannis agrees it would win little and that Axell's plan is **evil**, distinguishing punishment of Celtigar himself from killing dependents. He grants Davos influence precisely for speaking against an attractive but immoral raid.
20. **Davos is unexpectedly promoted.** Stannis creates him **Lord of the Rainwood, Admiral of the Narrow Sea, and Hand of the King**. Davos objects that he is lowborn, illiterate and unfit, but Stannis says Pylos can read for him and he prefers honest advice to aristocratic polish. This is a **completed formal appointment**, not an anticipated one.
21. **Lightbringer's visible appearance is unusual but its utility untested.** During Davos's promotion Stannis's sword visibly glows orange, yellow and red and brightens the room, but feels no warmer/different than ordinary steel to Davos's shoulder. **An extraordinary visual effect does not by itself prove it is the prophetic original blade.**
22. **Stannis identifies limited surviving loyalists.** He names **Gilbert Farring** holding Storm's End with about **200 men**, Lord Morrigen, the Bastard of Nightsong, young Chyttering and cousin Andrew. Conversely he reports Celtigar's abandonment, the Velaryon boy's age of six, Bar Emmon's youth, and the new Sunglass sailing to Volantis. These are **king's current reports**, not individually surveyed positions.
23. **Stannis claims to have seen the Fist disaster through flame.** He describes observing **snow falling, a hill in a wood, black-cloaked men behind torchlight and figures moving through snow** in a fire vision, then losing sight as the flames reverted. This plausibly corresponds to [ASOS 18 Samwell](18-samwell.md) and the Watch's Fist battle, but the identity of the event is **INFERRED**, not explicitly confirmed by Stannis.
24. **Melisandre qualifies her own predictive confidence.** She argues the coming war against the Great Other requires bold action, yet says flames can be **ambiguous**: a ritual with leeches 'will and will not' achieve the same result as burning Edric. This matters for prophecy methodology, not proof of precognitive omniscience.
25. **Three blood-filled leeches are burned on named usurpers.** Melisandre presents **three large black leeches**, identified by Davos as fed on **Edric's blood**. Under her instruction Stannis throws them into the brazier one at a time, naming **Joffrey Baratheon**, **Balon Greyjoy** and **Robb Stark**, all as rival claimants. The ritual is **directly witnessed**; none of the named kings dies in this chapter, and **the ritual's causal effectiveness is UNKNOWN**.
26. **End position:** Alester remains imprisoned, Axell's attack rejected, Davos now holds three offices, Stannis refuses to sacrifice Edric while consenting to the leech ritual, and the island leadership accepts a supernatural world-war framing without proof of the leeches' effects.

## Rival explanations and theory tests

- **King's blood magic:** The text shows blood-fed leeches and spoken names, not a controlled causal experiment. Any later deaths need chronology, independent agency, and counterfactual alternatives before being attributed to the ritual.
- **Horn at Claw Isle:** Axell's boast about summoning sea monsters is colorful and might reflect an artifact; test an actual inventory and use case, not a rumor alone. Do not equate it with the Horn of Joramun.
- **Davos versus Stannis's legalism:** Davos reminds Stannis he once faced a difficult divided loyalty himself. This challenges rigid absolutism while helping Stannis decide against collective punishment, not overthrowing his kingship.
- **Edric's danger:** Refusal now can coexist with later pressure if Stannis perceives worse global stakes; watch the exact terms under which he reconsiders.
- **Vision of the Fist:** Strong parallels to the Watch battle but all detail comes from Stannis's retelling of subjective flame experience. Check timing before calling it verified remote sight.
