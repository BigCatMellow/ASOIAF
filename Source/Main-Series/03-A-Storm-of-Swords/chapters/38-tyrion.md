## Chronological and political record

1. **Tyrion must greet Dorne for the crown.** Joffrey and Tywin do not personally meet the arrivals across the Blackwater. Tyrion brings **Bronn, Podrick, Addam Marbrand, Gyles Rosby** and a representative honor guard to welcome the expected Prince Doran Martell. Burnt woods outside the capital reflect Tyrion's Blackwater defense; the city remains under reconstruction.
2. **Nine substantial banners foreshadow a political delegation.** Bronn describes and **Podrick Payne identifies** arms of **Martell (sun and spear), Dalt (lemons), Blackmont (vulture clutching a child), Manwoody (crowned skull), Qorgyle (scorpions), Uller (flames), Allyrion (hand on a divided field), Gargalen (cockatrice), and Jordayne (golden quill)**. Pod is unusually well prepared because Tyrion had told him to study Dornish heraldry. No reason to treat house sigils as supernatural clues without independent narrative support.
3. **Pod notices the missing litter first.** Doran's well-known gout and travel in an elaborate palanquin make a large gap conspicuous. Tyrion speculates easier travel or improved health but feels unease. The arriving mounted leader is younger, healthier and more aggressive: **Prince Oberyn Nymeros Martell**, not Doran.
4. **Doran's absence is explicitly explained.** Oberyn says Doran remains in Sunspear for health and sent his brother **to serve on Joffrey's council**. The offer of council presence is real, while Doran's private strategic intentions are **not directly narrated** in Oberyn's introduction.
5. **Oberyn brings well-connected Dornish companions.** He introduces **Deziel Dalt; Tremond Gargalen; Harmen and Ulwyck Uller; Ryon Allyrion and his bastard Daemon Sand; Dagos, Myles, Mors and Dickon Manwoody; Arron Qorgyle; Myria Jordayne; Larra Blackmont with Jynessa and Perros; and Ellaria Sand** as his paramour. Distinguish the exact members from the banner-bearing houses: a delegation may include heirs/relations rather than reigning lords.
6. **Ellaria's status is deliberately challenging to capital protocol.** Oberyn publicly names a bastard-born **paramour** and expects her to be treated honorably. Tyrion expects Cersei may insult her through seating arrangements. This is a **potential dispute**, not an already witnessed humiliation.
7. **Oberyn's reputation involves alleged poison and past exile.** Tyrion recalls a duel in which adolescent Oberyn cut **Lord Yronwood**, who died when the wound festered; rumors alleged poison. Oberyn traveled in the Free Cities, forged **six Citadel links**, served with the **Second Sons**, then formed a company, and fathered bastard daughters called **Sand Snakes**. Many details are **reported reputation**, not this scene's witnessed biography.
8. **The Willas Tyrell grievance predates the current alliance.** Tyrion has heard Oberyn crippled Highgarden's heir in a tourney. Later in the same conversation Oberyn says Willas still writes him about horses and does not blame him; the injury occurred when a young rider's foot caught and his mount fell, and **Mace Tyrell** allegedly pushed Willas to compete too early. These are Oberyn's assertions and competing interpretations, **not proof the Tyrells share Willas's lack of animosity**.
9. **The arrival creates a triangular political risk.** King Joffrey's court relies on **Mace, Loras and Reach soldiers** while accepting a Dornish faction led by a man despised for Willas's injury. Tyrion rightly anticipates possible friction, but **no Dornish–Tyrell fighting occurs in this chapter**.
10. **Oberyn reveals a visit to Tyrion's infancy.** He says he visited **Casterly Rock with his mother, consort and sister Elia** after Joanna died giving birth to Tyrion; **Cersei and Jaime were eight or nine**, Oberyn around fourteen/fifteen. Tywin reportedly ignored the visitors and assigned Kevan to entertain them.
11. **Rumors about baby Tyrion were monstrous nonsense.** Oberyn recounts alleged horns/tail, claws, oversized head and other fantastical deformities, then says the actual child was a small red infant with stunted legs and an ordinary body. The contrast is strong evidence that court rumor is **not biological reality**.
12. **Oberyn recalls Cersei hurting the infant Tyrion.** He says **Cersei twisted the infant's genitals** and accused him of killing Joanna; Jaime intervened and made her stop. This is **firsthand testimony from Oberyn** given decades later, and Tyrion cannot remember it. It provides a plausible early pattern but requires caution about memory and Oberyn's motive for telling a hurtful story.
13. **Elia behaved differently.** Oberyn describes **Elia** as wanting to cuddle the baby, giving the infant sympathy. Her kindness and the children's early acquaintance may matter for motives, but they do **not** establish she knew future atrocities or shared the royal marriage plots.
14. **The 'dwarf's penny' is wrongly pinned on Tyrion.** Tywin imposed a **penny tax on each act of prostitution**, but as master of coin Tyrion bears the popular blame. Bronn reports street taunts. This is a concrete example of political responsibility being attached to the official who implements, not originates, a policy.
15. **Oberyn and Ellaria are candid about sexual interests.** Oberyn mentions shared tastes, and Tyrion anticipates potential scandal but no sexual contact at the royal court is shown. The point is Dornish openness clashing with Lannister etiquette, not proof the visit's main purpose is recreation.
16. **Oberyn makes his true objective unmistakable.** He came for **justice for his sister Elia and her murdered children**, not simply a council seat, wedding or hospitality. He says Doran investigated long ago through **Jon Arryn** and many other sources, and rejects Tyrion's suggestion of an overdue official inquiry.
17. **The chain of responsibility is central.** Oberyn wants **Gregor Clegane** to admit who gave orders to kill Elia and children before he dies, potentially reaching beyond Gregor to his patrons. This is the **investigator's objective**, not testimony proving a specific order from Tywin.
18. **He contrasts himself with prudent Doran.** Oberyn says he is more bloodthirsty and impatient than his brother; Doran's absence is therefore an important **style and risk difference**, not evidence the brothers have opposed goals.
19. **Threat is moderated by force arithmetic.** Tyrion estimates around **300 Dornish** in Oberyn's party against near **5,000 gold cloaks**, **20,000** of his father's men, and tens of thousands of Reach troops. These are **Tyrion's approximate military claims**, not a census or proof Oberyn cannot attempt targeted vengeance.
20. **Oberyn disputes allusion to Willas's tragedy.** He insists Willas bears no grievance against him and places blame chiefly on Mace. This makes Oberyn's perceived quarrel with the Tyrells asymmetric: Mace may still resent him despite Willas's reported forgiveness.
21. **Tyrion's final caution is coded.** He suggests Oberyn keep companions from **Chataya's** brothel nearby after Oberyn says he would prefer to die with a breast in hand. It is an insult and warning about his dangerous behavior. Their diplomatic relationship is antagonistic from the first ride.
22. **End state:** Oberyn and his delegation approach King's Landing for his council role and coming royal wedding; he has openly announced his demand for Elia's justice. No Gregor confrontation, inquiry, assassination, public reception or council vote occurs in this chapter.

## Rival scenarios and research tests

- **Who ordered Elia's murder?** Oberyn expects Gregor to know a sponsor; no confession is obtained here. Check Tywin's later explanations, Gregor's own words, the child-targeting policy and independent Sack testimony.
- **Doran versus Oberyn:** The diplomat/avenger distinction could be a purposeful coordinated strategy or genuinely different tolerances for risk. Avoid treating Doran's gout as passivity.
- **Willas/Oberyn:** Oberyn's direct account provides a possible accidental mechanism and ongoing friendship, but the Tyrell court may resent the political impact regardless.
- **Cersei and Tyrion childhood:** The nursery episode gives a witness-based account of early aggression; Oberyn's motive to provoke Tyrion and the decades-long memory gap remain ordinary rival explanations for embellishment.
- **Political magnitudes:** A 300-person delegation is not a siege army, but a highly placed representative with supporters can still create a violent crisis inside a court.
