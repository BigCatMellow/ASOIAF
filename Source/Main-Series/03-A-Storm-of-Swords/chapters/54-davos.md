# ASOS 54 — Davos: two leech deaths are not proof, Edric's sacrifice debate, and the suppressed Watch letter

## Chronology and major decisions

1. **News reaches Dragonstone by rumor rather than bodies.** **Salladhor Saan** reports the Red Wedding as celebrated in King's Landing and repeats a rumor that **Robb's head was removed and a direwolf head attached**, and **Catelyn was thrown naked into a river**. Robb's and Catelyn's deaths are corroborated in [ASOS 51 Catelyn](51-catelyn.md), but the postmortem treatment is **reported hearsay at this location**, not independently witnessed by Davos.
2. **Stannis rejects an easy supernatural explanation.** Axell and Selyse credit R'hllor for the killings. Stannis retorts that **Walder Frey**, not a god, appears to have committed them. Davos also recognizes that violation of guest right makes the Freys morally culpable. **No leech mechanism is proven.**
3. **Stannis initially seeks political advantage through mercy.** He orders Saan's fast ships to carry envoys bearing **full pardons to the Iron Islands and White Harbor** for those who swear fealty. This is a **new diplomatic plan**, not a completed embassy or resulting allegiance.
4. **Melisandre predicts the crisis will continue.** She says new false kings will arise and **pardons and leeches cannot win the realm**. Crucially she admits she can **mistake warnings for predictions in fire visions**, while arguing divine messages are true. This is first-person acknowledgement of **interpretive fallibility**, not proof every fire-reading is literal.
5. **The royal cause's remaining assets are specified.** Stannis says **1,300 men on Dragonstone, 300 at Storm's End**, Salladhor Saan's fleet, **no coin for mercenaries** and little prospect of recruitment. This is his own snapshot, compare ASOS 36 Davos's earlier force estimates instead of assuming identical reporting times.
6. **Selyse proposes awakening dragons as the missing force multiplier.** She compares Stannis's Dragonstone to Aegon's and invokes **a sacrificial price**. Stannis counters with earlier failed royal attempts: **nine mages invited by Aegon III**, **Baelor I praying over eggs**, **Aegon IV's wooden/metal dragons**, and **Aerion Brightflame dying after drinking wildfire**. These are **Stannis's historical summary**, not firsthand confirmation of every attempt or proof dragons cannot awaken.
7. **Melisandre explicitly demands Edric's death.** She claims sacrificing **Robert's son Edric Storm** will awaken a stone dragon. **Selyse and Axell** endorse it; Selyse refers to Robert's liaison with her cousin **Delena Florent in the marital bed** and imagines the boy's death curing her own reproductive humiliation. Edric's innocence is repeatedly affirmed by Davos and acknowledged by Stannis.
8. **Stannis is genuinely tempted.** He remembers young Robert taking him to court, seeing a man he mistook for Aerys but was actually **Tywin**, recalls the dragon skulls and imagines having dragons of his own. This is a **temptation** under strategic despair, not a completed agreement to burn Edric.
9. **Davos insists on naming Edric as a person.** He repeats **Edric Storm** rather than 'the boy' and warns that kinslaying is accursed. His argument centers specific personhood, kinship and the moral value of one life versus Melisandre's quantitative world-saving argument.
10. **Davos directly challenges the leech outcome.** Since **Robb** died at Walder's wedding and **Balon** reportedly fell off a bridge, he demands to know whom the leeches really killed. Of the **three named kings** only **two are reported dead so far**, leaving **Joffrey alive** at this scene. Stannis agrees **two is not three**; Melisandre treats the prospective third as a future public proof. **No experiment isolates magic from political murder or weather**.
11. **After dismissing zealots, Stannis frames the sacrifice as duty.** He recognizes Edric resembles his brother and is Shireen's companion, but worries that the **Great Other** threatens the whole realm. He says **one boy** is small against a kingdom; Davos replies **everything**. Stannis dismisses him with a threat of renewed imprisonment. **No decision to sacrifice is announced**, but risk has grown sharply.
12. **Stannis reports another fire vision.** He says he saw **a king wearing a burning crown that consumed his flesh**, associating it with threats against Joffrey. **This is Stannis's subjective description**, not a witnessed death or proof he identified the vision correctly.
13. **Dragonstone's stone dragons are physically pervasive.** Davos notices gargoyles, dragons on keeps, stairs, ovens and towers; he wonders if Valyrian magic might have **petrified living dragons**. Saan correctly observes the carvings form actual inhabitable **rooms, chimneys, windows and privies**, providing a **mundane architectural objection** to the literal-stone-dragon theory.
14. **Saan resents the foregone Claw Isle raid.** He jokingly recalls lost Celtigar wine, a trained sea eagle and **a claimed horn to summon krakens**. These are **looting rumors**, not an inventoried artifact or used magic instrument. This horn must not be conflated with Sam's old horn or the Horn of Winter.
15. **Davos has organized politically vulnerable supporters.** Men favoring the king without red-priest religious zeal include **Ser Gerald Gower**, **Ser Andrew Estermont**, and **the Bastard of Nightsong**; he recruited them discreetly like ship's crew. This is an **operational counterweight to queen's men**, not an openly declared armed opposition to Stannis.
16. **Saan warns about danger to Davos.** He knows Davos is accumulating allies; he questions whether Stannis would ever kill family, noting **Renly's death** as an uncomfortable exception. Davos insists Edric is innocent while Renly took arms, which is a legal difference but does not explain away supernatural killing mechanisms.
17. **Davos begins to read in earnest.** **Maester Pylos** teaches both Davos and his young son **Devan**, alongside **Princess Shireen and Edric**. Davos is humiliated by the children's facility but persists to serve as Hand. This literacy subplot creates a direct pathway for a strategic intelligence breakthrough.
18. **Pylos discusses merit versus lineage through former Hands.** He cites failures of **Ryam Redwyne, Murmison, Butterwell, Myles Smallwood and Otto Hightower**, contrasting **Septon Barth**, a smith's son who allegedly brought forty years of peace. This is a **maester's political-historical lesson**, not direct verification of each ministerial record.
19. **Shireen is known to survive childhood greyscale.** Her cheek and neck retain grey, hardened scarring, with life and sight spared. This is a physical character observation; **the chapter does not establish contagion or dormant disease**.
20. **Edric recounts Robert's three Summerhall victories.** He describes Lord **Fell** killed, **Silveraxe** captured, and **Grandison/Cafferen** turned afterward. Pylos corrects his hero worship by mentioning **Robert's defeat at Ashford**. These are **educational reports and childhood bias**, not newly witnessed battles.
21. **The Watch's letter is discovered through Davos's reading lesson.** He struggles through a previously received black-sealed message addressed **to five kings**, warning that **Mance's vast host comes south**, that **Mormont had been under attack in the haunted forest**, and later birds brought **no written message**. This corresponds to the known dispatch and missing-bird issue from [ASOS 32 Tyrion](32-tyrion.md).
22. **Stannis has not been shown the letter.** Pylos says he delivered it to **Alester Florent when Alester was Hand**, believes the queen heard, and was told Stannis had no men to spare; on explicit questioning, Pylos says **Stannis and Melisandre have not read it**. The specific blockage is **Alester's dismissal**, not yet a king's formal refusal. This corrects any simplistic claim that Stannis knowingly ignored the appeal.
23. **Davos's experience trading with free folk informs his judgment.** As a boy aboard **the Cobblecat under Roro Uhoris ('Blind Bastard')**, he saw wildling trade, capture of a cabin girl, and the Watch confiscating their steel/ivory/amber/obsidian cargo; Uhoris was executed for arms trade. Davos later traded with Eastwatch. He sees **wildlings as variable people**, neither intrinsically evil nor safely harmless.
24. **The moral dilemma grows at the chapter's end.** Davos realizes Melisandre's predicted cold and Stannis's vision of men amid torches might correspond to the Watch crisis. He thinks of **Nissa Nissa sacrificed by Azor Ahai** and fears Edric might become that analogue. **He initially puts the letter aside and asks for another** rather than immediately delivering it; thus **no Watch assistance is ordered on this page**.
25. **End state:** Stannis has not committed to killing Edric but is wavering; two named kings are confirmed/reported dead; Davos knows of a desperate northern plea that the king has not received; a potential shift in Stannis's mission exists but is **not yet enacted**.

## Counterreadings / investigation

- **Melisandre's predictive power:** Two actual deaths do not demonstrate that rituals caused them. Her fallibility admission warrants independent dates and mechanisms for all future claims.
- **Stannis's choice:** Sacrifice temptation grows alongside recognition of Edric's innocence. He is not reducible to either an immediate child murderer or a permanently certain protector at this moment.
- **The Night's Watch appeal:** The concrete letter exists and has **not reached Stannis**; build an institutional handoff audit including Bowen Marsh, Pylos, Alester and the queen. Any later change of strategy should be tied to a demonstrable handover.
- **Dragon mythology:** Historical failures and Dragonstone architecture do not refute all magic; they restrict the simplistic interpretation of an entire castle turning into hatchlings.
- **Davos's seamanship:** Navigation, choosing trustworthy crew and questioning absolute authority visibly translate into his work as Hand, while his inability to read initially made him dependent on others.
