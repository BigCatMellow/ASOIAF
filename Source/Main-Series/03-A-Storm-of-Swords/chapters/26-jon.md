## Chronological events / causal chain

1. **The raiders camp north of the Wall on a moonless clear night.** Jon leaves the concealed cave temporarily, claiming to look for Ghost. The Wall blocks stars to the south, while the nearby valleys and ridges obscure distant approaches. Jon estimates they are between **Shadow Tower and Castle Black**, likely nearer Shadow Tower, but cannot fix the position precisely.
2. **The same sky receives different cultural names.** Jon recalls the Ice Dragon, Shadowcat, Moonmaid and Sword of the Morning from Luwin. Ygritte calls the King's Crown the **Cradle**, the Stallion the **Horned Lord**, and the Faith's red Smith-star the **Thief**; a story about the Thief entering Moonmaid frames what 'stealing' a wife means to her. These are competing names and customs, not contradictory astronomical positions.
3. **Jon's family identity is unstable.** He wonders whether Arya is or was truly his sister, recalling his bastard status and that he has now apparently forfeited Night's Watch brotherhood too. This is his **self-doubt**, not evidence against the family relationships the narrative previously established.
4. **Ghost is found watching the stars.** His red eyes, white fur and silence are observed; he licks the scratches the earlier **Orell eagle** left on Jon's face. Jon thinks both he and Ghost were marked by the bird. Any literal magical significance of the two injuries remains unproven.
5. **Jon deliberately sends Ghost away.** He has no means to lift the direwolf over the Wall with the raiders, and gives verbal instructions to travel east along the Wall toward **Castle Black** as a potential warning. He lacks parchment, ink and quill to send written intelligence. Ghost runs off; Jon cannot tell if the wolf obeys or is simply pursuing prey. **No successful arrival is shown**.
6. **Jon's warging is possible but untrained.** He recalls a dream through Ghost's eyes of Mance's Milkwater gathering that matched reality but cannot consciously communicate orders through skinchanging. His recognition of limits avoids assuming an infallible private message channel.
7. **Mance has already split off an advance party.** After the ruined Fist, Jon joined **Styr, Jarl, roughly one hundred Thenns plus other raiders**. They sleep in deserted wildling settlements with guarded horses. He has not found a plausible gap in surveillance to escape to Castle Black.
8. **Jon questions his decision not to attack Mance.** He believes Qhorin might have risked killing the king beyond the Wall. This is guilt and **counterfactual imagining**, not proof an assault at the Fist would have changed the migration or been possible.
9. **Jon's relationship with Ygritte is established, not merely alleged.** Their intimacy began on the journey and has become frequent. Jon initially justified it as necessary espionage cover, but his repeated choices and increasing affection exceed the narrow tactical explanation. It puts his vow of celibacy and divided allegiance into genuine conflict.
10. **He recalls Ned's supposed lapse to explain his own.** Jon compares himself to the father he believes broke a marriage vow. This reflects **Jon's beliefs about Ned and his own parentage**, not independent evidence about Ned's conduct or Jon's mother.
11. **The cave entrance is intentionally concealed.** It faces north and a pine shields it, so its fires would not be visible to a watchman atop the Wall. Passage descends into an underground chamber with horses, water and a possible lower cavern. This is specific stealth logistics, not proof the cave connects to the other side.
12. **Joint leadership creates friction.** Mance put **Styr, Magnar of Thenn, and Jarl** in command. Styr brought about a hundred men—five times Jarl's force—and resents sharing command, yet Jarl is the experienced climber with a dozen crossings over the Wall. **Command hierarchy is not the same as competence at the passage task.**
13. **The wildlings extract operational Watch information.** Styr asks about patrol routes, frequency, strengths and whether **Stonedoor/Greyguard** are manned. Jon truthfully describes typical four-person patrols (**two rangers, two builders**), trained mules from Eastwatch, inspections atop or at the base, and the history of alternating or irregular schedules.
14. **Knowledge is conditional and partly historical.** Jon attributes older fixed schedules to **Qorgyle**, while Mormont varied patrol times and sent occasional temporary parties to abandoned castles. Jon says his uncle Benjen introduced some uncertainty tactics. These are his training-based recollections, **not a live duty roster**.
15. **The Arson Iceaxe tale is repeated.** Patrols once found an enemy digging a tunnel partway through the Wall and sealed his exit rather than digging him out; Edd jokes that Arson's axe can still be heard. This is a **Watch story**, not proof a surviving tunnel or ghost currently exists.
16. **Jon withholds reliable present numbers.** He knows only **Eastwatch, Castle Black and Shadow Tower** were manned when he left, not any temporary redeployments, and he claims 500/200/300 across the three. He internally acknowledges **inflating the combined total by three hundred**, and Jarl suspects a lie or inclusion of men lost at the Fist.
17. **A spy's deception is therefore mixed.** Jon gives useful tactical facts while falsifying troop numbers. Styr threatens to remove his tongue if caught lying, and Jon understands Ygritte would suffer for him. The mission is **not** safely preserving Watch secrets in total.
18. **Thenn culture differs from the wider free folk.** Jon thinks Styr's valley kingdom, bronze helmets and almost absolute discipline resemble lordly rule more than other raider communities, even though Styr calls himself a First Man and functions under Mance. This distinction will matter for later integration theories, not all wildlings being interchangeable.
19. **Ygritte explores a lower grotto.** Following Grigg the Goat's directions, Jon finds her beyond a crawlspace, a waterfall, pool and further downward passage. She says she walked about a hundred paces before turning back, not that she found an exit. The cave system's extent and safety remain unknown.
20. **Gorne's Way is told as a disputed legend.** Ygritte recounts Gorne and Gendel leading free folk beneath the Wall long ago; the North, Umbers and Watch attacked them after emergence. Jon remembers a history in which both leaders died, but Ygritte claims Gendel escaped back into caves, lost his way, and descendants still hide hungry below. **A remembered folk narrative is not present proof of an accessible passage.**
21. **A genuine strategic alternative is nonetheless raised.** If an under-Wall route exists, raiding groups could bypass the climb. But no map, tested entrance or recent crossing is shown, and Ygritte speaks partly to frighten and entertain Jon.
22. **The grotto offers apparent respite from war.** Alone there, Jon and Ygritte discuss earlier partners and customs, and further intimacy occurs. Their companionship is no longer plausibly reducible to Mance's cover story, although the need to fool Styr is still real.
23. **Ygritte interprets 'stealing' as a commitment and social norm.** She regarded Jon's attack on Orell and his capture of her as beginning courtship; he insists he did not knowingly do so. The exchange illustrates divergent social meaning even when both remember the same action.
24. **She gives her own earlier history.** Ygritte says she is **nineteen**, previously had a partner from outside her village, and regards marrying village kin as taboo. **These are personal reports**, not a representative survey of all free-folk customs.
25. **Craster's parentage is offered as an explanation, not tested history.** Ygritte says Craster was fathered by a Watchman who abandoned a Whitetree woman and that the Watch rejected mother and child at Castle Black. She condemns his marriages to daughters as cursed. The narrative elsewhere confirms his incest, but her account of the father is **reported tradition**.
26. **Jon's vow language changes.** He catches himself referring to having **been** of the Watch, and after returning to Ygritte feels weaker guilt. This is evidence of shifting identity and attachment; it does **not** show a formal release from vows or a completed defection in law.
27. **Ygritte proposes never leaving.** She wants to retreat into the caves and abandon Styr and Jarl. The proposition is emotionally real but no survival plan or underworld settlement is demonstrated. At chapter end they are **still in the cave north of the Wall**; the crossing has not begun.

## Questions and rival scenarios

- **Does Ghost reach the Watch?** The wolf may follow Jon's instruction or act on another urge. Test later independent Ghost sightings before crediting Castle Black with a warning.
- **Can Jon safely return?** He knows patrol protocol but is under Styr's surveillance. The cover relies partly on harming his own vows; whether/when he can pass on actionable information depends on the raiders' route.
- **Gorne's Way:** Could be an actual concealed passage, a cave network with a sealed/forgotten link, or oral tradition mingling several events. Seek physical use evidence, not repeated telling alone.
- **Free folk kinship:** Ygritte's taboo against local inbreeding is a meaningful contrast to Craster, but avoid mapping her village values to every clan.
- **Identity:** Love is not proof Jon has ceased to care about the Watch. His divided loyalties and immediate tactical risks can coexist.

**End state:** Ghost departs under uncertain instructions; Styr has partial Watch intelligence; Jon and Ygritte remain in hidden caves, relationship deepening. Independent close-reading challenge remains open.
