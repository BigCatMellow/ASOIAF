## Chronological causal sequence, memory and action

1. **LANDING AT A HISTORIC INN:** Jaime, **Brienne** and **Cleos Frey** see an apparently dark, abandoned building beside the **Red Fork**. The sign shows a kneeling king, and Cleos identifies the place as the **Inn of the Kneeling Man**, traditionally where **Torrhen Stark submitted to Aegon the Conqueror** rather than face dragons. Jaime interprets that surrender as shrewd. The site is **historical local tradition and visible sign**, not direct proof every detail of Torrhen's submission occurred at the precise porch.
2. **DANGEROUS OCCUPANTS:** A **fifteen-year-old boy with a loaded crossbow** demands whether they are lion, fish or wolf. An adult carrying a **cleaver** says he and his wife **found the previous innkeeper and his household dead and buried them**, then occupied the inn. He has lost sons to Lannister soldiers and sickness, while the boy's mother died at the **Bloody Mummers' hands**. Their claims are **self-reported** and neither the death circumstances nor the occupants' actual affiliations are independently confirmed.
3. **A WAR ZONE EATS ITS OWN HORSES:** The improvised proprietor serves them **old horse meat, stale bread and onions**, charging coin even amid danger. He possesses **three horses** said to be found or wandering after owners died or fled, including a saddled **dapple-gray palfrey with a blood-stained pink-and-black checkered saddlecloth**. The color pattern and blood are **specific forensic clues**, not proof of a particular slain knight or faction since Jaime does not recognize the colors.
4. **ROUTE INTELLIGENCE MIXED WITH SELF-INTEREST:** Their host warns the Trident below the inn contains **sunken boats obstructing the channel**, robbery around the **Skipping Stones and Red Deer Island**, and uncertain control at the **ruby ford** by Roose Bolton, Beric or Lannister men. He advises **leaving the river** and taking a **southeastern path through woods**, avoiding the **southern road toward Ser Warren's towerhouse**. The hazards may be real, but the man could also be steering them toward associates; none of his precise locations are directly investigated before they depart.
5. **BERIC'S NEW REPUTATION:** The cook reports **Beric Dondarrion, the Lightning Lord**, moving unpredictably across river crossings with **Thoros of Myr**, and rumors **he cannot die**. Jaime remembers Thoros as a heavy-drinking red priest joking about stained robes. The apparent supernatural invulnerability is **a smallfolk rumor**, not witnessed resurrection in this Jaime chapter.
6. **THE CHANGE TO LAND TRANSPORT:** Brienne chooses to **buy all three horses for three gold dragons**, over Jaime's protest that she is paying too much, and **gives the proprietor their river skiff**. The horses are unequal: old plow horse for Brienne, a sturdy grey palfrey for Cleos, and an **aged, one-eyed white gelding for Jaime**. This makes Jaime's escape by riding away much less feasible; Brienne knows the practical disadvantages while making her transport choice.
7. **SHACKLES ONLY PARTLY REMOVED:** Using an old **smithy**, Brienne cuts Jaime's **ankle chain** so he can ride, but **retains wrist irons**, resisting his demand to remove the entire restraint. Jaime's irritated jokes about the manure in the stables may be attempts to warn her of a trap, not a reliable declaration he wishes her well.
8. **THE INNKEEP'S LURE REJECTED:** The man presses them to **sleep overnight**, offering comfortable beds and coverlets. Cleos wants to stay, but Brienne and Jaime recognize the repeated pressure and refuse. Jaime becomes aware they may be walking into robbery or worse, while Brienne is competent enough to weigh a convincing guide's incentives.
9. **CHOOSING THE OPPOSITE ROAD:** At the burned-village fork, Brienne deliberately turns **south, toward the supposedly dangerous towerhouse**, not toward the forest route the cook recommended. She explains that the woods seem a likely outlaw ambush and the new proprietor was suspiciously eager to dictate their route. Jaime independently prefers that same road and begins to revise his low opinion of her judgment. **The actual existence of accomplices in the woods remains an inference**, not an encountered group.
10. **THE WATCHKEEPER'S LIGHTS:** The travelers see light from **Ser Warren's stone towerhouse**, but Brienne leads them around through fields, then rejoins the route after passing it. They avoid contact; the garrison's identity and alliances remain unknown. It is an observed fortification, not verified evidence the cook deliberately meant to save or trap them.
11. **CAMP WITHOUT FIRE:** After several hours by moonlight they stop by a stream under **oak and ash**, share salt fish and cakes, and **do not light a fire**, consistent with evading marauders. Their daytime goal is still **King's Landing**, under Catelyn's coerced hostage exchange. The passage shows a fragile truce but not that Jaime's prisoner status ended.
12. **JAIME APOLOGIZES BRIEFLY:** Jaime provokes Brienne about her gender and family, then thinks of **Tyrion** and apologizes for unintended offense. Brienne responds that his major crimes cannot be forgiven by a small courtesy. The moment is minor but real; he **can choose restraint** even amid his default cruelty.
13. **THE WHITE CLOAK QUESTION:** Brienne asks why Jaime took **Kingsguard vows at fifteen** only to violate them. Jaime initially claims youth and honor, but the viewpoint supplies a far more concrete motive: **to remain physically near Cersei** at Aerys's court, not primarily devotion to protecting the sovereign.
14. **TYWIN'S LYSA PLAN:** Jaime recalls **Tywin intended to marry him to Lysa Tully** and had arranged discussions with **Hoster Tully**. Cersei, then attached to the royal court, told him that **Harlan Grandison's Kingsguard death** created a vacancy; she expected Aerys would appoint Tywin's brilliant son without asking the Hand's consent. **The marriage plan and Cersei's intervention are Jaime's private memories**, stronger than later gossip but not directly narrated by Tywin or Hoster in this chapter.
15. **THE PRICE OF THE INVESTITURE:** Cersei met Jaime secretly at an **Eel Alley inn**, persuaded him intimacy and Kingsguard appointment were worth surrendering **Casterly Rock**, and promised to secure the appointment. **A royal raven arrived at Casterly Rock a moon later**, directing Jaime to take the white at the **Harrenhal tourney**. The sequence materially supports her successful intervention, while exact communications with Aerys are not shown.
16. **THE BACKFIRE THAT EXPLAINS THE HANDSHIP CHANGE:** Tywin was enraged by his heir's investiture, resigned the Handship and **took Cersei back to Casterly Rock**. Instead of bringing the twins together, Jaime's plan left him **alone with Aerys** while Cersei remained far away. This matches [ASOS 4 Tyrion](04-tyrion.md)'s later dispute: Jaime's Kingsguard vows make Tyrion the plausible heir by law, yet Tywin still refuses to grant the Rock.
17. **OTHER AERYS HANDS AND ILyn:** Jaime remembers Aerys ordering **Ilyn Payne's tongue removed** after Payne suggested Tywin was the real power. He recounts rapidly replaced Hands by heraldry, including **a horn-of-plenty figure, a dancing griffin, a mace-and-dagger figure burned by wildfire, and Rossart with a torch**. Some associations are remembered in a compact, emotionally charged passage; avoid inventing exact identity for every heraldry here without cross-checking chronological sources.
18. **JAIME'S EARLIER REAL KNIGHTHOOD:** Jaime says he won a tourney melee at **thirteen**, rode with **Ser Arthur Dayne** against the **Kingswood Brotherhood at fifteen**, and **Arthur knighted him on the battlefield**. He distinguishes **earned knighthood** from the **white cloak** that he believes morally compromised him. These are explicit participant memories, not proof Brienne's objections are entirely wrong.
19. **BRIENNE'S RENLY DEFENSE:** Jaime mocks her as a 'kingslayer' over rumors she murdered Renly. Brienne denies harming him and repeats **Catelyn's testimony of a cold shadow and sudden blood** ([ACOK 33 Catelyn](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/33-catelyn.md)). Jaime rejects her explanation as absurd, despite the reader's separate [ACOK 42 Davos](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/42-davos.md) evidence of shadow birth. This is an exemplary **unequal-knowledge confrontation**: Brienne's improbable-sounding claim has stronger independent reader support than Jaime realizes.
20. **REGICIDE MEMORY SPECIFIES ROSSART FIRST:** Jaime remembers entering Aerys's throne room wearing **golden armor** with sword already bloodied, and Aerys demanding whether the blood was Tywin's. Jaime replies it belongs to **Rossart**, the pyromancer Hand, whom Jaime **killed before Aerys**. This changes the popular causal story of a solitary king killing: a **second important pre-regicide victim** died as part of Jaime's actions.
21. **AERYS'S STATED DESTRUCTION THREAT:** Aerys rants that **traitors are inside the city**, Rossart has gone to make them a **fiery reception**, and Jaime should bring **Tywin's head or burn with all the rest**. The words directly associate **Rossart and royal plans for mass burning**, but the chapter **does not show the full locations or number of explosive caches, the chain of pyromancer subordinates, or their final disarming**. Index both the strong testimony and its limits.
22. **DEATH OF THE LAST DRAGONKING:** Jaime physically pulls the frightened, soiled Aerys away from the Iron Throne and **cuts his throat**, killing him instantly. He recalls **Rossart resisting** more than Aerys and **Ser Elys Westerling and Crakehall soldiers entering in time to witness the regicide**. Jaime wishes he could have removed the white cloak and even concealed the act, but public blame was inevitable once armed men saw the dying king.
23. **SUCCESSION NOT HIS INSTANT OBJECTIVE:** **Roland Crakehall** asks whether to proclaim **Tywin, Robert or a Targaryen successor**. Jaime briefly considers the child **Viserys or infant Aegon**, decides he wants none of it, and tells Crakehall to proclaim whomever he likes. He sits on the **Iron Throne with his blade across his knees** until Ned Stark comes in. The act of sitting is **directly remembered**, not proof Jaime actually attempted to usurp the crown.
24. **CRITICISM OF NED'S JUDGMENT:** Jaime recalls Ned arriving, interpreting Jaime as oathbreaker, and inwardly objects that Ned **never asked why Rossart died, what Aerys ordered or what prevented catastrophe**. His indignation is understandable given the remembered threat, but **does not exonerate every later act**, including Bran's attempted murder.
25. **THE NIGHTMARE OF GREEN FIRE:** Jaime dreams of **burning dead in green flame** who multiply each time he strikes them down. Like Tyrion's dreams after Blackwater, these are survivor guilt and pyromancer imagery; **the dream is not a literal continuing wildfire explosion at the camp**. Brienne wakes Jaime before dawn, and the three resume riding through rain.

## Theory priority tests

- **Jaime prevented mass murder:** His memory strongly supports killing Rossart **before** Aerys to halt an expected catastrophe; exactly how widespread, technically ready and counterfactually certain the plan was still requires subsequent hard source evidence. A total account of his motive cannot ignore Rossart, but an exculpation should not be overstated.
- **Cersei engineered her own loss of Jaime:** She obtained his white cloak as a device to avoid Lysa and cohabit at court, yet Tywin removed her immediately. Her knowledge of Aerys's temperament and appointment machinery is clear; her precise influence on Aerys's final yes is unshown.
- **Catspaw and Bran:** The passage makes Jaime more complicated without altering the **well-established fact he pushed Bran**. Caution against using later redeeming testimony to erase direct abuses.
- **Outlaw route trap:** Brienne correctly reasons a proprietor may lure armed travelers to associates, but the suspected ambushers are not actually encountered on the road she avoids. Preserve distinction between **plausible warning** and proven criminal network.
- **Guest sovereignty and Kneeling Man:** Torrhen's choice to kneel rather than burn mirrors the book's recurring contrast between **survival and honor**, evident in Brienne's vows and Jaime's regicide defense.
