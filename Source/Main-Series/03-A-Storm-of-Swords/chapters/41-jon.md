## Chronology and causal sequence

1. **Jon marches south within the Gift, already beyond the Wall.** Styr's raiders descended the Wall's southern face at the abandoned **Greyguard**, where broken stone steps still permit a descent. The company marches deep into the Gift, generally avoiding the Watch's usual patrol routes and inhabited settlements. **Do not place this chapter north of the Wall or imply the party remains on its crest.**
2. **Watch depopulation has long economic consequences.** Jon sees abandoned roundtowers, orchards and reclaimed fields: the Watch formerly obtained rent in goods and labor from the New Gift, but weakening patrol and farm populations mutually reinforce collapse. This is observed abandoned property plus Jon's informed historical interpretation, not an audited demographic count.
3. **A previously articulated settlement strategy has been lost.** Jon recalls **Ned and Benjen discussing restoring settlement in unused towerhouses** by creating new lordships, with the Watch ceding some land while receiving taxes from settlers instead of Winterfell. Ned considered it a **project for a coming spring**, not something accomplished. This is an especially important alternative to permanent militarized isolation of the Wall.
4. **Ygritte makes a rival claim about property and kingship.** She says land, food, woods, water and sky belong to everyone; kings manufacture ownership and call those who take food thieves. Jon counters that raids also seize jewels, weapons, stores and people. The conversation exposes **competing political ideologies and concrete harms**, not that either speaker's slogans resolve all abuses.
5. **Their views of wife-stealing remain irreconcilable.** Ygritte defends a woman's right to kill a violent captor, says men cannot own both a woman and a knife, and rejects Jon's objection to coerced capture; Jon points out scenarios of drunkenness and brutality. She simultaneously frames their own love as possession. The difference matters to future wildling integration and questions of freely given consent.
6. **Jon doubts Mance can win a conventional battle.** He contrasts individual free-folk bravery against trained discipline and warns Ygritte the migrating host may die. **Military outcome remains his forecast**, not established by one battle.
7. **He still means to protect the Watch.** Although he reciprocates Ygritte's love and has bonded with men in Styr's party, he explicitly thinks he remains Ned Stark's son and Night's Watch brother, seeking an opportunity to warn Castle Black. His worry that **Styr might punish Ygritte** if he flees restricts his options.
8. **Styr continues detailed questioning.** Every evening he asks Jon about Castle Black's defenders, deployment and fortifications. Jon lies sometimes but fears **Grigg and Errok** may recognize contradictions. Castle Black has **no southern palisade or earthworks**, only loosely grouped buildings and a largely noncombatant garrison, making a surprise assault potentially devastating.
9. **The planned targets are vulnerable and named.** Jon imagines **Bowen Marsh, Aemon, Clydas, Donal Noye, Septon Cellador, Hobb, Ser Wynton Stout, Pyp, Halder, Toad and other boys** unable to withstand a surprise nighttime raid. Jon guesses perhaps four hundred remain; the number is **his uncertain estimate**, not a census.
10. **His emotional cost is cumulative.** Jon has learned individual stories about **Errok, Grigg, Quort, Bodger, Hempen Dan, Henk, Stone Thumbs and others**; in particular Quort is roughly fourteen, already married in free-folk custom, and expects a child. Their eventual deaths are not anonymous 'wildling casualties'. Grigg wishes to see the **green men on the Isle of Faces**, a local cultural and mythic thread—not proof that he has been there.
11. **Ghost has not returned to Jon's awareness.** He does not know whether Ghost reached Castle Black or joined another wolf pack. Jon says he no longer senses the direwolf in dreams. **No unseen message delivery should be credited to Ghost.**
12. **An approaching storm redirects the column.** **Lenn** forecasts the weather, with some raiders attributing the ability to a woods-witch mother. Grigg directs them to an abandoned lakeside village roughly two or three miles away. This weather forecast is compatible with ordinary incoming clouds.
13. **The tower and inn are Queenscrown.** In lightning Jon identifies **yellow/gold-painted merlons** of an island tower and tells Ygritte the holdfast is **Queenscrown**, where **Alysanne** stayed and whose history Old Nan told him, with Luwin corroborating much. **The same merlons and hidden stone causeway are independently observed by Bran in [ASOS 40 Bran](40-bran.md).**
14. **A single traveler is caught by the raiders.** Scouting finds a fire at the ruined inn, one **silent older horseman** and his mare. Thenns seize his belongings and horse. Styr insists civilians they encounter be killed to prevent warnings from reaching Castle Black. The man's name, destination and allegiance remain **UNKNOWN**.
15. **Jon refuses a direct order to murder the old man.** Styr commands Jon to prove loyalty by cutting the kneeling captive's throat. Jon remembers Ned's execution of a Night's Watch deserter, considers the argument that the Thenns will kill the man anyway, and finally turns away, saying **no**. The choice is witnessed and irreversible from the raiders' perspective.
16. **Ygritte kills the captive herself.** She argues Jon's past killing of sentry Orell was equivalent, then cuts the man's throat with her knife. Her intervention does **not** exonerate Jon to Styr; she also calls him disloyal when pressed. **Do not credit the old man's death to Jon.**
17. **A grey direwolf disrupts the gathering at the exact turning point.** Lightning reveals a large grey wolf mauling Thenns, initially mistaken by Jon for **Ghost**, then recognized as grey, not white. Jon wonders if it could be **Robb's Grey Wind**, which is an on-page **mistaken identification hypothesis**. **Cross-POV: [ASOS 40 Bran](40-bran.md) has Bran entering grey Summer to investigate the same village, armed party, rain and frightened men; Summer is the strongly supported identification, though this Jon POV does not name him.**
18. **The disturbance allows Jon to turn on the raiders.** He slashes at men obstructing his escape, cuts one Thenn's leg, strikes **Bodger** in the face, and seizes the older captive's mare. He escapes into the rainy night; the horse also kicks another Thenn. **Specific casualties beyond observed blows remain uncertain.**
19. **The escape is morally costly.** Jon's flight abandons Ygritte among survivors and involves violence against men he came to know as friends. The sword he could not raise against a helpless civilian is used in fighting armed captors. Neither decision establishes indifference toward Ygritte.
20. **Jon suffers an arrow wound in his right thigh.** He later discovers an arrow lodged through the muscle, forces it through until the head emerges, removes it, washes with stream water and binds the wound himself. The self-treatment may allow travel but leaves infection, blood loss and mobility risks.
21. **The archer is not confirmed.** Jon notices fletching possibly **pale grey**, similar to Ygritte's goose feathers, and wonders whether she shot him or his horse; he cannot confidently determine even the feather color. **Do not convert suspicion into eyewitness proof of Ygritte's action.**
22. **He heads for Castle Black but has not reached it.** By dawn Jon has turned the mare north toward the Wall, thinking he is going home while feeling hollow. He is still wounded and on the road at the chapter endpoint.
23. **Cross-POV timing is unusually tight.** The fire at the inn, Queen's painted merlons, torrential thunder and grey direwolf are **shared independent anchors** tying Bran 40 and Jon 41. Bran does not physically appear before Jon, and Jon has not recognized him; identification requires the reader's comparison of chapters.

## Rival theories and tests

- **Can the Watch defend its southern flank?** Jon's assessment suggests little protection from a small skilled raiding party; test present Watch deployment rather than relying on nominal membership counts.
- **Resettlement versus exclusion:** Ned's and Benjen's abandoned lordships proposal provides a political model for rebuilding the Gift. It does not automatically guarantee peaceful free-folk admission.
- **Was Ygritte defending Jon?** She may kill the man to prevent Jon being exposed or simply because she accepts Styr's rule; the source permits both and does not narrate her private motives.
- **Was the arrow hers?** Fletching and opportunity suggest a possibility, but a grey-feathered shaft is not a named eyewitness.
- **Direwolf magic coordination:** The overlap strongly supports Bran-as-Summer inadvertently rescuing Jon. It does **not** prove Bran knew Jon was there, anticipated the precise intervention, or planned a rescue.
