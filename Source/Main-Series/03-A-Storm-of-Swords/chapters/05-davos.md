## Causal narrative and evidence grades

1. **DAVOS'S POV RESUMES AFTER BLACKWATER:** Davos is alive but marooned on one of the barren rocky pinnacles in **Blackwater Bay**, the dangerous **spears of the merling king** where sailors normally avoid submerged hazards. He has survived an uncertain number of days without a ship or human contact. This chapter resolves the immediate question from [ACOK 58 Davos](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/58-davos.md) of whether he drowned after the raised chain: **he survived that immediate catastrophe**.
2. **MATERIAL COST OF SURVIVAL:** His injuries and exposure include **fever, persistent diarrhea, cough, soaked clothing, one missing boot, cold nights and profound weakness**. Fresh water comes only from occasional rain trapped in shallow rock pools, while food is limited to small crabs caught when the tide is out. There is **no fire, timber dry enough to kindle or useful edible vegetation**. This chapter makes survival dependent on ecology and weather, not a generic miraculous escape.
3. **A STORM SAVED HIM BEFORE THE SHIP:** Davos nearly drank **seawater** in thirst; a passing squall refilled hollows and let him lie with mouth open and drink. Days later the water is running out again. This provides a recent causal mechanism for his having lasted so long; it does **not** prove the storm was supernaturally directed.
4. **SPECIFIC SHIP LOSSES, NOT PROVED FAMILY DEATHS:** Davos recalls **Dale commanding Wraith**, **Allard commanding Lady Marya**, **Maric serving as oarmaster on Fury**, and **Matthos alongside him on Black Betha**. In the burning, he saw Black Betha and Fury aflame; Wraith and Lady Marya disappeared into flame and wreckage. He believes his four eldest sons dead and fears **Devan** may have died as well. **No body, burial, final report or personal confirmation of each named son's death is seen here**.
5. **EXACT ESCAPE THROUGH THE CHAIN TRAP:** With the wildfire-covered surface and chain sealing the bayward route, Davos **deliberately dove beneath the burning ships and chain**, holding his breath and swimming with the current. He passed submerged drowning men weighted by armor, lost his sense of orientation in silty, green water, and finally **inhaled river water in desperation**. He next remembers **waking on the rocky strand**, near a broken mast, burned sail and bloated corpse that the tide later swept away. **How he survived inhaling water and reached the rock is not narrated between blackout and waking**.
6. **THE BATTLEPLAN'S SECOND PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCE:** Davos had worn little armor and was an expert swimmer, which gave him an avenue unavailable to armored soldiers; his survival is exceptional but grounded in identifiable skills and conditions. The **chain trap killed indiscriminately**, while the strong outgoing current and underwater passage could carry an unarmored swimmer into the bay. This is physical causal inference, **not proof of divine selection**.
7. **COMPLICATION OF MARTYRDOM:** His extreme thirst and grief make him consider remaining silent and **letting a distant sail pass**, preferring death to living after the presumed loss of his sons. He remembers his wife and other children only later. His despair is a reported private state, not an irrevocable promise to die.
8. **THE FOUR FINGERBONES ARE GONE:** Reaching for the little **leather pouch containing the bones of the four fingertips Stannis had cut off**, Davos finds it **missing**. He had kept those bones as a reminder of **Stannis's law and reward in the same moment**. The loss is **physical fact**; his belief that his **luck departed with the bones** is subjective symbol-making rather than proof the bones actually carried protective magic.
9. **THE MOTHER'S VOICE AMBIGUITY:** Davos prays to the **Mother** of the Seven and believes for an instant he hears a soft voice accusing him of calling the fire that burned the gods. The narration explicitly allows **wind or surf** as sources, and he is feverish, grieving and weak. Classify a **perceived voice** as directly experienced, but **the Mother's actual intervention remains unproved**.
10. **RELIGIOUS BLAME AND MEMORY:** Davos blames **Melisandre**, recounting her arrival at Dragonstone, the burning of **the Seven's idols** there, the burning of **Storm's End's heart tree**, and his own role **rowing her to Storm's End** where he witnessed the shadow birth ([ACOK 42 Davos](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/42-davos.md)). His conscience then rebukes his own passivity in watching earlier sacrilege and his complicity in shadow warfare. The text supplies **genuine events and retrospective moral interpretation**, not objective proof Melisandre personally caused the wildfire as a magic spell.
11. **THE FIRE AT BLACKWATER WAS ALCHEMICAL:** The other side of the battle is established by [ACOK 58–59](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/58-davos.md): Tyrion's chain and **wildfire hulks** created the mass killing. Davos's judgment that **Melisandre burned his sons** is emotionally intelligible within his religious worldview but **not directly supported as the proximate cause of these green explosions**.
12. **CHOOSING TO LIVE TO ACT:** Davos reverses his decision and **climbs the slippery fifteen-foot rock to signal the ship**. He believes he has **unfinished business** tied to opposing Melisandre, and remembers that his wife and other sons still exist. His climb and shouts are **witnessed action**, not just an imagined religious vow.
13. **VESSEL DISCOVERED:** A passing galley has a **striped hull and bronze figurehead**; Davos cannot read its painted name because **he is illiterate**. A sailor sees him, the galley shortens sail and launches a small boat with **four rowers and a man in the bow** to speak with him. These direct actions establish a genuine rescue approach before he declares allegiance.
14. **A DANGEROUS ALLEGIANCE TEST:** The man in the boat asks **which king Davos served**. Davos briefly fears it might be a Joffrey vessel, but identifies its stripes as belonging to **Salladhor Saan's Lyseni ships**. He openly proclaims **Stannis**; the rescuer confirms **they serve Stannis too**. A boat has come to retrieve him, yet the chapter ends before Davos is shown climbing aboard or meeting Stannis personally.
15. **STANNIS LIVES BY DAVOS'S INFERENCE:** Seeing ships still serving Stannis persuades Davos **his king is alive**; the crew directly confirm their own allegiance, **not Stannis's current whereabouts in person**. This complements Bronn's report of Lyseni evacuation in [ASOS 4 Tyrion](04-tyrion.md), without demonstrating the king physically stands aboard this ship.
16. **ENDING STATE:** Davos has been found by probable allies after exceptional survival. His **four eldest sons are presumed lost but not individually verified dead in this chapter**, Devan's fate remains explicitly doubtful, his pouch is missing, and he has developed a motive to oppose Melisandre even while remaining loyal to Stannis.

## Rival interpretations and future tests

- **Divine intervention vs chance:** Rain, current, body buoyancy, underwater passage and **an unusually routed Lyseni galley** form a contingent but material explanation. Prayer to the Mother and perceived accusation can be read as religious experience without assuming the deity spoke objectively.
- **The missing bones:** The bones were a personal reminder of Stannis's severe justice. Their loss may symbolize a changed Davos–Stannis relationship; nothing demonstrates magical luck. Track future comments by Davos and others on whether he discards, regrets or replaces the relic.
- **The dead sons:** Davos has direct knowledge of exposure, not positive confirmation of all fatalities. Future roll calls, sailors' testimony and family contacts may establish which children survived.
- **Melisandre's guilt:** Davos's claim that she burned his sons should not replace the established **physical pyromancer, hulk and chain** cause. The shadow child and idol burnings are genuine separate issues.
- **Next:** later Davos for Stannis's exact retreat location, Devan, rescue recovery and the practical consequences of his emerging anger toward Melisandre.
