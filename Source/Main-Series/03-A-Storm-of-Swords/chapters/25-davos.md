## Chronological spine / directly witnessed actions

1. **Davos is imprisoned deep below Dragonstone.** The cell is dark, damp and infested with rats, yet its stone walls are unusually warm. Davos has heard that the passages grow warmer farther down and wonders about legends of the castle's hellish stone. **VERIFIED:** warmth he feels. **UNKNOWN:** geological/magical cause.
2. **Maester Pylos treated a serious illness.** Following the Blackwater Davos had a worsening cough, fever, bleeding blisters and shivering. He recalls Pylos tending him, hot garlic broth, milk of the poppy and probable leeching inferred from marks. He subsequently recovers strength. **Pylos's precise intention and authority are not narrated.**
3. **Two gaolers maintain his health and isolation.** Neither will answer a question, though they speak to one another. Davos names them **Porridge** (stocky, kind-seeming, daily oat porridge) and **Lamprey** (older, dressed in a badly fitting star-embroidered doublet, brings richer meals). Their true names, prior offices and why one wears the doublet are unknown.
4. **Davos measures time by food and torches.** There are no windows; he repeatedly asks about Stannis, Devan, Shireen, Salladhor Saan and the weather. His estimate of elapsed time is therefore **approximate**, not a securely dated chronology.
5. **Past burnings make him fear execution.** He remembers Guncer Sunglass and Rambton kin imprisoned and subsequently burned, recognizes that someone has chosen to keep him alive, and fears the same eventual fate. The purpose of this continued care remains unconfirmed at this stage.
6. **Melisandre visits without extinguishing his sole torch.** She threatens to snuff the cell's only flame, exposing his reliance on its light. Davos asks her not to and rejects her claim that his need for fire validates R'hllor. This is an argument using physical dependence, not proof that her religious system follows logically.
7. **Melisandre's shadow-making is given a stated cost.** Davos names what he saw born beneath Storm's End. Melisandre says another shadow made from **Stannis's life-fire** could kill the king because his flame now burns low. She proposes borrowing a vigorous **different man's** life-force by intimacy; Davos refuses. **CHARACTER CLAIM:** mechanism and risk as described by the practitioner, not independently measured.
8. **Her dualistic doctrine is stated explicitly.** She says the real conflict is an ancient war of **R'hllor, fire and life against the Great Other, ice and death**, rather than a contest of Westerosi claimants. She groups many human opposites into two sides. **The doctrine is hers**, not the narrator's endorsement or proof the Others are straightforward agents of that named deity.
9. **Davos's response is doubt, not conversion.** Melisandre places three fingers on his breast as if testing him. He says his heart is full of doubts and refuses to pretend otherwise. She praises his truthfulness and says priestly power can detect deceit. Davos's fear and refusal are directly shown; objective mind-reading is **not**.
10. **The murder plot's reported detection changes his suspicion.** Melisandre says **nobody betrayed Davos** and that she saw his murderous intent in flames. Davos had feared Saan informed on him; he is relieved but disturbed by the implied ability to spy. **Source status:** her explanation is testimony; no independent account rules out every informer.
11. **The Blackwater disagreement persists.** Davos blames her advice for the loss of his sons and fleet. She says the disastrous fires were not hers, claims Stannis refused to let her join due to pride and unbelieving advisers, and argues that his defeat taught him a lesson. **This is interested religious interpretation**, not a witnessed counterfactual battle outcome.
12. **Stannis as Azor Ahai is an assertion with observable premises.** Melisandre identifies the bleeding red star, smoke and salt on Dragonstone, and her visions as proof **Stannis is Azor Ahai reborn**, destined to wake dragons from stone. The existence of the prophecy and her belief is established; the identity and literal fulfillment are **not**.
13. **She says Davos has served and will again serve her god.** Davos privately puzzles over this; he attempts to perceive something through his torch flames, sees only fire, and falls asleep. **No vision is shown to him.** Melisandre leaves the torch burning.
14. **Axell Florent introduces a second prisoner.** After Davos counts roughly three further rounds of visits by gaolers, guards bearing fiery-heart devices drag in **Lord Alester Florent**, who repeatedly demands to see Selyse and calls himself the king's Hand. Axell identifies both men as traitors. The arrest is witnessed; no formal hearing is.
15. **Class contrast in the same cell.** Alester complains of torn clothes and missing valuables lost at Blackwater while retaining his rings. Davos recalls his ship, crew and **four sons** lost, declining to equate silk with lives. Alester says his own son is safe at Brightwater and his nephew **Ser Imry Florent**, who commanded the ill-fated *Fury*, died with the ship.
16. **Davos's battlefield criticism has a specific target.** He remembers Imry driving the fleet into the Blackwater without heeding the little towers guarding the mouth. Alester confirms *Fury* burned and sank with all hands; his account includes Davos's son Maric. Davos treats this as battle news, not firsthand verification of each person's remains.
17. **Alester inventories Stannis's shattered political base.** He reports Celtigar submitted after capture, Monford Velaryon died at sea, Sunglass was burned, and young Lord Bar Emmon is not formidable; Lyseni Saan may abandon Stannis. These are **Alester's status reports and judgments**, not individually rechecked in this scene.
18. **A clandestine peace letter is confessed.** Alester says he wrote terms **to Tywin Lannister via a courier supplied by Salladhor Saan**. Proposed terms: Stannis renounces his crown and retracts Joffrey's alleged bastardy; in return he retains Dragonstone and Storm's End and Alester regains Brightwater lands. He additionally offers marriage of **Shireen to Tommen**. **The existence and contents of the proposal are Alester's admissions; dispatch/delivery/acceptance of the letter are not independently seen.**
19. **Alester believes delegated Hand authority suffices.** He says Stannis entrusted him with his seal and government, so a negotiated end to the war could be authorized in the king's name. Axell's imprisonment of his kin refutes political safety, but this chapter does not show Stannis himself examining the letter or stating his legal interpretation.
20. **Davos distinguishes power to govern from power to renounce.** He agrees the settlement might be materially advantageous but insists Stannis would never retract what he believes true or marry Shireen to a boy Stannis regards as incest-born. A Tommen marriage would likely turn Shireen into **a political hostage**, and potentially deliver claims to Dragonstone/Storm's End to another dynasty; Davos's dynastic logic is an **inference**.
21. **Alester fears the dragon program.** He cites **Aerion Brightfire, unnamed mages, alchemists and Summerhall** as warnings against schemes to wake a stone dragon, and says the king is consumed by Melisandre. These historical references warrant separate source audit; they do not prove what Melisandre can or cannot do here.
22. **End position:** Davos remains a prisoner but is alive and recovering. Alester joins his cell in disgrace. Davos rejects secret peace without Stannis's permission, despite personal losses and an objectively poor military situation. **No meeting with the king, execution or released peace terms occurs in this chapter.**

## Research priorities / countercases

- **Stannis's cost of shadowbinding:** If Melisandre's account is accurate, repeated magic might physically diminish a claimant's ability to rule or fight. Ordinary alternative: she is describing spiritual fatigue or manipulating Davos. Find objective, independently narrated symptoms and subsequent attempts.
- **Who learned Davos's assassination intent?** Melisandre names flame visions; Saan could also have supplied the courier for Alester's letter. These are **separate acts** and neither proves Saan informed on Davos. Test who knew which secret when.
- **The stone dragon:** A literal awakening, a figurative rising of a dragon claimant, and Melisandre's misread prophecy remain competing possibilities. Do not elevate the Red Star/Dragonstone coincidence to full confirmation.
- **Legal authority:** Alester's confidence in a Hand's seal contrasts Davos's insistence on an authorized royal settlement. Test Stannis's actual orders and how messages travel to Tywin rather than assuming this letter bound the king.
- **Values under pressure:** Davos has material reason to prefer peace but refuses a false retraction. This is fidelity to a person's claims, not conversion to Melisandre's cosmic frame.
