## Narrative sequence / durable evidence

1. **The party has left the wolfswood for high mountain valleys.** Bran, Hodor, Meera, Jojen and Summer travel through steep conifer valleys, narrow lakes, stony saddles and colder elevations. Their winding route frequently veers the wrong way; distance walked cannot be equated to northward progress.
2. **The Ice Dragon is practical navigation.** When night is clear, they locate a blue star in the dragon's eye that **points north**, according to Osha's earlier instruction. This is a navigational asterism; it does not prove a celestial dragon or supernatural guide.
3. **Bran imagines a safe home for Rickon.** He pictures Osha, Rickon and Shaggydog at **White Harbor or Last Hearth**, with Manderly or the Umbers. This is **Bran's hope**, not knowledge of their location. The family is separated and its communications broken.
4. **Avoiding roads sacrifices speed and food for concealment.** Meera and Summer struggle to catch enough game in the heights, while Jojen insists travelers on the kingsroad could report a crippled child, giant stableboy and direwolf. This is a reasoned anti-detection policy, not Jojen mechanically obeying a fresh dream.
5. **A philosophical exchange joins ice and fire.** Meera says mountains can be both loved and hated; Jojen remarks that apparently opposed elements might coexist. His words support thematic ambiguity, **not a demonstrated alchemical or prophesied phenomenon**.
6. **Named mountain clans provide a real social map.** Bran describes **Wulls** farther west, **Harclays** behind them, and **Knotts, Liddles, Norreys and certain Flints** in the high country, with Umbers mostly east of the kingsroad but grazing in high meadows. The named geography is useful for later Northern coalition and movement tests.
7. **Bran has maternal Flint ancestry.** He says his father's maternal grandmother was a mountain Flint. Old Nan attributed his former passion for climbing to her blood. This is recalled family information, not evidence the mountain clans know his current location or owe him automatic obedience.
8. **Mountain chiefs do not normally use southern-style titles.** Bran remembers the Wull's **three brown buckets** on blue with white-and-grey checks, and explains that Winterfell addressed him and the Knott, Norrey and Liddle as lords although their own people do not. This detail matters for diplomacy, kinship and house-title assumptions.
9. **Theo Wull/Buckets served the Reed family's account of Robert's war.** Meera asks about a Wull who rode with their father; Jojen names Theo and the nickname Buckets. This is a memory of a specific participant, not a current report of his survival or whereabouts.
10. **The mountain folk detect the travelers.** Bran says he has seen distant watchers **through Summer's eyes**. They do not interfere so long as no goats or horses are stolen. This is limited local surveillance and tolerated transit, not secrecy from all potential informers.
11. **A stranger offers shelter in a cave.** During freezing rain, Summer locates a covered cave where an older man invites them to share heat and provides oatcakes, blood sausage and ale. He neither gives his name nor requests theirs; Bran infers from a pinecone clasp and clothing that he is a **Liddle**. The identity remains an inference.
12. **The stranger contrasts Stark rule with the new disorder.** He says travelers once found food and lodging and women could safely use the kingsroad under Winterfell's protection; now doors are shut and violence is widespread. This is his own view of a remembered social order, not proof no harm ever occurred under Stark rule.
13. **He reports Bolton-aligned searchers.** According to the stranger, **flayed-men** ride the kingsroad asking about strangers, and **the Bastard**, once thought dead, is now alive and paying for **wolfskins** or news of certain surviving 'dead' people. He looks meaningfully at Bran and Summer. This is highly consequential **local testimony with possible knowledge of Bran's concealed identity**, not independent verification of an actual reward notice.
14. **The Wall's intelligence problem has reached the mountains.** The man says the Old Bear's ranging returned **ravens with hardly any accompanying messages** and warns travel toward the Wall is dangerous. Compare [ASOS 18 Samwell](18-samwell.md): Sam sent **two initial warning letters**, but later released birds unburdened by specific battle reports. Do not conclude every raven flew without a letter.
15. **Jojen makes a forecast, the stranger demands evidence.** Jojen says wolves will return to Winterfell because he dreamed it. The stranger responds with a dream of his dead mother who does not return. This is an explicit skeptical counterexample built into the scene, not a verified prophecy.
16. **The stranger quietly supports them.** After staying overnight, Bran finds the fire out and his host gone but **a sausage and twelve oatcakes**, some with pinenuts, some with blackberries, neatly wrapped for them. Bran vows to repay the kindness once Starks return to Winterfell. The food is real; the future restoration is his aspiration.
17. **An eagle sparks a failed skinchanging attempt.** Bran observes a **grey-winged eagle** soaring and tries to enter it as he does Summer, but cannot before it disappears. This proves an attempt, **not** successful possession, nor that the bird is identical to the Orell-linked eagle in [ASOS 15 Jon](15-jon.md).
18. **Hodor's original name is stated.** Bran says **Walder** is his true name according to Old Nan, and Hodor is a recurrent utterance. His account of the precise family relationship to Old Nan is vague; no cause of the word 'Hodor' is identified here.
19. **Old Nan's fate is unknown to Bran.** He recalls not seeing her body among Winterfell's dead and asks whether Theon would have hurt her. Meera notes the killings included dead ironborn and therefore were not simply Theon's acts. She counsels preserving Nan through remembered stories. This does not independently determine her survival.
20. **Stories begin with the Neck's military geography.** Jojen and Meera describe armored would-be invaders swallowed by bogs and unable to find **Greywater Watch**. This is Reed family lore with plausible logistical significance; do not infer a literal castle capable of supernatural teleportation.
21. **Meera reports the story of a young crannogman.** He supposedly traveled by skin boat down the Green Fork, passed the **Twins at night** to avoid hostile Freys, carried his boat overland toward the Gods Eye and visited the mysterious green men on the **Isle of Faces**. Meera deliberately refuses to narrate what he experienced there; **his precise training and revelations are UNKNOWN**.
22. **The crannogman reaches the great Harrenhal tourney.** A dragon king and his princely son are present, along with Storm, Reach and other lords; the great lord of the Rock remains absent after a quarrel with the king. A Kingsguard induction forms part of the event. The names and chronology can be compared with Jaime's court memories but are conveyed as **Meera's historical story**, not directly observed by Bran.
23. **The wolf maiden defends him from three squires.** Three older/bigger boys abuse the crannogman as a 'frogeater'; a fiery **she-wolf** intervenes with a practice sword and introduces him to her **wild, quiet and youngest wolf brothers**. This strongly suggests the Stark siblings, but the tale does not explicitly name the girl as Lyanna on page.
24. **Named-symbolic guests form a disputed historical network.** Meera mentions the dragon prince singing, a black brother seeking recruits, a storm lord drinking, a lady with laughing purple eyes dancing with a white sword, a red snake, a griffin lord and eventually the quiet wolf. **Potential identifications (Ashara Dayne, Oberyn, Jon Connington, Ned, etc.) are hypotheses until checked against other source testimony**.
25. **The crannogman recognizes his attackers' lords.** One squire serves a **pitchfork knight**, another a **porcupine**, and the last a **two-towers knight** strongly identified by Bran as Frey. The boy prays to the old gods near the lake and receives the chance for redress.
26. **The mystery knight's shield depicts a laughing weirwood.** A short rider in mismatched armor appears under a painted white heart tree with a laughing red face. The **Knight of the Laughing Tree** defeats the three guilty squires' knightly masters and demands they teach their squires honor, instead of taking the customary ransom. Crowd approval is part of Meera's story.
27. **The rider's identity is NOT disclosed.** Bran assumes the small crannogman was under the armor and imagines magic assistance, but **Meera never confirms that identity**. Viable hypotheses include the crannogman himself and the wolf maiden, with different consequences for the required jousting skill, available horse/armor, and ability to remain hidden.
28. **Royal suspicion changes the stakes.** The king denounces the unknown champion as an enemy, and the dragon prince is sent to find the vanished knight. Only the **painted shield** is found in a tree. This establishes how the mystery might have brought the royal heir into contact with the northern party, **not** exactly whom he met or why.
29. **The dragon prince wins and a sadder crowning is hinted.** Meera says the dragon prince ultimately wins the tournament and confirms Bran's preferred wish that the wolf maiden be crowned queen of love and beauty **did occur**, adding that the aftermath is a sadder story. Do not silently insert unshown private actions at this chapter's endpoint.
30. **Jojen is surprised Ned never told Bran the story.** He asks twice whether Bran heard it from his father; Bran says no. This is unusual **family memory suppression or omission**, but ordinary possibilities include Ned's grief, disinterest in storytelling or reluctance to discuss complex events. No deliberate long-term conspiracy is proven.
31. **Bran's reception is revealing.** He would prefer the three bullies' knights killed, and imagines the knight marrying a princess or bestowing love-and-beauty honors. His heroic-story expectations are being challenged by a tale about **discipline, honor, identities and the lasting cost of royal attention**.
32. **End position.** The party remains concealed on a difficult northern route, encouraged by a likely Liddle, with a warning of flayed-man searches and uncertain news from the Wall. Bran has **not** skinchanged an eagle and has not reached the Isle of Faces, the Wall or the three-eyed crow.

## Competing interpretations and disconfirmation

- **Knight of the Laughing Tree = Lyanna?** A strong candidate because she attacks the bullies, can ride, has the emotional stake and family support; but the chapter **never explicitly confirms the rider**. Competing candidates include the crannogman or another ally. Test equestrian training, available armor, all source claims about the Harrenhal tourney, and who could have moved unseen.
- **Why tell Bran?** Meera offers a tradition from her father, possibly revealing known past ties to House Stark and the Isle of Faces. Jojen's surprise may imply an important omission in Ned's family account, but the actual reason remains unknown.
- **Mountain clan agency:** The likely Liddle knows enough to suspect who Bran is yet provides safe food without announcing him. This supports a feasible hidden Stark-loyal network; it does not prove all mountain chiefs will follow any Stark claimant.
- **Eagle:** Cross-link the eagle with Jon's Orell encounter as a **test only**. The bird's color/flight alone cannot establish identity, geography, simultaneity or controller.
- **Wall warnings:** The stranger's 'few messages' fits Sam's unsent later drafts; ensure later Watch receipt scenes are audited before claiming complete silence or full foreknowledge.
