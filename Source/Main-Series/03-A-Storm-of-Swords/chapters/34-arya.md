## Sequential narrative, testimony and agency

1. **The Brotherhood's center is underground, not merely nomadic.** Arya and Gendry arrive blindfolded at a large **hollow hill** with a central fire, branching dark tunnels, and **pale weirwood roots** forming actual stairs and niches. Women and children shelter there. The tunnels' full length is unknown; physical concealment is an ordinary explanation without teleportation or proven underground city.
2. **The Mad Huntsman was forced to surrender his captive.** At Stoney Sept, Brotherhood fighters and his dog pack nearly clash; Tom's music, Tansy's scraps for the hounds, and **Anguy's visible drawn bow** help persuade him to deliver the prisoner for Beric's judgment. This is concrete power negotiation rather than universal Brotherhood obedience.
3. **The prisoner is Sandor Clegane, not Jaime.** He was reportedly found **asleep drunk beneath a willow**, tracked by dogs; he recognizes **Thoros of Myr** and mocks the former tourney priest. The capture circumstances come from the Huntsman, but Sandor's appearance and presence are direct.
4. **Thoros has changed materially and spiritually.** Arya remembers a shaved, fat red priest from King's Landing; the man is now thin and grey-haired. He admits once having a vain heart and describes his newfound belief in fire visions and awakening powers. **Character testimony** and observable transformation should not be confused with demonstrated prophecy accuracy.
5. **The hidden Beric has wounds incompatible with ordinary survival.** The aged-looking scarecrow knight descending from weirwood roots bears a **missing eye**, a smashed area of skull, a **dark mark round his throat**, and later a through-and-through **lance scar** in his torso. The physical scars directly confirm severe trauma; the exact order/number of earlier deaths is **not** directly re-enacted here.
6. **Beric asserts a larger purpose than Stark allegiance.** He says **six score** men originally marched under Ned's authority, and **over eighty** of the originals have died, replaced by peasants and other volunteers. Even though Robert is dead, they claim to defend his **realm**, not an individual Stark claimant or Lannister enemy indiscriminately. Claims of universal 'king's justice' face later counterevidence.
7. **The company crosses class boundaries.** Knights, former swineherds, masons, tanners and singers are made fellows; Beric claims to have knighted each person with a sword. This is **a stated internal rite and ethos**; it is not proof every member is a virtuous knight.
8. **Sandor challenges collective guilt.** Brotherhood witnesses accuse Clegane's house and Lannister army of numerous atrocities; Sandor replies that he did not personally commit those acts and that guilt cannot be inherited from Gregor or every allied soldier. This is a coherent **counterargument**, though it does not excuse his own confirmed acts.
9. **Historical murdered children surface.** Thoros recalls seeing the bodies of **Prince Aegon and Princess Rhaenys** presented after King's Landing fell. His charge is against Clegane family/crimes; **whether Sandor personally participated** is not shown. The distinction between House Clegane and Sandor matters.
10. **The dead are named individually.** The Brotherhood recites murdered smallfolk and lesser knights, including **Lothar Mallery, Gladden Wylde, the Darrys, Little Matt, Randa, numerous Pates and millers**. Beric insists the realm consists of such people, not just castles, trees and banners. Their attributions to specific perpetrators are not all independently proved, but the narrative preserves victims often omitted from noble histories.
11. **Arya provides a direct relevant accusation.** She charges Sandor with killing **Mycah**. Sandor initially cites Joffrey's story that the butcher's boy attacked a prince; Arya counters that she struck Joffrey and Mycah fled. Sandor admits his justification was derived from **royal lips**, not a personal sighting of Mycah attacking.
12. **Sansa's earlier story has consequences.** Sandor notes Arya's sister reinforced the prince's version before Robert; Arya feels renewed anger at Sansa. This is not fresh proof that Sansa witnessed the supposed attack; it shows how a falsely framed royal account travels into official punishment.
13. **Beric declines to decide by accusation alone.** He says the truth of the specific Mycah charge is not known to him and orders **trial by combat**, claiming the Lord of Light will judge. **His interpretation of the test as divine evidence is doctrinal**; the physical battle's victor is verifiable independently of guilt.
14. **Beric chooses himself as champion.** Sandor is untied and given his sword and shield, while Beric voluntarily removes his damaged iron breastplate. Sandor's lack of metal armor is not a one-sided handicap; both fight without Beric's plate. **Anguy, Dennet and Kyle** watch with arrows ready if Sandor tries to escape or seize a hostage.
15. **Beric ignites his own sword by blood.** After Thoros leads a responsive prayer, Beric deliberately draws his blade across his palm; **the sword bursts into flame**. Gendry distinguishes this from **Thoros's ordinary wildfire-dipped swords** explained in [ASOS 22 Arya](22-arya.md). The on-page ignition is extraordinary; exact mechanism and required magic are not independently established.
16. **The flaming duel presses Sandor toward a firepit.** Beric's flame and fast attacks exploit Sandor's exceptional fear of fire; Sandor still blocks, counters and shows powerful swordsmanship. A burning shield and sleeve force the Hound onto the defensive. This is meaningful tactical use of trauma, not proof Sandor is a coward in every combat.
17. **Sandor breaks Beric's sword and delivers an apparently mortal blow.** After extinguishing his burning shield, he strikes with such force that Beric's blade snaps, and **cuts deeply from the shoulder/neck into the chest**. Beric collapses, bleeding, and is removed by Thoros, Lem, Jack-Be-Lucky and a young squire **Ned**. These wounds and collapse are directly witnessed.
18. **Sandor is declared innocent by divine combat logic.** Harwin interprets Beric's defeat as R'hllor's acquittal; the Huntsman still favors a crow cage and Arya wants revenge. **Victory is not proof Sandor never killed Mycah**. Competing standards—fact-finding, trial-by-battle belief and vigilante vengeance—are left visibly irreconcilable.
19. **Sandor's burn injuries are severe.** His arm has open blistered/charred skin, and he cries and begs for help. Thoros orders care from a woman named **Melly**, distinct from allowing Arya to punish him. His suffering becomes visible without erasing culpability.
20. **Sandor explicitly admits killing Mycah.** Confronted by Arya after the duel, he says he ran Mycah down and killed him, adding that he laughed. **His direct admission supersedes any inferred acquittal of factual guilt by winning the duel**, though the details of his emotions may be deliberately cruel to Arya.
21. **Arya tries to kill him but is stopped.** She takes **Greenbeard's dagger** and approaches wounded Sandor, but **Lem disarms her**. Her desire for vengeance is active; she does not successfully kill Sandor.
22. **Beric reappears apparently alive.** After being carried away with a mortal-looking wound, the lightning lord **stands behind Arya**, hand bloody and gripping Thoros's shoulder. This is a directly depicted extraordinary return, supporting supernatural revival more strongly than secondhand survival rumors from [ASOS 22 Arya](22-arya.md).
23. **The precise revival method is still unseen.** This chapter **does not show every instant of what Thoros did in the tunnel**, nor explain metaphysical cost, how much memory Beric retains, or the limit on repetitions. A ordinary explanation of mild injury is strained by the observed blow and earlier scars, but mechanism should be corroborated later.
24. **End state:** Sandor is severely burned but alive; Arya has heard his confession yet is prevented from killing him; Beric is standing and able to speak after apparently fatal injury. No transfer of Sandor to a court, completion of Arya's Riverrun journey or concluded ransom is shown.

## Rival interpretations / tests

- **Is divine trial just?** Arya's direct accusations, Sandor's final admission and the winner's apparent acquittal show **combat result does not determine historical innocence**. Trial by combat can be considered a local rule and an unreliable epistemic test at once.
- **Does R'hllor bring Beric back?** The temporal link to Thoros and otherwise mortal injuries strongly supports the priest's claimed powers, but the book has not yet explained the specific rite and constraints in this scene.
- **Can a 'broken man' become a moral knight?** Sandor correctly challenges inherited guilt while owning a real murder, and Beric invokes noble ideals while operating coercively. Avoid flattening either into hero/villain purity.
- **Hollow hills and weirwood roots:** Tangible root architecture does not yet prove connection to the Isle of Faces or Nightfort passages; test specific location and function.
- **Arya's moral agency:** She is furious, attempts a killing and also sees the victim's suffering. The chapter does **not** establish she forgives Sandor.
