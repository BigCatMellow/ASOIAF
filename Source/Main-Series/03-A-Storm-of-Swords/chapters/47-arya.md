# ASOS 47 — Arya: the drowned Harroway ferry, Sandor's ransom plan, and the road to the Twins

## Sequential causal record

1. **Arya is Sandor's coerced captive.** After the abduction in [ASOS 43 Arya](43-arya.md), she rides ahead of Sandor on **Stranger**, his dangerous black courser; his burned left arm is still raw and partly covered by a protective vambrace. Arya is sick with fever, rain-soaked and exhausted, not a willing traveling companion.
2. **Sandor threatens binding, beating and gagging if she escapes.** On the first day, Arya tried creeping near him with a **jagged stone** to kill him as he slept; he caught her and confiscated it. She also attempted stealing Stranger; the horse nearly bit her face. Sandor subsequently **rolled her in a blanket and tied her top and bottom at night**. These are actual restrictions and threats, not merely rough speech.
3. **He becomes violent at Mycah's name.** Arya accuses him of murdering her friend; he threatens to beat her severely if she repeats the name. This corroborates the fact that winning the trial in [ASOS 34 Arya](34-arya.md) did not resolve his guilt, or make him safe for Arya.
4. **River geography is concealed from Arya.** She fears Sandor is taking her to **King's Landing**, assumes the swollen river must be **the Blackwater Rush**, and cannot use sun direction because of persistent storm clouds. Sandor knows the actual geography but refuses explanation until the end. Her perspective remains constrained by imprisonment.
5. **Arya attempts a trace for rescuers.** She scratches her name on tree trunks while separated to urinate, until Sandor catches her on the fourth attempt. She believes Thoros might find her through fire-sight, but no such pursuit is demonstrated.
6. **Flooding makes ordinary fords unusable.** Sandor follows the river toward **Lord Harroway's Town**, where Lord Roote's service operates a boat nicknamed **Old King Andahar's two-headed water horse**. The 'horse' is **a broad flat-bottomed ferry with carved horseheads fore and aft**, not a magical animal.
7. **The town has been inundated.** Only higher stories, chimneys, parts of a sept and roundtower emerge above brown floodwater. The boat is chained at a window, and a **crossbowman** watches from the tower. No claim that the floodwater is wildfire or prophecy is justified by imagery alone.
8. **The ferryboat requires many workers.** It has roughly a dozen oarlocks, a central sod-roofed shelter, a rudder and strong poles. Men from the boat and tower assemble to pilot it, many apparently kin. The broad crossing is genuinely dangerous: river debris, wind, submerged buildings and a violent current all threaten passengers.
9. **Sandor agrees to pay three gold dragons only after reaching the opposite shore.** The ferryman demands **one coin for the man, one for the horse and one for the assumed boy**. Sandor uses the threat of his sword to change payment timing and invokes **'knight's honor'** despite repeatedly rejecting knightly ideals.
10. **The ferryman mistakes Arya for Sandor's son.** Arya loudly protests, potentially jeopardizing their disguise, and Sandor shakes her hard and orders her indoors. This shows her physical vulnerability and how his coercive authority overrides her identity.
11. **Arya considers jumping into the river.** She believes she may be safer drowning than being returned to Joffrey and initially approaches the railing. An enormous uprooted tree approaches the ferry before she jumps; the incident supplies a concrete reason to abandon the attempted swim. **No rescue from the water is needed because she does not jump.**
12. **The debris nearly destroys the ferry.** Two boatmen fend off the trunk with poles; one **pole breaks**, the other deflects it. A later glancing impact shakes the boat, and **one boatman falls overboard and disappears**. Death is **strongly presumed** given the current but no body is recovered.
13. **The ferry lands about two miles below its normal crossing.** The force of the flood and need to row against it explain the difficult arrival, which is not sabotage by the crew.
14. **The ferryman raises the fare to six dragons.** He demands three originally agreed plus three for the presumed drowned worker. Sandor hands him **a written Brotherhood debt note** from Beric rather than actual coins, calling it a claim on about **9,000 dragons** and allocating **ten** of them to the boatmen. **No actual gold changes hands.**
15. **Sandor openly admits violating 'knight's honor'.** When accused of breaking his gold promise, he derides the idea that knights possess honor. His statement has strategic effect: the boatmen may refuse later travelers presenting similar papers and the flooded crossing may delay Beric's pursuit. **This is an intentional substitution of a promissory instrument for cash, whose legal value and collectability are unverified.**
16. **Sandor acknowledges fear of fires.** After crossing, he tries to build a campfire for feverish Arya, fails because the wood is wet, then angrily declares he hates fire. A moment of attempted practical care coexists with sustained captivity and threats.
17. **His account of Gregor is unusually intimate and brutal.** Sandor claims Gregor once killed his own man for snoring; he expresses a desire to kill Gregor himself. These are **Sandor's reports**, not independently corroborated murders in this chapter, though previous POVs establish Gregor's broader violence.
18. **Arya names specific past tormentors.** When she says Gregor's men captured her, **Gendry, Hot Pie and Lommy** at the lakeside village, and that **Raff the Sweetling killed injured Lommy**, Sandor realizes Gregor unknowingly held a valuable Stark hostage. This illustrates the effect of concealed identity on previous violence.
19. **Sandor claims to have saved Sansa from a mob.** He reminds Arya of saving her sister in King's Landing and of Sansa singing to him. The mob rescue is corroborated in Sansa's earlier POV; his details of her singing are a **character report** to Arya, who initially assumes he lies.
20. **The destination is revealed to be the Twins.** Sandor tells Arya the river was **the Trident**, not the Blackwater, and that he plans to hand her to **Catelyn** at the wedding and demand payment. This is the actual purpose **he declares**; he has **not yet arrived**, negotiated, or safely handed her over.
21. **He is strategically disaffected from Lannisters.** Sandor says he rejects Joffrey, Cersei and Tyrion as masters and hopes **Robb** will either make him a lord and employ him, or pay a high ransom; he imagines offering to kill Gregor. **These are his stated prospects**, not proof Robb would ever accept.
22. **Arya remains distrustful despite possible family reunion.** She states Robb would reject Sandor, and still repeats Sandor on her private death list. She cannot confirm the safety of the Twins or her mother; his ransom route is not a guarantee of rescue.
23. **End state:** Arya and Sandor ride north from Lord Harroway's ferry toward the **Twins**. The flooded river and death/disappearance of a ferryman underscore journey hazards; the promised gold is unpaid; Sandor has shown limited concern for Arya's health without relinquishing control over her.

## Research tests / rival explanations

- **Does Sandor intend to betray Arya to Lannisters?** He explicitly says he wants Catelyn's money and opposes his old employers; coercive bargaining gives him incentive to keep her alive. Nonetheless he threatens and restrains her; claimed destination is not proof of safe delivery.
- **Is the written note payment?** A recognized debt instrument is not cash; his use likely ensures the crew has no immediate means of compensation. Future redemption and balance of stolen gold are unverified.
- **Can Arya's captors be rescuers?** Sandor arguably aims to take her to her mother, but her lack of consent and his material interest separate the possible benefit from benevolent intent.
- **Flooding and travel:** Weather demonstrably changes wedding travel and interception routes for multiple POV groups without any need to treat every downpour as magical intervention.
- **Reconciliation with Sansa:** His accurate mob-rescue claim does not erase the killing of Mycah or establish Arya's willingness to forgive.
