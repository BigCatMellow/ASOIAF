## Sequential events, causal choices and evidence grades

1. **FULL DIREWOLF POV, NOT SIMPLE DREAM IMAGERY:** Bran is consciously within **Summer**, running across a wooded ridge of the wolfswood with acute smell and hearing and powerful limbs. He thinks of himself as **prince of the green**, enjoying the freedom denied to his paralyzed body. His wolf senses extend to the quiet sound of falling pinecones and distant prey, but the text directly alternates to Bran being awakened in human form, grounding this as **his lived skinchanging state**, not merely a symbolic dream.
2. **SCENT MEMORY OF THE ORIGINAL STARK WOLVES:** Summer recalls the direwolf litter by scent rather than human names: a **five-member group and a sixth standing aside**, an angry brother with green eyes (**Shaggydog**, closest but moving farther), others scattered, one **sister lost**, and the **white silent wolf** separately remembered. These are subjective **animal-memory clues about the surviving pack**. Summer's sense of kin from a distance is not identical to hearing their literal howls or locating each wolf.
3. **THE LOST SISTER AND HALLS OF MEN:** Summer perceives a sister as having gone into a **man-made stone place ruled by other hunters** and not finding her way out. The wording naturally evokes **Lady's death at Ned's order in `AGOT`**, but the location and sensation are filtered through an animal mind and should not become proof of a surviving imprisoned sister. This is a strong **death/separation image**, not a rescinded death.
4. **A REAL PACK AND A REAL KILL:** Summer smells a wounded deer and approaches **eight smaller ordinary wolves** with a clear dominance hierarchy: a head male, female, several followers and a low-ranking **tail** that waits to eat last. This supplies a concrete image of rank even among supposedly free animals and parallels human military hierarchy; its immediate function is **Bran's absorption in predatory dominance**.
5. **SUMMER ASSERTS ALPHA DOMINANCE:** He attacks and scatters rivals, wounding one leg, **tearing out another wolf's throat**, and fighting the older head male until that male rolls over and shows **throat and belly in submission**. The female and other pack members intervene repeatedly before the outcome. The victorious direwolf then **licks the defeated leader's wounds** and claims the deer. These are actual physical acts **narrated through Summer**, not uncorroborated hunter rumor; the precise number of injuries beyond those shown should not be invented.
6. **INTERRUPTED BY HODOR:** Hodor's call reaches Bran as an odd **sound only he perceives within the wolf**, before the image of Hodor's face and a damp stone vault replaces the woods. **Hodor gently but very forcefully shakes Bran awake**, and Bran angrily protests because he wanted to finish eating as Summer. The switch documents a **recoverable two-body perception** but significant preference and difficulty leaving the animal.
7. **PRESENT LOCATION:** Bran, **Hodor, Jojen and Meera Reed** are sheltering beneath the ruins of an old watchtower Bran calls **Tumbledown Tower**, overgrown with moss and ivy, in the wolfswood. This is a **temporary hidden refuge**, not Greywater Watch, the Wall or the promised teacher's location. Summer wanders independently outside while Bran can enter him.
8. **JOJEN'S REPEATABLE TESTS:** Jojen has instructed Bran to perform **small deliberate tasks while inside Summer**, including marking tree bark with claws, catching and delivering prey uneaten, or moving stones in an arranged line. Bran **repeatedly forgets**, because the living wolf's desire to run, hunt and feed overwhelms commands planned by the human boy. Jojen's instructions are practical **behavioral probes of agency and memory retention**; no task is successfully performed here.
9. **TWO AND ONE IDENTITY:** Jojen asks Bran to recite that he is **Brandon Stark, prince of Winterfell**, and to identify Summer as his direwolf. Bran says they are **two and one**. Jojen warns that the boy can be **consumed by the wolf identity** if he neglects the distinction. This is a knowledgeable mentor's explicit warning, backed by Bran's visible memory failures, but **the chapter does not show Bran permanently trapped**.
10. **HUMAN NOURISHMENT DOESN'T TRANSFER:** Jojen insists Bran must eat with **his human mouth**; wolf-fed meat provides **no nourishment to Bran's human body**. This independently reiterates [ACOK 69 Bran](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/69-bran.md)'s concrete three-day food-and-water danger, placing a hard **biological limit on skinchanging as bodily substitution**.
11. **MEERA'S FORAGING AND EQUIPMENT:** Meera returns with **two trout and six frogs**, collected using her **three-pronged frog spear and net**. Hodor brings wood, Jojen cooks, and they prepare a **fish-and-frog stew in Meera's helm**, with wild onions. These mundane survival details show how the fugitives can temporarily hide without outside support, and how their food depends especially on Meera's skills.
12. **MATERIAL AND GEOGRAPHIC SAFETY DEBATE:** **Meera** argues their ruin is hidden, fish and game plentiful and pursuit absent, so staying avoids exposure. **Jojen**, without having a new green dream, says it is not the right place; the civil war could bring a force unexpectedly, and **alive Bran would become prey** for people who think the Stark boys dead. These are **competing strategic judgments**, not an already verified pursuer approaching the tower.
13. **NORTHWARD QUEST HARD TO EXECUTE:** Jojen insists the destination is **the three-eyed crow, somewhere north beyond the Wall**, but has **no known precise location or direct travel instructions**, saying the crow may find them. Meera challenges the feasibility of taking **a disabled nine-year-old with Hodor afoot** through the far North with no horses and little money. Jojen's spiritual certainty is **not the same as a validated itinerary**.
14. **ALTERNATIVE ALLIED REFUGES:** Bran proposes stealing or acquiring horses and going to **Last Hearth/House Umber**, **Karhold/House Karstark**, or **White Harbor/House Manderly** (possibly using the White Knife), imagining reaching Robb. He remembers Manderly's shipbuilding ambitions and kindness, and **Hother Whoresbane and Mors Crowfood** among Umber kin. The plans are **ideas and political trust judgments** only: Bran has no confirmed news that these houses are intact or will accept him safely.
15. **OTHER WOLF CALLS AND UNCERTAINTY:** A howl sounds nearby; Jojen asks if it is Summer, but Bran asserts it is **an ordinary wolf** and that Summer is far away. This shows Bran claims increasing ability to distinguish familiar direwolf voice and location, though **no separate narrator crosschecks the animal making this single call**.
16. **CRYPT SWORDS REMAIN IN USE:** The group retains three **funerary swords stolen from Winterfell's crypts** ([ACOK 69 Bran](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/69-bran.md)); Bran claims his uncle Brandon's, Meera Rickard's, and **Hodor now carries a very old, large rusty sword**. The present text does not name Hodor's sword's original owner; **do not confuse it with Osha's earlier sword when parties separated**. Hodor uses the blunt weapon to cut a dead tree near the ruins, generating noise.
17. **GREENSEERS DEFINED, BUT BY A SECONDARY SOURCE:** Jojen denies being a greenseer: **he has green dreams**, while he says the great greenseers could **wear skins of creatures on land, air and water and see through the eyes of weirwood trees**. He attributes the lore to what crannogmen remember from the **First Men and children of the forest**, conceding much was lost. This is explicit knowledgeable **character testimony about abilities**, **not a directly shown Bran tree-vision or universal proven power of all greenseers**.
18. **THE WINGED WOLF AND THE OLD DREAM:** Jojen recalls his green dream of **a winged wolf chained to earth by stone**, interpreted as Bran's imprisoned potential. He says the **chains are off, but Bran does not yet fly**, clarifying the initial message as **ability and training imagery**, not a requirement Bran literally grow wings. The connection to a **three-eyed crow tutor** becomes the motive to risk a long journey.
19. **WHO MAKES THE FINAL DECISION:** Meera insists the risk and gift **belong to Bran**, since he is their prince and liege's son. She reminds him of the Reeds' oath by **earth and water, bronze and iron, ice and fire**, and offers to obey his choice. Bran considers hiding, allied castles and northward travel, explicitly weighs the chance of capture, and chooses **to seek the three-eyed crow** because safe hiding does not teach him to fly or change his inability to walk.
20. **THE BOOK'S MORAL AND SUPERNATURAL CROSSROAD:** His desire for instruction is partly the wish to **escape paralysis**, while Jojen warns too deep a connection with Summer could instead **erase Bran's human self**. The chapter makes this more than a standard power-up: there are **risks to memory, embodied survival and autonomy** and a strategic cost to revealing a living Stark heir.

## Active theories and tests

- **The warg's identity:** Summer's preference for animal dominance and Bran's repeated inability to execute prepared tasks make the **loss-of-self danger directly testable**. Follow subsequent episodes for remembered human instructions, animal resistance, and return to the human body.
- **Pack connection:** Summer's distant sense of littermates, especially a farther Shaggydog, invites the theory of **nonlocal wolf awareness**, but no precise mental message between separated wolves is demonstrated here.
- **Greenseer versus green dreamer:** Jojen makes a categorical distinction, explicitly defining **weirwood seeing** as a greater skill. Later chapters with actual tree perception should be prioritized; do not equate his own prophetic dreams with Bran's full potential.
- **Why beyond the Wall?:** Jojen has no direct map to the teacher and the journey endangers both Stark heir and Reeds. The three-eyed crow's actual identity, motive and degree of manipulation remain unanswered.
- **Potential Stark sanctuary:** Umber, Karstark and Manderly holdings are options based on old loyalty, not certified future safety. Compare Northern politics and risks of political detention as the war shifts.
- **Next:** later Bran for cross-body training and the route north; parallel Jon chapters for the Wall and the same ancient forces.
