## Narrative, sources of information and decisions

1. **Hoster Tully's death and river funeral are witnessed.** His body lies in a weighted narrow boat, in armor, under Tully colors with **helm, mock wooden sword, shield and hunting horn**, launched along the **Tumblestone into the Red Fork**. Seven people guide the boat in a Faith-of-Seven ceremony. Catelyn imagines her father resting in the river rather than Winterfell's crypts; this is Tully belief/imagery, not evidence of an actual afterlife.
2. **Frey representation at the funeral is politically calculated.** **Lame Lothar Frey**, Lord Walder's longtime steward, helps launch the boat beside **Robb, Bracken, Blackwood, Vance, Mallister and Marq Piper**. Lothar arrived with **forty armed men led by Walder Rivers**, eldest bastard of Walder Frey. Edmure views the crippled steward and bastard envoy as an intentional insult; Catelyn suspects Walder's spite yet Robb treats them courteously to preserve the strategic bridge alliance.
3. **Edmure cannot light the boat.** He misses **three flaming-arrow shots**; **Brynden Blackfish** fires the fourth, setting the boat alight in the distance. Edmure is grieving, ashamed and apparently weakened by drinking and exhaustion. This is not proof he lacks archery skill generally; Brynden observes that Hoster once missed his first shot at his own father's funeral.
4. **Catelyn suppresses Hoster's final word.** When the grief-stricken Edmure asks whether their father called for him, she lies that he did. Hoster's actual last remembered word was **Tansy**. This preserves an important unresolved name and evidence of a compassionate but deliberate family deception.
5. **Catelyn remains cut off from Lysa and the girls.** No reply has come from the Eyrie, and no raven confirms Jaime's arrival or completion of the hostage exchange. She speculates messages could be intercepted. The reader knows from [ASOS 31 Jaime](31-jaime.md) that Cleos is dead and Jaime/Brienne were taken to Harrenhal; Catelyn **does not** know.
6. **Robb's new companions partly fill family losses.** Catelyn observes that **Rollam Westerling**, squire, and **Raynald Westerling**, standard-bearer, have become close to Robb in roles reminiscent of lost Bran, Theon and Jon. The comparison is her interpretation of grief and companionship, not a claim that either Westerling is literally replacing a brother.
7. **Lothar carefully stages reconciliation.** At Hoster's funeral he offers Robb condolences and a deferential story in which old Walder understands youthful love; Catelyn doubts Walder actually spoke as generously as Lothar claims. Lothar asks for later audience. A pleasant intermediary can carry coercive terms without personally showing hostility.
8. **The Duskendale setback changes Robb's strategic options.** News says **Randyll Tarly defeated Robett Glover and Ser Helman Tallhart** at Duskendale; a third of Robb's foot is lost, and **Robett Glover** is later captured near the kingsroad. Robb is surprised his forces were operating at Duskendale and does not explain why the detachment went there. **This is strong evidence of a command/intelligence problem, not yet attribution of a deliberate Bolton betrayal.**
9. **Robb plans a separate captive exchange.** He offers **Martyn Lannister** in hopes of recovering Robett Glover; Willem's killing has made Martyn's safety crucial, so Robb tripled his guard. The exchange is **proposed**, not carried out.
10. **Robb acknowledges mistakes and incomplete resources.** He says he has won his battles yet is losing the war; believes Winterfell and Moat Cailin held by ironborn, his brothers slain and Arya perhaps dead. He regrets not trading Jaime earlier for Sansa and imagines a possible Sansa–Loras–Tyrell alliance. The imagined alliance is hindsight **counterfactual**, not a documented offer.
11. **Robb tells Catelyn Sansa has married Tyrion.** A raven reached him the previous night; he delayed the revelation until after Hoster's funeral. The reported ceremony is directly corroborated by [ASOS 28 Sansa](28-sansa.md). **Neither Robb nor Catelyn knows the marriage remains unconsummated**, a key information asymmetry with [ASOS 32 Tyrion](32-tyrion.md).
12. **Robb sees an immediate succession threat.** Believing Bran and Rickon dead, he calls **Sansa his heir** should he die and says the Lannisters married her for Winterfell. His living-heir assumptions are **false to the reader**, who has seen Bran survive; the threat posed by her formal marriage nonetheless exists under contemporary knowledge.
13. **Catelyn proposes negotiated submission; Robb refuses.** She cites Torrhen Stark bending to Aegon and Balon Greyjoy bending to Robert as precedents for preserving lives. Robb refuses to yield because the Lannisters killed Ned. This is an actual conflict over policy and justice, not merely a mother asking for reassurance.
14. **Catelyn argues the Greyjoys threaten Stark dynastic survival.** She reasons that Balon, Theon and the ironborn cannot hold the North if any recognized Stark heir remains, possibly including **Jeyne's future children**. Her logic is feasible; her certainty about Theon murdering Bran/Rickon is **dramatic irony**, not verified.
15. **The mother–king relationship fractures briefly.** In anger Robb asks whether Catelyn has forgotten Ned's death. She nearly strikes him, then leaves with permission while Robb draws his sword in the godswood. Catelyn does not know his purpose; **do not assert he threatened to stab her or that the blade was part of a ritual**.
16. **Lothar's dinner diplomacy is markedly flattering.** He praises Hoster, Edmure's Stone Mill defense and Robb's execution of Rickard Karstark. Walder Rivers is reserved and stern. They intentionally separate sympathetic conversation from bargaining substance, but inner motives are not narrated.
17. **The Freys transmit news attributed to the two former wards.** Lothar says **Big Walder wrote** and Little Walder also signed a letter from the **Dreadfort** stating Winterfell was burned, Rodrik Cassel slain, women/children rescued by **Roose Bolton's bastard** and ironborn responsible for the destruction under Theon. The fact the Freys **present this letter** is shown; the contents are **interested, indirect claims**.
18. **The letter diverges from the reader's direct Winterfell history.** [ACOK 66 Theon](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/66-theon.md) and [ACOK 69 Bran](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/69-bran.md) allow the reader to question the Freys' assignment of the fire to Theon and claims that all children are safe at the Dreadfort. **Ramsay's role and actual fate of northern survivors require cross-POV tracing**.
19. **Robb still assumes Ramsay Snow died.** He says Ramsay was allegedly a monster, murderer and dead coward; Walder Rivers replies war reports can be wrong, and Lothar declines to confirm Theon's fate. The earlier death report is **not independent evidence of Ramsay's death**, and their evasive answers do not identify his precise present location.
20. **The Frey reply is conditional reconciliation.** Lothar says Lord Walder will renew allegiance and accept a Tully marriage **only if Robb personally apologizes** at the Twins. Catelyn dislikes the humiliating condition but Robb accepts the value of restoring Frey support. **Words spoken by envoys are not independently verified secret intentions of Walder.**
21. **Roslin is selected, not offered as one of several choices.** The proposed bride is **Roslin Frey, sixteen**, youngest daughter of Walder's **sixth wife Bethany Rosby**. Lothar describes her as gentle and musical; **Edmure has not met her** and asks to see her before committing. Walder Rivers refuses and threatens withdrawal if Edmure requests another bride.
22. **The wedding must occur immediately.** Lothar invokes the risks of Edmure dying in war and Walder's old age as reasons not to defer until after fighting. That is a coherent explanation on its face, but the insistence on a specific bride, pre-wedding commitment and direct apology also creates **concentration of political risk** at the Twins.
23. **Edmure cannot bargain freely.** He initially says no, points out he is Lord of Riverrun and resents being assigned a bride by Walder; Robb declines to command marriage outright. Catelyn and Brynden point out how long renewed negotiation could take while Northern defense is collapsing. Edmure finally **agrees to marry Roslin as amends** for his role in the Battle of the Fords. **No wedding is performed in this chapter.**
24. **End position.** Hoster has been burned and sent downriver; Edmure is now lord, grieving. Robb knows of Sansa's marriage and reported Winterfell catastrophe; the Freys have obtained tentative agreement for personal apology and an expedited wedding but not performed either. Catelyn sees the alliance as necessary while distrusting its conditions.

## Rival hypotheses / disconfirming tests

- **Were the Freys conducting a trap?** The insisted personal apology, no-choice bride, swift wedding and questionable Winterfell news warrant scrutiny. But these could also be understood as an elderly lord's demand for respect and credible alliance. **No conspiracy is independently established inside this chapter.**
- **Duskendale as sabotage?** Robb's startled question and enormous losses make a bad-order problem clear; the author of any suspicious order must be located, not assumed.
- **Fate of Winterfell:** The reader's earlier Bran/Theon evidence is stronger than a letter the Freys quote. Preserve perspective rather than saying Catelyn 'learns' as fact Theon burned it.
- **Tansy identity:** Last word is important but lacks context here; do not conflate with **the Peach's Tansy** merely on a shared name.
- **Dynastic stakes:** The Sansa–Tyrion marriage exists, but Robb does not yet know of an heir by Sansa nor whether its sex-related legal status can be challenged.
