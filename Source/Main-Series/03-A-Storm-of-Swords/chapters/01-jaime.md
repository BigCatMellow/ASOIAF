## Chronological narrative and decisions

1. **FIRST JAIME POV / POST-CAPTIVITY PHYSICAL STATE:** Jaime moves down the **Red Fork** at dawn in a skiff with **Brienne of Tarth and Ser Cleos Frey**, after being confined under Riverrun for a long period. He is exhilarated by sun and air, physically weakened and filthy, with **iron cuffs binding wrists and ankles and a short chain** that restricts his stride. He is still a **prisoner under escort**, not unconditionally freed as a wandering knight.
2. **ESCAPE FROM RIVERRUN RECONSTRUCTED:** Jaime recalls being **intoxicated** when Lady Catelyn arranged his removal. Brienne dealt with a **gaoler**, helped an unsteady Jaime upstairs, and hid him in the boat while Catelyn ordered the **Water Gate raised** under a diplomatic pretext: Cleos returning to King's Landing with new terms. The precise sequence is **Jaime's memory under alcohol**, but the skiff journey and his manacles are directly verified.
3. **EXACT TERMS OF JAIME'S OATH:** Catelyn made Jaime swear **not to bear arms against Stark or Tully**, to compel Tyrion to honor his commitment to return **Sansa and Arya unharmed**, and bound the promise to **his knightly, Lannister and Kingsguard honor, his closest kin, and old and new gods**. She held **Brienne's sword against his chest** as she demanded the words. Jaime questions how binding an oath is **when drunk, chained and threatened with a sword**; that is a real coercion factor, not proof that he has already repudiated it.
4. **BRIENNE'S SPECIFIC ASSIGNMENT:** Brienne insists her oath runs to **Catelyn**, not to Robb's army or Tarth's Storm's End allegiance. She is to **deliver Jaime safely to Tyrion at King's Landing** and then bring the girls back or not return at all. She will not remove Jaime's shackles. The reader knows **Arya is not actually held in King's Landing** (`ASOS 3 Arya`), meaning the plan's formal terms cannot simply be fulfilled by handing over two royal-hostaged daughters.
5. **CLEOS AND COMPETING LINEAGES:** Cleos is Jaime's cousin, son of **Genna Lannister and Emmon Frey**. His quartered **Frey towers and Lannister lion** surcoat embodies cross-faction kinship. Jaime knows his uncle-by-marriage **Emmon chose the Lannister side over Walder's Tully alignment**, but judges Cleos timid. His contempt is biased, not a full measure of the cousin's courage.
6. **JAIME'S VIOLENT CONTEMPT FOR BRIENNE:** Jaime repeatedly mocks Brienne's gender, looks, body and speech and fantasizes taking her weapons. Brienne calls him a **monster**, explicitly citing **incest, regicide and Bran's attempted murder**. Jaime denies neither the king killing nor his sexual relationship with Cersei; he instead argues Bran was an intruder and demands greater courtesy toward Cersei. His **internal rationalization of pushing Bran** is not evidence the child was morally culpable.
7. **NEW CERSEI FLASHBACK / BRAN AFTERMATH:** Jaime remembers Cersei reproaching him **for throwing seven-year-old Bran from the window**, because a child who remembered could perhaps have been intimidated or dismissed as dreaming. Jaime threatened to kill **Ned Stark** if the king believed the accusation, then initiated sexual intimacy despite her initially resisting. This strengthens **Cersei's already documented displeasure over the shove**, not proof that she later hired Bran's attacker.
8. **JAIME REOPENS THE CATSPAW QUESTION:** Jaime explicitly wonders whether **Cersei hired the second assassin** while Bran was unconscious, but immediately reasons she would have told him and would not choose such a clumsy catspaw. His thoughts reveal both suspicion and self-serving confidence. **There is still no witnessed instruction, payment or confession identifying the catspaw's principal**.
9. **A NEW FACIAL APPEARANCE:** Jaime requests Cleos **cut and shave his famous long blond hair**, leaving a rough beard so he cannot be readily recognized chained in the boat. The hairstyle makes him look **older and unlike his twin sister**; he predicts **Cersei will dislike the difference**. This is an important identity marker: disguise makes even his own mental 'mirror twin' connection unstable.
10. **MATERIAL TRADE AND WAR DAMAGE:** River settlements and fishermen's nets are empty or burned, a **Lannister-red cloaked corpse** floats downstream, and peasants till under the shell of a burned holdfast. Jaime remembers the Trident as a **major commercial artery** of fishermen, barges and river markets, now disrupted. This directly grounds the war's economic cost beyond the noble political map.
11. **HANGED TAVERN WOMEN:** The skiff passes a **burned inn** with women's corpses hanged from a live oak, under a sign accusing them of sexual contact with **Lannister soldiers**. Brienne calls it dishonorable and moves to bury them; Cleos remembers the inn and believes previous escorts sheltered there. Jaime notes the area is **Bracken land** and considers **Jonos Bracken, Marq Piper, Beric Dondarrion or Bolton's men** as possible perpetrators. **The actual executioners are not identified**; even the claimed contact may have meant serving drinks or meals.
12. **BOLTON THREAT ENTERS JAIME'S ROUTE:** Cleos says **Bolton now holds Harrenhal** after defeating Amory's occupiers, contrary to Jaime's earlier assumption that Roose was broken at the Green Fork. Jaime immediately recognizes **roads and river crossings toward King's Landing may be watched**, a sound tactical implication. This places the innocent Brienne–Jaime convoy within the same dangerous Bolton zone recorded by `ACOK 64 Arya`.
13. **RIVER-SAIL PURSUIT:** Before the burials can happen, Brienne spots a **Tully-colored sail** closing behind them. The pursuer is a **river galley with nine oars on either side**; Jaime guesses 18–25 men. The leader is **Ser Robin Ryger**, Riverrun's longtime guard captain, who demands their surrender despite Cleos and Jaime invoking **Catelyn's authority**. Robin says **Catelyn is not the ruler of Riverrun**, confirming the competing internal orders rather than denying Jaime's claim that she released him.
14. **ARROWS AND GEOGRAPHY:** Ryger's archers fire after the refusal to surrender. Brienne steers into a **narrow secondary channel behind a wooded island** beside high northern bluffs, exploiting rock cover and currents. Jaime helps with an oar even in irons. The hard river physics, not magic or outside rescuers, determine the escape opening.
15. **BRIENNE'S UNEXPECTED ATTACK:** Brienne suddenly **dives from their skiff, swims to the north bank, climbs the cliff**, and dislodges a large boulder above Ryger's galley. Jaime distracts Ryger by offering **single combat in chains**, buying time; Ryger recognizes the bait as dubious but pauses for the exchange. The boulder fractures, snaps the pursuing vessel's **mast**, tosses archers into water, crushes a rower's leg and evidently **punctures its hull**, causing rapid foundering. **No total casualty count or Ryger death is shown**.
16. **AN IMPORTANT JAIME CHOICE:** Jaime initially delights in escaping the galley **and Brienne**, who is left on the bank; he considers striking her with an oar when she dives back into their path. Instead he **extends the oar and pulls her back into the boat**. The action is directly witnessed, though its motive (practical need, gratitude, oath or nascent respect) is not confessed. Brienne refuses thanks, stressing that **she means to keep her oath**.
17. **ENDING BOUNDARY:** The three continue downriver together. Jaime is still **chained**, Brienne still supervising, **Catelyn's exchange not completed**, and the danger of road patrols and regional vengeance remains. Brienne has displayed decisive **seamanship, strength and battlefield initiative**; Jaime has demonstrated both enduring cruelty and one countervailing cooperative choice.

## Live theories and future evidence tests

- **Kingslayer and Kingsguard 'honor':** Jaime's insistence on not being called Kingslayer exists beside his admission he killed a king and violated another grave duty by attempting to kill Bran. Brienne's absolute mission fulfillment forces a test of what **keeping an oath in changing circumstances** actually means. Neither person is yet fully transparent or uncomplicatedly honorable.
- **Catspaw and Cersei:** Jaime reconsiders the possibility Cersei arranged Bran's later murder, then dismisses it by profiling her habits. His **reasoning is not independent exculpatory evidence**.
- **Women killed for alleged collaboration:** Brienne's insistence on burial and Jaime's correctly uncertain perpetrator list make the material a **case study in sexual punishment by military occupation**, separate from generic lawless war.
- **Regional geography:** A riverboat fleeing Riverrun cannot safely assume the Trident's banks are held by one coherent faction. **Bolton control of Harrenhal** becomes a new threat to an already contestable exchange.
