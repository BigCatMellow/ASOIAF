## Source-first chronological record

1. **Meereen is much harder to take than the previous two cities.** Multicolored brick, high bastioned walls and massive towers enclose a city as large as **Astapor and Yunkai combined**. An **800-foot Great Pyramid** rises behind them, crowned with a bronze harpy. The city stands by the **Skahazadhan** at Slaver's Bay. This is a real fortification and supply obstacle, not evidence Dany simply lacks courage.
2. **The force is predominantly noncombatant.** Dany counts **more than 80,000 in her host after Yunkai, fewer than one quarter soldiers**. The rest are freed people, needing food, water, pack animals and care. Her officers' preference for abandoning them would alter the liberation project fundamentally.
3. **The Great Masters have implemented scorched earth.** Harvests are gathered or burned, fields destroyed, wells poisoned, and **slave children crucified alive on consecutive mileposts** from Yunkai. **Exactly 163** bodies have been counted by Dany's host on reaching Meereen; the reader is told the victims were nailed there living. Daario ordered them removed before she saw them, and she countermanded the order to witness and remember every child. The perpetrators' individual names are unknown.
4. **A heavily stylized Meereenese champion challenges the besiegers.** **Oznak zo Pahl**, mounted on a white charger with pink/white stripes, wears curled hair like ram horns and carries a **14-foot lance**. His defiant performance rallies wall defenders, including hundreds who join his act of urinating toward the besiegers. **Arstan** correctly argues a champion's symbolic effect on morale, while **Jorah** correctly notes winning a duel will not open the gate.
5. **Dany prevents her irreplaceable commanders from taking a needless duel.** Her bloodriders, Daario and leaders must remain with their troops. The **Stormcrows are not necessarily personally loyal to Daario**, some having followed the two captains he killed; Dany recognizes that maintaining their command requires him.
6. **Brown Ben Plumm now commands the Second Sons.** The company elected him after **Mero's flight at Yunkai**. Ben says he once guarded Oznak's uncle and tells of a man **Scarb** whose liver Oznak cut out on an honor pretext. This is **Ben's anecdote**, not an independent witness to Scarb's death.
7. **Dany chooses Strong Belwas as counter-champion for political reasons.** Belwas was formerly enslaved in Meereen's fighting pits, so victory over a noble humiliates the ruling class and defeat gives its hero little honor. Belwas is militarily less indispensable than Jorah, Daario or the bloodriders. Dany also seeks to discover what protector Illyrio provided.
8. **Belwas defeats Oznak in a clearly described sequence.** He evades multiple mounted lance charges, slices the charger's legs with his **arakh**, fights the dismounted champion, receives a cut **below his breasts**, then kills and decapitates Oznak. Belwas finishes off the dying horse, performs a humiliating bodily insult toward the city and returns alive. **The duel is a victory, not capture of Meereen.**
9. **Belwas deliberately permits an injury as fighting-pit custom.** He claims he lets each opponent cut him once before killing him, so his scars number his victims. This is **his own boast**, not independently validated tally. Remembering Drogo's disastrous wound, Dany insists on treatment: a skilled freed Yunkish healer **cleans with vinegar, stitches and binds with firewine-soaked linen**.
10. **Food and siege matériel determine the options.** Meereen has granaries, terraced crops, preserved stores and valuables; Dany's camp has nearly exhausted food. Meereen's masters burned the timber **within about twenty leagues**, leaving no ready wood for siege towers, ladders, rams or trebuchets. Sea/river approaches are guarded and Dany has **only three ships**.
11. **The city has anti-assault infrastructure.** Ben identifies **bronze harpy-head mouths above gates** through which defenders can pour boiling oil; Jorah later finds the river wall **even higher than the land walls**, protected by **a dozen tied fire hulks**. A seaborne assault would not be an effortless bypass.
12. **Grey Worm corrects stereotypes about the Unsullied.** Contrary to Daario's taunt, pain resistance **does not prevent burning, blindness or death** from boiling oil. He offers to lead a suicidal gate assault; Dany refuses to expend those lives needlessly.
13. **Disease and water make a long siege unworkable for besiegers.** The Skahazadhan receives Meereen's refuse while the city draws clean water from wells. Officers report **fever, brownleg and three cases of bloody flux** among Dany's camps; freed people's exhaustion worsens vulnerability. No diagnosis confirms poisoned wells caused each illness.
14. **Jorah proposes moving west and abandoning Meereen.** In his account the army was purchased to conquer Westeros, not all the world's slavers. He admits the thousands following Dany **would largely starve if abandoned**. Dany refuses to repeat the Red Waste death march and regards the freed as **her children**. This is a concrete ethical and logistical choice, not proof she can feed everyone.
15. **Brown Ben identifies a dangerous alternative sewer route.** Large brick drains empty into the **Skahazadhan**, with **iron grates** (some rusted through), waist- to head-deep foul water, rats, pitch darkness and a lengthy upward maze. Ben claims he escaped Meereen through a sewer after Scarb's death. **He declines to attempt a return himself**. Dany considers a limited infiltration but does not order it in this chapter.
16. **An unusual interaction with Viserion prompts a bloodline claim.** The white dragon clumsily lands on Ben's head and shoulder. Ben jokes he descends from **an old Lord Plumm who married a dragon princess** during some unspecified Aegon's reign. His grandmother transmitted a grotesquely exaggerated tale of the lord's anatomy; Ben cannot identify **which of five Aegons**. **Dragon interest is observed; descent, genealogy and any dragon-detection mechanism remain unverified.**
17. **Dany herself thinks of the 'three heads of the dragon'.** She contemplates future riders because her hatchlings remain **too small to carry people**, and wonders if intimacy with Daario could make him one of the three. This is her speculation about a cryptic phrase, not a confirmed allocation of riders or husbands.
18. **Her desire for Daario is strong but ambivalent.** His daily gift of wildflowers and herbs—**waspwillow, wild mint, daggerleaf, lady's lace, harpy's gold** and others—pleases her. She recognizes he is cruel, that he killed his fellow captains, and that Jorah would be jealous. **Neither marriage nor intimacy with Daario occurs here**.
19. **Dany inspects her unequal camps on horseback.** Unsullied drill and bathe daily, cleaning themselves with sand if water is unavailable. The ships are now **Balerion** (formerly *Saduleon*), **Meraxes** (*Joso's Prank*) and **Vhagar** (*Summer Sun*), names Dany chose; she notes that these dragons were gods in old Valyria. The freed people's camp is disorganized, visibly hungry, and some roast **a dead horse**. Jorah has organized their fighting members into **four companies**, but she sees no drilling.
20. **'Mhysa' creates physical access and an assassination vulnerability.** Freed people seek blessings and touch her stirrup. While Dany speaks with a pregnant woman who wants her to name a child, a **shaven-headed man** seizes her wrist and drags her down; he proves to be **Mero, the Titan's Bastard**, having concealed himself among those following from Yunkai.
21. **Mero's intent to kill is explicit.** He threatens mutilation, draws a sword and cuts a freedman who approaches. **Arstan Whitebeard**, armed with an ordinary hardwood staff, parries Mero, smashes teeth, breaks his leg and knocks him to the surf, where freed people swarm him with knives and stones. Dany subsequently says **Arstan killed Mero**; the final personal killing blow amid the crowd is **not separately documented**.
22. **Jorah knowingly withheld the news Mero was at large.** He says he offered a bounty but saw no reason to frighten Dany. She rebukes him for not warning her. **The assassin's survival after Yunkai and concealed presence directly refute a claim that her camp is safe because its followers love her.**
23. **Arstan's skill reveals his false role.** Dany asks Jorah to knight him; **Jorah and Arstan both refuse simultaneously** because Arstan was already knighted. He confesses he had used the name **Arstan** but concealed his real identity, which Jorah recognizes as **Ser Barristan Selmy**, former Lord Commander of the Kingsguard.
24. **Barristan admits service to Robert and late renunciation.** He accepted Robert's pardon after the Trident and served Robert and then the Lannister court; he says **Joffrey dismissed him and sent men to kill him the same day**, prompting him to seek Dany. His secret identity was meant to prevent Lannister intelligence from learning of his service. **His account establishes motive testimony, not proof every step in his escape occurred as described.**
25. **Barristan identifies a spy inside Dany's intimate circle.** He says he sat on the small council and heard **Varys repeatedly report Viserys's movements**, and that an informer accompanied Dany from the day she married Drogo. Jorah's reaction and confession corroborate the basic claim: he had **provided information to Varys for the prospect of a return from exile**.
26. **Jorah frames espionage as an earlier mistake.** He says it happened **'only at the start'** before he came to know and love Dany, but offers **no dates of his last message, precise reports, payment records or objective proof of cessation**. Dany associates the revelation with the Undying's warnings of betrayal for **gold and love**, though Jorah says the inducement was **permission to go home**, not a direct payment of gold.
27. **Two genuine rescuers are simultaneously proven unreliable narrators about themselves.** Barristan saved Dany from Mero and volunteered history about Rhaegar; Jorah counseled and protected her, but both withheld significant truths. Dany orders both away and threatens dragonfire, then hesitates when asked where they should go. **The text ends with her privately reaching a decision, without stating what new destination or order she will give**.
28. **End state:** Meereen remains unconquered; the 163 child victims and immense hungry freed population sharpen her siege dilemma. **Mero is dead**, **Barristan's identity is revealed**, **Jorah's earlier espionage is confessed**, and the two knights await Dany's next instruction.

## Competing readings and follow-up checks

- **Conquest versus guardianship:** Dany's stated responsibility to freed people is real, while an 80,000-plus column is difficult to feed or transport. Track genuine provisioning mechanisms and future political authority rather than assuming emancipation resolves all practical needs.
- **Who can be trusted?** Jorah's spy confession and Barristan's royal service differ legally, ethically and in timing. Both previously risked their lives to protect Dany. Her sense of a singular 'betrayal' should not replace granular motive/custody tests.
- **Dragons and lineage:** Viserion's physical behavior plus a transmitted Plumm marriage story are interesting clues, but no validated descent chain or evidence that dragons know ancestry is supplied.
- **Methods for taking Meereen:** Sewer entry is risky but real testimony from a past escapee; other options are materially constrained by scorched earth and river access.
- **Prophecy:** 'Three heads' and the 'betrayal for gold' are characters' interpretations. Jorah's actual promised reward was homecoming; match text before fitting a prophetic slot.
