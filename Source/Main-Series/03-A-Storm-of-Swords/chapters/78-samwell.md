## Detailed chronological reading

1. **Stannis is impatient with the ten-day deadlock** in choosing a Lord Commander. He summons the candidates and officers, keeps them kneeling unusually long, and requires a prompt resolution. **He has not appointed anyone himself**.
2. **Slynt openly seeks royal intervention in the ballot.** He flatters Stannis and implies kingly guidance would be welcome. **Cotter Pyke and Denys Mallister protest**, maintaining the Watch alone chooses its commander.
3. **Aemon gives the institutional tally.** Since the Wall's founding there have been **997 previous Lords Commander**; Stannis's interference would violate an ancient practice. **The office sought is number 998.**
4. **Stannis publicly exposes Slynt's former corruption.** He recalls Jon Arryn bringing evidence of **selling gold-cloak offices and collecting subordinate officers' pay** before Robert's small council. He says **two potential witnesses died while on rounds**. This is **Stannis's direct recollection of reports**, not judicial findings newly placed into the record.
5. **Littlefinger's indirect protection of Slynt is suggested.** Stannis suspects Petyr engineered Robert's casual acceptance of City Watch corruption because it enriched the crown as well. This is **Stannis's inference**, not verified proof of Petyr personally ordering the two officers' deaths.
6. **Aemon invokes Night's Watch amnesty.** Whatever Janos's crimes before taking the black, the legal rules of sworn brothers wipe those crimes clean. **Stannis accepts the point**, saying he will endure Slynt if the Watch truly selects him. The distinction between **legal eligibility and personal trustworthiness** matters.
7. **Stannis explicitly promises Watch military neutrality regarding his dynastic war.** He says he does **not ask** the Watch to fight Lannister claimants and that the new commander must defend the Wall against the real enemy. His pledge of non-interference in the southern war coexists with extensive demands on Watch property.
8. **The king formally demands the Gift and abandoned castles.** He recognizes **Castle Black, Eastwatch and Shadow Tower** will remain Watch garrisons, but plans to use other castles as his forward bases and **the Gift** for settlement. **Bowen Marsh** says the Gift was granted **in perpetuity** and cannot be seized lawfully; Stannis counters that permanent grant does not prevent the recipient from giving it anew. **No grant is signed here**.
9. **Stannis requests an engineering inventory.** He wants **Othell Yarwyck** and others to list the condition of every abandoned castle and what is required to rebuild it. He promises **garrisons and nightfires within a year**. This is an ambitious operational target, not demonstrated completion.
10. **Melisandre presents the conflict as a supernatural war for life.** She insists burning nightfires and R'hllor worship are essential and declares **Stannis is Azor Ahai / the prince that was promised**, citing comet and luminous sword. The assertion is **religious testimony**, not independently established prophecy identification.
11. **Aemon explicitly knows the promised-prince tradition.** He calls this a **war for the dawn** and asks where the prince is. This locates that terminology inside an ancient maester's knowledge rather than showing he endorses Melisandre's answer.
12. **Stannis appears uncomfortable with her identification.** Sam observes tooth grinding and dismissal. The visible behavior is real; the king's exact theology cannot be inferred from a facial expression alone.
13. **Stannis interviews Sam for firsthand combat intelligence.** He recognizes Sam as the Watchman who killed an Other and invokes Randyll Tarly's **victory over Robert at Ashford**, saying Randyll, rather than Mace Tyrell, actually won the battle. He also recalls Randyll killing **Lord Cafferen** with a great Valyrian sword and sending the head to Aerys. These are Stannis's historical recollections.
14. **Sam's birth could have made him a hostage, but his vow protects him.** Stannis notes he could use Randyll's son politically had he not joined the Watch; Aemon reinforces that Sam has taken the black.
15. **Dragonstone contains readily accessible dragonglass.** Stannis reports tunnels beneath the mountain with **black, green, red and purple obsidian**. He has ordered **Ser Rolland Storm**, the castellan, to mine it before Dragonstone falls. **The extraction order exists; transport and shipment north have not been observed**.
16. **The novel distinguishes Others from wights under controlled evidence.** Sam says his obsidian dagger **destroyed an Other**, but **shattered against a wight**. Melisandre says the wights remain dead flesh and that **steel and fire** can work against them, while Others require a different defense. Her metaphysical explanation is a **claim**, but Sam's contrasted weapon encounters are sourced to his own experiences.
17. **Stannis wants to use the Black Gate.** He knows Sam and Gilly passed through a hidden portal beneath the **Nightfort**, where Stannis plans to establish headquarters. He demands that Sam show it. **Its opening for someone not sworn to the Watch is an explicit open question in Sam's thoughts**, not demonstrated.
18. **Aemon deliberately tests Lightbringer without sight.** Stannis unsheathes the blade: Sam sees **yellow, orange and red light** shining strongly enough that the room dims by contrast when it is put away. This is **real light emission**, regardless of the explanation.
19. **Crucial negative result: the sword does not radiate heat.** Aemon asks Sam if he felt any warmth or whether its **wood/leather scabbard is scorched**. Sam says no. **Aemon's concern is evidentiary**, putting a test against the claim that this is literally a sword of living fire. It does not, by itself, prove the entire legend false or another object true.
20. **Aemon abstains from endorsing a candidate.** As a maester, he says he must counsel whoever wins and cannot appear to favor a man. When Sam asks if he can act instead, Aemon challenges him to decide for himself, neither expressly commanding nor forbidding the intervention.
21. **Sam approaches Cotter Pyke first.** Pyke is blunt, claims he never wanted Castle Black leadership, and fears Denys Mallister's age, inaction and noble privilege. Pyke says he is primarily **a shipboard fighting commander** and would rather not be stranded inland.
22. **Pyke sees the king as a grave operational challenge.** He fears Mallister lacks will to resist Stannis and Melisandre on Watch independence. He still refuses to yield to the 'eagle from Shadow Tower'.
23. **Sam proposes a compromise and receives guarded willingness.** Pyke dismisses **Bowen Marsh, Othell Yarwyck, Slynt and the cook** as unfit for different reasons, but says he **might support someone else**. This is not an immediate withdrawal.
24. **Denys Mallister refuses to support Pyke for distinct reasons.** He claims **33 years commanding Shadow Tower**, being a knight and highborn trained to deal with lords, and cites Pyke's ironborn piracy, alleged youthful crimes and dependence on a maester for correspondence. These are **Denys's interested evaluations**, not an impartial audit of Cotter's capacities.
25. **Mallister admits prior willingness to withdraw for another worthy candidate.** He says he stepped aside when **Qorgyle** and **Mormont** were named previously and would accept a capable third party now.
26. **Sam identifies Jon without initially naming him.** He cites **Jeor Mormont's trust, Qhorin Halfhand's mission, Noye's battlefield delegation, castle upbringing, educated service, Eddard's lordship and Robb's kingship**. Mallister considers the boy **very young but potentially suitable**.
27. **Sam uses deliberate, symmetrical deception.** To Mallister, he falsely says Stannis privately plans to impose **Cotter Pyke** if no election occurs that night; to Pyke, he falsely says the king plans to impose **Denys Mallister**. **Neither statement was made by Stannis** in Sam's earlier audience. This is not just creative diplomacy: the chapter directly narrates **two strategic lies intended to prevent division**.
28. **The two commanders' attitudes soften but no ballot is held within this chapter.** Mallister thinks; Pyke jokes that young Jon might irritate his rival, but does not yet declare committed votes. The election outcome follows in Jon's subsequent POV.
29. **End state:** The Watch faces potentially coerced land and castle transfers, genuine new data about dragonglass and Lightbringer, and a political coalition strategy based partly on **Sam's deception**. Jon's election is **not yet achieved** in this source.
