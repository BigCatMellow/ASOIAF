## Plot, motives, decisions, consequences

1. **The kingsroad corridor is devastated.** Jaime, Brienne and Cleos pass scorched farms, orchards and broken bridges in a swath about two days' ride on either side of the road. Autumn rains make streams difficult to cross; wolf howls replace human settlement. This is observable destruction, not proof which faction burned every stretch.
2. **Maidenpool has become a graveyard.** The Mooton salmon still flies over the castle, but the town's gates are wrecked, homes and shops plundered, and the legendary bathing pool is polluted with rotting corpses. Its connection to Florian and Jonquil contrasts courtly legend with the war's effect on actual women. **Lord Mooton's presence or intentions are not checked.**
3. **Jaime mocks Brienne and Cleos in a dangerous location.** Brienne warns that he can be gagged. Jaime insists she use her name rather than his contemptuous nickname, but continues needling her. Cleos fears Mooton men or other enemies might hear him; this is a real operational security concern.
4. **The group chooses the Duskendale road.** Cleos favors the slower coastal approach, Brienne rides away from Maidenpool toward Duskendale, and Jaime approves the shorter riskier road. Neither the precise final route nor the safety of the coastal alternative is proven.
5. **Jaime's childhood with Cersei is recalled.** He says intimacy predated puberty; Joanna caught them in behavior she considered alarming and separated their chambers under threat of telling Tywin. Joanna then died giving birth to Tyrion. This is Jaime's private memory, not Cersei's corroborating testimony.
6. **An alarming postwar fantasy.** Jaime privately imagines openly marrying Cersei as Targaryens once did, even pairing **Joffrey with Myrcella**, claiming military power might outrank dynastic law. These are **fantasies**, not current Cersei plans or lawful arrangements; compare Tywin's very different Cersei–Willas proposal in [ASOS 19 Tyrion](19-tyrion.md).
7. **Jaime still chooses to try keeping his oath to Catelyn.** He intends to return **Sansa** and **Arya if found**, surprising people who expect oathbreaking. This is intent, not an executed rescue; he knows neither girl's actual current situation.
8. **Roadside bowmen ambush the three.** Arrows strike Jaime's horse and Brienne in back and leg. **Cleos Frey** is hit, dragged by his bolting mount, and fatally injured. Jaime shouts a charge despite his shackles; Brienne joins, shouting Tarth's battle cry. The bowmen flee behind woodland cover.
9. **The original bowmen are not identified as Vargo's men.** Jaime later recognizes that the Brave Companions surrounding them by the brook are **a different group** from the road ambushers. Keep both encounters separate rather than turning them into one fully preplanned capture.
10. **Cleos's death is confirmed.** He has arrow wounds to arm and chest, but Jaime observes severe skull damage caused by the dragging; Brienne stays beside him briefly. Their envoy and traveling third party are gone, which changes the oaths' practical enforcement.
11. **Jaime arms himself against Brienne's refusal.** He wants Cleos's sword, clothes and horse, arguing he could share guard duty. Brienne refuses to arm a man she is charged to escort. Jaime draws the sword using chained hands and attacks her. **It is not a consensual sparring match.**
12. **A long, dangerous duel reverses Jaime's expectations.** Jaime begins with astonishing speed and technique, but his wrists remain manacled, he is deconditioned after captivity, and Brienne is unexpectedly stronger. She blocks, counters and keeps her sword against a former Kingsguard champion. Jaime inflicts a thigh wound; Brienne wounds his brow, knocks his weapon away, and gains physical control in the brook.
13. **Brienne's competence is directly witnessed.** Jaime revises his contempt for her swordplay. The duel does **not** establish that an unshackled, fully fit Jaime would inevitably lose; nor does it erase Brienne's advantage after enduring the opening assault.
14. **The oath becomes a trap in argument.** Brienne repeatedly orders Jaime to yield and holds him underwater to force it. Jaime taunts that drowning him would breach her promise to Catelyn. Their conflict tests the difficulty of protecting a dangerous captive rather than proving their moral positions identical.
15. **Brave Companions interrupt.** Armed men surround the stream just as the duel ends. Jaime identifies a cosmopolitan company including **Urswyck the Faithful, Rorge, and the Dornishman Timeon**. Their taunts include explicit threats of sexual assault on Brienne. The first bowmen are still not identified.
16. **Their political allegiance has changed.** Urswyck states the company no longer serves Tywin but **Roose Bolton and the King in the North**. He reports Vargo Hoat has been promised **Harrenhal**. The promise is attributed to a mercenary officer; a title's lawful transfer and Bolton's complete bargain are **not** verified in this scene.
17. **Brienne tries to invoke Catelyn's protection.** She explains Jaime was being escorted in a captive exchange and that Catelyn commanded it. Urswyck's men assault both regardless. Four men are required to subdue Brienne; her teeth are broken and both prisoners are injured. Formal allegiance offers no protection against these mercenaries.
18. **Jaime and Brienne are tied together.** They are carried back-to-back on her horse; the Companions strip the dead Cleos and divide his goods, including his quartered Frey–Lannister surcoat. A meaningful material sign of the exchange mission passes into enemy hands.
19. **Jaime tries bribery and a promised pardon.** He proposes Urswyck deliver both captives to King's Landing instead, offering Lannister gold, knighthood and absolution. Urswyck briefly entertains being called Ser Urswyck but knows he is a turncloak and that Tywin could seek retaliation for Vargo's defection.
20. **Jaime's reputation becomes a tactical liability.** Urswyck dismisses promises from the Kingslayer as untrustworthy, slaps him and rides away. Aerys's death again costs Jaime bargaining power even when his intentions about Stark hostages are newly sincere.
21. **The 'Sapphire Isle' is a ransom bluff.** Jaime says Tarth's name implies gemstones and encourages Urswyck to overvalue Brienne. Brienne explains the epithet refers to **blue waters**, not gem deposits. Jaime explicitly believes a mistaken expectation of ransom may discourage imminent assault. His cruel language does not erase that protective calculation.
22. **Vargo is encountered desecrating a sept.** His company has smashed windows, pried stones from images, hanged a septon upside down and used his corpse as a target. This is direct evidence of atrocities under this purported Stark-aligned company; **it does not establish that Robb authorized them**.
23. **Brienne's appeal is rejected again.** She names her charge from Catelyn to deliver Jaime to King's Landing and invokes the King in the North, but Vargo orders her silenced. Urswyck again stresses her supposed value in sapphires.
24. **Vargo intends coercive messaging.** Jaime offers Tywin's ransom, expecting money to reverse the defection. Vargo agrees money is desirable but insists on first sending Tywin a **message**. His collection of foreign coins worn round his neck is a distinctive material sign of mobility and greed, not proof of magical meaning.
25. **The chapter ends mid-attack.** Men force Jaime's chained arms forward while a Dothraki approaches with a sharp **arakh** and Vargo's companions immobilize him. Jaime expects an intimidation performance until the blow falls and he screams. **This chapter does not itself narrate the resulting injury; identify its nature from the next Jaime chapter, not retrospective assumption.**

## Competing readings / future tests

- **Was the capture engineered?** Coincidence remains viable: roadside bowmen flee, the duel carries far, and another force hears the noise. No ordering document or direct coordination between the two groups appears.
- **Was Jaime protecting Brienne?** His sapphire lie is plausibly protective, but his threats and earlier attack caused real injury and danger. Avoid using one calculation to erase his other decisions.
- **Bolton alliance:** The Brave Companions' claimed contract suggests complex northern collaboration. Inspect Roose's actual orders and factional chain of command; `serve the King in the North` may be self-serving camouflage for predation.
- **Honor:** Jaime's public Kingslayer stigma weakens ransom negotiations while the narrated private intention is to satisfy a difficult promise. Contrast with the actual protective constraints imposed by Catelyn and Brienne.
- **Next:** determine physical cost of Vargo's 'message' in **ASOS 31 Jaime**, and track who receives news of his capture, without assuming the reader's knowledge reaches the king or Tywin.
