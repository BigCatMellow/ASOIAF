## Sequential events / geography and agency

1. **Ghost is absent at departure.** Jon notices no wolf when the raiders emerge from the cave; he hopes the order to travel east to Castle Black worked. **Absence and hope are observed; destination and any delivered warning remain UNKNOWN.**
2. **Styr divides scouts east and west.** He sends a dozen mounted Thenns in each direction to check high ground for Watch patrols, equipped with bronze-banded warning horns. Meanwhile the main group moves to a crossing site chosen by **Jarl**, whose experience offsets the Magnar's political seniority.
3. **The Wall follows variable topography.** Jon recalls Benjen's comparison of a relatively straight eastern Wall and serpentine western section. Locally the crest looks **800–900 feet** above forest, but a stone ridgeline supplies about a third of elevation, shortening the true ice-climb. These are viewpoint estimates, not precise measurements of magical Wall thickness.
4. **The neglected forest is a security liability.** Sentinel and other trees now grow close to the ice because the Watch no longer clears them routinely. **Jarl climbs partway up a leaning sentinel** and transfers onto the Wall from roughly **300 feet** above ground. This is concrete evidence that staffing and maintenance, not merely supernatural force, protect a wall.
5. **Wildling perspectives on the Wall differ.** Many Thenns, including Styr, have never seen it; they hesitate in awe and fear. Jarl and his climbers have crossed before. Jon considers the Wall the end of the world only depending on which side one inhabits, and is unsure where he now belongs.
6. **Jon considers impossible-feeling alternatives.** Leaving Ygritte behind could put her in danger; bringing her to Castle Black would expose a wildling and apparent deserter; disappearing into Gendel's supposed caves is a fanciful fallback. These are Jon's risk calculations, **not decisions executed**.
7. **Three specialist climbing teams assemble.** Jarl selects **eleven others**, making **twelve in three groups of four**; **Jarl, Grigg the Goat and Errok** lead the teams. Some are younger than Jon, others under roughly twenty-five. Mance promises forged southron swords and a celebratory song to the first team to reach the top.
8. **Climbing tools are improvised but specialized.** Rope harnesses and hemp coils, spiked doeskin boots (iron, bronze or bone spikes), hammers, stakes, and deer-antler ice axes enable ascent. Iron stakes grow scarce; horn and bone replace them. Ordinary material limits and weather matter.
9. **Other crossing methods are catalogued.** Raiders may skirt the Wall by sea using boats in the **Bay of Seals** or by the western **Gorge**, while climbers attempt the middle. Jon recalls patrols finding climbers or bodies two or three times yearly and pursuing raiders who steal horses after crossing. These are his knowledge of practice, not proof routes are equally feasible now.
10. **Jarl's rapid start uses a dangerous natural shortcut.** His four rise well ahead by climbing the leaning tree. Grigg's more pitted, ledged ice provides a safer-looking route despite its slower start; Errok's face is smooth and wet, and his team traverses laterally looking for a chimney.
11. **No Watch patrol arrives.** Jon watches for a four-man patrol able to attack exposed climbers but neither any defender nor the Thenn scout horns sound. This demonstrates a **local absence at the moment**, not proof all Watch patrols have ceased or been destroyed.
12. **A first ice break is survived.** An ice pinnacle Jarl relied on collapses and his rope catches him while the other three cling to anchors. This earlier warning documents why the later catastrophe need not require supernatural intervention.
13. **A massive wall sheet collapses.** After hours of climbing a slab roughly **one foot thick and fifty feet square** separates, sending down falling ice and taking **Jarl's entire roped four-person team**. Jon pulls Ygritte down to protect her; a Thenn below is struck in the nose.
14. **Deaths and mercy are explicit.** Jarl is found impaled in a tree, two teammates dead, and a third gravely injured with smashed legs/spine/ribs. The survivor asks for mercy; a Thenn kills him with a stone mace. This is a **confirmed rescue failure/mercy killing**, not just four men 'vanishing'.
15. **Jon suspects the Wall defended itself.** His thought frames the catastrophe as agency attributed to the Wall, while warmth, slick ice, decaying surface and bad anchors supply a serious ordinary alternative. **No independent magical intervention is shown.**
16. **The dead are burned.** Styr orders a pyre; by the time the other teams finish, Jarl's group is bone and ash. Jarl's loss leaves Styr and surviving climbing specialists, and undermines the most experienced joint leader at a critical transition.
17. **Grigg and Errok establish a ladder route.** The surviving two teams reach the top and pull up **five huge hemp-rung ladders** using ropes, allowing the main force to climb. They are not a secret door or magical crossing. The long ascent remains dangerous.
18. **Two additional Thenns die using the ladders.** After the five ladders are set, **two** climbers from the larger force fall and die before the rest cross successfully. **At least six total** die during the assault (Jarl plus three teammates, then two Thenns), excluding any unreported casualties. No soldiers or horses have been shown descending south yet.
19. **Jon reaches the top near midnight.** Ygritte also arrives and admits she nearly fell multiple times. Jon's reassurance does not make her fear or cold imaginary. **At chapter end he is atop the Wall**, not yet safely marching among southern settlements.
20. **Ygritte reveals the Horn of Winter search failed.** She says the free folk opened **about fifty graves** looking for the **Horn of Joramun**, hoping to bring down the Wall, but never found it. This is a direct statement that they searched and failed **according to Ygritte**; it does not identify any other horn as the missing instrument, establish the Wall's exact vulnerability, or prove what opening the graves released.
21. **Her language links the Wall to blood.** Ygritte says its cold is not merely ice but blood, and she cries over the failure to find the legendary horn. The scene strongly marks a ritual/violent history interpretation of the Wall, but the literal composition or magical price is **not established**.
22. **End state:** Styr's raiders have reached the top of the Wall using ladders, Jarl and five others are dead, Ghost's whereabouts are unknown, and the Horn of Joramun remains **unlocated** in Ygritte's account. Jon has not delivered his warning or crossed down into the realm yet.

## Theory tests / competing cases

- **Does the Wall protect itself?** Evidence: Jon's interpretation and catastrophic ice collapse. Countercase: unstable, thawing ice and climber anchor loads were demonstrated earlier. Future episodes must show more than ordinary ice failure to adopt magical agency.
- **Where is the Horn of Winter?** Its existence/power remains traditional report; Ygritte explicitly says Mance's searching party never found it. Compare [ASOS 18 Samwell](18-samwell.md)'s separate old horn only as a **candidate to test**, never identify them by narrative proximity.
- **Strategic Wall defense:** Trees and understaffing permit climbers to reach the top despite extraordinary height. A few defenders might have stopped exposed climbers, but this is Jon's plausible untested counterfactual.
- **Leadership attrition:** Jarl's death raises Styr's control and reduces expert route knowledge; distinguish loss of joint commander from collapse of all climbing expertise.
- **Identity:** Jon's desire to protect Ygritte and the Watch is genuine but incompatible choices will soon have to be made; the chapter does not make that choice.
