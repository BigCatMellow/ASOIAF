## Sequential events and causal evidence

1. **The marriage feast is underway in the Twins' hall.** The chamber is crowded, smoky and overheated; music features **loud persistent drums**, pipes, fiddles and horns. Catelyn sits between **Ser Ryman Frey** and **Roose Bolton**. She notes the banquet's initially poor food—thin leek soup, vegetables, fish in almond milk, cold turnips, calves' brains and tough beef—contrasting with unlimited wine, ale and mead. Later lamb appears. This is a specific **food/drink asymmetry** that helps incapacitate guests while preserving the appearance of a feast.
2. **Edmure and Roslin genuinely undergo the wedding ceremony.** The ceremony has been conducted by a septon, and Edmure draped Roslin in a **Tully fish cloak**. They share a plate and cup and appear affectionate. **The marriage ceremony is completed**; the bedding and consummation are **not shown directly** to Catelyn, despite plans to conduct them shortly.
3. **Roslin continues to display fear.** Her smile looks fixed to Catelyn; she appears uncomfortable before the bedding and later is **stiff, terrified and crying** when Greatjon carries her away. Catelyn explains this through ordinary anxiety about sexual initiation. In retrospect it may signal foreknowledge or familial pressure, but **Roslin never speaks a confession of plot knowledge here**.
4. **Robb honors his apology.** He dances with the Frey daughters and granddaughters including **Alyx, Fair Walda, Fat Walda, Ami, Serra, Sarra and Shirei**, and with Roslin and Lord Walder's eighth wife. He does so in obedience to Walder's stated terms. The host's later betrayal is not justified by an incomplete apology.
5. **Several loyal northerners stand unarmed or nearly so.** A customary removal of **swordbelts to pegs on the wall** leaves men at a severe disadvantage. **Smalljon Umber, Robin Flint, Patrek Mallister and Dacey Mormont** are assigned the king's guards and remain sober; **Greatjon** and other guests become drunk. **Precise defense arrangements are on page; there is no fully protected royal battle formation inside the hall.**
6. **Roose is conspicuously sober.** Bolton drinks only **hippocras** and eats little. His wife **Fat Walda** explains that the dowry by bride's weight favored her over Fair Walda. Bolton pointedly toasts **the young Frey wards at the Dreadfort** under Ramsay's control, which Catelyn reads as an implied message to Walder. **His private pact is not directly dictated in this scene**, but his conduct and subsequent location are high-priority evidence.
7. **Grey Wind is deliberately banned from the wedding hall.** Walder uses the wolf's earlier lunge at Petyr Pimple ([ASOS 49 Catelyn](49-catelyn.md)) as justification, insisting guests and children must be safe. Robb yields though angered. Catelyn wishes Grey Wind were near him when the dogs fight over food; **an armed animal protector is absent when needed**.
8. **The feast has deliberate separated populations.** A second celebration across the river houses lesser-born guests and Frey bastards, while common soldiers drink in three outdoor feast tents. Walder's arrangements distinguish the king and lords from their followers; the distribution becomes causally critical in [ASOS 52 Arya](52-arya.md).
9. **Some possible dissenters have been removed.** Robb asks why his former loyal squire **Olyvar Frey** is absent; Ryman says Olyvar is gone on duty. Catelyn asks about **Alesander Frey**, cousin and singer, and is told he too is away. She recalls **Perwyn** was absent from her earlier visit to Maester Brenett. **All three disappearances are corroborated family absences**, not direct proof of their beliefs or of who excluded them.
10. **The bedding procession removes Edmure and many wedding guests.** Walder announces it is time for bedding; the crowds carry **Edmure and Roslin** toward their chamber, with Greatjon shouldering the tearful bride. Crucially **Edmure is physically separated from Robb, Catelyn and the imminent hall massacre**. The chapter does not show him murdered after he leaves.
11. **The hall contains conspicuous residual vulnerability.** Some Freys remain asleep at the tables (**Petyr Pimple, Ser Whalen**), **Merrett** drinks, **Wendel Manderly** eats lamb, and **Jinglebell** wanders. **Robb remains in the hall** rather than joining the bedding party. The continued presence of individual Freys, some apparently unaware, is a warning against labeling all members of a house active collaborators.
12. **Edwyn Frey betrays readiness by recoil.** When **Dacey** touches Edwyn to request a dance, he jerks away violently and curtly refuses. Catelyn senses the behavior does not fit normal celebration; her suspicion sharpens into a specific **testable material observation** as she follows him.
13. **The musicians switch to 'The Rains of Castamere'.** The song follows an extended, deliberately discordant entertainment and begins while Edwyn attempts to leave. Its association with **Tywin's destruction of the Reynes** becomes a pointed Lannister signal. This is **a performed song at the scene**, not proof every combatant knew its lyrics beforehand.
14. **Concealed armor confirms prearranged preparation.** Catelyn grabs Edwyn's arm and feels **iron mail beneath his silk sleeve**, then strikes him. This is decisive observable evidence of intentional combat preparation concealed under wedding clothes before the attack began.
15. **Crossbowmen emerge among the gallery musicians.** **Robb is hit first by quarrels in the side and leg**, later shown with a third in the chest; Catelyn is struck in the **lower back** as she tries to reach him. The music and drums suppress screams and commands, converting celebration into **an organized ambush**.
16. **Robb's guards intervene at great cost.** **Smalljon Umber** overturns a table to shield Robb from bolts, strikes **Ser Raymund Frey** with a meat joint and is himself wounded. **Robin Flint** is surrounded and stabbed. **Wendel Manderly** is shot through the mouth as he rises with lamb in hand. Individual defenses occur but cannot defeat entrenched archers and incoming armored men.
17. **Named victims and assailants are captured.** Catelyn sees **Ser Hosteen Frey** kill **Lucas Blackwood**, **Black Walder Frey** hamstring a Vance, and archers shoot **Donnel Locke** and **Owen Norrey**. **Dacey Mormont** strikes **Benfrey Frey** with a wine flagon and heads for the door. The identity and destiny of each victim should not be inferred from faction alone.
18. **Ser Ryman closes the escape route.** Ryman enters fully armored with **about a dozen Frey men carrying longaxes** and strikes **Dacey in the abdomen** with his axe. Armored men in **fur cloaks** also enter from other doors. Catelyn briefly assumes they are northmen coming to help, but one beheads **Smalljon**. **The presence of someone wearing northern-style clothing is not proof of loyalty to Robb**.
19. **Walder directs the massacre from his chair.** The elderly lord watches; later he raises his hand and most music stops, leaving a single funereal drum. He taunts Robb with a grotesque **'apology'** for slaughtered men, directly mocking the earlier reconciliation. **The hospitality breach is observable and intentional**, not chaotic fighting sparked by an insult.
20. **Grey Wind is still alive and audible to Catelyn during the hall slaughter.** She hears a distant **wolf howl** amid fighting outside. **This chapter does not show his death on page**; do not import a later animal outcome while citing this scene alone.
21. **Catelyn takes an innocent Frey hostage.** She seizes a dropped dagger, grabs **Aegon 'Jinglebell' Frey** by his hair, and presses the blade to his throat while offering to exchange his life for Robb's. Her desperate act targets a vulnerable adult whose own culpability has **not been demonstrated**.
22. **Catelyn proposes hostages and an end to vengeance.** She asks Walder to hold herself and Edmure rather than kill Robb and swears she will forgo retaliation. These are genuine attempted bargaining acts under extreme duress, even if Walder discounts them; the promise is not a settled truce.
23. **Walder refuses the exchange using family hierarchy.** He calls Jinglebell a **grandson**, not a son, and of little use. This refusal is directly witnessed and shows how an immense dynastic family can expend a vulnerable relative to secure the central political objective.
24. **Robb is killed by a man bearing Bolton colors.** A man wearing **dark armor and a pale pink cloak marked with blood** steps up to wounded Robb, invokes **Jaime Lannister** in his remark and **thrusts a sword into Robb's heart**. Earlier Roose wore the distinctive pale pink and departed before the attack; **the physical killing is witnessed, but the passage identifies the sword-bearer by apparel rather than spelling out his name at the instant**. Attribution to Roose is strongly supported; be precise about source wording.
25. **A quotation invoking Jaime is not proof Jaime ordered this.** The killer's invocation of Jaime could be a taunt, reference or intended signal; Jaime's actual communications and foreknowledge are **not established by this line**. Distinguish the phrase from Catelyn's earlier authorized release of Jaime and Roose's separate effort to secure Lannister favor.
26. **Catelyn kills Jinglebell.** After seeing Robb stabbed, she cuts his throat; the text explicitly identifies the blood and bells. **He has not been shown as a guilty conspirator**. This is an individual violent killing by a devastated mother, not a triumphant execution of Walder.
27. **Catelyn's last perceptions are disordered.** She feels as if **ravens tear at her face**, watches blood, thinks of her dead and lost children and Ned's fondness for her hair, and experiences laughter/screaming. These are **her dying perceptions**, not on-page objective evidence that literal ravens attacked the feast hall.
28. **Catelyn is killed by a blade across her throat.** Someone seizes her hair and slits her throat. This is a directly narrated fatal injury, not an ambiguous faint, temporary captivity or mere reported death.
29. **The principal historical result is irreversible.** **Robb and Catelyn die**, many bannermen are slaughtered, and guest right has been openly violated under Walder's protection. **Edmure's outcome, Roslin's private knowledge, Grey Wind's fate, the outdoor casualty toll and any surviving succession document remain unresolved inside this chapter.**

## Interpretive challenges and source tests

- **How premeditated was the Red Wedding?** Hidden armor, musician crossbows, synchronized entrances, the song change, selective attendance, guest separation and Walder's command establish **planned coordinated murder**; this is far stronger than merely prophetic symbolism from High Heart.
- **Who knew?** Walder, armed Frey participants and Bolton-colored swordsmen are demonstrably implicated. **Not every Frey** (Roslin, absent siblings, Jinglebell and younger children) can be assigned full knowledge.
- **Is the Hound or Jaime complicit?** Neither is present as a killer here. The invocation of Jaime's name is evidence of a message/taunt, not a witnessed order.
- **Does hospitality magic punish violations?** The cultural contract is explicitly broken. Any supernatural consequence is **not shown during the hall attack**; moral and political consequences are clear without invoking a curse.
- **Succession and survivors:** Distinguish the actual death of Robb from the status of his unseen will, missing Stark children, Edmure's custody, and surviving northern witnesses. Record each follow-up separately.
