## Chapter-spine chronology / source-grounded consequences

1. **Robb leaves Riverrun with Jeyne behind.** His farewell is repeated at the godswood, gate, and outside the castle when Jeyne rides after him. **Catelyn recommended Jeyne remain** because either her attendance or public insults by Walder Frey could undermine the alliance. The visible reluctance, love, and Robb's concealed irritation are partly Catelyn's interpretation, not a confession from either spouse.
2. **The Westerling party is deliberately split.** Only **Ser Raynald Westerling** travels as Robb's banner-bearer. **Jeyne, Eleyna, Rollam and Sybell Westerling** remain at Riverrun. **Rolph Spicer** takes young captive **Martyn Lannister** toward the Golden Tooth after Tywin consents to exchange, while news says **Robett Glover** was put aboard ship at Duskendale. This is an ongoing **exchange mission**, not a witnessed arrival by Robett at Riverrun. The separation of wife, squire, mother-in-law and uncle is potentially consequential, but intentional conspiracy is not proved here.
3. **The Wolf is back at Robb's side.** **Grey Wind** resumes accompanying Robb and the marching army. This contrasts with the earlier Westerling household discomfort and with future safety questions; neither Catelyn nor the narrative yet identifies a fixed supernatural threat.
4. **Catelyn notes reasons to fear Walder.** He holds old grudges and Lord Hoster did not trust him after the Trident. **Lothar Frey** openly compliments Jeyne's loving heart and gives a sentimental account of Roslin's impatience for the marriage; Catelyn worries polite speech may mask Walder's humiliating demands. The hospitality terms are **not yet tested by an actual ceremony** in this chapter.
5. **Robb's field force is small but veteran.** The marching column consists of **approximately 3,500 battle-hardened soldiers**, with **Greatjon** in the van, **Galbart Glover** directing scouts, **Wendel Manderly** guarding stores, wedding gifts, wounded and baggage, and **Robin Flint** the rear. Riverlords mostly hold their own lands. The 3,500 figure must not be confused with Robb's projected **more than 12,000 after joining Bolton/Freys**.
6. **Brynden remains behind to defend Riverrun.** Robb appoints the Blackfish **Warden of the Southern Marches**. Catelyn misses his counsel and credits him with earlier victories. **Galbart succeeds him in scouting**, but Catelyn considers the substitution less brilliant.
7. **Rain constrains the campaign.** Long storms turn roads to mud, flood waterways, slow infantry and baggage, and degrade morale. Catelyn repeatedly associates the rain with political and personal doom; those feelings are **her subjective response**, not supernatural evidence that rainfall is cursed.
8. **The Mormonts defend their unusual gender and military roles.** **Maege and Dacey Mormont** speak warmly to Catelyn despite her releasing Jaime, and describe Bear Island women defending against ironborn and wildling raids while men fish. Their gate allegedly depicts a **nursing mother wielding an axe**. Dacey reports fighting beside Robb in every battle. This is clan-specific history, not a statement that all northern women follow one custom.
9. **Lynesse Hightower adds an illuminating counterstory.** Catelyn recalls Jorah's second wife visiting Winterfell and privately confessing misery on Bear Island. Maege and Dacey remember her disliking the axe-bearing mother carving. This is first-person recollection from Catelyn plus their character judgments, providing a non-Jorah viewpoint on his failed marriage, not a definitive account of why he entered exile.
10. **Edmure fears his unseen bride, creating household friction.** He asks whether Roslin resembles Walder Frey; Catelyn sharply reminds him beauty alone is no virtue. She later regrets her severity and recalls her own initially dutiful marriage to **Eddard, whom she had expected to resemble Brandon**. Her memory establishes the duty-to-love trajectory of that marriage, not a prediction that Edmure's will be similar.
11. **The army revisits the Whispering Wood.** Catelyn sees cairns, exposed skulls and scavenged battle remains where Robb captured Jaime. She recalls that Ned and the boys were alive and Theon still an ally then. The older battlefield evokes her uncertainty about whether **Hallis Mollen and the silent sisters ever carried Ned's bones beyond Moat Cailin**. This remains unresolved.
12. **Bridges are physically washed away.** **Fairmarket bridge** over the Blue Fork and the older crossing by **Oldstones** are destroyed. Galbart nearly drowns testing a swim at Ramsford; a rider and horses are lost. The army must detour through **Sevenstreams and Hag's Mire**, delaying arrival at Walder's wedding. The river's impact is a proven cause of delay, independent of any intentional lateness.
13. **The route forces the army through Oldstones.** Robb recalls the song about **Jenny of Oldstones**; Catelyn recalls playing Jenny there as a child while **Petyr Baelish pretended to be the Prince of Dragonflies**. The childhood masquerade is a genuinely recorded link to High Heart's Jenny song ([ASOS 43 Arya](43-arya.md)); it does **not** prove Catelyn or Littlefinger knew the historical Jenny.
14. **The ruined grave is a Mudd dynasty clue.** Catelyn identifies the stone tomb as **King Tristifer IV Mudd, Hammer of Justice**, who allegedly won **99 of 100 battles** before seven Andal kings defeated him and his weaker heir **Tristifer V** lost the dynasty. This is her recounting historical tradition; numbers and genealogy are not independently verified by the eroded monument. The imagery of failed succession directly prompts Robb's concern.
15. **Robb and Jeyne have no confirmed child yet.** Robb explicitly says they tried to conceive repeatedly, but he cannot establish pregnancy. Catelyn says conception need not occur immediately. **Do not report Jeyne either pregnant or definitively infertile** from this chapter.
16. **The succession danger is openly named.** Under Robb's belief Bran and Rickon are dead, **Sansa** would legally inherit should Robb die without children, putting Winterfell within Tyrion's marital reach. Catelyn considers remote **Stark–Royce descendants in the Vale** (Waynwood, Corbray, perhaps Templeton). These are genealogical suggestions with uncertainty about the youngest daughter's match.
17. **Robb proposes Jon as successor.** Robb believes Jon is the closest remaining brother, says **a royal decree can legitimize a bastard**, and considers sending **100 men to the Night's Watch** in exchange for Jon's release from vows. He invokes Lannister removal of **Barristan Selmy and Boros Blount** from the Kingsguard as precedent for relaxing lifetime service. **Both release and legal legitimation are proposals**, not completed acts at the tomb.
18. **Catelyn opposes legitimating Jon on dynastic grounds.** She cites **Aegon IV's Great Bastards and the Blackfyre rebellions** to argue that the legitimate sons of Jeyne might one day face Jon's descendants; she also distrusts Jon in the wake of Theon's perceived betrayal. The analogy demonstrates her fear, **not proof Jon would harm any child or that his descendants must rebel**.
19. **Robb refuses to equate Jon with Theon.** His repeated statement that Jon is a true brother and Theon a betrayer shows his personal assessment; the reader knows the reported murders of **Bran and Rickon are false**, and Arya is alive. Catelyn and Robb are choosing an heir under **materially mistaken information**.
20. **A separate succession document is prepared later.** At the concluding council Robb directs his lords to **affix seals to a document witnessing his decision about who shall succeed him**. **CRITICAL SOURCE BOUNDARY:** the chapter makes Jon the clear **intended candidate from the preceding argument**, but **does not reproduce the document or pronounce its actual operative language, list its seals, or establish its later location**. Record as **INTENDED / DOCUMENT EXECUTED OR TO BE SEALED, CONTENT UNSEEN**, not incontrovertibly a present surviving decree of Jon's legitimization.
21. **A ship captain provides the first substantial news of Balon's death to Robb's camp.** **Jason Mallister** escorts an unnamed captain of the **Myraham**, a merchant vessel from Oldtown that had been detained **over six months at Lordsport on Pyke**. The captain says Balon was caught in a storm and a **bridge at Pyke broke**, with his body washing ashore two days later. This is **reported local news from someone who heard it at Lordsport**, not Balon's death directly witnessed by Catelyn or the captain.
22. **Euron's return is also reported, not directly seen.** The captain describes **Euron Crow's Eye** arriving unexpectedly aboard **Silence, black sails and a red hull, crewed by mutes**, taking the **Seastone Chair** and drowning **Lord Botley** for objecting. He has only **heard** Euron visited Asshai and elsewhere; voyages and exact scheduling must not be elevated above testimony.
23. **The death news connects to recent prophecy but not automatic causation.** The Ghost of High Heart proclaimed a dead wet kraken king in [ASOS 43 Arya](43-arya.md); Stannis burned a blood leech naming Balon in [ASOS 36 Davos](36-davos.md). A ship captain's description of storm and bridge provides **a mundane proximate mechanism** and no evidence the leech caused it, or that Euron arranged it.
24. **Robb expects competing Greyjoy claimants to weaken Moat Cailin.** He notes **Theon** would be lawful heir if alive, **Victarion** commands the Iron Fleet, and **Asha** occupies Deepwood Motte; he expects them to sail for succession politics, freeing men from northern garrisons. **Each predicted withdrawal is a contingency, not an observed departure.**
25. **Jason's fleet is small.** Seagard can contribute **six longships and two war galleys** and cannot defeat the Iron Fleet. Robb requests **two longships** to sail toward **Greywater Watch** via the changing waterways of the Neck. Jason doubts the ships can locate a **moving stronghold**, but Robb expects crannogmen to find ships flying Stark banners. **The move of Greywater Watch is reported as received geographic knowledge, not directly watched in this POV**.
26. **Maege and Galbart carry a covert plan to Howland Reed.** They travel on separate ships, increasing delivery chance, with **decoy written commands** in case intercepted and real instructions to seek **Howland's guides**, who know unmapped paths and channels. The content of their verbal mission is discussed in the tent; actual arrival and coordination are not yet shown.
27. **Robb proposes a three-direction assault on Moat Cailin.** After joining Frey and Bolton troops for an estimated **more than 12,000**, he would march three columns a half-day apart up the causeway; **Greatjon** commands a fierce van, **Bolton** the rear, Robb center. **Two** columns should attack openly, while Robb's own center secretly **disappears into the Neck** to emerge at the Fever, then attacks north/west with Reed support. He aims for the **first day of a new century** following a night when ironborn might drink heavily.
28. **The plan depends on unknown loyalties and weather.** It requires ironborn leaders to leave garrisons, crannogmen to locate the two ships and meet Robb two days after starting, the Freys/Bolton army to join intact, and movement through the Neck. The plan is genuinely inventive but **has not been executed** in this chapter.
29. **Robb orders Catelyn to Seagard after the wedding.** He frames the separation of mother and wife as avoiding keeping all family 'treasures' in one location, and invokes royal command despite her objections. Catelyn feels held as an honored prisoner; **this is his stated protective strategy**, not proof he intends to punish her for opposing Jon.
30. **End position:** The company is still moving toward the Twins; neither wedding nor Moat assault has occurred. Jeyne stays in Riverrun. Robb has clearly expressed an intent to secure Jon's succession, sought lords' signatures on an unseen document, and laid out a high-risk multi-front invasion dependent on possible Greyjoy fracture.

## Research questions, countercases

- **What did Robb's will actually say?** Locate any later surviving copy, witness, or confession; distinguish who saw sealed orders from who merely heard Robb debate Jon. Confirm whether release from vows was attempted or just planned.
- **How should Winterfell succession account for alive-but-unknown Bran, Rickon and Arya?** Robb's plan is rational within his mistaken intelligence. Reader knowledge does not automatically inform him or retroactively invalidate signed documents without legal evidence.
- **Why did Greyjoy succession unfold now?** Storm damage offers an immediate account, while Euron's uncanny timing and Melisandre's rite raise hypotheses. Test dated independent eyewitness reports rather than assuming sorcery or assassination.
- **What happens to covert Howland channels?** Maege/Galbart are significant **surviving command witnesses and couriers** if they successfully depart and remain alive. Their messages deliberately avoid revealing real instructions if taken.
- **Walder and Bolton politics:** Their promised military contribution is essential, so earlier conflicting signals of betrayal are major risks; nothing in the meeting confirms Robb anticipated an impending betrayal.
