# ASOS Epilogue — Merrett Frey: Red Wedding logistics, Frey succession tensions, and the return of Catelyn Stark

- Narrator: **Merrett Frey**, a participant in the Red Wedding's preparations. This is **the actual narrative epilogue**, not author back matter.

## Chronological and causal close reading

1. **The Riverlands face weather and agricultural disaster.** A small, unusually early **autumn snowfall** covers **Oldstones**; Merrett notes two full harvests and part of a third lost through flooding, warfare, fire and raids. Famine threatens ordinary residents. These are his local observations and projections, not a quantified region-wide food census.
2. **Oldstones is a meaningful former royal site.** The overgrown hill and broken circuit of walls mark the ancient castle of **Tristifer Mudd**, called the Hammer of Justice by a singer. The path curves twice around the hill. References to early river kings matter for later competing legitimacy claims, but **this chapter does not identify a revived Mudd dynasty**.
3. **Merrett's mission is ransom, not battle.** He carries **100 gold dragons** and wants to retrieve a younger relation known as **Petyr Pimple**, abducted by Brotherhood outlaws after pursuing a camp follower. The term 'nephew' is simplified within complicated Frey family relationships; Merrett himself says the relationship is more distant.
4. **Merrett is physically impaired and drinks heavily.** A childhood head injury causes debilitating headaches; he admits having already consumed ale and wine before riding despite calling himself sober. He repeatedly considers running away with the ransom and losing himself in ale. His interior voice is self-pitying, but he is still an informed witness to the Frey operation.
5. **His reputation at the Twins offers little security.** Merrett is **Lord Walder Frey's ninth son**, approaching forty, without independent land or wealth, even the horse he rides belonging to the house. He fears being dismissed after Walder's death. This explains why he risks a dangerous ransom trip for family favor.
6. **Walder's succession is a looming internal crisis.** Walder is approaching **ninety-two**, nearly blind, painfully gouty and reliant on servants to move; Merrett expects his death soon, though this is **his prediction**. Walder protects even disliked descendants in ways an heir might not.
7. **The senior Frey succession line is specified.** **Stevron** died while on Robb's western campaign. The anticipated heir is now his son **Ryman Frey**, followed by **Edwyn and Black Walder**. Merrett describes mutual hostility among these men and says **Lame Lothar** might be still more dangerous. **Character judgments and stories of infidelity are hearsay or interested assessments**, not independently confirmed acts.
8. **Merrett's personal history intersects Jaime's early career.** He trained under **Lord Sumner Crakehall** as a page/squire while **Jaime Lannister** gained renown against the Kingswood Brotherhood. Merrett was captured by **Wenda the White Fawn**, branded on the buttock, ransomed and then struck by a mace that shattered his helm; he lay unconscious about **a fortnight**. The blow produced persistent disability and ended prospects for knighthood.
9. **His marriage has a dynastic backstory.** Merrett wed a **daughter of House Darry**, once favored under Aerys, whose influence declined after the rebellion. Merrett's portrayal of her and their children is hostile and unreliable regarding their worth.
10. **Amerei 'Gatehouse Ami' was married to Pate, who died confronting Gregor.** Merrett says his eldest daughter was hastily wed to a hedge knight, then widowed after **Ser Pate fought Gregor Clegane**. These are family reports, consistent with Riverlands violence but not viewed by Merrett on this ride.
11. **Walda's Bolton marriage was chosen by body weight, according to Walder.** Merrett had hoped his daughter **Walda's marriage to Roose Bolton** secured him gratitude; Walder said Roose selected her because of the size-based dowry arrangement. This is **Walder's remark as remembered by Merrett**, not Roose's own explicit motive in this scene.
12. **Lame Lothar planned the Red Wedding in specific operational detail.** Merrett remembers **Lord Walder ordering the massacre**, while **Lothar coordinated it with Roose Bolton**, including the **songs timed to the attack**. This supplies a named organizer and an explicit command chain beyond broad Frey complicity.
13. **Merrett had a precise limited assignment.** Lothar charged him with getting **Greatjon Umber as drunk as possible**, to suppress his capacity to resist. Merrett carried out the drinking effort, though Greatjon still fought ferociously.
14. **Greatjon's capture was extremely costly.** Even drunk, he seized a sword, broke an attacker's arm, then fought **eight men** before chains subdued him. The struggle left **two wounded, one dead and Ser Leslyn Haigh missing part of an ear**. This is Merrett's participant account, not independent battlefield casualty bookkeeping.
15. **Bolton and Frey violence was prepared across multiple locations.** Merrett later tries to shift responsibility to named organizers: **Lothar rigged the tents to collapse and placed crossbowmen in a gallery with the musicians**, and **Bastard Walder led the camp attack**. He attributes larger command to **Lord Walder, Ryman and Roose Bolton**. These are specific, falsifiable mechanisms and assignments.
16. **The Red Wedding caused Frey casualties too.** Merrett claims **about fifty Frey men died in camps**, including named retainers and kin, and says **Grey Wind killed four hounds and tore away a kennelmaster's arm even after being shot repeatedly**. These are **interested numbers from a perpetrator**, not exoneration for a massacre under guest right.
17. **Merrett's personal defense is narrow but not wholly empty.** He says he merely supplied wine to Greatjon, and did not personally fire the arrows or cut Robb's throat. His own memories confirm he **knowingly participated in rendering a major defender vulnerable**. This is an example of compartmentalized conspiracy, not proof of nonparticipation.
18. **The Frey accounts of guest-right violation compete with retaliation claims.** Merrett says Robb shamed Walder and that the Freys were entitled to vengeance; outlaws call the Red Wedding **murder**. Both sides acknowledge actual killing, but lawful vengeance is Merrett's **attempted justification**, not accepted moral/legal proof.
19. **The ransom party meets a singer in green sitting on an old king's sepulcher.** He recognizes Merrett from a wedding performance and says he is kin to Merrett's son-in-law Pate through **Sevenstreams**. The singer's identity is not spelled out as a name within this scene; do not replace it silently with a later identification.
20. **Old Brotherhood-associated figures control the location.** A tall, broken-nosed man in a **patched yellow cloak** is later addressed as **Lem**, while a **one-eyed outlaw** is called **Jack**, and the singer acts as inquisitor. There are **roughly a dozen to a score** outlaws, plus a hooded woman.
21. **The ransom is taken but the captive has already been killed.** The outlaws seize the **100 dragons** and lead Merrett to **Petyr Pimple's corpse hanged in Oldstones' godswood**. The promise that payment by sunset would save the hostage is revealed as a lie, not a failed rescue due to Merrett's lateness.
22. **The outlaw song is identifiable and meaningful.** The singer first performs **Jenny's song** at the ancient king's tomb, later **'The Day They Hanged Black Robin'** while preparing the execution. Their music frames memorialization and killing, without proving that an older prophecy dictated their target.
23. **The outlaws ask about Sandor Clegane and a child.** They have questioned **Trident ferrymen** and a robbed traveler, and believe Sandor headed toward the Twins with a **thin girl about ten**. This corroborates other reports of the pair's movement but does **not reveal Sandor's survival or present location after Arya left him**.
24. **Merrett cannot identify Arya's presence at the wedding.** He denies seeing the Hound in the main feast and allows that the outlying camps could have held him. He is **ignorant of the true Arya's near-presence**, just as court sources have accepted a counterfeit.
25. **The singer manipulates an apparent promise of release.** He offers to ask that Merrett be freed after answering about Sandor, but Lem refuses; the exchange is an intentional verbal trap. This is evidence the Brotherhood itself now uses calculated false assurances, unlike any claim of pure impartial justice.
26. **Merrett seeks refuge in Beric's reputation for trials.** He says no eyewitness can prove his own role, and asks for Lord Beric to judge. The outlaws mock the idea that a title can shield him; **Beric himself is not directly seen in this epilogue**.
27. **The Brotherhood reveals its decisive witness: Catelyn Stark.** The hooded woman removes her covering and Merrett recognizes **Lady Catelyn**, whose death at the Twins he **personally saw**, and whose corpse was later stripped and thrown into the river. This independently corroborates the corpse identified in **Arya's ASOS 65 wolf-dream**.
28. **Merrett names Catelyn's immediate killer.** He recalls his brother **Raymund** cutting her throat from ear to ear. This provides a named assassin beyond the anonymous action previously seen in Catelyn's own final POV.
29. **Catelyn's body is physically altered, not restored to ordinary health.** Merrett sees water-damaged pale skin, **partly lost/whitened hair, scratches she inflicted on her own face**, and the concealed slit in her throat. She cannot speak intelligibly, according to Lem, but **she recognizes Merrett and communicates by nodding**. This is directly observed **animated or revived Catelyn**, not merely an allegorical resemblance.
30. **Catelyn authorizes Merrett's execution.** Asked whether he was involved, she **nods**. The outlaws hoist Merrett on a noose, and the chapter closes with him **hanging and struggling**. Death is overwhelmingly implied, but there is no subsequent independent body examination or account of who precisely completed the hanging.
31. **Her revivification method is not narrated.** No ritual, healer, intermediary or length of restoration is revealed here. **Do not attribute the act to a particular person or divine power at the ASOS epilogue reading boundary**, even though the fact of her moving and acting afterward is established.
32. **A factional transformation of the Brotherhood is observable.** An organization previously associated with Beric and trials now ransoms victims under knowingly false assurances and executes alleged guest-right offenders at Catelyn's decision. This is **real changed conduct**, not yet evidence that every original member approved or that the faction is identical in membership.
33. **End state:** The Red Wedding's operational chain is better documented; two Freys are hanged in one action; a revived but severely mutilated Catelyn commands anti-Frey vengeance; **Merrett's family expects him home but does not know his fate on-page**. ASOS closes on revenge, incomplete justice and an altered witness to the original massacre.

## Theory priorities

- **Stoneheart identity:** The evidence proves Catelyn's continuing agency after actual death, but **the mechanism, cost and extent of prior identity retained** are not shown. Resist importing later AFFC descriptions to this earlier point without cross-book citation.
- **Guest-right conspiracy:** Merrett is a rare insider for precise assignments: compare Lothar's song/ambush schedule, Bastard Walder's camps and Greatjon's deliberate intoxication to independent accounts.
- **Frey succession:** Walder's impending death may provoke catastrophic internal conflict, but it has **not happened here**. Ryman, Edwyn, Black Walder, Lothar and their cousins remain structurally important.
- **Outlaw ethics:** False ransom and execution are confirmed. Compare the Brotherhood's prior avowed justice with the punitive faction operating under Catelyn.
