## Evidence-first chronology

1. **Tyrion weighs forced confession against the Wall.** He might save his life by admitting to a murder he says he did not commit, but doing so would void his marriage and possible inheritance and stain him as a kinslayer. He doubts trusting Tywin. He thanks **Podrick Payne** for loyalty even when Pod cannot answer confidently whether Tyrion is guilty.
2. **The prosecution's last witness is Shae.** She claims Tyrion and **Sansa** conspired after Robb died, that Tyrion planned to kill Cersei, Tywin and Tommen, and that Shae learned this as Sansa's maid and Tyrion's lover. **No independent confirmation of such a murder plot is produced**; Tyrion's direct thoughts and scenes refute major allegations.
3. **Shae tells a fabricated coercion origin.** She says she was engaged to a noble squire Tyrion sent to die at the Green Fork, then brought to his tent by **Shagga and Timett**, forced to be his sexual servant. These claims conflict with the reader's earlier firsthand circumstances of their relationship. She also exposes their real sexual encounter **in the dragon-skull chamber on wedding morning** while adding asserted abuse and royal-coup dialogue not independently witnessed.
4. **Court ridicule becomes a breaking point.** Shae reports being made to call Tyrion her **'giant of Lannister'**; the court laughs. The private phrase has been weaponized, but **the narrative does not give Shae an internal POV explaining her actual threats, incentives or coercion**, and Tyrion only speculates about what Cersei promised.
5. **Tyrion's apparent confession is rhetorical and expressly not to murder.** He demands Shae be removed, says he is guilty, then specifies he is guilty of **being born a dwarf** and of wishing the murderous accusations against him had substance; he explicitly says **he did not kill Joffrey**. This distinction is essential: **no actual regicide confession occurs**.
6. **Tyrion invokes a valid trial-by-battle right.** Cersei immediately names **Gregor Clegane**, returned to the capital. **Oberyn Martell rises and publicly volunteers as Tyrion's champion**, completing the offer made in ASOS 66. Tywin schedules combat for the next day and angrily departs.
7. **Tyrion perceives political fallout in either result.** An Oberyn victory would outrage Mace Tyrell; Oberyn's death could inflame **Doran Martell** and reinforce a **Myrcella-over-Tommen** succession alternative. Those are **Tyrion's predictions**, not results yet.
8. **Oberyn fights with a distinctive long spear.** His weapon is approximately **eight feet of ash**, its final **two feet leaf-shaped steel**, with dark glistening residue on the edge when he spins it. Tyrion asks himself whether the sheen is **oil or poison** but does not physically test the coating. Do not claim poison is clinically verified in this chapter.
9. **Oberyn explains the armor-gap strategy.** He plans to exhaust Gregor and thrust at vulnerable **joints beneath arms, inside elbows and knees**. His long reach offsets Gregor's huge sword, while Oberyn chooses lighter armor and an open-faced helm for mobility and vision.
10. **The champions' heraldry is deliberately manipulated.** Gregor's enormous shield has **his Clegane hounds painted over with the seven-pointed star**, although the hounds gradually reemerge when Oberyn damages the paint. Oberyn's gear carries the **Martell sun and spear**, and his helmet visor is removed. The surface star is **Cersei's display of righteousness, not divine endorsement**.
11. **Oberyn supplies long-delayed Martell–Lannister marriage history.** He recalls visiting **Starfall, the Arbor, Oldtown, Shield Islands, Crakehall and Casterly Rock** with Elia and their mother. He claims the two mothers, who had served **Princess Rhaella**, contemplated **Jaime–Elia and/or Cersei–Oberyn matches**; Tywin offered young **Tyrion** as Elia's match and reserved Cersei for Rhaegar. This is **Oberyn's familial recollection, including a deathbed account from his mother**, not surviving betrothal contracts.
12. **A trivial nickname may have altered Elia's marriage route.** Oberyn remembers Elia had liked **Baelor Hightower**, but Oberyn mocked him as **Breakwind** after an embarrassing incident and she could no longer regard him solemnly. Tyrion privately considers the historical contingency. It is **an anecdote**, not proof Elia would otherwise have married Hightower.
13. **Oberyn explicitly seeks more than a combat victory.** His repeated accusation against Gregor demands confirmation about **Elia's rape and murder, baby Aegon's murder and the two children's deaths**, and he implicitly also seeks exposure of **Tywin's command responsibility**. His obsession with confession becomes a tactical liability.
14. **The fight begins with spear reach and constant motion.** Oberyn circles, jabs and feints, while Gregor's heavy armor absorbs some thrusts and his narrow visor obstructs his sight. Oberyn's superior mobility and long spear gradually tire the Mountain. At first **no penetrating wound** is observed, though his surcoat and shield are battered.
15. **A spectator is murdered during a charge.** After a bystander stumbles behind Oberyn, Gregor cuts off the **stableboy's arm**, then deliberately strikes again and shears away part of his head. This is a **second intentional lethal blow**, independent of any lawful combat between champions.
16. **Sunlight is turned into a weapon.** When clouds part, Gregor first maneuvers to get the sun behind him, but Oberyn angles his polished shield to flash sunlight into the greathelm's narrow slit, creating an opening for a strike.
17. **Oberyn severely wounds and floors Gregor.** His spear penetrates **under Gregor's arm**, then **behind his knee**; Gregor falls and drops his sword. Oberyn next drives the spear through the fallen warrior's torso so hard the shaft **snaps**, pinning him to the ground with a broken length protruding. Gregor is **gravely injured but alive at this moment**.
18. **Oberyn fails to end the fight before extracting a confession.** He discards his shield, takes Gregor's greatsword and approaches closely, demanding that he name Elia. Despite a clear opportunity to finish the wounded adversary, he stays in dangerous grappling range. **His emotional objective of public confession clashes with immediate survival**.
19. **Gregor delivers direct firsthand admission.** As he grabs Oberyn's knee and pulls him down, Gregor **confesses that he killed Elia's infant and that he raped and killed Elia**, specifying her death as a smashed head. This is **the perpetrator's on-page spoken admission in public**, not only Tywin's earlier hearsay. It does **not name the person who issued Gregor's orders or demonstrate that Gregor personally killed Rhaenys**.
20. **Gregor kills Oberyn.** He thrusts steel fingers into Oberyn's face, smashes his mouth, and finally crushes his head with a mailed fist. **Oberyn's death is witnessed**, unlike Gregor's long-term fate, which remains unresolved because Gregor is still pierced and bleeding at chapter end.
21. **Tyrion is apparently judged defeated.** Nauseated, he **does not hear Tywin speak any sentence**. Guards take him away through the castle, and he recognizes he is being transferred **toward the black cells rather than his former tower room**. The trial-by-combat outcome is catastrophic; **the precise words of legal judgment are not in his hearing**.
22. **End state:** Oberyn dead, Gregor desperately injured but **not confirmed dead**, Tyrion moved to the black cells, and Gregor's confession establishes personal responsibility for Elia and Aegon's murders. **No independent laboratory evidence of the spear coating or conclusive royal poisoning culprit is supplied in this chapter**.

## Theory tests

- **Does poison on Oberyn's spear explain later Gregor illness?** The black sheen is suggestive, and a subsequent medical report is required to make a stronger inference. Do not infer the recipe, dose or mechanism solely from the sheen.
- **Does the duel settle Elia responsibility?** It establishes **Gregor's personal crimes through confession**. It does not by itself prove the exact Tywin order, notwithstanding strong strategic evidence from ASOS 53.
- **What is Tyrion guilty of?** Whatever the court declares, this chapter presents no reliable eyewitness administration of Joffrey's poison by Tyrion; the final testimony materially relied on damaging false context.
- **Can political performance undo justice?** Oberyn's decision to extract theatrical confession instead of finishing Gregor supplies a directly observed mechanism for his death.
