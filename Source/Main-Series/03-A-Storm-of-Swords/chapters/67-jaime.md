## Chapter-spine chronology and evidence interpretation

1. **Jaime assumes the living working responsibilities of Lord Commander.** He relocates to the top of **White Sword Tower**, a four-story tower facing the bay; its first-floor **Round Room** holds the White Book, armory beneath and brothers' chambers above. His new white winter clothing hangs loosely, and a sword worn on the **right hip for his left hand** proves awkward to draw. This is **a functional and identity crisis**, not restoration of swordsmanship.
2. **Jaime has attended Tyrion's ongoing trial.** He stayed toward the rear and has not been recognized by Tyrion, or many at court. His son is dead, Tywin has verbally disowned him, and Cersei has avoided private intimacy since the sept. These facts complicate any description of Jaime's return as uncomplicated reconciliation.
3. **The White Book is an unusually explicit institutional archive.** Called the **Book of the Brothers**, the massive white-leather volume with gold hinges gives each Kingsguard knight his original heraldic shield, dated service and deeds, and an empty white shield. Septons from the Great Sept add heraldry and decoration **three times yearly**, while Lord Commander maintains prose updates. The historical entries are **official records**, valuable but not necessarily exhaustive or impartial.
4. **The table is made from old weirwood.** Shaped as a great shield supported by three white stallions, it seats the Lord Commander at the head and three sworn brothers each side. Its material is noteworthy for the series' sacred tree imagery, but **no magical property is seen in this room**.
5. **The archive is behind on multiple obligations.** Jaime must record **Ser Mandon Moore and Ser Preston Greenfield's deaths**, **Sandor Clegane's brief white-cloak service**, and enter **Balon Swann, Osmund Kettleblack and Loras Tyrell**. A septon is needed to illuminate new arms; Jaime must **learn left-hand writing**, creating personal difficulty maintaining the record.
6. **Barristan Selmy recorded his own dismissal.** His book entry states he was expelled at age **61 by Joffrey for supposed age**. It details his childhood mystery-knight exploit at Blackhaven, knighting by **Aegon V**, killing **Maelys the Monstrous** in the War of the Ninepenny Kings, rescue of **Aerys at Duskendale**, victories over **Simon Toyne**, the **Smiling Knight** and other adversaries, injury at the Trident, pardon by Robert and older duties. **Specific regnal and biographical statements belong to an institutional life record**, not an omniscient eyewitness narrator.
7. **Selmy's defeat of Maelys is an important end-of-Blackfyre fact.** The official text explicitly calls Maelys the **last Blackfyre Pretender** and credits Selmy with killing him in single combat. Do not infer the extinction of every collateral Blackfyre descendant from the demise of a claimant bearing that title.
8. **Jaime's own entry is very brief.** It confirms he fought Kingswood outlaws as **Summer Crakehall's squire**, was **knighted at 15 by Arthur Dayne**, inducted into the Kingsguard at 15, killed **Aerys II at the Iron Throne**, was pardoned by Robert and won at the coronation marriage tourney. Its omissions make Jaime ashamed of a life not recorded through meaningful deeds.
9. **Jaime remembers a specific Kingswood engagement.** He helped save **Lord Sumner Crakehall** from **Big Belly Ben**, held his own against the **Smiling Knight**, and saw **Arthur Dayne with Dawn** stop to let the Smiling Knight obtain an unbroken sword before killing him. These are **Jaime's remembered personal experiences**, not newly corroborated records.
10. **Jaime acknowledges a dark comparison in his own ideals.** His youthful wish to be **Arthur Dayne** has turned into resemblance to the violent **Smiling Knight**, or so he now thinks. The judgment is internal self-reproach rather than a formal pardon or completed moral change.
11. **Jaime assembles only five of his six fellow brothers.** **Arys Oakheart** is in Dorne; **Boros Blount, Meryn Trant, Osmund Kettleblack, Balon Swann and Loras Tyrell** attend. In standard opening procedure Jaime verifies the child king's guards—**Osney, Osfryd and Garlan Tyrell**, none of whom is counted here among sworn Kingsguard except via brothers—before allowing the session to begin. **Tommen is treated as king but no crowning ceremony is witnessed in this room.**
12. **The meeting directly tests Joffrey poisoning accounts.** Meryn says Tyrion had access while filling the cup, Boros cites dumping the residue; **Balon Swann points out numerous other attendees, servants and movements near the dais while guests watched the pie and pigeons**, so the cup was **not continuously observed**. **The direct custody gap strengthens opportunities for other poisoners**, without proving who acted.
13. **Loras offers a different suspect and motive.** He suggests **Sansa** could have wanted both Joffrey and Margaery dead since Margaery also drank from the chalice, and cites Sansa's flight. The hypothesis is his **mistaken or uncertain interpretation**, not confirmation Sansa targeted the Tyrells.
14. **Jaime appoints Boros an involuntary poison taster.** Because Boros yielded Tommen to Tyrion's hired men and fails to protect dependents, Jaime orders him to sample **everything Tommen eats or drinks**. Boros protests and insults Jaime's missing hand; Jaime provokes him to draw, relying on the man's cowardice because **Jaime privately knows even Boros could kill him in his current condition**.
15. **Jaime interrogates Osmund's origin story.** Osmund claims to have fought in **Stepstones and the Disputed Lands** with the **Gallant Men** for Lys and Tyrosh and been knighted on a battlefield by **Ser Robert Stone**, now dead. Jaime cannot verify the knighting and suspects the name might be invented. This is **character testimony and investigative suspicion**, not independently proven false lineage.
16. **Meryn is warned to exercise judgment rather than blind obedience.** Jaime confronts past physical punishment of **Sansa** and orders Meryn to distinguish protecting a child king from executing a child's harmful impulses. Tommen is **eight years old** according to this exchange; protect him from himself rather than enforce cruelty in his name.
17. **Balon's family loyalties are examined.** Jaime notes **Donnel Swann** served Renly, then Stannis and then yielded to the Lannisters after being wounded; their father held Stonehelm rather than rising. Jaime challenges Balon with a scenario where kin fight the sworn king. Balon swears he **will not repeat Jaime's kingslaying**. This tests divided allegiance, but does not prove future disloyalty.
18. **Loras's confidence mirrors young Jaime.** Loras joined the Kingsguard at **17**, Jaime at **15**. Jaime sees the younger knight as his own arrogant former self and learns to approach his grief through Renly instead of insults.
19. **A major Blackwater mystery is explicitly resolved.** Loras reveals **Garlan Tyrell** wore **Renly's armor** to inspire or frighten Stannis's men; the armor was too large for Loras. **Littlefinger proposed the deception**, according to Loras. This distinguishes a **deliberate morale trick** from Renly literally returning as a ghost. It is not the same as the separate actual shadow killing of Renly.
20. **Renly's burial is private.** Loras says he buried Renly personally in a **secret place Renly had shown him near Storm's End**, protected from desecration. This is his direct testimony; the exact location remains unknown.
21. **Jaime asks for a fair inquiry into Brienne.** He asks Loras to assess the impossibility of **fighting a shadow**, and draws a comparison: Loras insists Brienne must have killed Renly because she was guarding him, but **Joffrey was killed despite five Kingsguard present**. Failure to prevent death alone does not prove assassination.
22. **Physical evidence in Renly's death challenges the accusation.** Loras reports **one clean stroke cut through Renly's best steel gorget**, which he himself could not reproduce even after testing with a sword. This strongly contradicts a conventional lone Brienne sword-killing narrative. Loras now admits uncertainty and grief-driven accusation.
23. **Loras implicitly recognizes he killed people who may have been innocent.** His anger led to killing **Ser Robar Royce and Ser Emmon Cuy**, two other sworn protectors, because they failed to prevent Renly's death. Jaime says he would have done the same, then internally identifies it as an easy lie; **Jaime has not similarly slain his own unprotective sworn brothers for Joffrey**.
24. **Renly's interpretation of Brienne has unusual moral force.** Loras recalls Renly found Brienne's knighthood aspirations odd, but distinguished her from petitioners for land and office because **she wanted only to give her life for him**. This is **Loras's memory of Renly's words**, not a Brienne POV confirming every motivation, but it materially weakens the 'scheming killer' theory.
25. **A concrete avenue for Brienne's release appears.** Jaime directs Loras to question her himself, then **accuse her or let her go based on fair evaluation**. Loras vows to do so, but **the actual interrogation and disposition are not yet shown in this chapter**.
26. **Jaime contemplates a golden prosthesis.** Remembering **Jacelyn Bywater's iron hand**, he thinks a golden artificial hand could improve public image and please Cersei. It is a **future plan**, not an existing working mechanical replacement.
27. **End state:** Jaime exercises Kingsguard command, challenges inherited accounts of Joffrey and Renly, strengthens Tommen's protection, and supplies a route for Brienne's reconsideration. **No new poisoner is identified, Brienne is still held, and Jaime's left-hand fighting and writing remain impaired**.

## Critical research questions

- **What can official books conceal?** The White Book emphasizes glory, appointment and public perception; Jaime's private memories and layered motives differ from compressed entries. Use it for dates and named events but verify politically loaded assessments with independent POVs.
- **Are Littlefinger's manipulations broader than the poisoning?** Loras independently names him as author of the **Garlan-as-Renly performance**, corroborating Petyr's fondness for directing theatrical confusion. This does not establish Petyr directed *every* apparent 'ghost'.
- **Renly's death:** Loras's testimony about the undamaged steel until one impossible cut aligns with Catelyn's account of a shadow. The two sources together materially exculpate Brienne from a conventional murder, while the metaphysics of shadow-birthing remain complex.
- **Jaime's morality:** His improved insistence on inquiry and refusal to blame Brienne contrasts with coercion, crude insults and willingness to bluff lethal harm. Character transformation is incomplete, not binary.
