## Narrative sequence and causal developments

1. **A DIRECT VIEW OF GIANTS, NOT JUST SONG:** Along the Milkwater in snow, Jon watches **giants riding mammoths in pairs**; he counts more than **fifty** before losing count and estimates **hundreds** across the column. They are roughly **10–14 feet tall by Jon's uncertain estimate**, heavily built, with long arms, splayed bare feet, body hair and poor, small eyes. Their weapons are mainly **tree-trunk clubs and huge stone-headed mauls**, not the swords and castles of Old Nan's tales. **Their exact numbers and stature remain estimates by a mounted observer in snowfall**.
2. **NAMED GIANT AND OLD TONGUE:** **Tormund Giantsbane** exchanges coarse jokes in the **Old Tongue** with a big grey-white giant riding an enormous matching mammoth. He identifies the giant as **Mag Mar Tun Doh Weg / Mag the Mighty**, and says giants have **no kings**. The actual presence, speech and responding laughter establish **intelligent nonhuman culture**, while Tormund's 'no kings' is his familiar political characterization.
3. **THE SONG OF JORAMUN VS OBSERVED CAUSALITY:** Seeing real giants prompts Jon to remember the legend that **Joramun's Horn of Winter awakened giants from the earth**. He repeatedly wants to ask Tormund about the title **Horn-Blower**, wondering if Mance found that horn during the still-unexplained Frostfang diggings. **Neither a magical horn, its possession by Tormund, an actual sounding, nor its efficacy is witnessed here**.
4. **TORMUND'S PUBLIC NAMES ARE PERFORMANCES:** Tormund claims an outlandish adventure of climbing into a giant woman's body in a storm and being nursed after she woke, then jokes that **Giantsbane is preferable to Giantsbabe**. Another tale makes him **Husband to Bears** via an unlikely misidentified she-bear. These are deliberately comic and contradictory **self-told stories**, not reliable biological testimony, magical shape-shifting, or proof he bedded a bear.
5. **JON'S CLOAKS TRACK DUAL IDENTITY:** Jon now wears a **warm unwashed sheepskin cloak given by Mance**, but keeps his **black Night's Watch cloak folded under his saddle**. The clothing is direct, specific evidence of **an external free-folk role and a retained private Watch identity**, consistent with Qhorin's infiltration instruction in [ACOK 68 Jon](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/68-jon.md).
6. **RATTleshirt LEFT BEHIND:** Mance recognizes Rattleshirt's hostility to Jon, so Jon shifts into **Tormund's group**. Ygritte and **Longspear Ryk** elect to follow him because free folk can change bands. Ygritte repeatedly sleeps near Jon, often pressing under his furs, while he uses **Ghost as a physical buffer** to avoid intimacy. Jon's hesitation springs from his Watch vow and fears of fathering a **bastard**, not dislike of her.
7. **DIFFERENT FAMILY NORMS:** Tormund laughs at the notion that sex obligates marriage and regards bastardy as no social disability; he says women may obtain **moon tea** if they do not want pregnancy. He believes Jon **'stole' Ygritte** by taking her captive and sparing her life in the Skirling Pass. These are **Tormund's interpretations of free-folk custom**, not evidence Ygritte has consented to every subsequent encounter or that pregnancy is cost-free.
8. **JON'S CONTRADICTORY ATTRACTION AND PREJUDICE:** Jon increasingly notices Ygritte's humor, lively eyes and red hair, but initially evaluates her by southern court standards and calls free-folk life dishonorable. Tormund and Ryk become men he genuinely likes even as Rattleshirt, the Weeper and other raiders repel him. The **free folk are neither a uniform army of monsters nor uniformly generous**, a finding grounded in his direct acquaintance.
9. **VARamyr IS A MULTIPLE-BEAST SKINCHANGER:** Jon encounters **Varamyr Sixskins**, a small man riding a ferocious **white snow bear** reportedly thirteen feet high on its hind legs, attended by **three wolves and a shadowcat**. **Ghost's hackles rise** around the bear and cat. Their visible association and the man's title strongly suggest multiple animals under his influence, but **Jon does not directly inhabit Varamyr's mind or witness each transfer in this chapter**. This complements Jojen's reported greenseer abilities in [ASOS 9 Bran](09-bran.md).
10. **OTHER DISTINCT FREE-FOLK GROUPS:** Jon directly sees **Hornfoot people with thick barefoot soles** and hears reports of **Frozen Shore walrus-bone chariots drawn by dogs**, **Ice-river cannibals**, cave dwellers with painted faces and **Harma Dogshead's practice of taking dog heads for her banner**. Some are firsthand sightings, others **Jon's or camp reports**, not equally verified anthropology.
11. **A POPULATION, NOT A CONVENTIONAL HOST:** Jon observes many clan groupings whose members have **never seen the Wall or speak the Common Tongue**. Mance spent years acquiring consent through **songs, speeches, deals and violence**, settling feuds between leaders such as Harma and Rattleshirt and others. His coalition leadership is impressive precisely because **social and linguistic fragmentation** would otherwise prevent unified action.
12. **QHORIN'S CENTRAL QUESTION STILL UNANSWERED:** Jon recalls his orders to **infiltrate and discover what the wildlings sought while digging in the Frostfangs**, possibly a means of breaching the Wall. Despite traveling with the host, he has seen **no displayed new artifact**, and Mance has not shared his strategy. His conjecture **the diggings involved a magical weapon** remains a working hypothesis, not confirmed horn discovery.
13. **JON'S PREFERRED MILITARY THEORY:** He expects roughly **300 mounted Watch brothers on the Fist** to rout large segments of the wildling column, because many migrants are unarmed or have children, only **about one in one hundred** are mounted, and its fighters are distributed across **Harma's van, a giant/auroch/fire-thrower rearguard, Mance's center and roving groups**. Jon imagines killing Mance might disintegrate the alliance. This is **his prospective tactical estimate before seeing what happened at the Fist**, not a confirmed result.
14. **THE 'LAST OF THE GIANTS' SONG:** Ygritte, Tormund, Ryk and Tormund's children sing an elegy about giants losing territory, hunted by humans and displaced by the Wall. **Ygritte weeps** although Jon notes hundreds of giants still exist. The song's emotional truth concerns a **people's decline or expected extinction**, not a literal observation that this is the final living giant. Its motif qualifies pure anti-wildling language about raiders.
15. **THE EAGLE'S PHYSICAL ATTACK:** The familiar **blue-grey eagle** suddenly tears at Jon's face and throws him from his horse. Ygritte says his eye is spared but **skin is ripped and bleeding**. Unlike a simple mysterious aerial spy, the bird now inflicts a **direct new wound on Jon**, apparently with personal hostility.
16. **ORELL NAMED AT LAST:** **Rattleshirt** arrives and the eagle perches on his giant-skull helmet. Tormund calls it Rattleshirt's **'hellcrow'**, and Ygritte later says **Orell tried to take Jon's eye**. Jon recalls **killing Orell** at the high lookout and suspects part of the man's mind now survives in the eagle. This source **explicitly identifies the former human as Orell through wildling testimony**, strengthening [ACOK 68 Jon](../../02-A-Clash-of-Kings/chapters/68-jon.md)'s general 'he was a man' statement. The exact mechanism, permanence and control are still not independently explained in his POV.
17. **A SUMMONS TO THE FIST:** Rattleshirt says **Mance demands Jon**. Tormund first threatens to defend him, but relents when told it is Mance's command. **Ygritte insists on accompanying Jon**, illustrating both her own autonomy and the practical protection of an ally within a dangerous coalition.
18. **THE WALL OF THE FIRST MEN IN RUINS:** Crossing the **Milkwater**, they approach **the Fist**, where Jon expected Jeor Mormont and the Watch to be prepared. He instead sees many **dead, mutilated garrons**, a horse **impaled on outward-facing stakes while apparently trying to escape**, bloodied snow, **Chett's dead dog**, and wildlings looting saddles, horseshoes and metal. There is **no conventional wildling assault witnessed by Jon**, only its aftermath.
19. **THE OLD BEAR'S CAMP WAS OVERWHELMED BY THE DEAD:** **Mance** says he would have lost enormous numbers storming the fortified ringwall directly, but **the dead walked and overcame the rangers** despite pits, stakes, food and stored water. This explanation is informed enemy testimony and strongly corroborates **three horn blasts and undead signs in ASOS Prologue / Chett**. Jon **does not witness the actual battle or count the living escapees**. Therefore **Mormont is missing, not proven dead** here.
20. **WATCH INTELLIGENCE IS LEAKED UNDER DURESS:** Mance suspects Jon lied, sees a garrison of **more than 100 and fewer than 200 dead horses** with more concealed in snow, and demands the true Watch numbers and commanding officer. Jon admits **300 rangers**, **200 from Castle Black and 100 from Shadow Tower**, then identifies **Jeor Mormont** and says **Bowen Marsh** commands Castle Black. Mance explains that trusted knowledge of Marsh's limited military skills could facilitate his invasion. These are **significant secrets surrendered under threat** while Jon wrestles with Qhorin's order not to balk.
21. **THE DEAD ARE A THREAT TO MANCE TOO:** Mance orders **Varamyr's wolves to scout the wights**, doubles patrols, equips men with **torches and flint**, and urges haste. His concern about the cold and walking dead is explicit, so a model of his migration driven **only by conquest** cannot account for what his own men fear. The chapter does not settle the relative priority of flight, survival and offensive goals.
22. **YGRITTE'S CLOAK CLAIM IS TECHNICALLY TRUE BUT MISLEADING:** To protect Jon from execution as a Watch spy, Ygritte says she and Jon have **been close beneath his new sheepskin cloak on many nights**. Mance interprets this as sex. Jon agrees they are lovers to avoid exposure; afterward Ygritte clarifies **she deliberately left the timing and nature of the intimacy unspecified**. In this scene **a sexual relationship is not yet consummated on page**. Index the discrepancy rather than projecting later consummation backward.
23. **THE MANCE DECISION:** Mance does not fully trust Jon but chooses a **more stringent loyalty test** instead of killing him, assigning **Jon and Ygritte to Jarl and Styr the Magnar**, who are to **cross over the Wall at first light** and exploit Jon's knowledge of Castle Black. Styr remains suspicious and Mance permits him to kill Jon if he judges him false. This is **an actual operational command**, not an already accomplished crossing.
24. **GHOST DETERRENCE AND FINAL MOMENT:** Rattleshirt threatens Jon after the audience; Ghost appears **on the stone ringwall**, with hackles raised, stopping him from drawing his sword. Jon descends with Ygritte, torn between duty and the escalating demand for genuine intimacy as proof of defection. **The Old Bear's escape, how the Wall will be crossed, and whether Jon keeps his Watch identity remain open at the chapter endpoint**.

## Rival interpretations and follow-up tests

- **Others vs free-folk invasion:** The **Fist disaster caused by moving dead** makes it impossible to read Mance's host solely as expansionist invaders. The same army is still armed and intends to penetrate the Wall. Track the dual pressures of **refuge and armed incursion** together.
- **The Horn of Winter / diggings:** Jon's thoughts expressly link Joramun's song, awakened giants and Tormund's name. But **a link in Jon's mind is not a proven artifact**, and he remains without evidence that Mance unearthed such a horn.
- **Orell's second life:** Specific naming plus ongoing targeted eagle attacks markedly strengthen the **postmortem skinchanger residue** interpretation. The phenomenon can be compared later against **Varamyr's own POV** rather than borrowing that later explanation as firsthand evidence here.
- **Watch command error and escape:** Jon expects an attack against the wildlings; instead their fortified camp is **overrun by wights**. The ability of undead to ignore static defenses changes strategy at the Wall.
- **Jon and Ygritte:** Ygritte's protection is genuine even while she engineers a story that exposes Jon to harsher proof requirements. Jon's true membership oath and his growing attachment both matter.
