## Chronological reading and consequential events

1. **Sansa lives at the Eyrie as Alayne Stone.** She awakes from a dream of sharing a room with Arya, then remembers her false identity and loneliness. The Eyrie's small household and narrow access limit her ability to flee or secure allies independently.
2. **Littlefinger is exercising disputed power as Lord Protector.** He has been absent **four days meeting the Corbrays**. Sansa has heard that **Yohn Royce** and the **Waynwoods, Redforts, Belmores and Templetons** resent Lysa's neutrality and Petyr's authority; a sudden **Lord Hunter death** has provoked accusations among his sons. These are **reported political tensions**, not fully observed Vale rebellion.
3. **Marillion remains a threat.** Lysa favors him, ignores accusations of harassment, and has expelled two servants and a page for complaints. His privileged place undermines Sansa's safety.
4. **Snow falls unexpectedly in the Eyrie's garden.** Sansa recalls snow during her departure from Winterfell and childhood snowball games with Arya and Bran. **This is memory and weather**, not a demonstrated magical vision.
5. **The Eyrie garden cannot sustain a weirwood.** Sansa knows it was intended as a godswood but its thin, stony soil would not permit a heart tree to take root. She experiences it as spiritually empty. **The absence of a weirwood does not prove the place is invisible to all magical perception**.
6. **Sansa builds an exacting miniature Winterfell from snow.** She creates the **outer and inner walls, gatehouse, towers, square armory, kitchen, hall, stables, library tower, lichyard and godswood**. The detailed spatial memory reveals continuing identification with home, not proof she has forgotten northern life.
7. **Her early bridge structures fail until Petyr suggests twigs.** Petyr unexpectedly joins her, advises strengthening covered bridges with sticks and helps construct **the glass gardens' roof frame** from twigs. Material craft temporarily creates cooperative intimacy; it is not a reconciliation of their unequal power.
8. **Sansa corrects Petyr's imagination of Winterfell.** She remembers hot-spring water piped through its walls and genuinely warm glass gardens; Petyr had always imagined Winterfell as dark and cold because **Catelyn married Eddard and went north**. Their interpretations of the castle differ decisively.
9. **The broken tower is recreated.** Sansa builds the towers and marks snow-covered gargoyles as lumps. She eventually flings snow into Petyr's face, testing her confidence within the imaginary Winterfell.
10. **Sansa directly reproaches Petyr for deceiving her destination.** He acknowledges promising to take her home but bringing her to the Vale, then initiates an unwanted kiss. **Sansa tries to pull away, briefly yields in confusion, then turns her face and frees herself**. The scene is initiated by Petyr, **not a mutual seduction invented by Lysa**.
11. **Littlefinger sexualizes the resemblance to Catelyn.** He says Sansa is more beautiful than her mother was at her age and offers to 'warm' her. Sansa protests that she could almost have been his daughter. **His paternal cover identity and sexual interest operate simultaneously**.
12. **Robert Arryn destroys the snow castle.** The eight-year-old waves his **rag-and-sawdust doll** as a giant, damaging towers and walls. Sansa grabs the doll to stop him; it tears accidentally.
13. **Robert has a major seizure after the doll tears.** He convulses across the model, breaking the structures. **Petyr and servants restrain him**, and **Maester Colemon gives dreamwine**, then recommends leeching. His assertion that 'bad blood' causes fits is **medical opinion within the setting**, not a modern diagnosis or proof of treatment efficacy.
14. **Sansa places the destroyed doll head onto the snow castle.** Her angry act resembles a giant's head displayed on Winterfell's walls. Petyr comments this may resemble stories about prior giants. **This is an evocative symbolic parallel, not a confirmed prophecy of future literal giant decapitation**.
15. **The castle's destruction deepens Sansa's resolve against the planned cousin marriage.** She considers telling Lysa she refuses to marry Robert; she knows she is **thirteen**, already married to Tyrion, and regards herself as Winterfell's heir on her incomplete information. She knows consent at the ceremony matters. **No new marriage has taken place**.
16. **An alternative Vale household becomes imaginable.** Sansa thinks of **Nestor Royce's Gates of the Moon** and his daughter **Myranda** as potentially safer company, and has heard one of **Robert Baratheon's acknowledged bastard daughters** serves Nestor there. These are **reputation/hearsay descriptions**, not verified future allies.
17. **Marillion escorts Sansa to the High Hall.** He insinuates she is interested in him and speaks of composing a song about a seductive 'baseborn' girl. He has been rewarded with gifts and access by Lysa. **His influence is real but subordinate to Petyr's at the crucial final moment**.
18. **The High Hall is isolated for a dangerous confrontation.** **Two guards** stand outside; Marillion bars the main doors with an additional spear, while **Petyr later enters through a separate lord's entrance behind the dais**. These are crucial physical-access details for the coming crime.
19. **Lysa confronts Sansa over Petyr's kiss.** She insists Sansa initiated it, despite Sansa repeatedly saying Petyr kissed her. Lysa sees Sansa through long-standing jealousy of Catelyn; her accusation is **shown to be false by Sansa's earlier direct POV**.
20. **Lysa describes a former Riverrun incident with Petyr.** She says Catelyn danced with him six times, rejected his attempted kiss, and Petyr drank until insensible. Lysa subsequently went to his bed and was intimate with him, hearing him call her **'Cat'**. **This is Lysa's first-person reminiscence, not a separate account from Petyr or Catelyn**; it materially undercuts Petyr's earlier claim of intimacy with Catelyn.
21. **Lysa reveals a lost pregnancy by Littlefinger.** She says she conceived **Petyr's child** and that her father **Hoster Tully forced her to drink an herbal abortifacient** containing **tansy, mint, wormwood, pennyroyal and honey**. This is important new source testimony for Lysa's relationship to her father and repeated reproductive grief, but the chapter does not provide her own previous medical records.
22. **Lysa nearly murders Sansa at the Moon Door.** She orders Sansa to unbar a **white weirwood door secured by three bronze bars**, then seizes her and tries to push her into open air above a **roughly 600-foot drop to a lower waycastle**. Sansa struggles, briefly catching Lysa's hair. This is **a directly witnessed attempted killing**, not ordinary punishment or a mere threat.
23. **Marillion supplies a soundtrack rather than intervening.** He sings **'The False and the Fair'** while Lysa tries to force Sansa over the edge. His choice to keep playing is evidence of assistance or indifference; **the exact extent of an advance agreement is not shown**.
24. **Petyr arrives through the rear lord's entrance.** He draws Lysa away from Sansa and coaxes her to release the girl. He recognizes Sansa as Catelyn's daughter; **his physical rescue is real**, even though he himself created the preceding sexual conflict.
25. **Lysa confesses to Jon Arryn's poisoning and the forged accusation.** She explicitly says **Petyr instructed her to place 'the tears' in Jon Arryn's wine**, and that **she herself wrote to Catelyn saying the Lannisters murdered Jon**, again on **Petyr's instructions**. **This is a direct admission of participation in murder and deliberate disinformation by the perpetrator**. It decisively overturns the initial Lannister-culpability assumption. The chapter does **not chemically test the substance called 'tears'**, although the earlier references to **tears of Lys** make an identification plausible.
26. **She confirms initiating Petyr's rise through Jon Arryn.** Lysa says she secured his first appointment via Jon Arryn and sought closeness to Petyr throughout her marriage. These are **her own accounts of patronage**, consistent with the larger chronology but not independently audited financial records.
27. **Petyr manipulates Lysa into moving away from Sansa.** He reassures his wife and asks her to come into his arms. His soothing language culminates in a statement that he has loved **only Catelyn**, revealing the cruelty beneath his rescue.
28. **Petyr kills Lysa by pushing her out the Moon Door.** The act is directly witnessed: he gives a brief shove after his declaration, Lysa slips from the marble and falls into the open sky without a scream. **Lysa's death is established; it is not an accidental fall during an argument**.
29. **Littlefinger immediately frames Marillion.** He tells Sansa to admit his guards, then supplies an explicit false narrative that **the singer killed his wife**. **Sansa knows this is false; Petyr is the actual killer**. Marillion remains alive at chapter end and has witnessed at least the lethal confrontation.
30. **End state:** The book reveals that **Littlefinger and Lysa generated Jon Arryn's death and the letter that helped precipitate the war**, then Petyr kills Lysa while gaining Sansa's continued dependency. The **Vale's future leadership, Marillion's alleged guilt, Sansa's ability to contradict Petyr and Robert Arryn's custody** all remain open at the ending.

## Research implications

- **Original Jon Arryn murder investigation:** Distinguish the poisoned wine (now source-confessed) from the material causing Jon Arryn's earlier illness. Re-evaluate every witness who trusted Lysa's false letter or Petyr's political narrative.
- **Petyr's method:** The episode combines real protection with manipulation, confession extraction and immediately fabricated blame. His words are not reliable even when Sansa's immediate survival depends on him.
- **Winterfell's symbolic future:** Sansa independently rebuilds the ancestral castle in her memory, but its destruction by Robert's play does not determine literal political outcomes.
- **Vale witness risks:** Marillion is a direct witness and designated scapegoat; Sansa knows the truth but is a captive of Petyr's protection.
