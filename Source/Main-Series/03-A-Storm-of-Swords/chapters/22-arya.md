## Causal sequence and witness record

1. **Beric remains elusive.** At the half-ruined keep of the aged **Lord Lychester**, the elderly knight repeats a decades-old exploit against Ser Maynard rather than answering questions. His maester has a six-month-old raven report that Beric was hanged near the Gods Eye; Lem insists Thoros cut him down before death. Both accounts concern **reports**, not a current witnessed death.
2. **Civil-war trauma spans generations.** Lem says Lord Lychester's sons died on both sides of Robert's Rebellion, after which the lord declined mentally. The singer Tom argues that a song might preserve a military feat now almost forgotten. The narrator cannot independently date or validate Lychester's claimed bridge fight.
3. **A different horn signal finds a tree settlement.** After three days the band reaches woodland and Jack uses a call distinct from the earlier village signal ([ASOS 17 Arya](17-arya.md)). Rope ladders unfold into a concealed **branch-top village with walkways and houses**. This is concrete local defensive infrastructure, not magic transport.
4. **The Lady of the Leaves reports insecurity.** Autumn threatens the refuge, and she has seen reports of twelve 'wolves' hunting on the Hayford road nine days earlier. Their identity is not independently verified; wolf is used for armed northerners in this chapter.
5. **Beric's deaths are rumors with incompatible endings.** The Lady says she heard from intermediaries Gregor put a dagger through Beric's eye. Lem counters that injury need not kill him and invokes Jack's survival with one eye. **Do not convert these reports into two independently confirmed resurrections.**
6. **At Sallydance, a burned sept testifies to northern atrocities.** A septon says attackers stole sacred objects, including metal ornaments, jewels and the Maiden's carved breasts, and identifies the perpetrators as **northmen hunting Jaime Lannister**, not Vargo's mercenaries. This marks a moral cost of the Karstark bounty from [ASOS 20 Catelyn](20-catelyn.md), although no individual murderer is identified.
7. **The underground network uses false symbols.** Men occupy the crypt beneath the burned sept, and one man displays a crude **lightning-bolt badge** without being Beric. Greenbeard's 'everywhere and nowhere' description reveals distributed identity/loyalty and ambiguity for outsiders, not a literal omnipresent lord.
8. **A ransom motive becomes explicit.** Tom expects Arya's delivery to Riverrun to be chiefly for **payment**; Harwin says funds buy horses, armor, weapons and **seed for winter planting**. Arya worries a king must favor the realm over her and fears Catelyn's reaction to her past. This is not a completed ransom or a promise of unconditional reunion.
9. **Tom's earlier humiliation of Edmure is hinted.** The outlaws tease Tom about a song concerning Edmure's embarrassing encounter with a woman and drink. Its precise truth and intent are not proven; it helps explain tension between a scattered outlaw movement and formal Tully authority.
10. **High Heart has unusually specific geography.** Arya and Gendry count **thirty-one large pale stumps** in a crown-like ring where immense weirwoods once grew. Tom attributes the grove's destruction to Andal ruler **Erreg the Kinslayer** and associates the hill with children-of-the-forest spirits. The historical story is **folklore testimony**, while the stumps are directly counted.
11. **The refuge is tactically exposed and defensible.** Tom says no harm can come to those sleeping on the hill, while Arya independently reasons they can see anyone approaching across the flat surrounding country. Physical visibility offers an ordinary explanation; no absolute magic ward is demonstrated.
12. **The Ghost of High Heart appears at night.** A small, very old, pale-haired woman with a dark gnarled cane meets Tom, Lem and Greenbeard. Arya thinks her eyes look red, but she is watching through bushes in night conditions. Tom subsequently calls her a strange **elderly dwarf woman**, not a literal ghost.
13. **A sequence of remarkable dream symbols is spoken.** The old woman describes a **burning-heart shadow striking a golden stag**, a **faceless man on a swaying bridge carrying a drowned, seaweed-winged crow**, and a **river carrying a dead fish-woman with red tears whose eyes open**. The chapter confirms **she recounts these dreams**, not the identity or occurrence of every future referent.
14. **The dream-exchange requires a song.** She insists Tom sing a familiar, sad song in payment for her visions. The melody reminds Arya of something Sansa might recognize; Tom reports the old woman always requests the same song. Neither its title nor original event is identified here.
15. **The Brotherhood's dispersal is explained rationally.** Harwin says Beric withholds routes even from his own scattered men so captured members cannot betray him under torture; small bands forage without exhausting the countryside and can strike in numerous places. His estimate of **hundreds or perhaps thousands** is an estimate, not an audited roster.
16. **Arya testifies to the Tickler's interrogation methods.** She remembers the same repeated questions about food, hidden valuables and Beric's personnel/location, with torture methods changing. Harwin is horrified and vows their enemies should answer. He says Gregor reportedly lost half his men at the **Stone Mill**; the Tickler's personal fate is unknown.
17. **Acorn Hall offers guarded refuge.** Its lord serves **Lord Vance** elsewhere; his wife, Lady Smallwood, knows Tom well and admits the group. It is a real if undergarrisoned stone keep, not a secure long-term sanctuary for someone being hunted.
18. **Arya's Bolton clothing is dangerous.** Lady Smallwood warns the **flayed-man badge** on Arya's garments could have her hanged by hostile armed groups, regardless of who she is. The garment's provenance is not proof she serves House Bolton.
19. **Lady Smallwood imposes conventional girlhood.** She has Arya washed, perfumed and dressed in her daughter **Carellen's green acorn-embroidered gown**. She speaks kindly of talents as a form of prayer, misunderstanding Arya's reference to 'needlework' as embroidery rather than swordsmanship. Arya's involuntary return to highborn presentation contrasts the freedom of earlier false names.
20. **Two Smallwood children matter.** Carellen was sent to an **Oldtown motherhouse** where Lady Smallwood's great-aunt is a septa. Later Lady Smallwood lends Arya riding clothes formerly belonging to her **son, who died aged seven**. The details establish grief, dispersal, and the material basis of her generosity.
21. **Beric's last local appearance is reported.** Lady Smallwood says Thoros and others delivered a flock of sheep not a fortnight earlier; **three sheep** were left in thanks, and one formed part of supper. This is specific food redistribution, not simply exaction from the countryside.
22. **Karstark searchers are identified through arms.** Lady Smallwood reports rough men with a **white sun badge on black**, seeking Jaime and calling Catelyn his liberator. Arya recognizes the Karstark sigil, believes they are Robb's men and considers finding them; reader context from [ASOS 20](20-catelyn.md) shows the men have deserted to hunt Jaime and may be dangerous.
23. **Arya receives true but unwelcome information.** She rejects the claim that her mother released Jaime, although [ASOS 1 Jaime](01-jaime.md) and [ASOS 20 Catelyn](20-catelyn.md) establish it. The Brotherhood then excludes her from adult discussion. **Reader/POV information asymmetry** makes an attempted appeal to Karstarks hazardous.
24. **Gendry's craft and Thoros's trick are explained.** At the Smallwood smithy Gendry says **Tobho Mott** repeatedly supplied Thoros with inexpensive swords dipped in wildfire for spectacular flaming display; the process damaged blades. This is a practical alchemical method for tourneys, **not proof all red-priest fire is fake or that Beric's later powers are ordinary**.
25. **Gendry remembers an interrupted future.** Mott had prepared him to forge his first longsword before Yoren recruited him for the Watch. He now handles tools in a dormant smithy; Arya offers the prospect of making swords for Robb, unaware of the larger political and family constraints.
26. **A rough childhood game becomes an identity conflict.** Gendry compliments Arya's gown and scent; their wrestling damages the borrowed acorn dress. Other men mistakenly assume Gendry attacked her, but Arya defends him. The scene includes their actual banter and wrestling; do not impose a promised romance.
27. **Tom's forest-maiden song becomes a counterimage.** The song values a girl choosing the woods and a gown of leaves rather than a prescribed courtly marriage. Its placement near Arya's insistence on wolf identity invites a **thematic reading**, not a guarantee of where she ends her story.
28. **Lady Smallwood responds with care rather than punishment.** She dresses Arya again, first in lilac and pearls, later gives practical breeches, tunic and studded doeskin jerkin for travel. Arya apologizes for tearing the dress and sees the lady's kindness and bereavement. These are concrete signs Arya can feel remorse and connection outside revenge.
29. **End position.** Beric has **not** been found; the band intends to continue toward him and possible ransom. Arya knows of the search for Jaime and has heard rumors about Catelyn that she disbelieves. No High Heart vision is yet verified as a completed narrated event at this endpoint.

## Theory tests

- **Ghost predictions:** A tempting later reading connects dream-images to royal deaths, a maritime killing and a river resurrection. Preserve distinct text-level dream testimony, plausible alternatives, and precise chronology before matching identities, especially because the old woman can know earlier events in the world.
- **The Brotherhood's legitimacy:** Shelter and seed purchases support civic purpose, but forced escort/ransom of Arya and uncertain relations with Tully leaders show conflicting incentives.
- **Stark loyalists vs Stark justice:** Karstark searchers attack a sept and pursue Jaime for a private bounty, creating a direct test of whether Northern banners guarantee moral conduct.
- **Arya's homeward choices:** She considers finding the Karstarks based on incomplete news of their desertion; avoid crediting her with the reader's knowledge of Robb's judgment.
- **Highborn girl and wild wolf:** Lady Smallwood's care does not erase Arya's discomfort, and Arya's apology shows she is not beyond social attachment.
