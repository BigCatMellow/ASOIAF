## Core interpretations

**The Fragrant Steward candidate is concrete.** Moqorro explicitly translates the ship's name as *Fragrant Steward*. Quaithe's warning about a perfumed seneschal may therefore point to the actual vessel, to Reznak in Meereen, or another person/object. The linguistic match deserves serious weighting, but this ship's mere name does not prove malign intent or that the warning predicts its destruction.

**True and false dragons are not a certified genealogical verdict.** Moqorro sees young/old, true/false and light/dark dragons and a small man's huge shadow. With Aegon now campaigning west and Daenerys in Meereen, the prophecy is highly relevant to the false-Aegon and Tyrion-as-kingmaker theories. But Moqorro does not say which dragon is false, what 'false' means or even that every dragon is a person. Keep candidate assignments plural.

**The black eye and ten arms.** This resembles the one-eyed Kraken iconography and Euron's command at sea, but could be another sea-power figure or force. It is a vision of a shadow, not a direct picture labeling Euron.

**Penny complicates Tyrion's story.** She supplies a personal history of dwarfs murdered because killers sought Tyrion. The previously unseen hiring agent named roughly Osmund/Oswald in Pentos is a potential concrete clue to court arrangements around Joffrey's humiliation. She eventually corrects her blame, and Tyrion offers care while continuing to resent his own physical stigma and to hide his despair.

**Religious contradiction:** a priest speaking liberation and sending warriors to Daenerys rides in a ship operated by fifty slaves and temple slaves. This may be a political problem for Dany's emancipatory coalition.
