## Causal reading

**The city is not passive.** Directly witnessed coastal improvements, fortified Seal Rock, refugee management, recruitment, and finally twenty-three war galleys behind the jetty prove that Wyman's harbor is preparing for danger. They do not establish whether the fleet will serve Stannis, the Lannister-backed Boltons, or White Harbor's independent survival.

**The Frey bargain has a hostage underneath it.** The Lionstar's presence corroborates Godric Borrell's earlier ship report; local gossip distinguishes murdered Wendel from captive Wylis. The living son is a credible reason Wyman's outward conduct might look compliant despite private hostility. Treat rumored granddaughter marriage and the failure to help Robett Glover as visible facts/reports, not a final account of Wyman's allegiance.

**Real civilians face the consequences.** Old Mint shelters war refugees, with children, elders, girls pressured into sexual survival and boys recruited. A fleet's political value must be evaluated alongside food needs and population security, not just the binary of betrayal or loyal conspiracy.

**Rumor is demonstrably fallible.** Tavern talk garbles Tywin's death and wrongly attributes Riverlands atrocities to Sandor Clegane. Stories of Daenerys/Viserys circulate with confused names; the named merchant Sloe-Eyed Maid is a rare trackable object across the maritime accounts. Even an apparently corroborated rumor of the dragons comes third-hand here.

**Davos chooses exposure.** He has lost sons and his luck-charm, knows he can still return to family, and was publicly belittled by Florent. Instead he walks to the New Castle after confirming hidden military power. The chapter ends at the guard, not with Wyman's acceptance, imprisonment, or any bargain.
