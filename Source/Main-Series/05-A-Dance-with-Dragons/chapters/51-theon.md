## Main narrative, agency, and consequences

The murder of Little Walder brings Frey–Manderly hostility into open violence; Roose answers by sending Hosteen and the Freys into the snow rather than repairing trust. In that gap, Abel's people use ordinary servant routines and Theon's intimate knowledge of Winterfell to reach Jeyne. Theon cannot undo his earlier betrayals, but he rejects both an easy murder of Rowan and Reek's loyalty to Ramsay, names himself, comforts Jeyne as Jeyne, and takes responsibility for the only escape left. The chapter stops before the jump's outcome and before the scattered spearwives' fates.
