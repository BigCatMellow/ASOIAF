## Main narrative, decisions and consequences

Winterfell is materially closer to a besieged madhouse than an impregnable castle: rations and horse shelter fail, men are killed internally, and the Freys clash with Manderly and the North. Theon's helplessness remains visible even to Roose, who concludes that the mutilated captive is too weak to have committed the murders. Theon hears a possible Bran-linked whisper from the weirwood while a warhorn outside the gates heightens the garrison's fear. Abel's apparent washerwomen then reveal tactical interest in Theon, but the killers' complete identities and assignments remain unproven.

## Rival readings and epistemic boundaries

**Who is the ghost?** Multiple deaths are plausibly targeted killings by infiltrators, but a crushed stable and some outdoor fatalities have mundane mechanisms. Holly's teasing denial is not a confession, and no complete killer roster is established. **Horn and drum:** Winterfell's men shout Stannis's name, but no attacker is sighted; ADWD 42 simultaneously establishes Stannis's main army snowbound at the crofter's village, so do not treat the shout as proof his host reached the walls. **Tree voice:** Theon perceives his own name and then Bran's name, with an apparent Bran face. Coupled with ADWD 34 (Bran's earlier access to Winterfell weirwood memory), this warrants strong investigation, not an on-page verified physical visit by Bran. **The North remembers:** Dustin's speech suggests older loyalty and anger, but does not by itself prove any unified rebel network.
