## Central findings and competing readings

**The Astapor atrocity has many perpetrators.** Quentyn witnesses the death and desolation behind Yunkai's siege, including starving children and slaughter of boys drilled as newly made Unsullied. The armored “Cleon” is a *dead ruler physically propped up on a horse*, not a reanimated wight. The mercenary cavalry and New Ghis soldiers turn the sortie into a massacre. This builds a firsthand counterweight to the increasingly exaggerated pro-slaver stories about Daenerys: criticizing outcomes does not license treating manufactured allegations about her as facts.

**Quentyn's moral hazard.** His Doran-mandated objective is still to meet and marry Daenerys. He has meanwhile taken an actual one-year Windblown oath, killed defenders and grown uneasy about abandoning comrades. His willingness to betray them is not heroically cost-free; nor does the Yunkish regime's enslavement justify indiscriminate killing. The trio remain disguised and not confirmed admitted to Meereen.

**A defection inside a defection.** The Tattered Prince orders a patrol made predominantly of Westerosi fighters to pretend to switch sides. Quentyn and his two companions *already plan to switch for real*; each side is concealing different motives. The sellsword leader cites actual injuries he has inflicted to construct convincing cover stories. His instruction is not proof all would join Daenerys loyally. Tattered Prince motives, later bargain and precise members must be tested.

**Military material:** Slaver armies chain groups of ten to prevent flight and wield theatrical slave regiments while more disciplined mercenaries are tactically superior. The weak leadership structure gives weight to later battle predictions, but numerical superiority and outside legions still create danger.
