## Theory significance

**The greenseer is Brynden.** The half-dead figure provides his own birth name, further substantiating—but not spelling the full historical surname of—the Bloodraven identification. His immense age, single red eye, black clothing and old crow identity support the connection strongly. Keep Bran's confusion with Brynden Tully separate.

**The old gods may be a collective intelligence.** Bran experiences a dead singer's trace within a raven; Brynden says all his birds have been occupied, and Jojen says singers' knowledge enters weirwoods. This supports an ancestral memory network rather than a single anthropomorphic deity. It does not demonstrate that all dead humans participate or that every carved tree is conscious in the same manner.

**Time is both memory and contested communication.** Ned appears to hear Bran as a rustle twice, while Brynden says his own words never reached people in past visions and nothing can be changed. Distinguish a past person looking toward a sound from a verified alteration of history. This is strong evidence for the *possibility* of auditory leakage, not proof Bran can rewrite events.

**Jojen paste and sacrifice.** The bowl holds purported weirwood seeds mixed with red veins; blood stew contains unidentified meat. Jojen fears his fate, and Bran sees an *ancient* actual throat-cutting ritual and tastes blood through the heart tree. The theory that Jojen was killed to feed Bran the paste is **not established**. Jojen appears alive and depressed during the chapter; no unambiguous corpse, murder or human ingredient appears.

**Bran's human possession is ethically worsening.** Hodor retreats into a hidden mental space without resisting, and Bran routinely occupies him to wander. This is learned helplessness, not consent. Even useful physical rescue and affectionate intentions cannot erase the violation.
