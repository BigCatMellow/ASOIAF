## Causal reading

**Identity and political utility.** The prisoner is socially recognized by two visiting lords as the former Stark ward, Theon Greyjoy. Ramsay's fictive narrative that this Reek was a lifelong servant is not equivalent to Theon's real past; compare with the earlier original Reek killed in ACOK. Theon remembers the genuine Arya well enough to make him useful for validating or falsifying a purported daughter of Ned. No personal inspection of the proposed bride occurs in this chapter.

**Torture shapes testimony and agency.** His claimed devotion, willingness to obey and tendency to accuse himself are products of starvation, beatings, flaying and staged escape. His own recollection of Kyra's pursuit exposes the deliberate manufacture of helplessness. When a threat prevents even truthful naming, statements extracted from the captive are profoundly unreliable.

**False Arya as political weapon.** Ramsay treats a rumored Stark daughter's marriage as title security. This threatens the legitimate Stark succession despite the reader knowing Arya survives abroad. The text here does not give a physical portrait of the prospective bride or disclose her actual identity. Do not write Jeyne Poole as a directly witnessed bride in this note; cross-book knowledge may suggest it.
