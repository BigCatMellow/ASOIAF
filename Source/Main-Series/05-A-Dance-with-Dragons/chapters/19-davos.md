## Plot, information and rival readings

**A public court is not a reliable test of private loyalty.** Frey delegates are present, Wylis is a hostage, and Wyman's outward endorsement of Tommen serves his son's immediate survival. His visible revulsion at Rhaegar's anti-Stark speech is a meaningful cue to possible concealed hostility, yet does not in this chapter constitute an offer to Stannis or a completed plot against the Freys. Wyman commands that Davos be beheaded; the chapter ends *before* any execution.

**Wylla's oath is consequential.** Her appeal to the Manderlys' ancient Stark obligation exposes a conflict between ancestor-bound loyalty and the claim that the family was extinguished. Theomeore concedes the oath existed, while Rhaegar exploits the approaching sham Arya wedding to claim renewed Stark legitimacy. The reader has already learned from [ADWD 20](20-reek.md) that the woman being offered as Arya is Jeyne Poole; no one in this Davos audience makes that discovery.

**Propaganda with known disproof.** Jared Frey's tale of a monstrous Robb slaying his own guests conflicts with ASOS accounts, and Davos calls it false. It is not a credible alternative history simply because an envoy voices it. By contrast, the court's challenge to the paternity of Tommen is an evidentiary problem: the reader knows the truth, but Stannis's official word alone is not courtroom proof.

**The King's Hand lacks material leverage.** War fleets, hostages, Frey marriage terms, winter security and barely fifteen hundred surviving Stannis soldiers constrict negotiation. Davos sacrifices safety rather than retreat, but his claim to sacred duty does not pay a ransom or feed the city. His death remains unconfirmed at this boundary.
