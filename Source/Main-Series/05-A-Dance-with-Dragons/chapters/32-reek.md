## Causal and theory findings

**Arnolf Karstark is a trap, not a secure Stannis ally.** Roose explicitly says Arnolf tried to bait Stannis into taking Dreadfort and intends to use him when Stannis moves on Winterfell. This upgrades the theory of Karstark treachery from strategic suspicion to a conspirator's direct account, although Arnolf is not personally present to speak. The Bolton plan exploits northern attachment to a supposed Stark daughter whose real identity the reader knows is Jeyne Poole.

**The missing Freys are not yet solved.** Ramsay's riders return without Hosteen and Aenys's missing kin. Big Walder suspects Wyman, and Roose does not trust Wyman's claim that the men rode ahead. This is strong circumstantial suspicion, not direct proof of killing or any cooking of victims. The forty food wagons and Wyman's promised feast remain potential, not yet resolved, evidence.

**Roose's governing mechanism.** He claims peace and quiet while privately admits raping a miller's wife, hanging her husband, cutting a witness's tongue and using terror discreetly. This discrepancy is a political method, not hypocrisy to be excused. He does not mistake Ramsay's cruelty for good statecraft, yet depends on Ramsay's marriage for dynastic legitimacy.

**Theon's recovery is not simple release.** Physical shackles come off when Roose takes him, but Ramsay threatens another amputation and demands espionage. Roose insists Theon has political utility as the rightful Greyjoy heir; even his cultured pronunciation betrays the identity he is terrified to acknowledge.
