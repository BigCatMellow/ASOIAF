## Definitive reveal, unreliable interpretations

**Mance Rayder is alive.** This POV confirms Melisandre placed the *real Rattleshirt* under the appearance of Mance for the execution and disguised Mance as Rattleshirt. Jon himself sees the glamour fall; the actual Mance speaks under his own recognizable appearance. The earlier wildling execution and Jon's unexpected defeat in the practice yard now have an actual mechanism and a credible explanation. This is no longer a mere fan theory.

**What glamours can use—and what that does not prove.** Melisandre describes ruby-linked illusions of shadow and suggestion, strengthened by Rattleshirt's bone armor and personal tokens (boots, hair and fingerbones). She admits to having powders that fake or amplify fiery displays while also possessing stronger sorcery at the Wall. The description of a bag of fingerbones is provocative given Davos's lost charm; it does not prove she took or used *his* bones, or that every apparent resurrection in the narrative is glamour.

**Melony, Lot Seven, Devan.** Her inner voice repeatedly hears a person named Melony and the designation Lot Seven; the same chapter says she was enslaved as a girl at the great red temple. This supplies a concrete likely origin for her earlier name, not a complete birth record. Her keeping Devan at Castle Black comes from a stated wish to spare Davos a fifth dead son, demonstrating genuine human concern alongside ruthless manipulation.

**Seeing is not interpretation.** She sees a wooden-faced one-eyed-associated figure and wolf-headed boy and decides they must be champions of the Other. Other published POVs now establish Bran and the red-eyed root-entwined greenseer in the weirwood caves; her inference that they are enemies is unsupported and may be wrong. Equally important, she asks to see Stannis as Azor Ahai but repeatedly sees Jon in man-wolf shapes amid skulls and daggers. This is evidence relevant to competing champion theories, not direct proof Jon is Azor Ahai.

**One exact prediction lands.** Three severed ranger heads with eyes removed are delivered and directly observed; the victims are named. This validates her seeing *something* about the future, not her assumptions about who killed them, whether Eastwatch will be flooded, or who the girl in grey is. The reader knows that Ramsay's prospective bride is Jeyne rather than Arya, and the grey girl may be someone else entirely.
