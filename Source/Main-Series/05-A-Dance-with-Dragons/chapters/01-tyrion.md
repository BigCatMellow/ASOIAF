## Interpretation and rival tests

**Exile has not rehabilitated Tyrion.** He is injured, suicidal enough for Illyrio to test his intentions, murderous in fantasy, and threatening toward a servant who lacks meaningful capacity to refuse him. Naming this coercion matters as much as recognizing his grief, Tysha trauma and desire to live. His memory of Tysha's abuse is direct testimony to his participation under Tywin's command, not justification for further abuse.

**Illyrio's incentives are open.** The manse offers security, food and an unseen escape route, but guarded gates and secrecy give Illyrio effective custody. He knows Varys and claims to have a viable contender. His analogies about the Pentoshi ceremonial prince and the need for a savior form *salesmanship*, not independent proof that a particular prince or queen will be welcomed. The phrase **dragon with three heads** deliberately stops short of naming the candidate. The theory that Illyrio sponsors a Blackfyre-descended Aegon is not established by this chapter and must be tested against later disclosures.

**Poison and hospitality.** Tyrion collects a separate set of apparently deadly mushrooms in the courtyard, then is offered a dish of mushrooms by Illyrio. The latter are shown nonpoisonous when Illyrio eats them. They are **not the same demonstrated batch**, and the pocketed fungi remain a physical continuity item. Illyrio's theatrical suggestion that Tyrion wants to die calls attention to self-destructive drinking; his interest in keeping Tyrion alive serves his unknown scheme.

**Information mismatch.** Tyrion believes Janos Slynt may command the Watch and only learns Stannis is there through Illyrio. This is a clean example of overlapping publication plots: [ADWD 3 Jon](03-jon.md) concerns Stannis at Castle Black with Slynt still present as a disaffected brother, and Tyrion is uninformed.
