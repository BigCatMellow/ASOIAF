## Main narrative, agency, and consequences

Dany tries to humanize an institution she has agreed to restore: no children, more limited criminal sentences, and an insistence that participants consent. The dwarf/lion proposal and Barsena's death reveal how little those safeguards reach. Drogon's arrival converts a managed political pageant into an immediate threat. Dany rejects flight from the arena, confronts him alone, removes his spear, and is carried away. That choice gives her unprecedented physical contact with a dragon but leaves Meereen without its visible queen amid enemy hostages, an internal security dispute, and a fragile peace.

## Rival readings and verification tests

**Drogon's submission:** he may respond to Dany's voice, whip, her lack of flight, injury, or a combination; this scene alone does not establish reliable dragon control. **Hizdahr:** his wish to retain Dany at the games and order to kill Drogon can be read as political containment, terror, or an immediate defensive reaction. Later conduct and corroboration are needed. **The Windblown:** Meris's account is self-interested and contested by prior events; an actual change of sides, not her claim, tests it.
