## Causal reading

**An apparent execution is not a settled identity result.** Jon watches someone displayed as Mance burn, and orders arrows to shorten his pain. The prisoner's desperate rejection of his own kingship and the visual emphasis on Rattleshirt's later kneeling are important anomalies. At this sequential point, describe the dead man as the **prisoner presented as Mance**; investigate glamour/identity displacement later rather than suppressing this clue or prematurely naming a confirmed substitution.

**Conversion is conditional survival.** Hunger, exposure, the threat of burning, and rewards of soup, land and shelter frame the free folk's choices. A woman flees with children; a man is killed while resisting the branch ritual. The fact that leaders kneel cannot establish inner religious conversion or safe integration. Jon's objection to coerced kneeling and his fear of Tormund emphasize the cultural problem.

**Bowen Marsh makes a serious strategic case.** Closing the gates would remove vulnerable choke points and exploit Wall height; Jon answers that it also destroys the Watch's ability to range and gather intelligence and risks leaving fellow humans to the Others. The disagreement concerns real tradeoffs, not simply wisdom versus villainy. Marsh's fear of legitimizing Stannis's rebellion remains a political risk.

**Lightbringer test.** The sword's light is strikingly brighter than before. Yet Jon examines Aemon's flagged source and notes that legendary Lightbringer gives off heat, while Stannis's steel is cold. Brightness is evidence of a luminous phenomenon, **not proof this is the actual original weapon or Stannis the prophesied savior**.

**Isolation:** Jon is too severe with Pyp and excludes himself from the meal he wants. He then separates Grenn/Pyp and other peers by Watch orders. These acts may be necessary for command yet reduce informal warnings and loyalty.
