## High-priority identity and geopolitical junctions

**Connington is definitively identified, Aegon's genetic identity is not.** This POV names the speaker Jon Connington and provides independent memories of Rhaegar, Stoney Sept, his exile and time in the Golden Company. It confirms an organized conspiracy to raise the boy as Rhaegar's son. Yet **a sincere mentor raising a claimant is not itself proof that the boy is biologically Elia's surviving child**. Harry's worry about a pretender is explicitly in council. The Company's Blackfyre founding culture and Myles Toyne's secret contract keep alternative ancestry interpretations live.

**The westward pivot is real and traceable.** Tyrion convinced Aegon that begging Daenerys would put him beneath her; Aegon repeats that reasoning. The Golden Company changes its intended eastern plan and kneels to the prince as candidate king. This is no longer speculation about potential travel: the decision is made, but the fleet, landing, Dorne's acceptance and victories are future events.

**The golden skulls and blood contract.** Bittersteel's skull, Maelys's skull, the institutional memory of failed Blackfyre rebellions, and an agreement written before Strickland's command give stronger context than a simple mercenary job for Daenerys. However, neither the pact's exact wording nor Aegon's lineage are shown. “Golden Company backs a Blackfyre child” should remain **theory**, not sourced fact.

**Greyscale materially changes the timetable.** After contact near Sorrows, Connington now shows deadened, grey/black fingers. He cannot know whether he has five years or much less; his estimate of one to ten years is a belief drawn from sufferers, not a reliable life expectancy. His secrecy could infect others and drive rash decisions. Contrast Tyrion's painful tests in ADWD22.
