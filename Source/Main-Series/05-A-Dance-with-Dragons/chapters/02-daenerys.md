## Interpretation and rival tests

**Governing after emancipation requires conflicting justice decisions.** Dany's court hears cases from former masters and slaves; the inherited legal order, violence of the sack, the absence of effective livelihoods and an insurgent faction create incompatible claims. She protects weavers from an ex-master, withholds a mutilation punishment in a retrospective enslavement rape case, and denies a bereaved child justice under her blanket pardon. These decisions are *not morally equivalent*: the old law's classification of an enslaved woman as property does not erase rape, and a general amnesty creates real unjust outcomes.

**Security and food as constraints.** The Harpy murder is a real killing and factional attack, but Skahaz's proposed collective murders would not establish culprit identity. Barristan's organizational criticism is specific: field infantry are ill-equipped to find secret plotters. External forces are dispersed across the countryside, frontier and Lhazar trade mission while stocks decline. Hizdahr's pit-share purchases give a material incentive for his apparent public-spirited petition; this is not proof he leads the Harpy.

**Dragon harms.** Dany begins with theoretical compensation for sheep and goats and ends looking at a child's remains. The petitioner says a black winged shadow caused the death; Barristan agrees these are not sheep bones, and Dany's internal identification is powerful, but **the actual attack was not directly witnessed by the POV**. Drogon is strongly implicated, not formally observed killing this child here. This is a major ethical and practical failure mode for future rule.

**Prophecy skepticism.** The fear of a third betrayal is explicitly broad and unresolved. Naming Skahaz, Hizdahr, Reznak or Barristan as the 'third treason' is speculation unless independently corroborated.
