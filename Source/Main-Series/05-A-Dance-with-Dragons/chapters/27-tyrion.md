## Reading and implications

**Jorah's queen is Daenerys, not Cersei.** The destination is explicitly clarified at the waterfront widow's table, resolving the previous Selhorys cliffhanger. Mormont's desire to serve Dany is plausible from his history, but the widow notices he has other motives. His treatment of Tyrion as a chained slave is an ethical contradiction, not a benign rescue.

**A divided Volantis.** The elite economy depends on coerced labor, and a putative Volantene war fleet prepares to support the slavers. But Benerro's red temple, itself owner of enslaved initiates, has mobilized massive crowds for the Breaker of Chains; a faction of temple soldiers may not obey elite anti-red violence. The Widow explicitly foresees an internal conflict that differs from the triumph envisioned by Triarchs. The prophecy that the Selaesori Qhoran will never reach Qarth is a *claim*, not a completed shipwreck or a definite route change.

**Penny and collateral damage.** Tyrion discovers the other dwarf from Joffrey's wedding performance is Penny, whose brother Oppo was beheaded by sailors seeking Tyrion. Penny's grief and mistaken target reveal the human cost of a kingdom-wide bounty; the implication is causal but it does not make Tyrion responsible for personally arranging those sailors' murder.

**No savior identity certified.** Benerro preaches Daenerys's singular calling, while Melisandre calls Stannis the prophesied hero; more than one sect has a rival reading. Tyrion reasons Aegon is vulnerable if the red clergy insists on one dragon queen. This remains an inference, not an explicit church verdict.
