## Verified reversal

**Davos's death was staged.** Manderly explains the substitution: an expendable criminal of similar face was beheaded, physically altered with matching shortened fingers, tarred and displayed with an onion. Davos himself was alive and well-fed in a secret cell. This resolves the earlier sentence, while showing that Wyman could have surrendered Davos if the lie were exposed. The *public* beheading was real, but the victim was not Davos.

**A northern bargain founded on Stark legitimacy.** Manderly reports Wylis released; he has maintained a convincing façade before the Freys because captive kin, bribed men and a Lannister-born maester threaten him. His promise to support Stannis hinges on recovering a surviving son of Eddard and the direwolf. A wolf is being used as proof of identity rather than assumed magical evidence. No boy has yet been retrieved or Stannis alliance consummated.

**Wex's testimony revises the Fall of Winterfell.** As a mute witness who hid in the Winterfell heart tree, Wex has nonverbal means to dispute the Bolton account. Wyman credits Ramsay with Rodrik's death and the burned castle, where the world has blamed Theon. Wex also saw two Stark boys and two wolves escape, then followed a woman, boy and presumably wolf away from the main group. This strongly points to Rickon with Osha and Shaggydog, but the names are not spoken in the final conversation and must be cross-checked against Bran/Rickon scenes. Wex is fallible and the account reconstructed via chalk/yes-no; do not treat every detail as indisputable objective narration.

**The Skagos itinerary is inferred at this point.** The dagger fixes the direction on the map and Davos thinks of lands reputed to practice cannibalism. Though Skagos is the clear likely target by context, the chapter's quoted ending gives no direct named spoken locator; avoid pretending someone said the name explicitly.

**Guest gifts.** Wyman says he will give each Frey a palfrey as a gift at the wedding. That is a strong ritual cue because a host gift traditionally ends guest right. But the narration does not show any Frey accepting the gift, the journey or an assassination. Future Frey-pie claims must be sourced from later direct text.
