## Central theory, chronology, and ethical findings

**The Torgon Latecomer precedent matters.** At the kingsmoot Asha participated, so Rodrik warned her that a rival claim would breach the assembly's custom. Torgon the Latecomer reportedly overturned an election because he was *absent and excluded from standing*. Theon's torture and concealment made him absent from Euron's election; the patch of flayed skin gives Asha new reason to believe he lived after Winterfell. **The theory that Theon's nonattendance may void or challenge Euron's kingsmoot is plausible**, but neither Asha nor Tris explicitly files a formal challenge in this chapter. The precedent remains a claim from ironborn lore, not a demonstrated current court judgment.

**The north's assault is coordinated material warfare, not walking forest magic.** Masked northerners creep through the trees under branches, attack gates, and outflank the retreat. Asha initially associates the advancing 'trees' with children's magical tales, but rapidly understands the human camouflage. Trumpets, a great force and an image of flaming stag in her fading consciousness point toward Stannis's allied mountain clans; the captain and full outcome have not been independently named at the chapter's boundary.

**Asha's political captivity and moral contradictions.** Euron has arranged a political marriage to Erik without her presence, using a proxy seal by report. Her love of Qarl gives a genuine alternative to dynasty, complicated by the coercive initiation of the encounter in this chapter. At Deepwood she has held Sybelle's children as hostages and tortures a wounded Flint for troop estimates. These facts should stay alongside her intelligent efforts to spare her men through retreat. She is neither simply innocent victim nor simply cruel invader.

**Theon is alive.** Other direct Reek chapters prove Theon survives Ramsay's captivity; Asha's skin token independently links his suffering to Ramsay's political terror. Her initial reading of it as worse than death does not make him dead. Her belief that Robett and Galbart have died also conflicts with independent report of Robett at White Harbor.

**Outcome remains open.** Asha is struck by a blow near her temple, hears a trumpet and thinks in red and black flames. The chapter does not certify her death, capture, rescuer or later role. Do not classify it as a confirmed execution.
