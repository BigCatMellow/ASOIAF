## Evidence, causation, and theory discipline

**The boy's identity is operationally accepted but not biologically verified.** The claimant describes a specific infant exchange with a tanner's boy and Varys facilitating the substitution. Tyrion is already confident that he is presented as Rhaegar's son, and he manipulates him toward an invasion of Westeros rather than awaiting Daenerys in the east. The story of the substituted corpse is testimony from an interested alleged survivor, not a witnessed event from 17 years ago; a genuine Aegon, deceptive Aegon or Blackfyre-backed alternative must all remain testable.

**Tyrion's causal influence.** His cyvasse lesson strategically changes the boy's incentives by emphasizing Cersei's vulnerability and portraying the act of waiting as weakness. Jon Connington's subsequent decision must be read against this conversation, not attributed solely to spontaneity. The actual proof of effective landing remains unwritten.

**Volantis may not be a unitary enemy.** Yunkai's bribes and elite dependence on enslavement coexist with red-priest support for the Breaker of Chains; Benerro identifies Daenerys as Azor Ahai even while Melisandre champions Stannis. None of these priests has independent validation of an exclusive savior. The brothel and head-rubbing scenes expose the hierarchical social order Tyrion sees.

**Tyrion's ethics are severely compromised.** The young enslaved worker does not share his language, displays injuries and no active consent. Tyrion nevertheless has sex with her twice, acknowledges her vulnerability and heavily drinks. Neither his grief nor payment to the proprietor mitigates coerced sex. Contrast this with his saving Aegon from the stone man and his still-hidden poisonous mushrooms without assuming a consistent redemption arc.

**Immediate greyscale result is negative, but uncertain.** Painful needle tests show Tyrion can feel his digits; Haldon explicitly warns internal progression is possible. The bear-emblazoned knight who captures him is almost certainly Jorah Mormont by prior material, but this chapter does not name the captor outright.
