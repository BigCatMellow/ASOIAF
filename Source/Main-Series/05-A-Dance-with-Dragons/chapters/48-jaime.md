## Main narrative, decisions and consequences

Jaime secures Raventree without an assault by mediating a Blackwood surrender, limiting Bracken territorial demands and taking hostages from both sides. His outward promise of king's peace remains coercive, including a threat against Hoster if Lord Tytos aids outlaws. The former war destroyed Pennytree civilians' trust in every monarch. Brienne returns injured carrying Oathkeeper and presents a dangerous, uncorroborated claim about a rescued Stark girl, leaving Jaime's decision open.

## Rival readings and epistemic boundaries

**Brienne's invitation:** the chapter does *not* depict Jaime accompanying her. Her claim that she found the girl and that the Hound is holding her is a character report under potential coercion; compare AFFC 42, where Lady Stoneheart compels Brienne to choose. A Hound identity may refer to a helmeted impostor, not automatically Sandor Clegane. **Raventree:** Tytos says the huge leafless weirwood has drawn ravens for millennia despite alleged Bracken poisoning; the claim supports possible old-gods significance but does not prove active sentience here. **Blackwood/Bracken origin:** mutually partisan histories and contradictory Andal timelines show uncertainty; do not assume ancient legend is chronological fact. **Hostages:** Jaime mitigates some demands while still threatening to execute a child for his father's acts.
