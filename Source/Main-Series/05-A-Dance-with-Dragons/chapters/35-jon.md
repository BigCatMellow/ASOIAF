## Strategic and thematic reading

**The vows reinforce the political basis for sheltering free folk.** The weirwood circle is a dangerous place for oath-taking, but these southern-born recruits chose it freely. Jon's view that the Watch shields the *realms of men* includes starving wildlings. His previous critic Bowen Marsh has good reason to fear further deaths, given three ranger heads and serious fights between new recruits and original brothers.

**The corpses are not a completed experiment.** Jon deliberately carries two dead wildlings back and puts them in ice cells after his men question the decision. The clear possible inference is that he wants observable bodies should they rise again, though he does not explain the precise plan to Emmett. This must not be written as proof that the corpses actually reanimated or that cold storage makes them safe.

**Stannis's Deepwood victory is now triangulated.** Asha's defeated garrison, Robett Glover's report, Roose's admission and Stannis's own letter all establish the result. Stannis names Alysane Mormont's surprise attack on offshore longships and reports a force of roughly five thousand. The march against Winterfell remains future, and Roose's prior statement reveals Arnolf Karstark is bait for a trap.

**Jon crosses neutrality for the supposed Arya.** The letter confirms Jon knowingly released Mance and six spearwives to rescue a northern bride, a political mission shaped by Melisandre's prophecy. Jon believes she is his sister, while Theon has explicitly identified her as Jeyne Poole. His motives remain compassionate but he is taking an active role in a dispute beyond Wall defense.

**Ghost and awakened senses.** Jon occasionally experiences a sudden mingling of his and Ghost's scent abilities even when awake. This supports latent skinchanger talent but does not amount to a fully trained, consciously directed possession like Bran's.
