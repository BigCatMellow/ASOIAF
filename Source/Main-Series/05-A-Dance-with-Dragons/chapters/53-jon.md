## Main narrative, agency, and consequences

Jon makes a survival compact with Tormund and explicitly reframes the Watch oath as protection of all humans against the Others. It requires wealth, child hostages, settlement, food imports, and armed incorporation—each a practical answer that also sharpens internal resistance. Selyse rejects the treaty's religious limits; Marsh calls it treason but is tasked with carrying it out. Independent challenge remains open.

## Rival readings and tests

Tormund's oath may be a desperate bargain rather than lasting loyalty; entry conduct, captains' oaths, and hostage treatment will test it. The Wall officers may be safeguarding institutional survival rather than merely bigotry; food and command outcomes test Jon's feasibility. Val's greyscale warning is a cultural claim, not diagnosis.
