## Main narrative, agency, and consequences

Dany accepts a peace whose immediate terms preserve slavery outside her gates, restore pits, restrict her use of force, and bind key companions as hostages. She does not passively accept it: she begins a carefully concealed effort to turn the enemy's sellsword screen, using Meris as a signal to the Tattered Prince. That planning coexists with extraordinary insecurity—her own husband controls more of the city guard, Daario is exposed, and the dragons are nearly uncontainable. Her private presentation of the dragons gives Quentyn both motivation and dangerous information without giving him a promise.

## Rival readings and verification tests

**Hizdahr's peace:** it may be a genuine, brittle commercial settlement or a pause that entrenches Yunkish leverage; troop withdrawals, the market's fate, and hostage treatment will test it. **Plumm:** survival rather than cash is his stated reason, but it does not make a renewed deal impossible or reliable; later Second Sons actions are decisive. **Quentyn's dragon blood:** genealogy is a claim to descent, not evidence he can control a dragon. A later direct encounter—not lineage rhetoric—could test that proposition. **Pale mare:** the illness report and Quaithe's riddle do not establish supernatural causation.
