## Causal reading

**An anti-slavery queen uses state coercion.** Nine murders are real, but guilt of the wine seller and daughters is unproven. Dany escalates from gentle interrogation to sharp questioning and authorizes Skahaz's discretion after he explicitly proposes hurting daughters to pressure father. She levies a collective blood tax and demands hostage children from elite families while renaming them court attendants. Those actions must be examined beside her sincere duty toward freed people.

**Free fighters reveal a real autonomy problem.** Hizdahr's financial interests remain important, but Goghor and other former slaves independently ask to fight for their own reward. The Spotted Cat's worsened material living standard illustrates why abolition without economic opportunity is unstable. A decision to reopen pits is *not* made in this chapter; dismissing every volunteer as manipulated would erase their agency.

**Quaithe and glass candles.** A shadowbinder apparently communicates beyond normal guarded entry. This aligns with the visibly burning glass candle in AFFC 45, not a demonstration of which technology she used. Her cryptic list provides candidate matches to groups now moving toward Daenerys, but neither infallible prediction nor trustworthy allegiance has been established. The perfumed seneschal may be Reznak, a ship, or another bearer of the description; first answer is not proof.

**Hazzea and captivity.** Only the father saw the alleged Drogon attack, which leaves alternative explanations open. Yet Dany's own assessment of his restrained public disclosure makes fabrication less likely. Her decision to bury the child honorably and support relatives is undermined by compelling their silence. The chained dragons reveal the bodily costs of human safety and the uncertainty of exercising power through creatures she cannot reliably command.
