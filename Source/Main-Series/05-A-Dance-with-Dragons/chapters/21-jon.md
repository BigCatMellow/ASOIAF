## Causal and theoretical interpretation

**Conversion failed to erase the old gods.** Three fresh faces cut into ash, chestnut and oak—not magical weirwood trees—show a deliberate reassertion of religion soon after Stannis's coerced burning. The carvings are a statement by human actors unless independent evidence establishes magic or who made them. Guard infiltration is real, but no evidence identifies a spy or the first sculptor.

**The central tradeoff is no longer hypothetical.** A thousand people inhabit tunnels made for a hundred; infants have died, provisions are thin, and arguments over one apple threaten violence. Jon offers jobs and better food in exchange for service. That is a materially constrained bargain, not simply voluntary enlistment or forced slavery. A child's decision to enlist at about twelve raises an important question of capacity to consent.

**63 recruits, not 63 warriors.** The number includes elders, wounded people, children and spearwives; Jon proposes training and assigning them among four garrisons and opening three more. The chapter does **not** report that those transfers have happened. The Thenns refuse, showing the free folk are not one bloc. Halleck's personal choice triggers dozens of other recruits, consistent with Mance's earlier observation about earned prestige.

**Marsh's objections are partly justified.** Food and transport are finite, and he fears retaliation, sexual violence and divided loyalties during a future wildling attack. Jon's retort that armed spearwives can protect themselves does not guarantee they will. The calculation 'sixty-two ahead' is a rhetorical simplification of institutional risk, not a verified net-strength equation.

**Jon has lost his support system.** He needs Sam's archival scholarship, fears a shipwreck involving his friends, refuses higher quarters and carries an implicit suspicion of internal enemies through remembering Mormont's murder. None of this automatically predicts his specific attackers but it exposes a fragile management structure.
