## Plot and hypothesis resolution

**Moat Cailin's collapse is coercion plus a broken treaty.** Theon becomes a negotiator only because Ramsay trained him to fear mutilation and flight. The garrison is ill, starving, exhausted, leaderless and still tactically formidable; surrender saves Bolton casualties. Ramsay's written safe conduct is not merely disputed later: sixty-three corpses, including a man apparently Adrack Humble, and a parchment still sealed in one victim's mouth directly expose the deception. Theon saw men screaming at night but not each execution; that boundary remains.

**Theon has not forgotten who he is.** He remembers the Stark host, Smiler, the ironborn siege, and finally the real Arya's eyes. His recurrent Reek formulas show deliberate psychological captivity, not total loss of self. Ramsay's offer of release is a familiar trap after the staged hunt with Kyra.

**False Arya confirmed.** The girl presented to Ramsay is **Jeyne Poole**, identified directly by Theon with a reasoned memory of her identity. This changes the evidence status from a hypothesis to an observed POV identification; corroborate with additional accounts, but do not keep calling her a simply unknown girl. Why Roose and Ramsay think their political audience will accept the deception remains open. Jeyne's wishes are not independently accessible here.

**Roose's risk management.** Traveling behind a closed wagon with a decoy in armor and crossbowmen implies significant fear of attack on the route north; the scene does not establish that the same protective method is used at every future march.
