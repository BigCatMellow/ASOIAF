## Causal and identity audit

**Ranger mission provides a falsifiable test.** Melisandre's prediction precisely identifies three dead with removed eyes from the nine men Jon has sent. At the ending this has *not happened*; ADWD 31 will supply later outcome. The ranging has a defensive rationale because Pyke and Mallister report massing free folk, but it also exposes officers Jon distrusts. Sending Thorne may be both useful assignment and politically expedient.

**The duel is a physical inconsistency.** The apparent Lord of Bones displays extraordinary greatsword technique, strength and speed, defeats Jon and deliberately spares him. Combined with his reference to Stannis burning the wrong man, it supports the earlier substitute theory. At this point Jon still believes he is Rattleshirt; ADWD31 later directly reveals Mance beneath a glamour.

**Jon is ignorant of the false Arya.** Bolton's pink letter describes a Stark wedding. Jon sees a danger to *his actual eleven-year-old half-sister* while the reader now knows Theon identified Jeyne Poole. This gap may create risky decisions, especially because Jon believes his oath forbids involvement and thus withholds a reply.

**A wolf and a woman.** Melisandre's temporary rapport with Ghost and her direct statement about Jon's latent power are evidence of something unusual, but no successful command over the direwolf is demonstrated. Jon's momentary Ygritte misidentification can be grief, surface resemblance or influence; none should be locked as answer.
