## Core interpretation

**Identity as operational deception.** Illyrio introduces Tyrion as Yollo; Tyrion claims Hugor Hill; Young Griff claims Tyroshi maternal identity and dyes his hair blue; Griff wears dyed blue hair over visibly red roots and denies rank though Duck says he knighted him. These are meaningful discrepancies and concealment signals but no personal name is directly confirmed for Griff or Young Griff in this chapter. Their true status remains a next-chapter evidence issue.

**Illyrio's personal stake:** The six light chests are reportedly clothes, armor and gifts, and Illyrio sends candied ginger and speaks of missing the boy's wedding, explicitly swearing by Serra's hands. This is stronger personal connection than a hired courier arrangement. It still does not prove family ties to Serra or any Blackfyre descent.

**Unreliable historical narration:** Haldon invokes Maester Munkun for the Byron Swann incident, while Tyrion invokes a squire's contemporaneous letter and faction logic to place the encounter at Syrax rather than Vhagar. This is a miniature source-critical example: rival accounts do not resolve without the documents themselves.

**Agency and security:** Griff possesses a boat, the company is threatened by mobile Dothraki and river pirates, and Tyrion has no secure independent route. His value comes from knowledge of the Lannister court; Griff's acceptance is conditional. The explicit falsity of Tyrion's Joffrey poisoning boast cautions against taking every narration in his dialogue literally.
