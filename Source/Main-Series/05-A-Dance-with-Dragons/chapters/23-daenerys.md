## Causal audit

**The 90-day bargain is not an agreed marriage yet.** Dany sets a measurable test: no corpses from the Harpy campaign for ninety days and nights. Hizdahr claims he can negotiate peace and secure survival while Yunkai continues trafficking *other* people. That creates a moral limit to abolition, explicitly noticed by Dany. His willingness to cooperate is evidence of influence, **not proof he commands the Harpy**, and absence of murders in later periods must be checked for causation rather than assumed to reflect command.

**The Green Grace's position is powerful but not proven villainous.** She arrives with elite girls and has kin among the hostages, can organize elite marriage negotiations, and advocates sparing captive children. Her priorities naturally protect aristocratic families. This fits several theory alternatives: peacemaker, broker with insurgents, hidden leader or genuine religious mediator. None can be selected from this chapter alone.

**Rhaegar/Elia and Summerhall new context.** Barristan remembers Rhaegar treating Elia with fondness and Aerys/Rhaella marrying without affection at the command of Jaehaerys II because a woods witch foresaw a promised-prince descendant. The witch came with Jenny of Oldstones and is linked verbally to Summerhall. These are direct **Barristan testimony**, not independently witnessed prophetic truth or proof the witch is the Ghost of High Heart. The sequence supplies a dynastic mechanism behind tragedy beyond personal romance.

**Daario's brutality versus Daenerys's need for love.** Daario returns with actual partial success from Lhazar and losses/recruits from mercenary skirmishes. He also proposes a wedding massacre and to kill every great master without evidence; Dany correctly identifies the atrocity and bars him from her presence. Her desire for him and self-description as a monster should not erase the difference between contemplated and performed mass murder.
