## Main narrative, agency, and consequences

Cersei capitulates selectively: she admits sexual offenses already likely provable, avoids the claims that destroy Tommen's legitimacy, gains visitors and physical relief, but remains exposed to trial. Kevan's report makes clear that the realm now operates through a Tyrell-dependent regency. Cersei responds by pursuing the only route she sees to control a trial—an unnamed Kingsguard champion through Qyburn.

## Rival readings and tests

Kevan's political reorganization may be principled stabilization or Tyrell capture; council decisions and Mace's conduct test it. Cersei's Tyrion theory rests on fear rather than the reports Kevan gives. The Qyburn instruction signals a plan, not proof of a champion's identity or eligibility.
