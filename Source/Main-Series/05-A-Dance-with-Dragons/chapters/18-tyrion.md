## High-impact theory and narrative audit

**The bridge repeats.** The boat passes beneath the Bridge of Dream, sees it diminish behind, then encounters the same bridge again without sailing upstream. The entire crew recognizes the repeated structure; Haldon says this is impossible. This is stronger than Tyrion alone hallucinating. Possible explanations include spatial/temporal sorcery, river currents and disorientation in fog, or a narrative ambiguity about similar structures. No explanation is explicitly certified in the scene. It should be recorded as a live physical anomaly, not merely local superstition.

**The claimant is named, ancestry not authenticated.** Tyrion reasons that the young man is Prince Aegon Targaryen, supposedly killed as Rhaegar's child, and that Griff is former Hand Jon Connington. The pair react with fear/confirmation of secrecy, but no infant swap or independent genetic evidence is provided. A surviving genuine Aegon, a Blackfyre-descended impostor, or another deceived participant remain viable readings.

**Stone men are living victims.** Greyscale progression, hungry sufferers and mercy ships differ from undead wights at Bran's cave. The stone man attacks, Tyrion rescues the prince and is dragged underwater. This proves immediate immersion and contact, not death or infection. Avoid converting a cliffhanger into a death record.

**Poison continuity.** Tyrion explicitly still holds the suspicious mushrooms and contemplates poisoning Griff. His heroic defense of the prince is real, but does not erase active murderous thoughts and unresolved guilt.
