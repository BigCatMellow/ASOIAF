## Core interpretation

**Transport, debt and defection:** The failed Saan bargain ends in an actual withdrawal of the surviving Lysene ships. Severe storms have destroyed or scattered a fleet that started with twenty-nine vessels. Saan puts Davos ashore out of residual friendship while abandoning Stannis's campaign. His prediction that the king's rewards will kill Davos is a warning, not proof of future death.

**Hospitality versus political sale:** Godric demands Davos's identifying fingertips, then entertains him with bread and food. Davos understands this as guest right protecting him for a night. Godric's order to conceal the encounter leaves him room to choose a side. The Night Lamp is both a coastal safety beacon and part of a known world of false lights; this local wrecking knowledge is not itself proof that Stannis knows or plans a later ice-battle trap.

**The Manderly narrative is reported and contested.** Godric reports Freys on the Lionstar, a bag of bones and marriage talk with Wyman. His interpretation that Manderly has truly submitted does not prove Wyman's private loyalty. Later ADWD 29 includes Wyman's different intentions, but those future facts must not be imported into Davos's own current knowledge. Davos's inability to send word of Tywin's death similarly matters for Stannis's information set.

**Jon Snow's rumored mother:** The fisherman's daughter story is an important alternative to more prominent theories about Jon's parentage. Godric repeats a local story heard after Ned's wartime passage. No mother, birth, pregnancy, naming or paternity is witnessed here. A woman using the name Jon Snow after Jon Arryn is compatible with folklore, an erroneous identification, concealment or an unrelated bastard; none is established. This is an evidence node, not a challenge that automatically disproves R+L=J.

**Political prudence over moral certainty:** Godric's father allegedly protected Ned only after calculating the rebels might prevail. Davos wins similar silence not ideological fealty. Hospitality creates space for negotiation, not a promise of military aid.
