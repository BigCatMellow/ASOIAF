## Plot and competing interpretations

**Material capacity drives military policy.** The food inventory looks enormous, but the ration model assumed a smaller Watch and three or four years; king's people, refugees and the free folk make those stores inadequate. Jon orders 25% ration cuts *during autumn*. The problem is not hypothetical: scurvy, ammunition, livestock, merchant credit and the cost of hospitality now constrain his independence. The crowd of hungry dependents is not solely Jon's choice; Stannis's stronger garrison could requisition the food.

**A changed war plan and a neutrality boundary.** Jon opposes marching on Dreadfort because warning signals, the Bolton army and its stone fortifications undermine surprise. Instead he trades some three hundred free folk for Stannis's promise to leave them, names two to three thousand mountaineers, offers guides, and recommends Deepwood Motte. This is a genuine political/military intervention. It may save the wildlings from a sacrificial vanguard, but it also makes the Watch's professed neutrality harder to defend. No victory has occurred yet.

**Ruby-bound identity problem.** The man introduced as Rattleshirt wears a ruby that pulses with Melisandre's and speaks of singing. Coupled with the odd execution behavior in [ADWD 10](10-jon.md), this makes an identity-changing glamour a strong hypothesis. The chapter does not explicitly prove it or identify the man as Mance; preserve direct observation versus inference.

**Northern coalition conditions.** Mors's claim that he will not fight Hother and his demand for Mance's skull are real obstacles. The Karstark intelligence about a weak Dreadfort is a report from an interested lord. Stannis's view of Arnolf as a worthy Stark-blooded lord is not objective evidence of allegiance. Contrast Dynastic Bracken-style bargaining with Jon's counsel to ask mountain chiefs personally.
