## Causal reading

**Wards have discriminating consequences.** The cave is a refuge from hostile wights, but its protection excludes the helpful undead Coldhands as well. Leaf explicitly confirms he died long ago. This strengthens the physical reality of a magical threshold without proving every old Wall story, every Coldhands identity theory, or what specific spell/agent enforces the barrier.

**Combat clarifies corpse mechanics.** Black-cloaked wights rise from snow and persist after losing limbs or heads, while flame forces them back. A severed hand continues to move. Wights can attack Coldhands despite his own dead condition. Distinguish his purposeful speech and defensive action from their attack patterns.

**Bran's human possession becomes less defensible.** Bran saves Jojen and the party through Hodor, but hears Hodor whimper while steering his body. His thought of expressing love to Meera from Hodor's mouth hints at merging his desires with a captive person's voice. The emergency explains his decision; it does not grant consent or remove an ongoing ethical cost.

**The greenseer is not a simple mentor archetype.** A one-eyed red-eyed crow in black, roots through flesh, hidden skulls and a centuries-old child-of-forest guide supply unusually strong Bloodraven clues. Still, the pale lord is not named Brynden Rivers here. His mild uncertainty when Bran asks whether he is the three-eyed crow is important, and the claim to lifelong surveillance needs corroboration. Bran's desire to walk is definitively denied, replaced by a promise to fly whose meaning remains untested.
