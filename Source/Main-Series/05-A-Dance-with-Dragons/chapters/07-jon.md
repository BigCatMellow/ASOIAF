## Major causal and theoretical junctions

**Gilly's coerced exchange:** Jon uses a burn demonstration and threatens her own infant to force Gilly to carry the supposed king's-blood baby away. Gilly's words at departure strongly support the substitution, but Jon admits he cannot himself distinguish which child she holds. This creates a critical knowledge boundary that AFFC Sam chapters help resolve. His protective objective is credible, his chosen abuse of the mother and infant morally real.

**Archive uncertainty:** The discrepancy of 998 purported Watch commanders against an older list of 674, late prose records of the Long Night, and dragons/dragonglass traditions all warn against treating old legends as precise historical facts. The last hero's dragonsteel might be Valyrian steel, but neither Sam nor Jon proves it on this page.

**Command and political agency:** Jon signs a letter to Tommen while relying on Stannis, dispatches skilled scholars, and takes steps to garrison empty castles under Watch officers with some king's men. These are difficult real-world choices with costs in recruitment, food, communication and legitimacy. Jon's belief in Tywin's current influence is inaccurate from the reader's vantage.

**Slynt:** Slynt defies a legitimate order twice, publicly and insultingly. Jon offers him actual command and time to comply, but also carries personal grievance over Ned. He switches from a hanging order to personally beheading Slynt, an act consonant with Ned's example. Stannis's nod reflects acknowledgment, not proof of unanimity or a long-term solution to Watch factionalism.

**Aemon's counsel is no blanket pardon:** The recurring admonition to grow from boy to man stresses responsibility, not moral immunity. Jon's guilty nightmare and deception of Sam preserve competing moral readings of his leadership.
