## Major sequence

- **VERIFIED:** A badly wounded Varamyr continues to experience his wolf pack while his human body fails. His history shows deliberate domination of animals and other people and a violent inheritance of another skinchanger's wolf.
- **CHARACTER TEACHING:** Haggon forbade eating human flesh while occupying an animal, mating in an animal's body, and invading a person's body. His ethical rules are real teachings, not immutable natural laws.
- **CHARACTER TEACHING + PARTIAL CORROBORATION:** Haggon explains that a skinchanger may continue in an animal after human death but gradually loses human memories. Varamyr's narrated transition into a wolf directly supports second-life survival; **its ultimate duration and degree of memory loss are not yet established by this scene alone**.
- **VERIFIED:** Varamyr attempts to seize the human Thistle as his body dies. She resists horrifically; he does not establish stable control. His dying vision includes a momentary perspective through a weirwood.
- **VERIFIED:** Varamyr persists among the wolves after bodily death and observes animated dead, including Thistle's corpse. Her returned gaze is eerie, but the text does not settle whether a wight recognizes his identity.

## Mechanics, ethics and limits to track

| Detail | Supported inference and caution |
|---|---|
| Canine bonds differ from other beasts | Training and species affect control; wolves are not simply tame dogs |
| Other animals carry distinctive effects | Haggon's taxonomy is experiential opinion and should be tested rather than made absolute |
| Previously inhabited eagle retained Orell's hatred | Residual mental imprint is possible; does not establish uniform persistence of personhood |
| Dead skinchanger can enter a surviving wolf | Direct narrative precedent for a disembodied continuation |
| Human possession violently resisted | Central evidence for consent and the ethical cost of Bran's use of Hodor |
| Flash of weirwood sight near death | Important for greenseer/Old Gods mechanisms, but the metaphysical intermediary is unconfirmed |
| Wighted Thistle | Reanimation and skinchanging are both on the page, but are not demonstrated to be the same process |

## Crucial cross-POV tests

1. **Jon and Ghost:** If Jon's human body dies after `ADWD 69`, a second life is *possible by analogy*, not actually shown in Jon's last chapter. Check Jon/Ghost bond, spatial proximity, and Haggon's warnings before adopting a resurrection scenario.
2. **Bran and Hodor:** Varamyr's human-body attempt supplies a severe moral counterexample to any claim that skinchanging another person is harmless.
3. **Others' necromancy:** Separate wight reanimation from a skinchanger's animal afterlife. The novel has not established one shared mechanism.

**Disposition:** second-life phenomenon `VERIFIED` for Varamyr; Jon application `THEORY`; exact metaphysics `UNKNOWN`.
