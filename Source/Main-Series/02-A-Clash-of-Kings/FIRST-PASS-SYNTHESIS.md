## 1. Major causality: a war the winners do not control

- **Renly's enormous alliance** is undone after his sudden death at Storm's End; characters' accounts of the event differ, but the eyewitness shadow killing and [42 Davos](chapters/42-davos.md)'s shadow-birth mechanism are of unusual supernatural and political importance. The Reach is subsequently realigned, helping Tywin defeat Stannis at the **Blackwater**.
- **Tyrion's defense combines manufacturing, engineering, intelligence and a disastrous tradeoff**: a river-mouth chain, wildfire-filled hulks, waterfront demolitions, harsh anti-treason repression and sorties. [58 Davos](chapters/58-davos.md) directly witnesses the disaster; [59 Tyrion](chapters/59-tyrion.md) explains the intended chain timing. Crucially, [61 Tyrion](chapters/61-tyrion.md) discovers that chained wrecks also become a **temporary bridge** for enemy soldiers. The naval inferno is only a partial victory.
- **Edmure holds the river crossings** in [45 Catelyn](chapters/45-catelyn.md), but Tywin then moves **southeast** and reportedly joins the relief army at Blackwater ([62 Sansa](chapters/62-sansa.md)). The precise allocation of military responsibility for Tywin's route remains a **future cross-check against actual orders**, not an already proved assertion that Edmure caused the outcome.
- **The public rescuer 'Renly'** is a figure wearing the slain Renly's green armor and antlers according to [62 Sansa](chapters/62-sansa.md). The wearer remains **unnamed in the current close-read passage**; the event supports organized morale/political deception more than literal resurrection, pending subsequent direct identification.
- **Tywin becomes Hand, Tyrells enter ruling institutions, and Petyr acquires enormous nominal riverlands authority** ([65 Sansa](chapters/65-sansa.md)). Titles, public honors and alliances often outrun actual control of contested castles and subjects.

## 2. Concealment and false public knowledge: the strongest resolved chain

- **Theon raids Torrhen's Square as bait** and takes undermanned Winterfell ([24 Theon](chapters/24-theon.md), [46 Bran](chapters/46-bran.md)). Hostages Bran and Rickon appear to escape north; the direwolves' spoor reaches a stream **without the expected human footprints** ([50 Theon](chapters/50-theon.md)).
- **Theon kills the miller's sons and fabricates the Stark boys' deaths** ([56 Theon](chapters/56-theon.md)); both Catelyn ([55](chapters/55-catelyn.md)) and Tyrion ([54](chapters/54-tyrion.md)) respond to sincerely or politically used false reports. The false bodies' clothing and injuries permit observers to assume identity without verifying anatomy.
- [51 Jon](chapters/51-jon.md) places a **crypt refuge inside Winterfell** in Ygritte's Bael-the-Bard tale, a nearby but not conclusive symbolic clue. [69 Bran](chapters/69-bran.md) then **directly confirms** the fugitives really doubled back to **Ned's crypt**, sending wolves onward as a decoy. Luwin retrospectively noticed the substitute corpses' legs had different musculature. This sequence is a model for evidence-weighted story inference: **object clues, physical negatives, legend and later first-person confirmation**.
- The 'servant Reek' is suspicious in [46/50/56 Theon](chapters/50-theon.md) but **his identity is resolved by his own on-page confession and appearance as Ramsay Snow** in [66 Theon](chapters/66-theon.md). Ramsay's men destroy Rodrik's relief army and burn Winterfell, while protecting the Frey boys. Theon caused the occupation and child murders but **Ramsay ordered the final fire**. Do not flatten these into one actor.
- **Separating two heirs** is the maester's last strategic act. Rickon and Shaggydog leave with Osha; Bran and Summer go north with Hodor and the Reeds. The locations of their subsequent refuges are **not established in ACOK**.

## 3. Extraordinary perception: observed mechanics vs interpretation

- Bran's wolf perception is directly narrated; he can remain with Summer for **three days**, but **his human body is not fed when the wolf eats**, and he risks starvation ([69 Bran](chapters/69-bran.md)). A sustained magical skill has a concrete **physical cost**.
- [53 Jon](chapters/53-jon.md) depicts a **Bran-faced three-eyed weirwood** apparently enabling Jon to see through Ghost; a real eagle attack leaves **corresponding claw wounds** on Ghost. [69 Bran](chapters/69-bran.md) independently recalls touching Ghost and speaking to Jon. The contact is **well corroborated**, whereas control, training and the exact role of a third party remain open.
- [68 Jon](chapters/68-jon.md) records **Ygritte's specific assertion that the eagle 'was a man'** before Jon killed a wildling at the mountain post. This strongly supports a human skinchanger's consciousness persisting through a bird, but is still a **character explanation** rather than direct internal eagle POV.
- **Jojen's predicted ironborn 'sea'** is partly and then thoroughly matched by **Alebelly, Mikken and Chayle** dying in the Winterfell conquest, while the related **flayed-faces** warning has to be checked with precise timing and targets. Fulfillment of one symbolic vision does **not** grant all dreams infallibility.
- The **returning-magic hypothesis** has multiple independent or semi-independent traces: **Quaithe** speaks of renewed magic after dragons ([40 Daenerys](chapters/40-daenerys.md)); **Hallyne** reports improving wildfire spells and wonders about dragons without personally knowing they returned ([49 Tyrion](chapters/49-tyrion.md)); **Xaro** reports glass candles burning and stranger marvels in Qarth ([63 Daenerys](chapters/63-daenerys.md)). The last cluster is hearsay, and **correlation is not complete causal proof**.

## 4. Prophecy and language: preserve each unsolved referent

- [48 Daenerys](chapters/48-daenerys.md) contains the highest-density prophecy register: the **wolf-headed banquet**, an infant **Aegon** and harp-bearing prince Rhaegar-like figure, **three dragon heads**, nine slots in **fires / mounts / treasons**, a **cloth dragon**, **blue flower at a wall of ice**, a shadowless sword-bearing king, and many other visions.
- The visions happen after **shade-of-the-evening** and amid **deceptive, predatory warlocks**; Pyat admits they may include futures that never happen. **An exact-looking image is not automatically a literal future event**.
- [63 Daenerys](chapters/63-daenerys.md) adds two context tests: **Jorah recognizes Rhaegar's harp** and identifies Aegon as Rhaegar/Elia's son, while Dany explains a **cloth dragon is a mummer's theatrical prop**. The latter sharpens a false-claimant theory but does not itself name a political claimant.
- **Blue roses** recur in Ygritte's tale ([51 Jon](chapters/51-jon.md)), **Lyanna's dream crown** ([56 Theon](chapters/56-theon.md)), and Dany's **flower at an icy wall** ([48 Daenerys](chapters/48-daenerys.md)). The three-link motif merits high-priority **Jon/Lyanna/Wall** tests in later volumes, but **none of these individual passages states a genealogical equation**.
- **Bael's other material**—hidden Stark lineage, child killing parent, an unnamed lord flaying a Stark—may be embellished. Only the **crypt-hiding technique** is independently mirrored by the princes' real escape within this book.

## 5. Political loyalty, false sponsorship and personal agency

- **Catspaw dagger:** [55 Catelyn](chapters/55-catelyn.md) records Jaime directly admitting **he pushed Bran**, while denying he sent the subsequent catspaw. His recall of **Tyrion's failed wager and Robert's displayed knife** contradicts Littlefinger's claim that Tyrion won the knife. **The attacker sponsor remains unproved**, and a dagger ownership chain cannot stand in for a killer's commission.
- **Mandon Moore:** [61 Tyrion](chapters/61-tyrion.md) witnesses Moore deliberately trying to kill Tyrion, twice, and [67 Tyrion](chapters/67-tyrion.md) adds Podrick's confirmation Moore drowned during the rescue. Tyrion **suspects Cersei ordered it**, but no payment, order or third-party confession is on page.
- **Dontos's backer and hairnet:** Sansa's betrothal to Joffrey is formally voided but **her hostage status continues**. Dontos schedules an escape for Joffrey and Margaery's wedding and gives her a delicate silver net with **deep-purple 'black amethysts from Asshai'**, calling it justice and vengeance ([65 Sansa](chapters/65-sansa.md)). Its function is **unresolved at ACOK's endpoint**, however ominous the phrasing.
- **Frey and Bolton drift:** [64 Arya](chapters/64-arya.md) shows Freys arguing Robb should surrender, **Elmar's promised princess marriage canceled**, and Roose ordering Glover/Tallhart toward distant Duskendale. These are meaningful coalition-fracture data points; a **specific private pact to betray Robb has not been directly shown in those scenes**.
- **Qhorin's ordered infiltrator:** [68 Jon](chapters/68-jon.md) explicitly instructs Jon to **appear to break Watch vows** and join the wildlings as a spy, then Jon directly kills Qhorin in a coerced test. The outward betrayal must **not** be misindexed as spontaneous abandonment of the Watch.
- **Danger behind supposed protection:** Cersei confesses she had **Ilyn Payne ready to kill herself and Sansa if Stannis won** ([60 Sansa](chapters/60-sansa.md)); Sandor both threatens and at times protects Sansa ([62 Sansa](chapters/62-sansa.md)). The book resists simple categories of protector/villain even while preserving victims' lack of consent.
