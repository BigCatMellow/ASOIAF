## Main narrative and causal consequences

1. **VERIFIED:** At Dragonstone, statues of the **Seven** are burned by Melisandre's religious faction while **Stannis** watches. The sept and stained glass were destroyed by the queen's men. **Ser Hubard Rambton and three sons** resisted and killed four attackers before being overwhelmed; **Guncer Sunglass**, refusing to support Stannis after the desecration, has been imprisoned with Septon Barre and Rambton's two surviving sons. The burning is a **coercive political rupture**, not a harmless ceremonial change.
2. **SEPTON BARRE'S ACCOUNT:** The statues were made from masts of the earliest Targaryen ships from Valyria. Even if not independently authenticated here, this locates the destroyed objects within Dragonstone's remembered dynastic history. **Davos** is personally disturbed though only loosely observant of the Seven. He knows **Cressen** tried to poison Melisandre, contrary to the circulating claim that Cressen fell merely for impiety; whether supernatural protection explains her survival is **not proved from Davos's inference**.
3. **VERIFIED / RELIGIOUS CHARACTER CLAIM:** Melisandre declares the red comet, long summer and darkness signals that **Azor Ahai** has returned, and orders Stannis to take a sword from the burning image of the Mother. Stannis uses a **thick glove and leather cape** to retrieve it; his glove catches fire, the blade's surface flames die, and **Devan and Bryen Farring** later wrap the charred sword away. The queen's party acclaims it as **Lightbringer**. The scene confirms a staged extraction of burning metal, **not supernatural identity or magical properties**.
4. **VERIFIED:** Davos watches highborn lords' guarded reactions: **Celtigar, Velaryon, young Bar Emmon**, the queen's men and others disagree in outward affect. Lowborn Davos remains excluded from their private confidence despite Stannis's patronage. He owes knighthood, lands and advancement of his family to the king and carries his **amputated fingertips in a pouch** as a self-understood luck token. His sons command ships and serve in other capacities, but he must control their expressions of dissent.
5. **VERIFIED:** Davos's sons find their ships' **water casks newly made from green pine**, which they fear will spoil the water. The queen's men allegedly requisitioned the seasoned wood. This is a mundane, campaign-relevant supply problem caused by court favoritism, not an omen. Dragonstone's harbor is crowded with vessels including **Fury, Lord Steffon, Stag of the Sea, Pride of Driftmark, Red Claw**, Davos's **Black Betha**, and Salladhor Saan's Lysene ships.
6. **SAAN'S INTELLIGENCE/ASSESSMENT:** Saan has received trade news via **Bird of Thousand Colors**: **Tyrion has removed Janos Slynt** and installed an iron-handed commander (cross-check: directly depicted in `ACOK 8`); he judges King's Landing thinly defended and urges an immediate strike. He reports **Renly is marching from Highgarden with his bride and a mighty host**, and says he has already informed Stannis. The King's Landing strategic appraisal is Saan's opinion; future movement and capture are unverified.
7. **VERIFIED / ACCOUNT OF LEGEND:** Saan disputes the authenticity of Stannis's burning sword and recounts the **Azor Ahai / Nissa Nissa** tale: first blade shatters in water, second through a lion's heart, third allegedly forged through sacrificing the hero's wife. **This is a narrated religious legend, not eyewitness history or a proven requirement for a future magical weapon.** Davos pictures his own **Marya** and rejects such a sacrifice as an unacceptable cost.
8. **VERIFIED:** Stannis summons Davos to the Painted Table. Davos cannot read the prepared written proclamation; **Maester Pylos** reads it. It declares Stannis the lawful heir and alleges **Joffrey, Tommen and Myrcella** result from incest between Jaime and Cersei. Stannis demands correction of ceremonial language: Jaime is still a **knight**, but pretending Robert was a beloved brother is an untruth. This simultaneously displays Stannis's exactitude and bitterness.
9. **VERIFIED (PLANS, NOT ARRIVALS):** Stannis proposes using **117 ravens** to disseminate 117 proclamations; Davos's **Black Betha** is to carry letters to **Gulltown, Fingers, Three Sisters and White Harbor**, **Dale's Wraith** to southern ports as far as the Arbor, and **Allard's Lady Marya** across the narrow sea to **Braavos and other Free Cities**. Letters are to be displayed on septs and inns, read aloud where possible, and replenished if needed by copying. Stannis knows ravens may perish or lords may burn the letters. **No recipient acceptance is yet shown**.
10. **VERIFIED / STANNIS'S ARGUMENT:** Davos warns that the published incest case **lacks publicly compelling proof**. Stannis points to recognized royal bastard **Edric Storm** at **Storm's End**, supposedly strongly resembling Robert, but admits making that comparison visible to the realm is difficult. He also concedes the wording invoking R'hllor may alienate old-gods and Seven-worshipping subjects. Davos speaks plainly because Stannis explicitly asks for advice rather than flattery.
11. **VERIFIED PERSONAL DISCLOSURE:** Stannis explains that he lost religious faith when his parents died on the **Windproud** in a storm. He considers justice the work of human beings, yet uses Melisandre because she commands fear and *may* wield genuine power. He feels outnumbered by other kings, his lords unable to secure support, and chooses to test a new instrument rather than accept defeat. His childhood injured goshawk **Proudwing**, derided by Robert and replaced on great-uncle **Harbert's** advice, supplies the analogy for trying a different hawk: Melisandre. **This is political pragmatism amid need, not proof Stannis is a converted believer.**

## Rival readings, authority and next tests

- **Stannis as zealous convert:** The burning ceremony and letter's invocation permit that public reading, but he **explicitly disavows conviction in gods** privately and values Melisandre's effectiveness. Alternative: grief and military desperation lead to selective religious patronage, with escalating dependence as a danger.
- **Authentic Lightbringer:** Melisandre proclaims it; Davos witnesses ordinary protections and dying blade-flames; Saan calls it merely burnt metal. Both magical certainty and total certainty of fakery exceed what is established here.
- **Azor Ahai/Nissa Nissa as a future demand:** The legend explicitly connects sacrifice to magical power, but its historicity and predictive role are unverified. Davos's refusal provides a human cost test if any later character tries to invoke it.
- **Stannis's claim:** The published allegations match a line of inquiry known elsewhere; **the scene establishes distribution plans rather than popular belief, court response or successful deliveries**. Edric's whereabouts and whether messengers can actually show him remain material.
- **Davos:** His gratitude is not blind approval. He serves Stannis yet questions religious coercion, proof and logistics. Evaluate future conscience-vs-duty episodes against this established independence.
- **Next checks:** `ACOK 22 (Catelyn)` for Renly's progress; `ACOK 42/58 (Davos)` for consequences of Stannis's strategic and magical gambles; later chapters for Edric Storm's political value, letter reception, Saan's debt and any fulfillment of the cask warning.
