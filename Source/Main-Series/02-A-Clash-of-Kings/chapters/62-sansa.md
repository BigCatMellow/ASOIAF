## Narrative and high-priority event chain

1. **WAR NEWS / NEAR DEFEAT:** Severely wounded **Lancel Lannister** enters the Queen's Ballroom with blood spilling beneath his arm and says the **battle is lost**. He believes **Tyrion has died** when the bridge of wrecked galleys broke apart and **Mandon Moore** may be gone as well. `ACOK 61 Tyrion` establishes Tyrion alive immediately after Moore's attack, so Lancel's death report is **mistaken or premature**, while his description of the shattered bridge corresponds to an actual direct event.
2. **IMPACT OF JOFFREY'S WITHDRAWAL:** Lancel explicitly blames **Cersei's order to bring Joffrey off the field** (`ACOK 60`) for **hundreds of gold cloaks abandoning their posts**, explaining that seeing the king withdraw broke their morale. **Osney Kettleblack** separately reports defenders fleeing, some killing their officers, mobs at **the Iron Gate and Gate of the Gods**, and widespread disorder in **Flea Bottom**. The causation is **Lancel's informed firsthand judgment**, reinforced by contemporaneous troop reports but not an independent controlled causal test; it nevertheless substantiates a real strategic cost to the royal recall.
3. **CONFLICTING BATTLE REPORTS:** Osney says **Stannis's men may be fighting other men on both sides of the river**; he admits it is unclear who is fighting whom. Some **royal guards have fallen back inside** the city, **Sandor is missing**, the **King's Gate is attacked again**, and the riverfront is claimed by Stannis's troops. These are real-time reports amid confusion, not a final verified battle map or proof Stannis already rules the capital.
4. **CERSEI CLOSES THE INNER KEEP:** Cersei orders **Osfryd** to raise **Maegor's Holdfast drawbridge** and bar its doors, denying entry even to women who earlier went to the sept to pray unless she permits it. She demands **Joffrey moved into Maegor's**, despite Lancel's plea that a visible king might sustain defenders. She chooses **maternal custody and inner-castle security over public command presence**.
5. **VIOLENCE AGAINST LANCEL:** When Lancel continues arguing, Cersei **deliberately strikes his open wound** and pushes past him to leave the ballroom, abandoning her protective public role and **not returning during the chapter**. This is directly narrated. Her dismissal may reflect genuine fear for Joffrey, but it also visibly destabilizes the elite court she had gathered for morale.
6. **SANSA PROVIDES ACTUAL LEADERSHIP:** As frightened women and children cry, **Sansa rises to reassure them**—describes the moat, walls and drawbridge, says Joffrey is back and the struggle continues, and **asks Moon Boy to perform**. She knows Cersei may not return but says she will, deliberately telling **a calming untruth**. Her comfort is **an observable act of public care**, contrasted with Cersei's coercive fear strategy in `ACOK 60`.
7. **SANSA HELPS THE WOUNDED ENEMY:** Sansa finds **Lancel bleeding afresh** and directs servants to carry him to **Maester Frenken**. One servant abandons them, but she helps another raise the knight. Lancel is politically loyal to the family holding her prisoner, yet she **does not wish him dead**. Her compassion does not imply she endorses Joffrey's claim or lacks strategic understanding; she just saved a badly wounded person.
8. **DONTOS'S COUNSEL:** **Dontos** quietly tells Sansa to go to **her bedchamber**, lock herself in and await his help after the battle. This is **advice, not a completed rescue**. She complies while aware the queen's executioner **Ilyn Payne** might be sent for her. On the way she sees a **red-cloaked guard carrying stolen silver and a jeweled cup**, direct independent evidence that some supposed royal defenders are already **looting their own holdfast**, as Cersei predicted.
9. **THE HOUND WAITING:** In her dark chamber **Sandor Clegane** grabs Sansa, smothers her threatened scream, and warns he will kill her if she cries out. His face and clothes are bloodied, and he is **drunk and traumatized by fire**. He admits **he has lost the battle for himself**, not that he knows which army finally wins. He plans to leave **King's Landing through the Iron Gate** and go north or away from flames. His **white Kingsguard-style cloak** may facilitate passing guards, but **his actual exit route is not observed**.
10. **SANDOR'S OFFER AND COERCION:** He says **he could keep Sansa safe** on the road and threatens potential attackers, but he **restrains and shoves her** on her bed and draws a **dagger against her throat**, demanding a song under threat of her life. Sansa briefly fears he may kiss her, but **no kiss is narrated**. Treat the offer of safety as occurring within **an explicitly threatening encounter**; protection is not a free or accepted bargain.
11. **SONG CHANGES THE SCENE:** Unable to recall Florian and Jonquil's song, Sansa sings a **Mother's mercy hymn** from the sept, praying to stop weapons and soothe rage. Sandor **removes the dagger**. She instinctively touches his face and feels **wetness that is not blood**, suggesting tears without an explicit statement of his emotional cause. The song provides a striking alternative to both the combat cry and Cersei's use of fear as power. **Sansa does not go with Sandor**.
12. **THE WHITE CLOAK LEFT BEHIND:** Sandor tears off his **white cloak**, exits Sansa's room, and leaves it **blood- and fire-stained** on the floor. Sansa later uses it **as a blanket while shivering**. This is an actual portable object **left in her possession**, not proof he has entered a specified organization or that he and Sansa had a sexual encounter. It also materializes his break from service to Joffrey, though **formal resignation or departure from city is not yet witnessed**.
13. **BELLS AND CHANGE IN PUBLIC MOOD:** Near dawn Sansa hears **bells ringing joyfully** and men cheering across King's Landing, including the **Great Sept** and Red Keep. She recognizes the bells sound unlike mourning knells at Robert's death. The abrupt change is **direct public evidence of a victory celebration**, not yet a source confirming each claimed army deployment or enemy casualty.
14. **DONTOS'S VICTORY ACCOUNT:** Dontos arrives drunk and celebrates the city's rescue. He says a **combined Lannister and Tyrell army** arrived by **roseroad and the riverbank**, with **Tywin's western troops, Mace Tyrell's Reach forces, Randyll Tarly and other powerful houses** overwhelming Stannis's remaining forces. This account is **corroborated in broad direction by the city's bells and relief**, but the exact disposition—Tywin right, Randyll center, Mace left—and details from heraldry are **Dontos's reported information**, not Sansa's battlefield observation.
15. **THE 'RENLY' APPARITION AND MISIDENTIFICATION:** Dontos reports the vanguard was led by **a figure wearing dead Renly Baratheon's green armor and crowned antler helm**, mistaken by troops for **Renly returned to life**. He claims this figure **slew Guyard Morrigen** and other knights, with the story passing from **Osney → Osmund → court**, and reportedly supported by **Balon Swann and gold-cloak accounts**. **Renly's death has been directly established** in `ACOK 33 Catelyn`; the figure's **true identity is not named here**. A disguised living commander, symbolic reenactment, ghost or unreliable camp rumor are rival possibilities—**never record Renly as resurrected on the strength of a drunken retelling**.
16. **STANNIS'S OUTCOME STILL UNCERTAIN:** Dontos says **Stannis might be dead or might have fled**, emphasizing that **no one he trusts knows for sure**. Stannis's exact location and survival, despite the rout of his main assault, are **not verified in this chapter**. Distinguish **defeat of the assault** from proof of the king's personal death.
17. **ENDING:** The capital's defense apparently succeeds thanks to **external reinforcement**, not exclusively the wildfire trap. Sansa is **not executed by Ilyn**, is still within the Red Keep and has a pending Dontos escape plan. **Sandor has left her room** but his ultimate route, allegiance and return remain unknown. Her daily danger has changed rather than ended.

## Theory questions and next verification

- **Renly's armor and morale warfare:** Renly's likeness can exploit loyalties of his former followers now with Stannis and of Tyrell bannermen. But **the actual wearer's identity** and whether soldiers believe in literal resurrection must be checked against later explicit testimony. The heraldic details invite **organized deception**, not certainty about magic.
- **Cersei's command failure:** Lancel directly links withdrawal of a visible king to Watch panic, making political display itself a battlefield resource. Subsequent casualty and rescue accounts can clarify the magnitude.
- **Sandor and 'unkiss':** There is **a threat, a song, a cheek-touch and a cloak**, but no on-page kiss. Later remembered versions should be compared as **memory alteration**, not silently imported backward into the original scene.
- **Relief and Edmure:** Earlier `ACOK 45 Catelyn` showed Tywin march southeast after failed crossings; `ACOK 62` now reports an army arriving at the Blackwater. This establishes a **plausible temporal and geographic link** but not the complete route, orders or responsibility for Edmure's earlier decision.
- **Next:** subsequent Tywin/Tyrion/Sansa references for Renly armor identity, Stannis survival, King's Landing power redistribution and Sansa's escape sponsor.
