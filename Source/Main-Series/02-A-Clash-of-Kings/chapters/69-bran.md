## Whole-chapter chronological record

1. **DIREWOLF WITNESSES THE FALL:** The chapter opens within **Summer's wolf consciousness** alongside **Shaggydog** outside smoking and burning Winterfell, sniffing dead horses, men and ruined houses. Summer briefly sees a **great winged 'snake' and river of flame** in the smoky sky; this is **an animal's metaphorlike perception of fire and smoke**, not on-page proof a literal dragon appeared at Winterfell. Both wolves survive the blaze and inspect the ruins after the raiders leave.
2. **BRAN'S CONTROL AND THE THREE-DAY DANGER:** Bran reluctantly returns from the wolf state after **Jojen and Meera** call him, having spent **three days** mentally with Summer while his human body lies still. They have only been able to provide **dribbled water and honey**; Jojen stresses the wolf eating **does not nourish Bran's human body**, which is at risk of wasting or starving. This establishes a critical limitation on skinchanging, along with **Bran's preference for the strong, mobile wolf body over his paralyzed one**.
3. **BRAN'S THIRD EYE AS AN ACTIVE ABILITY:** Bran describes reaching Summer at will and says he **once touched Ghost and spoke to Jon**. This is direct retrospective corroboration of the **Bran-faced three-eyed weirwood and Ghost encounter** from `ACOK 53 Jon`. He questions whether he only dreamed it, so the **two POVs independently share highly specific matching contact** without revealing who, if anyone, directed the encounter. The evidence for a real psychic connection is now materially stronger than an isolated dream.
4. **THE REAL HIDING PLACE REVEALED:** **Bran, Rickon, Jojen, Meera, Hodor and Osha** have actually been **hiding inside Winterfell's crypts beside the empty tomb and granite statue prepared for Ned Stark**—**never fleeing far into the wolfswood**. The crypts, cold, lightless and stocked with bread, water and meat, preserved their lives while Theon and Reek hunted outside. This **decisively confirms the crypt-hiding hypothesis** suggested by `ACOK 51 Jon`'s **Bael the Bard** song. The parallel with Bael is striking but **does not prove that every legendary detail of Bael's story happened**.
5. **SIX FUGITIVES AND TWO SEPARATE WOLVES:** The group is **Bran, Rickon, Jojen, Meera, Hodor and Osha**, with **Summer and Shaggydog** scouting outside. Bran explains later that they **went briefly toward the woods, doubled back, and sent the direwolves onward to manufacture a trail**. `ACOK 50 Theon`'s absent human footprints at the stream are therefore an **accurate physical clue** and a successful decoy, not evidence the princes teleported or an external magician moved them.
6. **CRYPT HISTORY AND OBJECTS:** Torchlight illuminates Winterfell's buried Kings and Lords: **Lyanna, Brandon, Rickard, Eddard's empty stone tomb**, and many older Kings in the North including **Torrhen, Theon the Hungry Wolf and the Brandon kings**. **Mikken's funerary swords** are reused: **Osha** takes the blade meant for Ned, **Meera** carries Rickard's and **Bran** his uncle Brandon's. This links **the dead Stark lineage, physical iron, and living descendants** as a literal survival resource; it does not establish supernatural sword ownership rules.
7. **ACTIVE EXIT FROM CRYPT:** Bran watches Winterfell still burning through **Summer's eyes**, convincing the others that fighting has passed. Osha scouts the stair while **Hodor physically forces open the heavy ironwood crypt door**, against rubble and collapsing stone, allowing them to emerge near the ruined **First Keep**. Hodor's strength matters materially; the concealment is **not a sealed mystical bunker that can only open to Stark blood**.
8. **DESTROYED BUT NOT ENTIRELY LOST:** The party sees **collapsed First Keep walls**, **shattered gargoyles**, **the Great Hall's fallen roof**, **destroyed glass gardens**, **burned stables and horses**, **wrecked Luwin tower and rookery bridge**, and damaged storehouses. The **granite curtain walls, much of the stone towers and the godswood** largely survive. A broken conduit or crack near the **Library Tower** releases **hot water**, reinforcing Winterfell's unusual **geothermal springs infrastructure** already known from the early story. The destruction is physical and severe, but **not annihilation of the castle's stone core**.
9. **GODSwood PRESERVED:** **Jojen** remarks that damp, living trees have **power to resist fire**, and the **weirwood with its black pool** stands unharmed deeper within the godswood. This may function as a thematic counterpart to fire and dragon motifs, but **ordinary wet timber and earth** provide a physical explanation for surviving the blaze too; do not infer an active magical protective barrier without further evidence.
10. **WHO ACTUALLY BURNED WINTERFELL:** Bran first blames **Theon**, then Osha points out dead **ironborn warriors, Black Lorren and Theon's arrow-pierced black horse** among the ruins, and a fallen attacker with **the Dreadfort flayed-man badge**. `ACOK 66 Theon` directly showed **Ramsay ordering the sack and burning**, confirming Osha's inference and correcting Bran's initial assumption. The combined viewpoint evidence unequivocally makes **Ramsay's men the immediate arsonists**, not proof Theon authorized the final destruction.
11. **LUWIN FOUND DYING:** Summer leads them to **Maester Luwin** beneath the godswood heart tree. He has **crawled there** after his spear wound (`ACOK 66`) and is still alive, bleeding heavily and coughing blood. Bran and Rickon are recognized as genuine living Stark princes, and Luwin explicitly notices **the bodies displayed by Theon had the wrong leg musculature**, despite clothing that fit. This is a crucial **forensic clue from Luwin's earlier access to both boys' actual bodies**, independently verifying the false-head deception revealed in `ACOK 56`.
12. **THE DECOY AND CRYPTS CONFIRMED TO LUWIN:** Bran tells Luwin explicitly **the people turned back from the edge of the woods**, and **only the wolves ran onward** to create an apparent path. The maester finally learns they were in **Ned's crypt** all along. This resolves Theon's failed wolf-stream tracking (`ACOK 50`) with firsthand testimony.
13. **HEIRS MUST SEPARATE:** Knowing Robb's heirs cannot both risk falling to one captor, Luwin insists the princes **must not travel together**. He names **White Harbor and the Umbers** as possibilities but admits the north is fractured: **Rodrik, Tallhart and Cerwyn slain, Deepwood Motte taken, Moat Cailin threatened/held by Ironborn, Stony Shore occupied, and the Bastard of Bolton operating in the east**. The locations are **options/known reports**, not a prescribed secure destination. His concern is dynastic survival amid strategic collapse.
14. **THE UNSEEN LAST BOON:** Luwin says he **is dying**, asks Osha for **water and 'another boon'**, and sends the children away before Osha remains briefly alone with him. She later rejoins the children without explaining what happened. **It is plausible he requested a merciful death**; that is **inference**, not a directly narrated killing or last spoken words. Do not claim she visibly stabbed him or state a witnessed time of death.
15. **THE ACTUAL TWO ROUTES:** **Osha takes Rickon and Shaggydog** via the **East Gate**, saying she may follow the **kingsroad** for a time. **Bran, Hodor, Jojen and Meera**, with **Summer**, leave via the **Hunter's Gate**. Jojen announces that **their road is north**, not to Greywater Watch, when Bran asks. The destinations after departure are **future plans and directions**, not evidence either group arrives safely at the Umbers, White Harbor or beyond the Wall.
16. **EMOTIONAL AND SYMBOLIC END:** Rickon sobs at the separation; Bran watches the soot-marked but recognizable towers of Winterfell shrink behind him. He thinks **stones are strong, the tree roots run deep, and Kings of Winter remain beneath the ground**, so Winterfell is **broken, not dead**, like Bran himself. This is Bran's deeply grounded **interpretive hope**, not a future guarantee that the castle will be recovered without further violence.
17. **BOOK-LEVEL CLOSE:** The final viewpoint of ACOK is **a living Stark heir, newly confirmed as a skinchanger, departing a ruined ancestral home** while the other heir takes a separate route. The castle's destruction is now fully explained, but **Ramsay's ultimate goals, Theon's fate and the boys' later safety** remain live mysteries.

## Competing readings and high-value next tests

- **Cleverness, not teleportation:** `ACOK 50` gave the **negative human-footprint evidence**, `ACOK 51` supplied crypt folklore, `ACOK 56` established false bodies, and **this chapter physically confirms the hiding place and the deliberately separated wolves**. This is an exemplary multi-POV Martin mystery where an apparently dead character remains concealed in the same physical setting while outside parties and even friendly kin draw wrong conclusions.
- **Bran and Jon's psychic exchange:** Bran's memory of touching Ghost matches Jon's experience in `ACOK 53`. Strong evidence of a real communication channel exists, but whether a **third party** facilitated it, how far distance matters and what the 'third eye' exactly permits remain open.
- **Ramsay vs Theon:** Theon committed grave crimes and public hostage terror, but `ACOK 66` shows Ramsay ordered **the final sack and fire**. Track responsibility with separate causal stages rather than conflating two occupiers.
- **What Luwin asked Osha:** Water and a boon strongly signal a request to end suffering, but without the act or reply detailed, the correct ledger remains **'probable mercy request; outcome unwitnessed'**.
- **Crypt/geothermal/weirwood:** The physical crypts, springs and resistant godswood will matter in future Winterfell-control theories. Nothing here identifies an underground city, buried dragon, secret network beyond the crypts or magic source in the heat; save those as **unsupported until verified**.
- **Next:** `ASOS 1 (Jaime)` begins the next previously unexamined full series section, while future Bran and Rickon chapters can test whether either route delivers a Stark heir to refuge.
