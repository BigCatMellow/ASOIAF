## Chronology, evidence and moral decisions

1. **GRIEF FROM A REPORT, NOT DIRECT FATE PROOF:** In Riverrun, the populace celebrates **Edmure's Red Fork defense and Robb's capture of the Crag**, while Catelyn suffers private grief. A raven **from Castle Cerwyn via Ser Rodrik** says **Bran and Rickon escaped Winterfell, were recaptured near a mill on the Acorn Water, and Theon mounted their severed heads on the castle walls**. Catelyn believes the report and tells **Brienne** she has only Robb left. The immediately preceding `ACOK 50 Theon` narrated **an uncompleted search and Reek's claim about a mill**, with **no killings or identities verified on page**. The **reader must classify the princes' deaths as reported/alleged** pending later direct evidence, no matter how real Catelyn's mourning is.
2. **CATELYN'S DISTRIBUTED GRIEF:** She relives leaving Bran unconscious after his fall, Rickon's young age and lost bond with both, and compares their supposed deaths to **Sansa and Arya's vulnerability** without wolves. She explicitly says she fears **Arya also dead**, although `ACOK 47` directly showed Arya alive and now serving **Roose Bolton**. Her fear is **a true psychological state, not a verified disappearance outcome**.
3. **BRIENNE'S RESPONSE / OATH:** Brienne attempts comfort, unsure whether to touch Catelyn, and optimistically suggests **Cersei as a mother may return the Stark girls out of pity**. Catelyn rebukes this hope and wishes vengeance on **Theon, Jaime, Cersei and Tyrion**. Their bond established in `ACOK 39` is tested by bereavement. **Brienne supports Catelyn with sworn service**, not because she has independently checked the raven's truth.
4. **WAR VS PEACE:** Catelyn dreams not of a crowned victory but of **Robb laying down his sword, wedding one of Walder Frey's daughters and giving her future grandchildren**, while **her dying father Hoster** still lives upstairs. The people celebrate Edmure as lord-to-be prematurely in her hearing, showing the contrast between political victory, succession and the experiences of household grief.
5. **JAIME VISIT ARRANGED:** Catelyn has sent **Jaime wine** and asks Brienne to accompany her at **midnight**. Before entering his dungeon she sits with Hoster, remembers childhood **Lysa and herself becoming lost in fog near Seagard until young Petyr found them**, and speaks of war and separation. These first-person recollections establish their prior familial closeness, not evidence Petyr has always been reliable.
6. **DUNGEON ACCESS AND AUTHORITY:** At midnight a drinking **gaoler** objects that **Edmure ordered no entry without his sealed writ**. Catelyn uses the fact that **Hoster is still legally lord** to overrule him; he opens the cell, **Brienne guards outside**, and Jaime remains chained by hands and ankles in a foul low dungeon after the failed rescue (`ACOK 39`). She gains unauthorized access as Edmure would define it, without producing the required writ.
7. **WINE AS INFORMATION TACTIC:** Jaime initially **refuses Catelyn's earlier wine** as a possible trap, then agrees to drink during a negotiated exchange of **answers for answers**. Catelyn calculates that wine may loosen his tongue, and later notes he has become drunk. **There is no evidence the wine was poisoned or contained truth serum**.
8. **DIRECT JAIME PATERNITY ADMISSION:** Under questioning Jaime acknowledges he is **Joffrey's father**, likewise father of **Cersei's other children**, and openly acknowledges the incestuous relationship. This is **direct admission against interest from the central participant**, substantially stronger than Stannis's political pamphlets and Ned's hair-color reasoning; he has no laboratory paternity test, but his statement is explicit.
9. **DIRECT BRAN ASSAULT ADMISSION:** Jaime directly states **he threw Bran from the tower window** because Bran had seen the lovers, and **he intended to kill the boy**. This self-incriminating admission independently confirms earlier action and Cersei's account in `ACOK 54`. Catelyn's former supposition of an accidental fall is irreconcilable with what Jaime now says.
10. **CATSPAW DISTINGUISHED FROM BRAN SHOVE:** Catelyn asks whether Jaime, fearing Bran might recover, ordered the **subsequent knife-bearing assassin**. Jaime says **no**, argues the boy seemed likely to die anyway, claims he does not hire others to kill for him, and swears by Lannister honor. He also says he would have known if **Cersei** hired anyone. Catelyn **believes his denial**, but this remains a **self-interested character statement**, even alongside his candid admission of the shove; neither Jaime nor Cersei's innocence of the later catspaw is proven solely by his word.
11. **THE DAGGER ALIBI AND BAELISH PROBLEM:** Catelyn repeats **Petyr Baelish's story** that Tyrion won the **Valyrian-steel, dragonbone-handled dagger** wagering on **Jaime against Loras Tyrell** at Joffrey's nameday tourney. As she recounts it, she remembers the bet's direction may be backwards. Jaime recalls that **Loras unhorsed him**, that **Tyrion routinely backed Jaime**, and that **King Robert possessed/showed the dagger that evening**. This supports **Tyrion's earlier account of losing the wager**, directly undermining Petyr's narrative that Tyrion won the knife. The **ownership chain after Robert's possession and identity of actual catspaw sponsor remain unsolved**. Do not replace one unverified accusation with another as a fact.
12. **IDENTITY AND TRUTH-COST OF LITTLEFINGER:** Catelyn is shaken because **Petyr was an almost-brother and former childhood friend**, and both estranged Lannister brothers independently give accounts pointing against his prior testimony. Neither had coordinated a recent story with the other as far as she knows. Catelyn wonders if this is a trap rather than declaring immediately that **Littlefinger knowingly lied**, a high-priority confidence distinction.
13. **MUTUAL INTELLIGENCE BARGAIN:** Jaime asks about the survival of **Cersei, Tyrion, Tywin**, and **the Baratheon brothers' war**, while Catelyn asks about the dagger, Bran and royal secrets. The scene explains how a prisoner **withholds or reveals information strategically**. Catelyn tells him **Stannis marches on King's Landing and Renly is dead** (her attribution to Stannis is an inference from the shadow incident), and about Robb's **Crag victory**. Jaime mocks Robb but acknowledges the Whispering Wood ambush.
14. **WAR CASUALTIES AND HONOR:** Jaime says he killed **Rickard Karstark's sons** in battle while trying to reach **Robb**, not targeting the Karstarks by personal malice. He contrasts that **fair-fight killing** with widespread claims that his own Kingsguard oathbreaking makes him irredeemable. His perspective is a **participant's defense**, not a justification for Bran's attempted murder.
15. **VOW PARADOX:** Jaime lists conflicting injunctions to **obey the king, keep secrets, obey father, protect the innocent, defend the weak and observe laws**. He argues that faithfully fulfilling one oath may break another. This is a major thematic challenge to simplistic Kingsguard honor, **but does not by itself excuse the deliberate attempt to kill Bran**.
16. **JAIME'S EYEWITNESS AERYS TESTIMONY:** Jaime describes **Aerys II's execution of Rickard and Brandon Stark**: Rickard sought **trial by combat** but was suspended above a fire as the king declared **fire his champion**, with pyromancers regulating heat. Brandon was bound with a **Tyroshi strangling device** and a sword placed just beyond reach; attempts to free his burning father **tightened the cord** until he died by strangulation. Jaime says he stood guard during the atrocity, and **Gerold Hightower** afterward reminded him his duty was to **guard, not judge** the king. This is unusually detailed participant eyewitness testimony; it strongly grounds the atrocity's mechanism.
17. **IMPORTANT NEGATIVE EVIDENCE:** Jaime **does not claim he killed Aerys to avenge the Starks** when Catelyn raises that explanation; he explicitly denies having made it. Instead he describes being **reviled for his finest act** and believes Ned should have thanked him for Aerys's death. The precise circumstances and **real motive for the later regicide** are **not disclosed in this chapter**; avoid replacing uncertainty with a retroactive wildfire-murder justification that Jaime does not yet provide.
18. **OTHER COURT DETAILS:** Catelyn names **Ethan Glover** as Brandon's surviving squire and other captured companions **Jeffory Mallister, Kyle Royce and Elbert Arryn**, killed with their fathers. Jaime describes Robert requiring **Jaime, Pycelle and Varys to kneel for pardon** at his coronation. He makes abusive remarks about Catelyn's fidelity, Petyr's alleged intimacy and **Jon Snow**, none of which establish the truth of his sexual insult or Jon's parentage.
19. **FINAL ACTION BOUNDARY:** Jaime's insults about **Ned and Jon Snow** prompt Catelyn to summon Brienne and **demand her sword**. **The chapter stops there.** It does **not** show her kill Jaime, release him, trade him, or formally authorize Brienne to harm him. The demand is a major escalation and cliffhanger requiring next POV confirmation.

## Rival interpretations and high-priority later checks

- **Catspaw sponsor:** Jaime's denial and Tyrion's corroborated **lost wager** weaken the initial theory that the dagger proved Tyrion commissioned the attack. Robert's earlier possession matters, but **ownership is not the same as commissioning**. Track exact date/transfer, Joffrey's opportunity, Littlefinger's motive, and later firsthand confessions rather than proclaiming a winner prematurely.
- **Bran and Rickon:** A message with apparently precise severed heads is **not physical identification**. Distinguish Theon's mill plan (`ACOK 50`), Rodrik's received report, Varys's corresponding report (`ACOK 54`), and any eventual survivor POV.
- **Kingsguard honor:** Jaime's list of irreconcilable duties and account of witnessing Rickard's death deepen his disillusionment. The reader must keep **actual atrocity, witness role, claims of later heroic action, and admitted child attempted murder** in the same record.
- **Catelyn's intended action:** Her sword request arises from grief and provocation, but **does not prove the forthcoming prisoner exchange or execution**. Test the immediate next chronological references.
