## Narrative and physical events

1. **VERIFIED / SITE:** Daenerys arrives at **the House of the Undying** accompanied by **Drogon, Jorah, Xaro, Aggo and Jhogo**. The structure is unexpectedly **a low, serpent-shaped, windowless grey ruin**, surrounded by **black-barked trees with blue leaves** used to make **shade-of-the-evening**. **Pyat Pree** alone enters as guide; he forbids her protectors entry. Xaro and the bloodriders distrust the place for different reasons. Her decision is voluntary but influenced by her lack of ships and the promise of truth.
2. **PYAT'S RULES / UNVERIFIED INTENT:** He instructs her to turn **always right**, take any **upward stairs**, and never enter side chambers before the true audience. He claims the front path admits guests but never lets them leave. He warns she may see **past, future and things that never occurred**. This is a crucial **in-world control against treating every vision as literal prophecy**. Pyat's stated rule eventually helps her navigate, but his motives are not automatically benign.
3. **VERIFIED / DRUGGED PERCEPTION:** An unusually small attendant offers Daenerys a glass of blue **shade-of-the-evening**. The liquid first tastes foul then shifts to a riot of remembered flavors. Pyat claims it heightens sight and hearing. **Every subsequent vision occurs after ingestion**, so sensory experience is established but **external factual status must be graded**, particularly for images no other viewpoint shares.
4. **VERIFIED / ROUTE TEST:** Four-door rooms loop into stranger geometry and a hall with **torches only to her right and doors only to her left**. **Drogon** repeatedly resists or detects strange sounds, and his attempts to fly are clumsy. Dany sees a room where a **beautiful naked woman is attacked by four small, rat-faced figures**, a **slaughtered feast with a wolf-headed crowned king**, and a **red-doored house with a lemon tree** and the image of deceased **Ser Willem Darry** welcoming her home. She recognizes the red-door vision as the strongest emotional temptation and **refuses to step in because Willem is dead**.
5. **VISION: PAST KING'S THRONE:** A silver-haired **aged man on a barbed throne**, in a hall of dragon skulls, wishes an enemy made **king of ashes**. This strongly resembles **Aerys II's destructive endgame**, but the chapter **does not name him within the vision**; verify via other narrators before declaring the words a historical transcript. The vision is one candidate for inherited Targaryen history, not proof all surrounding scenes are true.
6. **VISION: RHAEGAR, BABY AEGON, PROMISED PRINCE:** A **taller, dark-indigo-eyed silver-haired man resembling Viserys** talks to a **woman nursing a newborn**. He calls the infant **Aegon**, declares him **'the prince that was promised'** and says his is **'the song of ice and fire'**, then adds that **'the dragon has three heads'** and another is needed. He picks up a **harp**. The historical identification **Rhaegar** is strongly suggested by his appearance and harping and a son named Aegon, yet his **exact meaning, the referent of 'one more', and the prophecy's validity** are not externally proved inside this vision. Do not collapse 'three heads' into an established set of named future riders.
7. **VERIFIED / ESCAPE THROUGH LOGIC:** When a hallway appears to lead only **down** rather than up, and lights go out behind her, Dany realizes **the last door on her left may also be the first on her right from the opposite perspective**, passes through and resumes turning right. A seemingly welcoming **Pyat apparition** then tries to lure her out to the grass; she **rejects him to take the right-hand door**. His apparent face withers as she leaves. Another lavish 'Undying' hall invites her to a feast, tells her **the comet was sent for her**, offers magical weapons and answers; **Drogon gnaws the door**, causing her to discover **a hidden plainer door** on the right. These elaborate offers are **demonstrably unreliable traps** as the false audiences later dissolve.
8. **VERIFIED / TRUE AUDIENCE AS EXPERIENCED:** Beyond the concealed door, a **huge floating blue, rotten, beating heart** pulses above a long table. The aged, discolored Undying figures seem **motionless and not breathing**, yet whisper into Dany's mind, claiming to live. These impressions are directly narrated from the intoxicated POV, but **their physiological nature and claimed conquest of death are unverified**.
9. **UNDYING'S THREEFOLD ORACLE:** The voices name Dany as **mother of dragons, child of storm and 'child of three'**. They say the **dragon has three heads**, speak of **three fires** associated respectively with **life, death and love**, **three mounts** associated with **bed, dread and love**, and **three treasons** associated with **blood, gold and love**. The words are audible to Dany in the scene, but **no referents are specified** and even the direction of 'treason'—betrayals suffered or committed—is not fixed by grammar. Treat as **nine distinct open entries**, not a solved checklist.
10. **UNDYING'S 'DAUGHTER OF DEATH' SERIES:** Dany sees **Viserys destroyed by molten gold** (an earlier witnessed event, plausibly validating at least this image), **a tall copper-skinned silver-gold-haired lord beneath a burning stallion banner before a burning city**, and **a prince dying in water with red rubies scattering from his chest** as he utters a woman's name. The last strongly recalls **Rhaegar at the Trident**, but neither the identity of the central battle in the second vision nor the **woman's name** is revealed. Do not invent it.
11. **UNDYING'S 'SLAYER OF LIES' SERIES:** A **blue-eyed king** raises a **red-glowing sword** but has **no shadow**; a **cloth dragon on poles** receives cheers from a crowd; a **stone beast** leaves a **smoking tower**, breathing **shadow fire**. The blue-eyed sword bearer **plausibly echoes Stannis and Lightbringer** (`ACOK 31/42`), but **the absent shadow is part of the vision** and no actual Dany-Stannis meeting or killing has occurred. **The cloth dragon may signify staged royal legitimacy** but does not name a specific claimant; the beast's identity and literal/symbolic character are open.
12. **UNDYING'S 'BRIDE OF FIRE' SERIES:** Dany rides her **silver mare under the stars to a dark stream**; a **smiling, bright-eyed corpse with grey lips stands at a ship's prow**; a **blue flower blooms in a chink in a wall of ice**, filling the air with sweetness. All three are precise sensory images with **unestablished future referents**. The blue flower may invite a **Lyanna/Wall/hidden-parentage** hypothesis, but **neither Lyanna nor Jon is named in this image**, so do not promote it to revealed parentage.
13. **FURTHER VISIONS:** After the three series, fast images include **shadows dancing in a tent**, the **young Dany running toward the red door**, **Mirri burning**, **a dragon appearing from Mirri's brow**, a **naked man dragged dead behind her silver**, a **white lion in tall grass**, **naked crones kneeling beneath the Mother of Mountains**, and **ten thousand slaves calling Dany 'mother' and reaching for her**. Some images correspond to already seen events (Mirri, the silver), others may represent potential futures (slaves). **Do not treat every image as a guaranteed prediction**; Pyat explicitly allowed 'days never were' and the audience is hostile.
14. **DIRECT THREAT AND RESCUE:** As Dany reaches to embrace the apparent needy slaves, **Undying bodies crowd her, grasp her, and begin physically feeding or attacking her**. She cannot move and feels her heart stop. **Drogon tears apart the floating heart and breathes real flame**, igniting the chamber and the withered Undying figures. Dany physically escapes while the palace burns. This supports the **Undying as predatory manipulators with material power**, not benign impartial historians of the future.
15. **EXIT AND PYAT'S HOSTILITY:** Dany emerges into daylight, sees **smoke from the Palace of Dust**, and **Pyat Pree lunges toward her with a knife**, until **Jhogo's whip** disarms him and **Rakharo** restrains him. **Jorah** supports Dany. This observed hostility independently checks the Undying's apparent promises: their associated guide attempts violence when she survives, and the building's burning is externally visible.

## Competing interpretations and priority next tests

- **Epistemology:** This is the highest-density prophecy chapter so far. Dany has swallowed an explicitly consciousness-altering drink, and Pyat admits **unrealized/nonhistorical days** can be shown. Record all images as **visions witnessed**, then rank later identifications by exact matching **objects, numbers, actors, setting and causal role**—not just thematic resemblance.
- **The Undying's goals:** Repeated diversions, false welcomes, predatory attack and Pyat's knife demonstrate hostility. Their prophecies may contain valuable data **and** calculated deceit. A true detail such as Viserys's death does not establish universal truthfulness.
- **Threefold statements:** No text warrants filling all nine fires/mounts/treasons today. Maintain them as open research tests with contradictory candidate lists and later confirmations.
- **Rhaegar/Aegon and the dragon:** His apparent statement is pivotal to series-level theory work; keep **what the vision literally says** distinct from which child, parent and political plan are known through other narrators.
- **Blue flower / cloth dragon:** High-value hidden-parentage and false-dragon theory signals, but both lack named identities; downstream evidence must support the leap.
- **Next:** `ACOK 40` for Quaithe and magic revival; subsequent Daenerys/Quaithe, Stannis and Jon/Wall POVs; `ASOS` for actual royal feasts and their attendees; later Westerosi dragon claimants for cloth-dragon disconfirmation.
