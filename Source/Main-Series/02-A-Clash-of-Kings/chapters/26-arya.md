## Chapter-wide sequence, consequences and choices

1. **VERIFIED:** After the capture at Gods Eye in `ACOK 19`, Arya endures **eight days imprisoned in the storehouse** before Gregor Clegane orders the captives marched. **Every day at least one prisoner is selected and killed**, even when displaying apparent loyalty to Joffrey or offering information. A village elder who praises Joffrey repeatedly is selected on the fifth day, and a mother who supplies information for her child's safety watches her daughter selected next. The narrative provides repeated evidence that neither loyalty nor cooperation ensures survival.
2. **VERIFIED:** **Gregor** chooses victims and observes interrogations. The apparently ordinary **Tickler** repeats questions about hidden gold, food, **Beric Dondarrion**, allies, arms, numbers, movement and banners while torturing victims; **Chiswyck** sometimes assists. None survives this questioning. The responses contradict one another (ten starved men vs one hundred mounted knights, multiple directions); this is direct evidence that **information extracted through terror is unreliable**, not a sound survey of Beric's army. Some stolen valuables are found nonetheless, and the prisoners' bodies are later hung for wolves.
3. **VERIFIED:** Arya has lost **Needle**, is beaten into silence and loses the ability to conceal her sex when forced to relieve herself openly on the march. **Hot Pie** sees that she is a girl but her **Stark identity remains concealed** from these captors. Her insistence on not responding stems from genuine danger, not surrender of self. She judges herself harshly by imagined standards of **Syrio Forel** and her direwolf; she perceives herself as another defenseless lamb.
4. **VERIFIED:** A **very young boy (about three)** cries for his father and is killed with a mace, and his mother is killed by **Raff the Sweetling**. Soldiers sexually abuse detained women on the journey. One young woman resists an assault by striking a soldier with a rock; **Gregor personally beheads her with his enormous sword**, compelling the group to watch. These acts show direct, systematic terror and extreme violence against noncombatants, not merely rumors of a wicked commander.
5. **VERIFIED:** The prisoners travel **on foot** while their captors ride and drive stolen provisions: **a dozen pigs, chickens, a cow and nine wagons of salt fish**. Those unable to walk fast enough or attempting escape are killed. **Gendry remains alive because he demonstrates metalworking ability**, particularly that he forged his own bull helmet. His craft gives him value that wounded Lommy did not enjoy.
6. **VILLAGERS' CHARACTER CLAIMS:** The captives argue about whether any armed faction protected them. One villager says **Beric's men and Thoros** came but paid for supplies; another says the red priest gave only **paper marked with a token** for two chickens, worthless for immediate food. An old man praises **King Aerys** rather than Robert as a former protector; a guard punishes this speech. These sharply different accounts show deprivation, coercion and **political nostalgia**, not independently fair accounts of prior kings or true market value of Thoros's notes.
7. **VERIFIED / NAME LEDGER:** Arya identifies several captors by conduct and possessions: **Polliver carries her Needle**, **Dunsen wears Gendry's horned helm**, **Chiswyck** struck her earlier, **Raff** is now identified as the actual spearman who killed surrendering **Lommy**, and **the Tickler** runs interrogations. She learns **Shitmouth** may spare extra food despite coarse speech; outward manner cannot reliably predict conduct. Arya starts repeating a **nightly litany of people she hates**, including Gregor, Dunsen, Polliver, Chiswyck, Raff, the Tickler and enemies associated with other losses (Sandor, Lorch, Trant, Payne, Joffrey, Cersei). This is an observed psychological ritual, **not yet a successful revenge campaign**.
8. **VERIFIED:** The destination is **Tywin Lannister's Harrenhal**. Its enormous **melted and distorted towers**, massive gatehouse and walls, five great towers, and siege engines are seen by Arya, corroborating the legendary **dragon-fire destruction of Harrenhal** as a physical ruin without proving every embellishment in Old Nan's account (such as human blood mortar). The immense distances are deceptive: after sighting its towers the party must march more than a day before reaching it. Tywin's camp outside has overflowing latrines, flies and mold on stakes.
9. **VERIFIED:** The captives are forced to wash in scalding baths under **Goodwife Amabel and Goodwife Harra**, evaluated for work and stripped of control over placement. Arya cannot keep the male name Arry, so she selects **'Weasel'**, borrowing the missing toddler's nickname. She asks for stable work to improve chances of stealing a horse; **Harra slaps her** for speaking without permission, and **Amabel** retaliates by assigning her not to comfortable kitchens but to **Weese**, understeward of the **Wailing Tower**. This is a distinct new cover identity and a concrete lost escape opportunity.
10. **VERIFIED:** **Weese** receives Arya and five others and tells them they must not look at nobles, talk unbidden or show pride; he claims he can smell fear and defiance. The chapter ends with **Arya under his supervision**. It does not show her actually obtaining Needle, escaping Harrenhal or taking any oath to a supernatural assassin.

## Interpretive tests and unresolved

- **Terror as intelligence:** The Tickler asks strategically relevant questions, but Gregor's system kills sources and invites contradictory guesses; more pain is not equivalent to more accurate military intelligence. Ordinary competing explanation is that the torture primarily **terrorizes** communities and extracts goods.
- **Arya:** Her apparent loss of courage is survival behavior in captivity. Her nightly names show attention to perpetrators and a new ritual, but **do not warrant predicting which deaths she will cause**. She preserves her highborn identity and imagines stealing a horse.
- **Gendry:** Survives by craft rather than a recognized noble/Baratheon claim; this contrasts with class-based ransom norms and with dead Lommy. Do not claim his parentage was discovered by captors.
- **Smallfolk political accounts:** Beric's paper payments may be credible attempts at compensation that still fail a hungry farmer. Nostalgia for Aerys is the view of one frightened old man, not a measure of popular support.
