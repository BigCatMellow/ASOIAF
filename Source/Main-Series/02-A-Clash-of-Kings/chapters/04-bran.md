## Full cause-and-effect

1. **VERIFIED:** Bran, now addressed as **prince and Robb's heir**, watches Winterfell from his room, still unable to walk. **Summer and Shaggydog** howl beneath the red comet. He finds the wolves’ voices almost intelligible, and makes his own howl, which they answer. He thinks painfully about his scattered pack and lost siblings.
2. **VERIFIED/CHARACTER INTERPRETATIONS:** Ser Rodrik refuses to speculate on the howling; Farlen says confinement, Gage says hunting, Luwin suggests mistaking the comet for a moon, **Septon Chayle** calls it the blade that kills summer, **Osha** says “blood and fire,” and **Old Nan claims she smells dragons** though nearly blind. **No one proves an objective meaning for the comet**, even as a white raven announces autumn.
3. **VERIFIED:** At Catelyn's instruction, **Big Walder and Little Walder Frey** are fostered at Winterfell. Bran initially welcomes them, despite Rickon's fury, and offers food and hospitality. The boys later occupy **Jon Snow's old bedchamber**, a specific perceived invasion of Bran's family space.
4. **VERIFIED:** The Freys teach the children **“lord of the crossing,”** a game using a log over a **hot godswood pool**. The figure with the staff demands oaths from entrants; crucially, an oath has no binding force **if the entrant says “mayhaps,” even quietly enough the guard misses it**. This is a directly described *game rule*, **not a confirmed legal exception for adult Frey contracts**. The possibility of equivocal promises warrants future close comparison.
5. **VERIFIED:** **Little Walder** is physically larger and more forceful despite being the younger of the two; **Big Walder** is smaller, older by **52 days**, genealogically farther from succession and predicts he will someday inherit the Twins. They explicitly identify their fathers as **Jammos** and **Merrett** respectively and name **Black Walder, Red Walder, Walder Rivers, Walt(y)r** among the family.
6. **VERIFIED:** During the children's game **Little Walder hits Rickon with a stick**, triggering **Shaggydog to attack and draw blood** before Hodor intervenes. The Direwolves are confined to the godswood afterward because **Shaggydog bit Little Walder**. Rickon subsequently enjoys the Freys' company, while Bran remains resentful and isolated.
7. **VERIFIED:** Maester Luwin has **iron handholds installed by Mikken** so Bran can maneuver around his room with his arms. Bran rejects dependence while also resenting loss of Dancer access. He says his dreams contain animal smells, tastes and blood, plus an occasional **weirwood that calls to him**. Luwin says all animals dream but trees do not; this is a **character's assertion**.
8. **VERIFIED:** Luwin gives Bran a **sweetened sleeping draught promised to produce dreamless sleep**. Despite the drug, Bran experiences an extended **first-person wolf-body dream**: four functional legs, prey smell/taste, memory of eating squirrels, identification of **Shaggydog by smell**, prisonlike stone walls and frustration at barred exits. The dream records richly nonhuman sensory content and a persistent wolf's urge for freedom; **the narrative does not yet supply an independent observer to verify the exact wolf activity during sleep**.
