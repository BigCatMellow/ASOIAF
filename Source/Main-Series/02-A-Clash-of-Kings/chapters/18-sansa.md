## Major narrative and decisions

1. **VERIFIED:** Sansa finds an **unsealed unsigned summons** hidden under her pillow, promising a chance to go home if she visits the godswood at night. Her current fear is physical as well as political: **Ser Meryn Trant's mailed fist** left a painful bruise after she wished harm on Slynt. She wonders whether the summons is Joffrey's trap or a true rescuer; she has no trusted person to ask.
2. **VERIFIED:** Cersei rotates Sansa's bedmaids **every fortnight**, limiting relationships and creating fear of informants. Sansa burns the note and initially tries to sleep, assuming **Ser Preston Greenfield** will question a nocturnal crossing. She mourns executed **Septa Mordane** and missing **Jeyne Poole**; she *mistakenly assumes Arya has returned to Winterfell*, an important information gap against `ACOK 19 (Arya)`.
3. **VERIFIED:** Shouts and a disturbance draw guards away and leave the drawbridge of Maegor's Holdfast unattended. Sansa takes a **small table knife**, slips past soldiers and heads into the dark **godswood**. Her prayer for a true knight is answered by the presence of **Dontos Hollard**, the former knight Joffrey reduced to a fool after Sansa saved his life in `ACOK 2 (Sansa)`.
4. **DONTOS'S CLAIM / OATH VERIFIED:** Dontos says nobody sent him, that he wants to repay Sansa, and invokes **Florian the Fool** as model of a true knight hidden in a fool. He swears by the **heart tree** to take her home, but must still find a route out of the castle, funds and a ship. He says the godswood is the **only safe meeting place** and warns her to avoid revealing acquaintance in public. Sansa accepts despite his drunkenness and the lack of immediate implementation. The vow is real dialogue; **independence, honesty and eventual rescue remain unverified**.
5. **VERIFIED:** The rhetoric of **Florian and Jonquil** affects Sansa profoundly, but her choice also reflects a practical lack of alternatives. She kisses Dontos and returns toward her captivity with new hope. No successful exit is depicted.
6. **VERIFIED:** Sansa runs into drunk **Sandor Clegane**, who roughens his grip on her, questions why she was out, yet escorts her to Maegor's Holdfast. She says she visited the godswood to pray for Joffrey's safety. At the bridge **Boros Blount** reports the alarm came from hungry people outside, stirred by talk of **Tyrek's wedding feast**; Joffrey dispersed them in a sortie. It was **not a Baratheon invasion**.
7. **SANDOR'S FAMILY ACCOUNT:** Sandor tells Sansa why **three dogs on gold/yellow** are Clegane arms: his kennelmaster grandfather's hounds supposedly saved **Tytos Lannister** from a lioness, three dogs dying and the grandfather losing a leg, rewarded with lands and an heir's squire service. He claims dogs are more honest than false knights and demands Sansa one day sing for him. This is an attributed family story, not a directly witnessed past scene. His intimidation and sporadic protection cannot be collapsed into uncomplicated chivalry.

## Agency and rival hypotheses

**Sansa** consciously chooses between reporting the invitation and a risky attempt at connection. Dontos's protestations may be genuine repentance, deliberate manipulation or a mixture, and his behavior must be checked later. Sandor's discomfort with knightly hypocrisy does not excuse his violence toward Sansa. Distinguish his individual conduct from Gregor's.
