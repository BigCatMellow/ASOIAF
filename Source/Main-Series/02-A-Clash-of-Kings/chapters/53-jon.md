## Narrative and cross-viewpoint verification

1. **VERIFIED / AFTER YGRITTE:** Qhorin's five rangers advance into the heights of **Skirling Pass**, where cold, hidden paths and the prospect of more watchfires force them to move chiefly under cover of shadow. Ghost is restless, often turning as though he senses pursuit. **Squire Dalbridge** scouts ahead because of exceptionally keen vision; the other men conserve light and food. This is a continuation of the scouting mission authorized in `ACOK 43`.
2. **QHORIN'S HISTORY OF MANCE:** Jon admits to Qhorin that he let **Ygritte** go (`ACOK 51`); the Halfhand says he already suspected it. He acknowledges her **Bael the Bard song** was one **Mance Rayder sang when he ranged with the Watch**. According to Qhorin, Mance was **born free folk, taken as a child by the Watch after a raid**, and later departed the Shadow Tower out of love for the wilds and inability to obey. Qhorin calls him simultaneously among the **best and worst rangers**. Accounts that he left solely for a woman or crown are **rumors**, and Qhorin's psychological analysis is informed testimony, not Mance's confession.
3. **CRITICAL REVISION OF PRIOR 'EXECUTION ORDER':** Qhorin says explicitly that he **'did not command'** Jon to kill Ygritte; he told Jon to do what he judged necessary, then **observed his choice as a test of leadership and individual character**. If Qhorin had needed her dead, he says he could have given her to **Ebben** or killed her himself. **ACOK 51's scene nevertheless reasonably sounded like a directive to Jon**; this chapter provides Qhorin's *retrospective* account. Preserve **Jon's interpretation and Qhorin's later qualification** rather than quietly erasing the genuine initial ambiguity.
4. **JON'S ETHICAL REASON:** Jon explains that Ned Stark taught him to **look personally upon the people whose deaths he decreed**. When Jon looked at Ygritte he found **no evil in her**, although he had killed two other armed sentries to prevent an alarm. Qhorin replies the two dead men had no greater inherent evil; **necessity**, not moral wickedness, drove those killings. This is a substantive moral challenge to Jon's rescue reasoning without a statement that his choice was wrong.
5. **GHOST DEPARTS:** Jon sleeps under an overhang; **Ghost refuses to curl up with him and moves away alone**. Jon assumes he is hunting, but no actual cause for Ghost's departure is established. This is important because a subsequent detailed wolf dream can be tested against a wolf physically separated from his waking human.
6. **DREAM / DIREWOLF LITTER:** In sleep Jon **is a direwolf** in a vast cold forest, senses **five wolves where there should be six** and feels a lost sibling, while the others are scattered. This evokes **Lady's earlier death** and living direwolves' separation, but no voice identifies which wolf is missing. Do not interpret the dream as all six still alive or an updated geographical census.
7. **DREAM / BRAN COMMUNICATION:** A voice silently calls **Jon**; he finds a **weirwood growing out of stone**, apparently expanding in size, with **Bran's face and an extra third eye**. The voice says the third eye came **after the crow**, echoes Jojen's teachings in `ACOK 21/28/35`, and tells Jon to **open his eyes**. Jon detects **wolf, tree, boy, warm earth, stone and death** near the tree, and the figure **touches him**, changing his perspective. This is direct **dream perception**, unusually consistent with Bran's established wolf dreams, but no waking scene from Bran confirms he consciously sent this particular message or gives the identity of a third agent.
8. **DREAM / SURVEY OF MANCE'S HOST:** Jon seems to look through Ghost's eyes from a high snowy precipice upon a **glacier, ice lake and a crowded valley** in the upper Milkwater area. He sees **thousands of wildlings**, mounted formations practicing, disorganized encampments, household animals, cooking fires and civilians. He recognizes the formation as **a people traveling together**, not only a field army. He also seems to see a **mammoth with an enormous, nonhuman rider**. This supports but does not yet **independently verify** `ACOK 43`'s tortured captive statements about mammoths, giants and the gathering; his later wound check will validate one more limited element.
9. **DREAM / THE EAGLE ATTACK:** A **blue-grey winged bird** swoops toward his wolf viewpoint; Jon wakes crying for Ghost, with a sensation of **talons cutting the back of his neck**. He immediately reports the dream in detail after Qhorin asks, and **Ebben wonders if Jon is a skinchanger**. The reaction is suspicion, not a formal demonstration of the technical limits of skinchanging.
10. **QHORIN'S EPISTEMIC RESPONSE:** Qhorin treats the dream as **potential intelligence rather than proof or foolishness**, noting Craster's report that Mance gathered at the headwaters of the **Milkwater**. Stonesnake identifies the **great glacier-fed lake** from ordinary landscape knowledge. Qhorin also observes that **dead men walk and 'trees have eyes again'**, so he is not categorically dismissing supernatural danger. He authorizes renewed movement with caution.
11. **DIRECT EAGLE SIGHTING AND WOUND MATCH:** On the descent an **eagle appears on a high rock** watching the rangers. Later Jon finds **Ghost wounded and bleeding in the neck**; Qhorin and others inspect torn flesh matching **bird talons** and wash/pack the injuries with **water, wine and a bandage cut from Jon's cloak**. The direct, physical injury **strongly corroborates that Jon experienced a real attack on Ghost during the dream**. It does **not, by itself, prove the whole wildling host survey, Bran encounter or that the eagle was a skinchanger**.
12. **TACTICAL REVERSAL:** Qhorin sees the bird and concludes **their movement has been detected**. He orders the group **back toward Watch-held territory**, not farther into the valley; he says an eagle sees better than men. This is a defensible strategic inference, but **who controls or observes through the eagle is not yet known**.
13. **DELIBERATE REARGUARD:** After a night of dangerous retreat without lights, Qhorin selects a narrow position where **one determined archer could hold a larger force**, and entrusts **Squire Dalbridge** with remaining arrows and a rearguard. Dalbridge accepts calmly and requests his **garron receive an apple at home**. Jon interprets the mission as **certain death**, but **Dalbridge's actual death is not narrated in this chapter**, and his delaying effect remains unknown.
14. **FINAL CONFIRMATION OF PURSUIT:** The survivors hear **a distant hunting horn behind them at dawn** as an eagle circles overhead. This is **actual audible pursuit evidence**, even if the identity of watchers, their route to this position, and Qhorin's ultimate escape are not yet established.

## Ranked theories and next verification tests

- **Bran–Jon communication:** Distinct from merely dreaming a dead relative: the weirwood has **Bran's face, three eyes, the crow's teachings**, and touches Ghost to open perception. The evidence supports **possible live psychic linkage** but should be paired with a later **Bran POV matching exact timing, words or method**.
- **Eagle skinchanger:** Dream injury and real Ghost wounds establish at least significant wolf POV accuracy. They do not tell whether the eagle is a **wildling skinchanger's creature, trained bird or ordinary eagle**. Later wildling accounts and eagle behavior can discriminate.
- **Mance's coalition:** The dream's thousands of civilians, animal herds and improvised shelters adds plausible evidence for a **mass migration**, beyond conventional invasion. This matters to theories of fear of the Others, but no dialogue from Mance has established his deepest motive here.
- **Qhorin's leadership:** He deliberately tests Jon's ethics, adapts to evidence, and sacrifices a rearguard. His certainty he can command the future is not absolute; **the army pursuing them is real but unseen**.
