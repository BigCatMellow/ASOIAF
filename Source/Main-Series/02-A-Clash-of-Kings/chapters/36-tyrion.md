## Narrative, political consequences and military implications

1. **VARYS'S MULTIPLE REPORTS:** At the Small Council, Varys confirms **Renly was killed with his throat cut through his armor** amid his army, corroborating `ACOK 33 (Catelyn)`. Different low-level witnesses blame **Brienne**, Stannis, a resentful maid, a camp follower or **Catelyn**. These **mutually inconsistent rumors** must not be flattened into an established public verdict. The reader's firsthand Catelyn POV has materially better evidence for an unnatural sword shadow and Brienne's innocence; Varys's informants do not.
2. **VERIFIED / TYRION'S DEDUCTION:** Tyrion and Cersei are dismayed that Stannis and Renly **did not fight a conventional bloodletting battle**. Tyrion assumes Stannis stood behind the murder because he stands to gain; the deduction parallels Catelyn's inference but is **not independent direct proof of spellcasting**.
3. **VARYS'S SITUATION REPORT:** Most of **Renly's mounted lords** who rode to Storm's End have now **sworn to Stannis**, with **Alester Florent** among the first. This partially validates Catelyn's forecast from `ACOK 33`, but not *all* Renly's troops transferred: **Loras Tyrell, Randyll Tarly and Mathis Rowan did not**, and perhaps **one-fifth of Renly's cavalry** departed with Loras. Renly's **infantry and supplies remain at Bitterbridge** and their allegiance is unsettled.
4. **VERIFIED AS REPORT / INDIVIDUAL FATES UNCERTAIN:** Varys relays word that **Loras killed three Rainbow Guard/guards** in rage after Renly's body was discovered, including **Emmon Cuy and Robar Royce**. Their deaths are now specifically reported and are more than merely unknown, but **not witnessed in Tyrion's scene**; `ACOK 33` showed Robar had helped Brienne escape. No on-page evidence in this chapter establishes Loras knew of Robar's protective act when attacking him.
5. **VARYS'S REPORT:** **Cortnay Penrose** still holds **Storm's End** and demands to see Renly's corpse before surrender, but the **body has disappeared from the expected place**. Penrose's persistence delays Stannis. The body may have been removed in the immediate chaos; **cause of disappearance is not directly observed or explained**.
6. **VERIFIED / EMERGENT ALLIANCE STRATEGY:** Tyrion sees a narrow chance to win **Loras** and the **Tyrell/Reach** alliance before Stannis absorbs the whole coalition. Gold and honors might induce lesser lords, but the critical prize is **Highgarden**, where **Mace Tyrell** is thought to favor his youngest son Loras despite having two older sons. Tyrion proposes **Joffrey marrying Renly's widow Margaery Tyrell**, using marriage as Renly previously did. It is a **proposed diplomatic approach** at chapter end, not concluded wedding or Reach defection.
7. **VERIFIED / CORRECTION TO SANSA PROMISE:** **Cersei objects that Joffrey is still betrothed to Sansa Stark**, but Tyrion replies that contracts may be broken and the defeated Stark house offers less strategic value than Tyrell armies. This gives concrete political backing to his statement in `ACOK 32` that he does not mean Sansa to marry Joffrey; **no actual cancellation or Sansa's freedom is shown**.
8. **VERIFIED / COUNCIL SEXUAL-POLITICAL CONFLICT:** During debate Tyrion invokes Joffrey's stripping of Sansa, and Cersei minimizes it as anger. Tyrion privately considers **arranging a visit to Chataya's brothel** to direct Joffrey's cruelty or appetites elsewhere, and has asked Varys to identify when **Sandor Clegane** would be away from his guard post. Varys notes such knowledge could facilitate harming the king. This is an unsettling contemplated scheme and a spy's warning—not an enacted trip.
9. **VERIFIED:** Cersei eventually **consents to making the proposal**, arguing Joffrey might resent Renly's former wife. She tries to remove Tyrion from the city by nominating him ambassador; Tyrion counters by suggesting Cersei. Both fear losing home control while abroad. **Littlefinger volunteers**, emphasizing acquaintance with Loras, his council rank and ability to negotiate.
10. **VERIFIED / NEGOTIATING CONTROL:** Tyrion reluctantly authorizes Littlefinger as emissary to **Bitterbridge** with **300 gold cloaks plus 20 knights and 20 squires**, a substantial remount reserve and virtually unlimited gifts. Petyr insists on written, sealed authority from **Joffrey and all council members**, empowering him to negotiate the marriage and **other binding arrangements in the king's name**. He also hints at future rewards but does not specify them. This is an extremely broad principal–agent delegation to an already suspect and well-connected official.
11. **VERIFIED / REDWYNE HOSTAGES:** **Paxter Redwyne**, master of valuable fleets and Mace Tyrell's friend, has two sons **Hobber and Horas ('Horror' and 'Slobber')** effectively held at the Lannister court. Littlefinger asks to return both as goodwill. Cersei wants to keep both to coerce the Redwyne fleet. Tyrion engineers compromise: **send Hobber to the Arbor and retain Horas**. It creates a demonstrative bargain combining hostage release and continued leverage, not proof Paxter has switched allegiance.
12. **VERIFIED / DEFENSE AND FORESHADOWING:** Tyrion says the massive harbor **chain is being assembled link by link**. He notes that Penrose's defense of Storm's End temporarily delays Stannis's march north. At the end, Cersei is inexplicably **warm**, thanks Tyrion and **kisses his forehead**; Tyrion suspects a new counterplot. Her pleasant behavior itself is observed, **her precise intent not yet verified**.
13. **INFORMATION NETWORK:** Renly's death news now travels from `ACOK 33` witness level into King's Landing in contradictory fragments, while councilors reallocate troops by negotiation. The novel specifically shows how **uncertain reports can still rationally produce urgent political action**.

## Theory and continuity tests

- **Tyrell agency:** Loras, Margaery, Mace and the other Reach lords can redirect Stannis's numerical advantage. None has agreed to Littlefinger's marriage bid yet; do not count all 'Renly's men' as Stannis men.
- **Brienne/Robar:** The novel uses differently reliable sources: direct testimony demonstrates Brienne's innocence and Robar's help; the council receives apparently credible *reports* that Loras retaliated against Robar. Distinguish **report of death** from direct in-scene confirmation and examine subsequent corroboration.
- **Littlefinger's authority:** The most consequential overlooked detail may be the **binding commission** plus escort and hostage negotiations. Even an initially legitimate marriage mission lets him set new terms beyond a simple message delivery.
- **Cersei:** Her warmth could be calculated; no specific plot is disclosed. Avoid narrating Tyrion's suspicion as fact.
