## Chronological narrative and decisive events

1. **VERIFIED:** Davos returns with **Black Betha** to the fleet off **Storm's End**, after eight days without a direct royal audience. **Devan Seaworth**, his son serving Stannis, reports that since Renly's death the king **sleeps poorly, experiences alarming dreams, and is soothed by Melisandre**. Davos notices Stannis has become haggard and his camp is thick with **newly converted Renly bannermen**, with **Alester Florent** first to take Stannis's side and the red god after Renly's death. Devan has adopted the **flaming heart** on his clothes. Their beliefs and loyalties are differentiated from Davos's private unease.
2. **VERIFIED / SECOND PARLEY:** **Ser Cortnay Penrose** meets Stannis outside the huge curtain walls. He refuses to call him king and instead insists on continuing to hold the fortress for deceased **Renly** until **Edric Storm**, Robert's bastard son by **Delena Florent**, is protected. Stannis offers pardon, no harm to the garrison and permission to depart with possessions, **but demands both Edric and the castle**. Penrose repeatedly refuses, exposing that this is more than a simple contest for the garrison's surrender.
3. **VERIFIED / LOYALTY INVERSION:** Penrose denounces the new converts—**Guyard Morrigen, Bryce Caron, the Fossoways, Estermont, Errol, Varner and Florent**—as men who have repeatedly changed lords. He defends Brienne's character based on **knowing her from youth**, while **Guyard, Caron and Florent** repeat inconsistent accusations that **Brienne** or **Catelyn** killed Renly. **Caron** reports Emmon Cuy blamed Brienne before dying: his report is not direct proof she did so, and `ACOK 33 Catelyn` explicitly contradicts it. Davos recognizes the pragmatic irony: an officer devoted to a dead man may be more trustworthy than newly sworn royal followers.
4. **VERIFIED / THE OFFER OF SINGLE COMBAT:** Penrose **throws down his glove** and challenges Stannis or a champion to a duel to settle Storm's End. Guyard and Caron offer to fight. Stannis refuses with a rational military reason: he says he has **20,000 soldiers**, a land-and-sea siege, and no need to stake victory on one man. **Estermont** wants one-on-one combat to avoid thousands dying in an assault, and other lords seek personal glory. Neither the challenge nor Stannis's refusal is proof Penrose plans a hidden betrayal.
5. **VERIFIED / HONEST COUNSEL:** Away from flatterers Stannis asks Davos for his assessment. **Alester** has suggested hanging the aging, sickly **Lord Penrose**, Cortnay's father, in sight of the castle. Davos argues correctly that killing him would be both dishonorable and unlikely to make Cortnay abandon his trust. Stannis presses him on whether current lords should be called traitors; Davos observes that a year ago they served Robert, recently Renly, now Stannis, and their allegiance tomorrow is uncertain. Stannis **laughs and explicitly praises Davos for telling hard truths**.
6. **VERIFIED / PERSONAL JUSTICE:** Stannis recalls cutting off the joints of Davos's smuggler fingers despite rewarding him for delivering onions during the old siege. His principle is that **good acts and bad acts require separate rewards and punishments**. He resents pardoning powerful deserters whom he considers worse than lesser men he earlier judged harshly. Davos still wears his **fingerbones** in a pouch, recalling both background and the king's painful, uneven definition of justice. **Do not classify these lords as legally proven treasonous** simply because Stannis does.
7. **STANNIS'S ACCOUNT / CRITICAL CONSCIOUSNESS CLUE:** Stannis recalls **dreaming of Renly's death**—green tent, candles, screaming woman and blood—while **his body remained in bed** near dawn. He says **Devan tried and failed to wake him**, that he thrashed and cried out, and he awoke with **clean hands**. This closely matches directly narrated `ACOK 33 Catelyn` details and strongly links Stannis's *dreaming consciousness* to the shadow killing, but is **his account of an experience, not eyewitness proof that he deliberately directed the blow**.
8. **VERIFIED / PEACH FOLLOW-UP:** Stannis remains fixated on the **peach Renly offered** at the earlier parley (`ACOK 31`), wondering whether it was a jest, trap or invitation to enjoy life. He admits he **loved his brother**, though he thinks Renly brought his fate on himself. This supports the peach's strong **personal and psychological** significance; it does not prove the peach itself caused or forecast the murder.
9. **VERIFIED / STRATEGIC ALTERNATIVE REPEATED:** In Stannis's plain military pavilion, **Davos explicitly urges sailing immediately to King's Landing** and leaving Cortnay to hold Storm's End, since Tyrion and Joffrey are the true strategic threats. Stannis sees the logic but declines because leaving the fortress untaken would be read as **a personal defeat that would destroy his rule by fear**. This echoes **Mathis Rowan's alternative in ACOK 31**, creating a cross-party missed-strategy pattern. Stannis is worried about **Doran's Dorne movement** and the **~60,000 infantry remaining at Bitterbridge**, which his **envoys Ser Errol and Ser Parmen Crane** have not yet brought over; he fears **Loras** reached them first. His numbers and worries are informed estimates, not independently counted forces.
10. **STANNIS ON MELISANDRE'S VISIONS:** Stannis says Melisandre had predicted Renly would die at Storm's End and that Stannis would gain the greater part of Renly's power there. He adds a **different vision** of Renly defeating Stannis before King's Landing if they fought there. Davos correctly objects that outcomes cannot both occur; Stannis replies that flames show **multiple possible future 'shadows'**. This **complicates Jojen's fixed-future claim in `ACOK 35 Bran`** and may represent branching potential, selective forecasting or post hoc rationalization. Visions are character reports, not proof all seen outcomes would actually happen.
11. **STANNIS'S EXPLICIT FOREKNOWLEDGE / COMMAND:** Stannis says **Cortnay will be dead within a day**, not by a lawful duel, and that command will pass to the more pliant **Lord Meadows**; he credits Melisandre's flames. He asks Davos to **smuggle Melisandre in a small boat beneath the castle without witnesses**, and states he **must have Edric Storm** because Melisandre saw it in the flames. Davos recognizes he is being asked to facilitate death and protests. This is an **operational authorization with foreseeable deadly objective**, materially stronger evidence of responsibility than Stannis's earlier denial of physically killing Renly.
12. **VERIFIED / NIGHT VOYAGE:** Davos sails Melisandre across **Shipbreaker Bay** in a black-sailed skiff, following the tide and rocky coast as he had done **sixteen years earlier** to smuggle food to besieged Stannis. The identical route has been transformed from **life-saving onions to delivery of a killer**. He recognizes that every plan about the castle's alleged inevitability still depends on an agent like himself to make it happen. **Melisandre explicitly calls out his participation** despite his claim to take no part in the actual act.
13. **VERIFIED / IDEOLOGICAL CONFLICT:** Melisandre proposes a binary morality: a person with any rot is entirely rotten. Davos rejects that as inconsistent with ordinary people, who are **mixtures of good and bad**—a strong contrast with Stannis's separate-rewards justice. She also claims **Cressen tried to poison her**, so his fatal poison was self-inflicted, and denies **personally** killing Renly. Cressen's attempted poisoning has independent earlier POV support, but the exact truth conditions behind her 'not I' answer are **partly semantic**, not exoneration.
14. **EXPLICIT MAGIC CLAIM / STORM'S END WALLS:** Melisandre says she needed **no smuggler's boat for Renly** because he was **unprotected**, while **Storm's End's masonry contains old, still-effective spells that no shadow can cross**. Davos passes by water into the **ancient sea cave and landing tunnel under the castle** at high tide, but cannot proceed through its barred **portcullis**. This grounds the route in specific geography and suggests an **ancient magical threshold** rather than a generic assassin simply sneaking in through a door. The efficacy of the alleged wall spells is **Melisandre's explanation**, not directly tested by an independent physical experiment.
15. **VERIFIED / DIRECT SUPERNATURAL WITNESS:** Below the fortress Melisandre removes her robe and Davos sees her body **grossly swollen with pregnancy**. In an intense, unnatural birth she produces a **full-sized, moving shadow shaped like a man**, which slips through the bars and races along the water beyond the portcullis. Davos recognizes the shadow as belonging to **Stannis**. This is a direct eyewitness event and the decisive supernatural corroboration for the earlier **Renly death shadow**, without yet showing the shadow strike **Penrose** on-page. The method of forming the shadow and its dependence on Stannis, intimacy or flames remain incompletely specified.

## Hypothesis ranking / challenges

- **Shadow murders:** `ACOK 33` provides Catelyn's direct Renly death by a sword shadow while the actual blade remains sheathed; this chapter provides **physical manifestation of a Stannis-shaped killer via Melisandre**. A supernatural assassination method is now strongly corroborated from distinct POVs. **Mechanism, number of shadows, Stannis's volitional awareness during Renly's death, and exact Penrose outcome remain separate tests**.
- **Foreknowledge vs execution:** Melisandre predicts Penrose's death, but Davos's actions help manufacture the predicted outcome. These are not independent trials proving prediction can override free choices; the apparent fulfillment may require human compliance.
- **Edric:** Stannis insists on custody of his brother's bastard for unstated flame-based necessity, while Cortnay regards the boy as his trust. The risk to Edric remains open and should not be filled with later-book knowledge prematurely.
- **Politics:** Loyal Penrose vs opportunist former Renly lords helps test the strength of public oaths. Stannis admires truth and intends assassination-like methods; contrasting virtues and defects coexist.
