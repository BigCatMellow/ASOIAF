## Main chronology and immediate consequences

1. **VERIFIED / THEON'S FRAGILE OCCUPATION:** Theon wakes uneasy in **Ned Stark's former bedchamber** with **Kyra**, an eighteen-year-old from Winterfell's winter town, while his mute squire **Wex** sleeps on the floor. He remembers taking Winterfell with **fewer than thirty men** (`ACOK 46`) but fears he cannot defend it against a loyal northern uprising. He has asked **Asha** for reinforcements; messenger **Stygg** may or may not have reached Deepwood Motte. Quiet rather than noise alarms him: the **direwolves have stopped howling**.
2. **VERIFIED / THE WARDS GONE:** Theon sends **Wex** to check Bran and Rickon's rooms and **Urzen** to investigate the wolves. Both return with the rooms **empty** and wolves **gone**. Theon regrets spending guards on walls and gates rather than directly watching the Stark boys. He orders all inhabitants assembled to identify missing servants and an immediate investigation of **Hunter's Gate**. **This is direct proof of escape from custody**, not that the boys have reached safety or left the North.
3. **VERIFIED / THIRD SEA-DREAM FATALITY:** Theon internally acknowledges **Septon Chayle** was thrown into a **well** as an offering to the Drowned God, a choice he rationalizes by saying his men expected it. `ACOK 35 Bran` named **Mikken, Alebelly and Chayle** among the sea-dream dead; `ACOK 46` directly confirmed **Alebelly and Mikken** killed; this chapter now **confirms Chayle's death through Theon's memory**, strongly completing that prophecy's named-casualty component. **The exact interval and witnesses at the well are not narrated live here.**
4. **VERIFIED / GATE GUARD SCENE:** **Drennan**, who participated in the sexual assault on **Palla**, is found with his throat cut while partly undressed inside the gatehouse, with cheese, an empty flask and **two drinking cups** nearby. His back shows scars from a prior whipping ordered by Theon. **Squint**, the other sentry, is discovered in the moat **disemboweled and partially torn apart**, with wounds consistent with two direwolves. Theon deduces a **female conspirator distracted and killed Drennan**, lowered the drawbridge and let the wolves kill Squint. The evidence and circumstances make this plausible, but the narrator **does not witness the woman strike or identify the exact murderer**.
5. **VERIFIED / OVERSTRETCHED FORTIFICATIONS:** Winterfell has **two massive granite walls and a moat**; Theon abandoned the **outer eighty-foot wall** because his small detachment cannot man both systems, guarding the taller inner wall instead. The attacker's own success has exposed how **insufficient personnel** make formal fortifications brittle. He imagines direwolves killed Squint too quickly for a horn signal; it is **Theon's reconstruction**, though confirmed animal wounds make it plausible.
6. **VERIFIED / SIX PERSONS MISSING:** **Reek** reports precisely **six persons** gone: **Bran, Rickon, Jojen, Meera, Hodor and Osha**, alongside the **two missing direwolves**. **No horses** are missing; **Dancer** remains in his stall. Theon reasons Hodor carried Bran in a basket and Osha perhaps Rickon, moving slowly on foot, so searchers should overtake them. The exact split of burdens and route are **Theon's inference, not firsthand witnessed departure**.
7. **VERIFIED / OCCUPATION RESENTMENT:** Theon's threats at a predawn muster elicit only silence from **Old Nan, Gage, Farlen, Joseth and other known household members**. He realizes their long familiarity has turned to hatred. He credits himself for having two rapists flogged and not permitting wholesale killing, while **blaming Mikken and Benfred for causing their own deaths**. His self-justification does not change his accountability for rule enforced by armed coercion and Chayle's killing.
8. **REEK / TORTURE LOGIC:** **Reek** counsels **flaying** the servants to extract information, citing Bolton practice. Theon loudly refuses, claiming he will allow **no flaying under his rule**; he wants to appear less cruel than the Dreadfort servant. Reek's readiness to recommend Bolton methods and his detailed local knowledge are meaningful **character signals**, not final proof of his hidden identity.
9. **VERIFIED / SEARCH PARTY:** At sunrise Theon assembles **eleven men, two boys and about a dozen dogs**, taking skilled huntsmen **Gariss, Murch, Farlen, Joseth**, his own Ironborn **Aggar, Gelmarr, Rednose** and **Reek**, plus **Wex**, Maester **Luwin** and a **Walder Frey** ward. **Black Lorren** is left in control of Winterfell. **Farlen** is pressured to serve through implied threats against **Palla**; Luwin is brought partly because Theon distrusts leaving him in the castle. This is a coerced hunt, not voluntary unanimous participation.
10. **DIRECT TRAIL:** Bootmarks and the wolves' tracks show the group initially **went north/northwest** into the **wolfswood**, not south toward **Ser Rodrik**. Farlen's red bitch follows the wolves' scent. The group encounters a **freshly killed elk** on which wolves fed, but **no cuts made by human knives**, a discrepancy Theon notices. Theon has no independent observation that the six people were ever present at the kill.
11. **CRITICAL TRACKING DISCONTINUITY:** At a swollen, muddy stream, the wolves' tracks run into water. **Wex demonstrates with his heel** that someone as heavy as **Hodor carrying Bran** would leave obvious deep footprints in the soft mud. **No such human prints are present**, so the wolves reached the creek **without the human party**, strongly implying **a deliberate decoy/separation earlier**. This is a substantial physical inference, independent of whether the wolves can run upstream or downstream for miles. **Do not assume the wolves are dead or that Bran's group crossed that point.**
12. **THEON'S FAILED SEARCH:** Men and dogs divide along both banks and retrace their way toward Winterfell but cannot recover the human trail, even when Theon forces search well into dusk. Moving water has erased the direwolves' scent and prints; the hunt is **not successful by ordinary tracking**. Theon considers the possibility of **crannogman stealth**, blaming Jojen and Meera. The **Walder** repeats grotesque unverified stereotypes of mudmen breathing swamp water, while **Luwin** recounts a historical belief that crannogmen were close to **children of the forest** and greenseers who attempted a 'hammer of the waters' in the Neck. The magical claims are **legends and hypotheses**, not direct confirmation a spell hid the boys.
13. **VERIFIED / THEON'S POLITICAL FEAR:** He knows an escaped Stark heir could rapidly obtain food, horses and fighting men from northern villages and lords. **His own legitimacy as Winterfell's lord depends on retaining the boys as living hostages**. Luwin earlier warns that **Howland Reed** could disrupt **Moat Cailin** if his children were harmed; Theon accepts the strategic value of living Reeds. Yet he threatens to kill **Osha** for violating her forced oath and increasingly fears mockery from **Balon and Asha** more than he fears the cost of cruelty.
14. **REEK'S CRITICAL INTERVENTION:** Reek suggests the Stark boys may have gone toward **an old mill on Acorn Water**, where he says a **miller's wife once sold hay to his captors**. Theon knows the mill and has previously had sexual encounters with the miller's wife. Reek's **specific knowledge** and timing are important; no physical boy tracks at the mill are seen or verified in this chapter, and his assertion is no more than an **interested proposal** at this point.
15. **DIRECT OBJECT / CLIFFHANGER:** Reek reveals items in an **overstuffed washerwoman's sack**, among them a **wolf-head brooch of silver and jet**. `ACOK 46 Bran` directly described **Bran fastening a favorite silver-and-jet wolf-head clasp** when dressed for surrender. The exact description is a strong **object-continuity match**, but **possession of Bran's brooch is not direct proof that Reek knows the boys' present location, that the boys are at the mill or that anyone has died**. Reek may have found or taken it earlier, and no provenance is explained.
16. **ENDING DECISION:** Theon quietly keeps only **Gelmarr, Aggar, Rednose and Reek** with him, dismisses other trackers back toward Winterfell, declares he now knows where Bran and Rickon are hiding, and **rejects Luwin's reminder of promised mercy** because he feels humiliated. **The chapter ends before any mill assault, killing or presentation of bodies**. This is the precise boundary against later-story knowledge.

## Hypothesis tests and continuation targets

- **Wolf-diversion hypothesis:** Absence of human prints at the stream is robust, supporting a **decoy to draw trackers**. The text does not reveal **who planned the diversion** or where the people actually went.
- **Reek identity and motives:** His unusually accurate knowledge of Bolton practices, escort history, local mill and possession of distinctive clasp warrants scrutiny, but no scene yet proves he is someone other than **Heke**. His mill direction could be help, a trap or a plan to substitute other victims.
- **Threefold sea dream:** **Alebelly, Mikken and Chayle** are now all established killed under the Ironborn occupation, matching Jojen's named-drowning vision as **symbolic Ironborn deaths**, not actual rising seawater.
- **Theon's rule:** He believes raw violence, image and holding the heirs will sustain a conquered people, but conducts self-defeating rape reprisals, ritual sacrifice, terror and secret hunts with too few loyal troops. His rejection of Luwin's mercy advice at the end is a sharp directional signal, **not a witnessed conclusion**.
