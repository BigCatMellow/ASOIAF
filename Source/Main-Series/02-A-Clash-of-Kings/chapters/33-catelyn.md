## Sequence, direct observations, and outcomes

1. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn visits a **deserted village's modest sept** overnight with **Wendel Manderly and Robar Royce** escorting. The seven-sided building has only charcoal drawings of the Seven, with a **star-eyed black oval representing the Stranger**. The narrator relays religious imagery of the **seven aspects of one god** and Catelyn's own grief, not supernatural proof that the figures animate.
2. **VERIFIED / CATELYN'S REASSESSMENT:** Flickering torchlight lets Catelyn project faces of **Hoster, Renly, Stannis, Ned, Arya and Cersei** onto the drawings. She remembers her mother **Minisa Tully** died attempting to bear Hoster a second son, with the baby dying as well. She draws an empathy link between her own protection of Bran and **Cersei's feelings as mother**, and independently infers that **Bran may have seen Jaime and Cersei together** because Jaime stayed in Winterfell when the hunt departed. This is a **strong inference from the timing and failed assassination**, not a remembered eyewitness account from Bran; `AGOT 8` directly narrates the encounter to the reader.
3. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn and escort return to Renly's massive mounted host in the predawn darkness. **Renly, wearing elaborate green armor**, is being armored by **Brienne** inside a torchlit green-silk pavilion; **Randyll Tarly and Mathis Rowan** discuss tactics. Their mounted army waits amid cool dawn light and a time-sensitive plan to attack Stannis's smaller force.
4. **TACTICAL DISCUSSION:** Rowan proposes launching **before daylight** while the enemy is unprepared. Renly refuses on chivalric appearance grounds, insisting dawn was the named hour. **Tarly warns the rising sun will blind their eastern advance**, but Renly trusts **Loras's vanguard** to break Stannis's line at first impact. Thus both the planned timing and an explicit operational drawback are established; no conventional battle actually occurs in this chapter.
5. **RENLY'S PAST ACCOUNT:** Renly recalls a story of an earlier **siege of Storm's End**: **Gawen Wylde** and others allegedly tried to surrender, Stannis threatened catapult execution but **Maester Cressen** persuaded against wasting potential food in the famine; **Gawen died in his cell**. Rowan disputes that anyone was hurled from the walls. This is attributed siege history and includes **an explicit in-scene correction**, so avoid false claims that Stannis actually launched captured knights.
6. **VERIFIED:** Renly orders that Stannis's corpse not be desecrated if he falls, and wants **Barristan Selmy spared** if found in Stannis's army, having no proof where the dismissed Kingsguard has gone. Renly tells his commanders to prepare for attack and permits Catelyn to raise one last issue.
7. **CATELYN'S UNFULFILLED PROPOSAL:** Catelyn asks permission to bring her theory about Bran witnessing incest to Stannis, and proposes **all three kings (Robb, Renly and Stannis) setting aside their crowns**, convening a **Great Council**, obtaining Bran's testimony and having assembled lords choose a ruler. She hopes she can persuade Robb; her claim **Robb would willingly give up his crown is an unverified pledge**, not a decision he made. Renly rejects council and deliberation with a wolf-pack analogy; his version of legitimacy remains military strength.
8. **VERIFIED / CRITICAL PHYSICAL EVENT:** As Brienne finishes fastening Renly's sword and gear, a **gust enters** the tent. Catelyn sees a strange **moving sword-shaped shadow** against the green canvas, initially thinks it is Renly's, but notices **Renly's actual sword remains sheathed**. The shadow appears to cut his **gorget**, which opens despite no physically present visible blade, and he suffers a catastrophic **throat wound**. He dies moments later in **Brienne's arms**, blood covering his green-and-gold armor. This is an extraordinary *directly narrated physical outcome* and a character's visual perception, not an observed assassin in solid form.
9. **EVIDENCE LIMITS:** Catelyn says this shadow belonged to **Stannis** and later calls the event **sorcery**, but she does not witness **Stannis or Melisandre** enter the tent or narrate the precise magic/assassin-generation mechanism. The connection with Stannis's ultimatum and religious practitioner is **highly suspicious**, not a license to supply unseen mechanics. A purely conventional blade seems inconsistent with the observed sword-in-scabbard anomaly; dismissing the shadow as entirely fanciful would also be under-supported.
10. **VERIFIED / FALSE ACCUSATIONS:** Rainbow Guards **Robar Royce** and **Emmon Cuy** with two men-at-arms rush inside and find **Brienne drenched in Renly's blood**. Emmon attacks her with an axe; Brienne takes **Renly's own sword** to defend herself, cuts an axe shaft, wounds an attacker, and resists their assault. She is **not shown murdering Renly** and Catelyn directly witnesses the preceding impossible wound. The guards' suspicion arises from a misleading crime-scene tableau.
11. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn wins **Robar's trust**, swearing by Ned's memory that Brienne did not kill Renly, and asks him to **hold other pursuers off**. Robar stays behind, while Catelyn incapacitates Emmon with a **brazier** and Brienne cuts through the pavilion wall. They leave amid fire and chaos, walking rather than running to avoid attention. **Robar's future fate is unverified in this chapter**; do not mark him dead solely because he stays behind.
12. **VERIFIED:** Brienne, now stripped of her independent reputation, swears to **kill Stannis with Renly's sword**, accepting Catelyn's account even though she cannot explain it. Catelyn and her escort provide a mount, abandon Brienne's armor and flee camp before dawn. Rumors of fire, murder and sorcery disrupt the army before a battlefield clash. Catelyn infers the now-leaderless Baratheon supporters will transfer to **Stannis**, but no actual mass pledge or defection has yet occurred on-page. The chapter ends with Catelyn recalling Stannis's threat that Robb's day would come.

## Competing theories and immediate verification gates

- **Physical cause:** The gorget's inexplicable severance and unnatural shadow are directly narrated, giving exceptional weight to a **supernatural assassination hypothesis**. Still keep **who made the shadow**, **which magic**, and **Stannis's conscious participation** as separate questions needing additional POV corroboration.
- **Catelyn:** She identifies a potential evidence-based succession settlement in a Great Council but cannot obtain consent from any rival king, illustrating how close alternatives can be to catastrophe. Her observational credibility helps protect Brienne.
- **Brienne:** Her private devotion to Renly, direct presence at his death and entirely understandable survival defense yield a public suspicion radically at odds with events. Do not confuse her vengeance oath with a completed act.
- **Stannis succession:** The loss of a younger claimant could free Renly's lordly coalition for Stannis, but the immediate political consequences remain in **Catelyn's forecast**, not sworn realignments witnessed here.
