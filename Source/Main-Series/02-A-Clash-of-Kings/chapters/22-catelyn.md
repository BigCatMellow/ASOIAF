## Causal sequence and directly narrated events

1. **VERIFIED:** Sleeping on the road, Catelyn dreams of an intact family, healthy **Bran**, reunited **Sansa and Arya**, infant **Rickon**, crownless **Robb** and living **Ned**. Waking produces fatigue and a wish to be allowed to mourn rather than continually perform strength. Her **scarred hands** recall the Valyrian-steel dagger she stopped during Bran's attempted assassination. She drinks **Shadd's nettle tea** and rejects Wendel Manderly's hoped-for game breakfast to conserve travel time.
2. **VERIFIED / BACKDATED CONVERSATION:** Robb compelled Catelyn to visit **Renly** because he could not go, **Hoster** is dying, **Brynden Blackfish** is indispensable for intelligence, and **Edmure** must hold Riverrun. Robb needs movement before northern and river lords disperse for home/harvest; he **denies any intention to march straight on Harrenhal**, despite Catelyn's fear of Tywin's trap. He uses the implicit alternative of sending **Greatjon Umber** as a persuasive maneuver. Catelyn reports Hoster confused her with **Minisa**, calling for his young daughters; his medical prognosis is **inferred from condition**, not witnessed death.
3. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn travels with **twenty selected Stark men** and **five highborn escorts**, among them Wendel Manderly, with **Shadd** and **Hallis Mollen**. They avoid population centers and are intercepted by **Ser Colen of Greenpools's twenty mounted outriders** after a lookout watches from a windmill. Colen courteously insists Renly is king of *all* Seven Kingdoms; Catelyn says she is Robb's envoy from the North. Whether this is an escort or detention remains **her initial uncertainty**, not a proven arrest.
4. **VERIFIED:** Renly's camp stretches about **Bitterbridge** on the Mander, divided by river and marked by a giant golden **crowned black stag** standard. **Highgarden's golden roses** and numerous Reach/Stormlords' sigils dominate; the host has horses, wagons, siege engines, followers and vast food/logistical requirements. These are direct observations of a large field force, **not direct verification of Renly's later numerical totals**.
5. **VERIFIED:** Instead of campaigning Renly is holding a great **melee/tourney**. **Ser Loras Tyrell** fights well, but the blue-armored **Brienne of Tarth**, Lord Selwyn's daughter, defeats him by wresting his weapon away and forcing a yield, becoming last victor of **116**. Renly grants her wished-for place in the **Rainbow Guard**; she openly swears her life to him. This is a proven display of ability, not merely Renly's patronage. The nicknames invoking 'beauty' are often cruel; Catelyn's own pity and gender expectations must be separated from Brienne's actual competence.
6. **VERIFIED:** **Renly**, aged **21**, visibly resembles dead Robert (coal-black hair, blue eyes, presence), and his young wife **Margaery Tyrell**, daughter of Mace, furnishes the alliance's central dynastic bond. Her youth is noted, but her personal assessment is largely courtesy observed by Catelyn. **Loras** has strong proximity to Renly and holds a Rainbow Guard cloak; Renly publicly favors him, with no need to insert unsupported private conversations.
7. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn refuses to call Renly 'Your Grace' or kneel, correcting Colen that Robb is also a king. Renly laughs off the protocol insult, receives her graciously, promises vengeance for Ned, and asks about Robb's forces and **Jaime**. Catelyn confirms Jaime alive at **Riverrun** but refuses deployment intelligence. **Randyll Tarly** calls Robb weak and disparages sending his mother; she rebukes him for tourney play during war.
8. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn is lodged in Renly's luxurious field pavilion with a near-armory of weapons, books, harps, maps, hawks, bathing and comforts. The lavish train helps explain its slow movement without demonstrating Renly cannot fight. **Lord Caswell's** farewell banquet showcases abundant Reach food, noisy summer knights, cruel jokes about Tyrion's size, and Brienne's relative isolation. **Mathis Rowan** notes their youth; Catelyn calls them '**knights of summer**', vulnerable to war's realities. Brienne answers that songs give knights a perpetual summer, a moving **romantic ideal** rather than immunity from violence.
9. **RENLY'S CHARACTER ACCOUNT:** While walking apart on castle stairs, Renly recounts his earlier offer of **one hundred swords** to Ned to seize Joffrey after Robert's death; Ned refused. Renly thinks Ned's rigidity helped cause the crisis and that he had to flee Cersei. Catelyn inwardly thinks Renly's support might instead have saved Ned. Both are **counterfactual judgments**. Renly separately asks if **Barristan Selmy** has appeared at Riverrun; the vacant Rainbow Guard slot was kept for Barristan before Brienne won it, but neither side knows his current location.
10. **VERIFIED / RENLY'S NUMERICAL CLAIM:** On Caswell's roof Renly shows Catelyn a sea of campfires and estimates his present force at **twice his estimate of Robb's perhaps forty thousand** (thus ~80,000 present), with **10,000 more under Mace at Highgarden** and a Storm's End garrison. He **asserts** Dorne will join and describes Stannis as holding Dragonstone, but this is *not proof Doran committed*. Catelyn thinks Robb has nowhere near 40,000. Renly deliberately treats Robert's conquest as a better precedent than **Stannis's legal priority**, arguing the throne goes to who can take it.
11. **VERIFIED / CORE DIPLOMATIC IMPASSE:** Renly offers Robb the right to keep the **word 'king'** and rule Winterfell locally, **only if Robb bends the knee, pays fealty and is subordinate**. Catelyn recognizes that this does not recognize Robb's sovereign independence. Renly says he will not rule a broken kingdom; refusal would mean an eventual conflict. There is *no concluded alliance*.
12. **VERIFIED / CLIFFHANGER:** As he speaks, a lathered rider with winged helm reaches **Bitterbridge from Storm's End**, announcing **Stannis has besieged the castle**, while **Ser Cortnay Penrose** resists. Renly had expected news of Tywin's movement, not an attack by his elder brother. The siege is a **messenger report within the scene**, not direct observation at Storm's End; it changes the strategic situation and Renly's assumptions.

## Character agency, competing explanations, future tests

- **Robb:** Politically fragile coalition must keep moving, but Catelyn fears a direct advance on Harrenhal. His denial of that specific objective is **real dialogue**, not evidence of his unannounced actual plan.
- **Catelyn:** Loyal to independence terms yet seeking an anti-Lannister coalition, keeps Robb's military information private, and treats negotiations as more urgent than titles. She may underestimate the usefulness of Renly's pageantry for recruitment.
- **Renly:** His concession of the word 'king' is meaningful only as cultural courtesy, because **fealty and sovereignty are different**. He openly knows Stannis is his elder brother and dismisses primogeniture in favor of military power.
- **Brienne:** Physical victory and genuine vow justify taking her seriously, despite Rowan/Catelyn/other onlookers' projections. Her songs about knighthood are idealistic, not proof she cannot respond to harsh circumstances.
