## Narrative — Winterfell's political and personal sequence

1. **VERIFIED:** A northern **harvest feast** brings lords and stewards to Winterfell with petitions. Eight-year-old **Bran**, acting as Robb's heir and the Stark in Winterfell, would rather be riding or practicing arms. **Hodor** carries him in a strapped wicker/leather back basket. Bran helps get his legs into it using **Mikken's wall bars** (cross-check `ACOK 4 (Bran)`). Hodor's movement is gentle but requires precautions: an earlier impulsive run toward bread once injured Bran's head, and he generally declines to wear an improvised protective helmet because the Frey wards mock him.
2. **VERIFIED:** Watching squires tilt at painted **Lannister quintains**, Bran studies the Walders' distinct equipment and heraldry. **Little Walder** bears Frey towers alongside **Crakehall's boar and Darry's plowman**; **Big Walder** has **Blackwood's tree/ravens and Paege snakes**. They mock Hodor's intelligence and threaten Bran's wolf. **Big Walder** apologizes more readily; **Little Walder** openly resents Luwin's rebuke and physically bumps Hodor with his horse. **Luwin** states that a good lord protects the vulnerable, and instructs them to keep away from the wolves. Their differences matter more than treating all Freys alike.
3. **VERIFIED:** **Wyman Manderly** asks recognition of **new White Harbor customs officers** because their predecessors withheld silver for King's Landing; he proposes a **northern coin mint** and construction of a **new royal fleet**, invoking the destruction of older northern ships attributed to **Brandon the Burner**. He estimates that with gold he could float sufficient galleys in a year to challenge King's Landing and Dragonstone; this is his **salesmanship/projection**, not a built fleet. Rodrik agrees to forward proposals to Robb. Taxes, currency and naval power are actual institutional challenges of independence.
4. **VERIFIED:** Manderly suggests himself or unmarried son **Wendel** as potential husband for widowed **Donella Hornwood**, who is his relative. He also reports **Tywin's offer** to free his captive elder son **Wylis** if Manderly withdraws from Robb's war. Manderly declares he will refuse, while seeking a legitimate prisoner exchange. The coercive offer is **reported from a letter**, not independently witnessed terms from Tywin.
5. **VERIFIED:** Lady Donella Hornwood arrives with just **six weary men-at-arms** after losing her husband at the Green Fork and son in the Whispering Wood. She stores **one-fifth** of her harvest for winter, raising it to **one-quarter** on Luwin's advice. Her vulnerability is personal, economic and military. She reports that **Roose Bolton's bastard** is mustering at the Dreadfort and, when asked why, refused to answer a woman; his servant **Reek** is allegedly foul and cruel, and the two allegedly hunt humans. Donella has heard **rumors**, not seen the crimes; Rodrik notes Roose did not previously acknowledge the boy, as far as he knows, and only brought him home after **Domeric** died.
6. **VERIFIED:** An heirless **Hornwood** estate threatens competition. Rodrik and Luwin name ties among **Tallhart, Flint, Karstark**, Manderly, **Glover** custody of bastard **Larence Snow**, and the opportunistic but no direct recorded claim of **the Dreadfort**. The widow dislikes **Mors Crowfood Umber** and Manderly as suitors, but she hints she might favor **Ser Rodrik**. Bran suggests Rodrik marry her or legitimizing/favoring the Hornwood bastard; both options receive serious analysis, not mere dismissal. Rodrik fears his age and **Beth's future** would leave the estate unstable.
7. **VERIFIED:** Bran visits **Summer** in the godswood; **Shaggydog** watches then withdraws. **Osha** has been swimming in the cold heart-tree pool and jokes about its depth, displays numerous scars and says she killed a Watchman. She warns that **Little Walder** could hurt both Bran and Hodor and remarks on Bran's **wolf dreams**, which he denies to her. None of this independently confirms a bottomless pool or how she learned of his dreams. Bran sleeps and dreams the **weirwood speaking/looking at him** with a three-eyed crow; this is the dreamer's experience.
8. **VERIFIED:** **Mors and Hother Umber**, elderly uncles of Greatjon, petition Winterfell. Mors wants Lady Hornwood's hand; his dragonglass false eye and tale of being pecked by a crow are part of his distinctive history as **Crowfood**, with Old Nan's spectacular elaborations attributed to her. **Hother** reports increasingly frequent **wildlings crossing the Bay of Seals in small boats** and Eastwatch's insufficient numbers, asking for northern **longships**. Rodrik urges cooperation with **Manderly's shipwrights** and the men reluctantly accept. Hother also warns the Greatjon took too many laborers south, endangering the harvest.
9. **VERIFIED:** The **Glover steward** currently withholds only **one-tenth** of harvest, trusting a **hedge wizard's warm-season prediction**. Luwin rejects that confidence and Rodrik orders **one-fifth** set aside. The steward reports **Larence Snow**, Hornwood's approximately twelve-year-old bastard fostered at Deepwood Motte, is clever and courageous. Distinguish his second-hand assessment from Bran's own knowledge.
10. **VERIFIED:** **Leobald Tallhart** warns that **Benfred** leads inexperienced young mounted **Wild Hares** wearing rabbit skins and seeking glory despite orders to remain in the north. He proposes his younger son **Beren**, a Hornwood nephew, be fostered by the widow and potentially **take the Hornwood name**. Luwin finds this legally plausible; Rodrik fears a child cannot hold lands against stronger challengers. Bran says the choice should go to **Robb and Lady Donella**, showing a genuine small political improvement in his judgment. Rodrik and Luwin weigh whether Robb may need a different marriage to consolidate the riverlands. The decision remains **OPEN**.
11. **VERIFIED / MESSAGES NOT ALL RECEIVED:** More envoys and ravens arrive; notably **Howland Reed** does not appear, and the bastard of the Dreadfort declines the invitation. **Cley Cerwyn**, a friend of Bran, arrives with some two dozen lances and says **Stannis has declared himself king and accused Cersei of incest**. This independently confirms the *story has reached Cerwyn*, not that Stannis's raven necessarily reached Winterfell directly (compare `ACOK 10,15`).
12. **VERIFIED / DREAM-RECOLLECTION:** Bran has a bodily panic response to news of Cersei's relationship with Jaime. His subsequent nightmare shows the **three-eyed crow** pecking out his eyes until he gains another vision, then a **golden man** pushing him from a great height and repeating the earlier phrase about doing things for love. The dream echoes **Jaime's directly witnessed conduct in `AGOT 8 (Bran)`** while raising the question of repressed or emerging memory. The chapter verifies the dream, **not external proof of the crow's real-world identity or intervention**.

## Character agency and rival interpretations

**Bran** struggles with disability, rank and a wish to be a knight, but learns that leadership means taxation, crops, protection, succession and coalition-building. He defends Hodor against bullying even while he occasionally imagines disability as loss of possible greatness.

**Hornwood:** Donella's grief and need for security coexist with neighbors' strategic marriage interests. No candidate is presently chosen and no Bolton annexation accomplished. **Countercase to 'nobody knew danger':** she explicitly warns about the bastard and Rodrik voices concern; they may still underestimate the risk or lack capacity.

**Bolton/Reek:** Distinguish Donella's early rumors from independently verified cruelty in later Theon narratives. The Dreadfort's lack of direct Hornwood claim is not a lack of military opportunity.

**War logistics:** Manderly's future warships, Umber boats and reserves are plans; their approval, funding, launches and harvest implementation remain open. **Cley/Stannis:** Stannis's proclamation now demonstrably shapes northern political talk without assuming direct arrival of a specific copy.

**Bran/Jaime dream:** The precise recurrence of Jaime's action and phrase strongly favors memory resurfacing within Bran's experience. Alternative readings about the three-eyed crow's external identity are not justified solely by this nightmare. **Next:** `ACOK 21/28/35/46/69 (Bran)`, later Bolton/Hornwood source, and continuity of Stannis's proclamations.
