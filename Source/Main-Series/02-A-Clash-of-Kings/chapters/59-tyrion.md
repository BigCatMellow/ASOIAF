## Narrative sequence and decisive operational choices

1. **DIRECT OBSERVATION / SAME BATTLE:** Tyrion watches **both fleets burning on the Blackwater** from a merlon near the Mud Gate. He has to be **lifted up onto the wall** to see over it, a detail contrasting his command authority with physical vulnerabilities. He sees wildfire spreading in the river, killing **Stannis's and Joffrey's crews alike**, as flames reflect green from low clouds. He compares the spectacle to **dragonfire** and wonders whether Aegon felt similar emotions at the Field of Fire. This is **Tyrion's comparison**, not an actual dragon present at the siege.
2. **VICTORY RECOGNIZED AS INCOMPLETE:** The city's defenders cheer, but Tyrion knows the inferno is **a 'half victory'**: some southern galleys have reached or escaped toward their own bank, **eight or more enemy craft landed or wrecked below the walls**, and elements of Stannis's early lines are **upstream of the conflagration**. He estimates **30–40 enemy galleys may remain**, enough to ferry the remaining army. Those figures are his battlefield estimates obscured by smoke, not a final exhaustive inventory.
3. **CONFIRMATION OF THE CHAIN PLAN:** Tyrion recalls **Bronn's oxen** pulling the slow **chain winches** once Stannis's flagship had entered upriver. The chain rose **link by link** only after the fleet passed over it, sealing the escape toward the bay. This **inside-planner's account** corroborates `ACOK 58 Davos`, whose sailor spotted the lowered boom and later saw it raised. The chain is a **controlled engineering trap**; the subsequent spread of wildfire is **not fully controlled**.
4. **DELIBERATE ROYAL FLEET SACRIFICE:** Tyrion tells **Joffrey** that the royal navy **had to meet the invaders** or Stannis would have suspected the trap. By then **Kingslander, Queen Cersei, Loyal Man and Seaflower** are among the royal craft engulfed. The defenders' victory thus **consumes their own available ships** as part of making the enemy move into the kill zone, beyond accidental friendly fire.
5. **WILDFIRE AS AN UNPREDICTABLE FORCE:** Tyrion knows **firepots, arrows or stones can be aimed**, while released wildfire can shift with water and wind. The mixture of brilliant spectacle and human screams leaves him unable to cheer. He assigns part of the moral burden to **Stannis for leading an assault**, but also accepts he designed the devastating countermeasure. This is the viewpoint's **self-understanding**, not an objective division of responsibility in equal shares.
6. **NO TIME TO ADMIRE THE TRAP:** Tyrion notices men moving through the charred buildings outside the **Mud Gate** and orders **Jacelyn Bywater** alerted, while ordering **Ser Arneld** to rotate the **Three Whores trebuchets about thirty degrees west** to strike nearer the enemy landings. These are **responsive combat commands** to prevent a south-bank foothold, not a finished defense.
7. **ANTLER MEN PAYOFF / POLITICAL CRUELTY:** The **Antler Men** conspirators, identified by **Varys in `ACOK 49`** as would-be gate-openers for Stannis, have been **captured**, stripped and had **antlers nailed to their heads**. Tyrion permits **Joffrey to use the three trebuchets to throw the living prisoners toward the enemy**, fulfilling Joffrey's boast he would send them to Stannis. Earlier claims the men planned betrayal remain **Varys intelligence allegations**, but their **capture and punishment are now depicted**. This is not simply neutral military use of siege engines; it is **ritualized cruelty authorized by the king and indulged by Tyrion**.
8. **JOFFREY'S SAFETY AND PR:** Tyrion instructs **Osmund Kettleblack** to keep the young king **safe and in sight** while guarded by **Meryn Trant** and veteran gold cloaks, aware Cersei has threatened **Alayaya** if Joffrey is harmed (`ACOK 54`). Joffrey's apparent role is morale/command display and punitive entertainment; he is **not shown leading front-line attacks personally**.
9. **SECOND LAND ATTACK / KING'S GATE:** A runner announces **hundreds of Stannis's troops** have landed on the **tourney grounds** and a **battering ram is striking the King's Gate**. Tyrion rides there with **Podrick Payne and Mandon Moore**, reaching an organized but frightened mixed force of **sellswords, gold cloaks and horses**. The gate's heavy hinges visibly shudder under renewed blows; Stannis's landing forces **have not been annihilated by the wildfire**.
10. **SANDOR'S CONDITION:** **Sandor Clegane** has already led **three sorties**, losing **about half his men**, his horse, and having his helm **scorched, damaged and his face wounded**. The fires and screams have made him **physically shaken and unwilling to lead more men back into burning conditions**. Tyrion notices his **pale terror** despite Sandor's fierce record (`ACOK 52 Sansa` had established his aversion to fire). The Hound's refusal is a traumatic break and exhaustion, not proof he was a coward in all encounters; three preceding sorties are actual service.
11. **THE HOUND'S ALTERNATIVE:** Sandor proposes **opening the gate so the enemy surges in and then surrounding them**, rather than another sortie outside the walls. He curses Tyrion and explicitly refuses further exposure to wildfire. **Mandon Moore** invokes Tyrion's formal authority, but Sandor does not obey, draws his sword and asks for wine. Tyrion decides forcing Sandor back into combat may collapse the other frightened men.
12. **LEADERSHIP NOT REPLACED BY FORMAL OFFICE:** Tyrion considers appointing Ser Mandon instead, but concludes **Mandon inspires fear, not willingness to follow**, and so cannot hold the unit together. With the gate near breaking, Tyrion assumes **direct command of the sortie**, despite his own inexperience and physical limitations. This creates a new danger to the acting Hand, not an already proven attack success.
13. **TYRION'S MOTIVATIONAL LEVER:** At first **roughly twenty mounted men** respond. Tyrion calls out their contempt for his dwarfism, shaming others into joining until his small column grows substantially. He refuses to shout **Joffrey's name or Casterly Rock**, calling on them instead to **defend their own city** from destruction. That shift from dynastic legitimacy to **local defense and shared survival** is an important political motivator during a legitimacy crisis.
14. **CHAPTER END:** Tyrion rides toward the **sally port**, uncertain whether the newly rallied men actually follow him; he **does not look back**. The chapter **does not yet show Tyrion's success or failure at the King's Gate, his wound, or how Cersei's forthcoming orders affect the defenders**. This is a decision and launch, not a completed repulse.

## Competing readings and later tests

- **The chain's effectiveness:** Both Davos and Tyrion independently confirm the chain plus wildfire catastrophe. **Attacking troops still got ashore**, so a purely naval analysis overstates victory. Test the remaining assaults and outside forces.
- **Sandor's refusal:** He already led three costly sorties and has longstanding burn trauma. His refusal at the King's Gate matters tactically while remaining psychologically comprehensible; it should not be lazily labeled cowardice or entirely excused.
- **Antler Men:** Their treason was reported by Varys and the regime has evidently arrested them; the scope and fairness of all guilt claims are still unproved because no first-person conspirator narrative is available. Their spectacular deaths reveal Joffrey and Tyrion's willingness to use terror as morale ritual.
- **Leadership:** Tyrion becomes a credible field leader when formal force is insufficient, but his rhetoric relies on his soldiers' shame and fear for their homes, not simply love of him.
