## Chronological narrative and causal stakes

1. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn attends the brother-kings' **parley** in a cleared grove outside **Storm's End**, against her escorts' protests, desperate to prevent another war that leaves Robb isolated. She recalls **Ned's bloodless relief of Stannis** during the last siege, a model of victory without slaughter. Stannis's troops have felled trees to construct siege equipment. The castle is observed as an extraordinary fortress, with roughly **100-foot walls**, popularly said to be **40 feet thick in places and up to 80 on the seaward side**, and a single vast drum tower. The legendary builder **Durran Godsgrief**, sea-goddess **Elenei**, six failed castles, magical help from children of the forest or the youthful **Bran the Builder** are **variants of inherited myth**, not historical observations in Catelyn's own lifetime.
2. **VERIFIED:** Stannis arrives with **Melisandre**, a scarlet-clad red priestess who carries a yellow banner emblazoned with a **flaming red heart enclosing a much smaller crowned stag**; he has a flame-pointed crown and an unusually bright sword later called **Lightbringer**. His simpler clothes differ from Renly's green-and-gold finery. Neither iconography nor the visual brightness alone proves his Azor Ahai identity or that the physical blade's light is truly supernatural.
3. **VERIFIED / MOTIVES:** Stannis says he should have been **Robert's Hand** and resents the appointment of Ned, despite acknowledging Ned saved him at Robert's order. Catelyn values recovery of **Sansa and Arya** more than revenge on Cersei. Stannis promises to return them if found after taking King's Landing, without claiming they currently survive or that he will take it successfully. He states his strategic reason for attacking **Storm's End rather than King's Landing**: he must strip the great southron lords away from Renly first.
4. **VERIFIED:** Renly arrives last on purpose, escorted by **Brienne** carrying the traditional crowned-stag standard; the brothers open by arguing over banners and titles. Catelyn protests that Lannister armies continue to ravage the country while Baratheon claimants dispute protocol. **Stannis** calls Robb no less traitor than Renly and insists there are only subjects or enemies for a king, explicitly refusing genuine northern independence.
5. **VERIFIED / IMPORTANT INTELLIGENCE CROSSOVER:** Catelyn initially treats **Joffrey and Tommen as Robert's sons** for purposes of lawful succession. Stannis asserts that **all three children are Jaime and Cersei's incest-born offspring**, and says he told **Jon Arryn** rather than Robert because his personal interest in succession would make Robert suspect a self-serving accusation. He says Arryn was acquiring proof and alleges **Cersei had Jon poisoned** to silence him. Catelyn connects this to **Lysa's conflicting allegations** in `AGOT`: a letter blaming Cersei, later a story blaming Tyrion. **These are character claims and Catelyn's recollection**, not a newly witnessed poisoning. `ACOK 25` reveals Pycelle separately admitted obstructing Maester Colemon but denied administering poison.
6. **VERIFIED:** Renly heard Stannis's raven-distributed allegation at **Horn Hill** through **Randyll Tarly** and doubts it can be proven; Stannis offers no documentary proof in this parley. Crucially Renly explicitly concedes that **if the allegation is true, Stannis has the better succession claim**, then says **his own larger army** makes the legal point irrelevant. This separates Renly's knowledge of the claim from belief in it, and legal right from military ambition.
7. **VERIFIED / THE PEACH:** Renly pulls a ripe **Highgarden peach** from his cloak, offers it to Stannis and comments on life's brevity and enjoying what one can while one can. Stannis, expecting a weapon, reaches for his sword and interprets the peach talk as threat. The scene signals different attitudes to mortality and pleasure. **No direct indication here that the peach is a coded assassination signal, poisoned object or literal prophecy**; these are theories requiring later evidence.
8. **VERIFIED:** Renly offers Stannis **Storm's End** as a brotherly gift; Stannis responds it is his by right and rejects the framing. They trade harsh personal mockery about **Margaery**, Robert's earlier intended arrangement, **Shireen** and the legitimacy of Renly's marriage. Catelyn regards their pride as politically disastrous, while Melisandre observes and warns Renly to consider his sins. Brienne interposes herself when Stannis draws steel.
9. **VERIFIED / LIGHTBRINGER SIGHTING:** Stannis draws a sword that **appears to glow in shifting red/yellow/white light**, the air seeming to shimmer. Catelyn's horse reacts. Stannis identifies it as **Lightbringer**, says he will not stain it with his brother's blood, and gives Renly **until dawn** to submit. In return he offers Storm's End, a seat on the council and recognition as **heir until Stannis fathers a son**. The gleam is a **firsthand sensory report**, not proof of the sword's metaphysical power. Stannis's 'mercy' is conditional on Renly's capitulation.
10. **VERIFIED / ARMY NUMBERS AS BOASTS:** Renly lists Reach and Stormlords banners (Tyrell, Rowan, Tarly, Caron, Tarth, Florent, etc.), claims his large infantry is still coming behind, **100,000** swords and spears, and disparages Stannis's siege force as at most **5,000** and **under 400 cavalry** on his scouts' report. Catelyn independently sees Renly's **large mounted host** and Stannis's **smaller camp**. These exact quantities and the likelihood of mass defections are **Renly's figures/forecasts**, not verified battlefield counts.
11. **VERIFIED / TACTICAL LIMITATION:** Renly left **Margaery, footmen, wagons, draft animals and siege engines** behind at Bitterbridge and rushed with horsemen toward Storm's End. Catelyn sees that he has **outstripped his food and fodder** and thus cannot keep his present force in place indefinitely. This supplies a real logistical explanation for pressing battle, distinct from Renly's public fear that delay makes him look timid.
12. **VERIFIED / LOST CHANCE:** **Mathis Rowan** counsels avoiding battle, letting Stannis vainly blockade well-provisioned impregnable Storm's End while Renly takes **King's Landing**. **Randyll Tarly** warns that Stannis would grow stronger if left unchecked. Renly chooses **immediate battle**, assigning **Rowan center, Bryce Caron left, himself right, Lord Estermont reserve and Ser Loras the van**. This demonstrates a live strategic alternative, not inevitability of civil war.
13. **VERIFIED / DIPLOMATIC COERCION:** Catelyn asks to return to Riverrun when the negotiation fails. Renly **refuses her departure**, requiring her to witness what happens to those who deny him so she can tell **Robb**. He claims she will be safe but makes her an instrument of intimidation, not a wholly free envoy. She is permitted to pray at a nearby sept with **Robar Royce** as escort on condition she return at dawn.
14. **VERIFIED:** **Robar Royce**, son of Bronze Yohn and a **second son**, explains he joined Renly seeking distinction despite family Arryn allegiance. Brienne reluctantly accepts an assignment to **bear Renly's banner into battle beside Loras** and requests to **arm Renly** first. Her desire to shield him and her evident affection produce personal risk; neither action yet establishes her location at the instant of the next crisis. The chapter ends with Catelyn's men asking whether to fight or flee and her reply that they must **pray**. **No battle or royal death occurs in this chapter.**

## Interpretation, theory challenge, next tests

- **Stannis** has the strongest legal argument only if the royal bastardy allegation is true, and he admits manipulating who heard it first. His use of religion and a luminous sword is a political and possibly magical statement; neither grants battlefield strength on its own.
- **Renly** believes charisma, military support and governance talent outweigh inheritance; Catelyn finds alternative strategy in Rowan's advice, but Renly prefers a quick decisive victory. He is not shown winning or dying yet.
- **Catelyn** arrives as Robb's envoy and makes a real attempt to unite brothers, but is coerced into remaining. Her priority is reunion with her daughters, not collecting heads.
- **Magic vs plot theory:** The observed sword-glow is real as perception. It does not prove true Lightbringer or a future supernatural attack; follow `ACOK 32 (Sansa)` and the next Catelyn appearances for outcomes. The peach's meaning must be evaluated against later character recollection before assigning it predictive power.
