## Chronological mission and decisive action

1. **THE SCOUTING PARTY COMES APART:** Jon and **Qhorin Halfhand** are now alone with **Ghost** in the Frostfangs. **Squire Dalbridge** remained to delay pursuers in `ACOK 53`; a distant pursuit horn suggested he was overcome, but **no direct scene or corpse proves his exact death** in Jon's experience. **Ebben** was later sent east with **Dalbridge's garron** to carry news to **Lord Mormont**; **Stonesnake** concealed traces and later lost his own mount when a starving **shadowcat** panicked it on a cliff. Qhorin ordered Stonesnake to travel **alone and afoot to the Fist** to report. **The subsequent actual outcome of Stonesnake's journey is unknown here**.
2. **QHORIN'S MILITARY INTELLIGENCE PRIORITY:** Before separating, Qhorin instructs Stonesnake to report that **Jon saw thousands of wildlings, giants and mammoths, and the evidence 'old powers are waking'**, with trees apparently seeing again. This is Qhorin's **interpretation of Jon's wolf dream plus existing intelligence**, not an observation of giants by every surviving ranger.
3. **AN EAGLE THAT ALWAYS FINDS THEM:** A distinctive **blue-gray eagle** repeatedly watches the fleeing rangers. Qhorin and Ebben suspect **a skinchanger**; `ACOK 53` supplied **an eagle attack on Ghost matching real neck wounds**, which makes ordinary bird surveillance less satisfactory as a sole explanation. **The identity of the controlling person is still an open inference until Ygritte's later testimony in this chapter.**
4. **EXTREME SURVIVAL CONDITIONS:** After the shadowcat startles Stonesnake's horse and breaks its leg, the rangers feed **horsemeat to Ghost**, collect raw strips for themselves, and mix **horse blood into oats** to endure cold and hunger. This gives hard logistical reasons for splitting the party and the dwindling options; Qhorin is not simply seeking dramatic martyrdom.
5. **THE LAST SHARED FIRE AND OATH:** Qhorin asks Jon to make a fire despite having enforced fireless stealth throughout the mission. Qhorin and Jon **recite the Night's Watch oath together**. The watch's line about guarding **'the realms of men'** takes on greater meaning after Jon has seen a huge migrating free-folk population and nonhuman threats. They recognize their own deaths may be near; the oath is **an explicitly revisited covenant**, not a newly sworn enlistment.
6. **THE FALLING WALL WARNING:** Qhorin cautions that if **the Wall falls**, fires all across the realm could be extinguished, shifting Jon's attention from fighting 'wildling raiders' to **the scale of the ancient defense**. It is Qhorin's strategic and possibly supernatural warning, not evidence the Wall has already fallen or a precise final prophecy.
7. **THE DEEP-COVER ORDER, NOT DESERTION:** Qhorin explicitly commands Jon that **if captured he must yield, go over to the wildlings, cut his cloak if required, denounce the Watch outwardly and cooperate**. He must remain a black brother **inwardly**, watch their movements and **return with intelligence**. Jon reluctantly accepts the dangerous mission and asks Qhorin to tell Mormont it was ordered. This is the decisive **on-page authorization for subsequent outward oathbreaking** and should be preserved in all future Jon loyalty analyses.
8. **WHAT MANCE IS DIGGING FOR:** Qhorin identifies the central unanswered target: Jon's wolf saw wildlings **digging in the Milkwater valley**. **What object or resource do they seek, have they found it, and why is it worth gathering so many people and armed watchers?** Neither commander knows. Treat as an explicit **priority unresolved mystery** rather than instantly assuming horn, dragonglass or another artifact.
9. **ATTEMPTED EVASION THROUGH A WATERFALL:** Qhorin builds a **deliberately bright decoy fire**, doubles back along their trail and leads Jon through a narrow passage **hidden behind a waterfall**, a route he remembers secondhand from an older ranger who followed a shadowcat. They travel beneath the mountains by **oil-soaked moss torches**. When they emerge, **the eagle is waiting**, rendering the secret passage ineffective against the apparently persistent surveillance. A physical route really exists; its secrecy alone did not hide them.
10. **ENEMY CONTACT:** **Fourteen wildlings and eight dogs** approach their defensible cave mouth, including **Rattleshirt (the Lord of Bones)**, an armored figure whose clothes and horse are covered with animal and human bones. He wears a **giant skull helm**. The free folk include **spearwives and archers**, not solely mailed male warriors; Qhorin's close-terrain position may negate numerical superiority briefly.
11. **EBBEN'S HEAD SHOWN:** A wildling spearwife lifts **Ebben's actual severed head** from a sack. Unlike the uncertain Dalbridge outcome, Ebben's death is now **directly verified by a recognizable body part**. His message to Mormont therefore cannot have been delivered by him after this interception. Stonesnake's potential independent report remains unresolved.
12. **YGRITTE STEPS FORWARD:** The archer who removes her hood is **Ygritte**, previously spared by Jon (`ACOK 51`). She recognizes him as **the Bastard of Winterfell** and argues for sparing him now. Her earlier mercy **materially changes his chance of entering the wildling camp**, although Rattleshirt is eager to kill him as a dangerous crow.
13. **THE EAGLE'S POSSIBLE HUMAN OWNER:** When the eagle screeches, Ygritte says **'he was a man, before you killed him'**, referring to a man Jon slew at the mountain lookout. This is **explicit testimony from someone in the free-folk party** that the bird's animating intelligence is connected with a **dead human skinchanger**. It is stronger than vague suspicion or coincidence, but the transfer mechanism, exact identity and whether a human mind permanently occupies the eagle are **not directly witnessed here**. Jon says he didn't know.
14. **INFILTRATION TEST:** Rattleshirt demands Jon **prove defection by killing Qhorin**. Jon offers to yield, recalling Qhorin's secret command, while Qhorin loudly calls him a **coward** and advances with real killing strokes. Their staged opposition could expose an infiltrator if either falters; **there is no whispered coordination or signal** specifying a planned fatal outcome, but Qhorin knew what might be demanded.
15. **DIRECT HALFHAND DEATH:** Qhorin's stronger swordsmanship initially overwhelms Jon. **Ghost bites Qhorin's calf**, opening his stance; Jon's **Longclaw** slashes the man's throat with **only the last portion of the blade**, and Qhorin falls dead after one last reference to the sword's sharpness. This is a **direct, intentional mortal sword blow delivered by Jon**, complicated by extreme coercion and Qhorin's established mission—not a fake battle or concealed survival.
16. **WILDLING DECISION:** Rattleshirt still demands Jon be killed, calling him a **warg** and a crow. **Ygritte, Ragwyle and others** contest that decision, pointing out that Jon surrendered and killed his brother, and **Rattleshirt reluctantly allows him to live**. The lack of an unchallengeable king at that moment offers evidence for the **free folk's contested and negotiated command structure**, not total egalitarianism.
17. **QHORIN'S FUNERAL AND TROPHIES:** Qhorin's body is **burned on a pyre**, limiting immediate wight risk; Rattleshirt claims charred bones and others cast dice for his possessions. **Ygritte wins his cloak**. Jon is disoriented and grieving yet still in position to **carry out Qhorin's espionage order**, with a real dead ranger as the cost of his acceptance.
18. **MANCE'S MOVEMENT CONFIRMED AS CLAIM:** Ygritte tells Jon **Mance Rayder has already marched well down the Milkwater toward the Wall**. This is informed testimony from a friendly wildling fighter, not a POV of the whole army reaching the Wall or a precise troop count. Jon's Watch oath remains intact **in his own stated intent and Qhorin's original directive**, even as his public role shifts.

## Competing theory and continuity questions

- **Diggings:** What in the Frostfangs could change the strategic options of the whole free-folk coalition? Track actual excavations, time spent at lake, carriers and unusual objects in following Jon/Mance chapters. **Do not promote any specific guessed artifact to verified here.**
- **Eagle and second life:** Ygritte's statement is unusually specific evidence for a skinchanger's continued consciousness in an animal after human death, but a firsthand POV of the transfer is absent. Compare `ADWD Varamyr Prologue` (already selectively noted) during its full-book review rather than assuming perfect equivalence.
- **Qhorin's intent:** It is certain he **ordered infiltration** and fought Jon. It is a reasonable inference he accepted sacrificing himself to make Jon credible, but the fatal outcome was not separately documented in advance by a direct order to kill Qhorin.
- **Jon's oath:** Actions performed under this explicit command should not be scored as straightforward opportunistic betrayal; later decisions still require individual analysis.
