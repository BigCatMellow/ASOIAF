## Full narrative: positions, actions, decisions and consequences

1. **VERIFIED:** Returning after roughly **ten years** as a Stark hostage, Theon requests that the Oldtown merchantman **Myraham** sail past Pyke so he can experience the castle appearing from the sea. It has **no safe anchorage**: a broken rocky headland, separate keeps on small islands and sea stacks, narrow bridges and dangerous swaying walkways. He remembers being taken away by Robert's war galley after Balon's rebellion. The Greyjoy **golden kraken** flies without a Stark direwolf, intensifying his expectation of belonging and future power.
2. **VERIFIED / THEON'S PRIVATE INTERPRETATION:** Theon holds **Robb's sealed letter** in oilskin and imagines it effectively grants him a crown. The red comet is read as personally auspicious. His account of island poverty and the old reaving culture frames raiding as preferable to mines, fisheries and thin agricultural soil. His readings of ironborn history and entitlement are **his motivated views**, not a neutral economic or ethical history.
3. **VERIFIED:** Theon has a sexual relationship with the merchant captain's daughter, uses her and then abandons her despite her warning that her father will punish her. He speculates she **may become pregnant** and treats a prospective child as a boast. Neither pregnancy nor the girl's subsequent fate is verified. His low opinion of her intelligence and exaltation of historical **salt wives** expose a coercive version of the Old Way even while he claims it has ended.
4. **VERIFIED:** At **Lordsport**, rebuilt after Robert's attack, Theon sees about **fifty or sixty longships** and ships marked with **Wynch's blood moon, Goodbrother's warhorn and Harlaw's scythe**. Balon's **Great Kraken** is present, but Euron's **Silence** is not seen. The **Botley** keep has been rebuilt in stone while the sept remains a seven-sided foundation. Theon expects a welcoming honor guard and assumes his father may have anticipated the letter; instead, merchants **do not recognize him**. His pride obscures how thoroughly local loyalties have continued without him.
5. **VERIFIED:** **Aeron Greyjoy (Damphair)**, his formerly convivial uncle, arrives under Balon's instructions. He has become an austere Drowned God priest after surviving a storm and near drowning. He publicly pours seawater over Theon and requires a ritual profession. The physical rite occurs; any divine transformation is Aeron's religious interpretation. Aeron says **Dagmer Cleftjaw** is recruiting **Stonehouses and Drumms** on Old Wyk, Balon's daughter **Asha** is carrying messages in her own longship **Black Wind** to Great Wyk, and Theon's ailing mother resides on **Harlaw** with her sister.
6. **AERON'S CLAIM / POLITICAL CONFLICT:** Theon says he is Balon's only surviving son and the heir; Aeron pointedly notes **Asha survives** and questions whether a Stark-raised man can rule the holy islands. Theon cites customary preference for a male heir but **his succession is not conceded**. Aeron says Balon has forbidden disclosure of mustering plans, including to Theon. His expectation that kin and rank automatically grant access fails.
7. **VERIFIED / THEON'S SUBJECTIVE RECOLLECTION:** Theon recalls being a hostage and fearing Ned's authority, feeling less included than Jon and Robb; nevertheless he privately acknowledges authentic affection for Robb. His resentment toward Ned coexists with the factual correction that **Ned personally slew neither of his brothers**: **Rodrik** fell fighting **Jason Mallister** at Seagard and **Maron** died beneath Pyke's collapsing tower. Theon selectively attributes both deaths to the war led by Robert and Ned. Mallister's ancient alarm bell and recent meeting with **Patrek Mallister** further show how feud memory survives despite personal ease between younger heirs.
8. **VERIFIED:** Re-entering Pyke, Theon discovers old servants **Sylas Sourmouth** and Maester **Qalen** are dead, replaced by **Helya** and **Wendamyr**. Instead of his old Sea Tower room, he is given a damp, little-used suite in the **Bloody Keep**, named for an old massacre of the River King's sons. **Thralls** attend him. Theon selects ornate garments and a **purchased gold chain** to impress his father; even the bridges he once crossed effortlessly now frighten him. His imagined familiar homecoming collapses into signs of estrangement.
9. **VERIFIED:** **Balon**, aged and physically diminished but forceful, receives Theon coldly and ridicules his velvet clothing and gold chain. Under the Old Way, a male warrior's ornaments are supposed to be won through violence—the **iron price**—not bought. Balon **snaps the chain and throws it into the brazier**. Theon insists Ned was gaoler rather than father and tries to convey that Robb regards him as a brother. Balon forbids calling the son of his old enemy brother, even though Theon knows his lost brothers' own treatment of him was unkind.
10. **VERIFIED / THEON'S OFFER:** Balon reads Robb's letter proposing that Greyjoy ships attack Lannister enemies in exchange for a crown. Theon says Robb is advancing through the **Golden Tooth**, with Tywin at Harrenhal, Jaime hostage and Stafford's new levies vulnerable. He proposes to seize **Lannisport**, perhaps even **Casterly Rock**, and asks for Casterly Rock as his reward. This is a **plan and optimistic assessment**, not a successful assault or verified new location for every army.
11. **VERIFIED:** Balon rejects the premise that Robb could **give** him a crown, which might later be taken away. He **burns Robb's letter** beside Theon's gold chain, strikes Theon, declares he will pay the iron price for kingship and refuses a western campaign: Casterly Rock is too strong and Lannisport cannot be held while Tywin remains dangerous. His ships are **already mustered independently of Theon's proposal**.
12. **VERIFIED / BALON'S DECLARED INTENT:** Balon says he will carve out a kingdom but **not in the west and not under Robb's command**. He refers to a less wealthy, more vulnerable target; Theon understands what is implied. The text clearly invites an attack on the **North**, but **no such attack has yet occurred on-page in this chapter**. The ironborn plan's precise targets, orders, dates and field results require later verification.

## Character agency, alternative hypotheses and implications

**Robb and Catelyn (`ACOK 7`):** Catelyn argued Theon was leverage and Balon a former king who might revive his own ambitions. This chapter directly confirms Balon will **not accept Robb's proposed subordination**, and that armed preparations preceded the envoy. It does *not* prove that retaining Theon would have stopped Balon's plans already underway; that causal counterfactual remains uncertain.

**Theon:** At sea he imagines self-authored conquest; at home he confronts competing citizenship, personal history and an insecure inheritance. He can still choose how to respond to Balon's alternative plan, but he has already surrendered his immediate freedom as an independent hostage-turned-envoy. His reported memories about Stark household discrimination are **subjective**, while his genuine affection for Robb is disclosed privately.

**Balon:** Plans independent expansion and chooses a weak opponent over a rich well-fortified target. His account of buying a crown through conquest is ideological, yet refusing a strike on Casterly Rock has a strong conventional strategic justification. Do not reduce the choice to simple irrational hatred when a target's defensibility differs.

**Asha/Aeron:** Aeron denies that male primogeniture settles political legitimacy here. Asha's independent ship and diplomatic activity are documented, but she is not seen in this chapter; her precise plan and claimed inheritance require her own scenes.
