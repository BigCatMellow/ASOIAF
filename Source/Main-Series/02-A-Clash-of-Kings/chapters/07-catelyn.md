## Main cause and effect

1. **VERIFIED:** At Riverrun, fifteen-year-old **Robb** is burdened by his newly forged crown. Its bronze circlet, First Men runes and **nine sword-shaped iron spikes** evoke the lost Kings of Winter crown, supposedly surrendered when Torrhen knelt to Aegon. The fate of the actual original is **UNKNOWN**. Robb emphasizes kingship through his bare sword, **Grey Wind**, and the lords' deference; his Frey squire **Olyvar** handles the regalia.
2. **VERIFIED:** Robb summons the captured **Cleos Frey**, son of **Genna Lannister** and **Emmon Frey**. Unlike other Freys pledged to Robb, Cleos fought beneath the Lannister banner. Robb sends him to Cersei under a peace banner with **thirty escorts**, requiring a witnessed knightly oath to return to captivity with her answer. Dispatch, not delivery or oath performance, is shown.
3. **VERIFIED:** Robb's peace conditions include: his sisters brought by sea through **White Harbor** safely to Winterfell and Sansa released from betrothal (followed by exchange of **Willem Lannister and Tion Frey**); Ned's bones and his household guards' remains returned; **Ice** handed over; prisoners exchanged except **Jaime**, held against Tywin's misconduct; and recognition of an independent North **and riverlands**, withdrawal of Lannister forces, cancellation of fealty/revenue demands, and **ten noble hostages**, two to be released annually while peace endures. **Vyman's map** defines lands above the Neck plus the Trident watershed between the **Golden Tooth** and **Mountains of the Moon**.
4. **VERIFIED / KNOWLEDGE GAP:** Robb and Catelyn assume **Arya** is in Cersei's power; in `ACOK 3 (Tyrion)`, Cersei says Arya escaped. The proposed initial hostage exchange therefore requires a prisoner Cersei does not possess. The terms are also politically hard to accept while Jaime is withheld, which Catelyn tells Robb plainly.
5. **VERIFIED:** **Rickard Karstark** leaves during the speech. He lost two sons to the Whispering Wood and opposes peace. **Edmure** presses for attacking Harrenhal, but Robb lacks forces; the riverlords have dispersed to defend their own lands with Edmure's permission. Catelyn accuses her brother of weakening the host; **Marq Piper, Karyl Vance, Jonos Bracken and Jason Mallister** figure in the dispersal. The Greatjon's cries of support contrast with Karstark's exit.
6. **VERIFIED:** Catelyn would exchange Jaime to protect her daughters. Robb refuses: powerful lords could *unmake* the king they chose, and he fears Jaime returning to war. Catelyn realizes that her accusation about daughters being less valued than their father has wounded her grieving fifteen-year-old son. Their clash is both strategic and personal.
7. **VERIFIED:** Robb suggests Catelyn retire to the Twins or Winterfell; she stays with dying **Hoster Tully**. He plans to send **Theon** with captives to Seagard and then by sea to **Balon Greyjoy** for ships. Catelyn warns that Balon has already crowned himself once and that Theon is useful **as a hostage**; she proposes other envoys. Robb trusts his friend and would recognize Balon as King of the Iron Islands to fight the Lannisters. That bargain is *not* accepted on-page.
8. **VERIFIED:** **Brynden Blackfish**, recently returned from scouting, visits Hoster, who sleeps under dreamwine and milk of the poppy, remembers regrets and sometimes mistakes people. Brynden and Hoster had quarreled but reconciled. A **white raven** formally announced autumn about a fortnight previously, although the woods remain green.
9. **BRYNDEN'S REPORT:** Beric Dondarrion and a **red priest** are reportedly attacking Tywin's foragers; reports about **Burton Crakehall** falsely claiming Beric's death underscore rumor unreliability. Bracken and Blackwood lands are devastated. Gregor Clegane reportedly recaptured Darry and killed its child lord; Amory Lorch and a Qohorik mercenary are also named in atrocities. Brynden has directly seen destructive aftermath, but the named killings are not all eyewitnessed here.
10. **BRYNDEN'S ASSESSMENT:** Tywin is feeding his army at **Harrenhal** from seized harvest, terrorizing civilians to draw Robb into a battle on advantageous terrain. A further western host is **reportedly mustering** under **Stafford Lannister**, whose son Daven is judged more formidable; it has not yet arrived. Catelyn suggests **Renly** might induce Tywin to move from Harrenhal. Brynden notes that another king will seek **homage**, creating a problem for northern and riverlands independence. No Renly agreement occurs here.

## Agency / alternative hypotheses / tests

**Robb:** Peace needs credible leverage and lords' acceptance. He chooses to retain Jaime and release Theon, each against Catelyn's advice. **Countercase to “mere stupidity”:** hostile Lannister armies and unstable bannerman loyalties are real constraints.

**Catelyn:** Her priority is recovering children and keeping her son alive, but her valid points carry an emotional cost and she cannot command her king. Her faith that returning Jaime saves the sisters is not guaranteed.

**Brynden and Tywin:** Brynden's theory of Tywin's trap has tactical logic, but Tywin's private intention is not directly established in this POV. Review Lannister actions before promotion from assessment to verified motive.

**Renly:** A southern offensive might divert Tywin, but homage demanded by Renly could undo the very sovereignty Robb demands. Cross-check `ACOK 22 (Catelyn)`.
