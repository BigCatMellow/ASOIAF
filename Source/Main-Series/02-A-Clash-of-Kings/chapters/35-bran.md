## Narrative, causes and confirmed events

1. **VERIFIED / NEWS:** Luwin summons **Bran, Rickon and both Walders** after a raven message from **Robb**, who reports winning **Oxcross** against **Stafford Lannister** and taking western castles. Robb writes from **Ashemark**, the former Marbrand seat. This **corroborates the western victory** described by Tyrion in `ACOK 32`. Bran and Rickon immediately ask whether Robb will come home, but Luwin says the campaign continues. Rickon sometimes speaks as though dead **Ned** could return; Bran understands this as grief and willful refusal of loss, not proof Rickon does not know.
2. **VERIFIED / CRUCIAL GREEN-DREAM TEST:** Luwin also reports **Stevron Frey** died **three days after a not initially serious battle wound** at Oxcross. The young Walders respond less with grief than enthusiastic calculations of **Lord Walder Frey's succession**, citing **Ryman, Edwyn, Black Walder, Petyr Pimple, Aegon and others**. Bran feels sick and recognizes in their reaction **Jojen's dream of Bran being served rich red meat while the Walders liked grey dead meat** (`ACOK 28`). This is a **specific narrated interpretation/match** in the chapter—succession benefit from an older relative's death—rather than an observed literal dinner. The dream's scope beyond this event remains unproved.
3. **VERIFIED:** **Osha** carries Bran from Luwin's turret, gives navigational advice for the far North via **the Ice Dragon constellation and blue star in its rider's eye**, and says she has **seen giants** but knows of children of the forest chiefly from stories. She does **not** claim to have encountered a three-eyed crow. Bran remembers Osha earlier said **Robb marched the wrong way**, which may reflect her supernatural-threat priorities rather than precise knowledge of western campaigns.
4. **JOJEN'S REPORTED DREAM / URGENT WARNING:** **Meera and Jojen** arrive and Jojen says a dream showed **black sea waves filling Winterfell**, with **Alebelly, Septon Chayle and Mikken** among the dead. Bran protests the sea is geographically remote, but Jojen insists the dream is true. With the immediate military background of **Balon's northern coast operation** (`ACOK 24`), a **symbolic Ironborn invasion** is a strong interpretation, **not a directly witnessed flood**.
5. **VERIFIED / WARG INTERPRETATION:** Bran finally describes **wolf dreams** of hunting and killing in Summer, dreams of the **three-eyed crow**, a calling **tree**, and the worst **recurring falling dream** tied to his accident. Jojen names him a **warg/shapechanger**, says folk may **fear or kill** such people, and distinguishes Bran's animal perception from ordinary dreams. He claims a **third eye** can enable abilities and urges him to open it. Bran is terrified and says he wants to be a **knight**, not a warg. The prior `ACOK 21/28` correspondence strengthens the explanation but does not prove every claim of future power or precise soul mechanics.
6. **VERIFIED / WARNINGS PROVE DIFFICULT:** Bran attempts to warn **Mikken, Chayle and Alebelly**. Mikken laughs, Chayle says he is a strong swimmer, but Alebelly grows terrified and refuses to wash or approach water. Other guards eventually force him into a hot bath as he screams that the dream will come true. Jojen's intelligence may have value, yet **its immediate effect is to endanger a man's well-being through panic**, even though no death has yet happened.
7. **VERIFIED / HORNWOOD FOLLOW-THROUGH AS REPORTED BY RODRIK:** **Ser Rodrik Cassel** returns from the east with a foul-smelling captive called **Reek**, reported servant of **Roose Bolton's bastard**. Bran and Rodrik have been told the bastard was **killed by Rodrik's men with arrows** when trying to flee during an encounter on Hornwood land. **Lady Donella Hornwood** has died after being locked in a tower without food; horrifying reports include **chewed fingers and bloody mouth**. These are reports relayed to Bran, not murders he personally witnessed. **Reek's identity and the truth of the reported bastard's death remain subject to later verification**, particularly given the importance of captive testimony.
8. **LEGAL/POLITICAL DISPUTE:** Rodrik says Hornwood's coerced husband had her recite vows **before a septon and a heart tree**, consummated the marriage with witnesses and obtained a **signed and sealed will naming him heir**. **Luwin rejects the validity of vows at swordpoint**, while Rodrik fears **Roose Bolton may uphold the marriage for land advantage**. Manderly and Dreadfort men are already fighting in Hornwood woods. This illustrates how competing legal forms and military facts—not simply blood relationship—govern contested northern lordships.
9. **VERIFIED / COASTAL THREAT NOW REAL:** Luwin tells Rodrik of **longship raiders attacking the Stony Shore**, plundering fishing villages, which fits **Balon's assignment to Theon in `ACOK 24`**. **Leobald Tallhart** has sent his nephew **Benfred** to fight them. Rodrik complains the raids exploit the absence of Robb's northern army and says the attackers may merely retreat offshore and hit elsewhere. The exact attackers' individual identities and success of Benfred's expedition are **not seen by Bran**.
10. **SECOND GREEN DREAM / NOT YET FULFILLED:** Jojen tells Bran that **Reek** appeared in another green dream holding a **long red blade**, with **Bran and Rickon dead at his feet while their faces are removed**, and later seen **in the crypts** with the Stark dead. Bran considers escaping to **Greywater** or seeking the **crow**, while Meera argues he could preemptively kill Reek to avert the forecast. Jojen claims green dreams **cannot be changed**, although he admits he does not understand why warnings arrive if they cannot be averted. **This chapter does not depict Bran or Rickon killed, Reek attacking them, or a literal drowning of Winterfell**.
11. **VERIFIED / FAMILY TENSION:** Bran and Meera argue with Jojen over whether foreknowledge implies fate. Meera sees an ethical reason for warnings—prevent harm—where Jojen believes fixed outcomes. Bran worries specifically about the named victims rather than treating prophecy as entertainment. The exchange establishes **a sharp internal disagreement among the Reeds**, not a settled metaphysical answer.

## Theory challenges, unresolved tasks and cross-book relevance

- **The first dream's success:** The Frey succession celebration following Stevron's death is a compelling **narrative decoding** of the strange supper. Nevertheless the earlier dream did not accurately describe an actual supper; it demonstrates **symbolic foreknowledge** if green dreams are genuine. Do not interpret every image with equal confidence.
- **The sea dream:** Northern **Ironborn longships** are now reported along the Stony Shore, and Balon intends wider penetration. Black salt waves therefore plausibly represent people rather than seawater, while the prophecy's **named fatal casualties remain outstanding**.
- **Reek identity:** Rodrik thinks he killed the bastard and brought a servant. The validity of those identifications, legal consequences and Jojen's disturbing Reek prophecy must await later on-page evidence. Do not retroactively tell Bran a twist he has not learned.
- **Hornwood succession:** The blood-claim/legal-title dilemma intersects Bolton violence and Manderly self-help, potentially foreshadowing legitimacy conflicts with Starks and wildling marriages. Track separate **coercion, witness, legal doctrine, and de facto control**.
- **The fixed-future claim:** Jojen believes the dreams cannot be altered; Meera opposes this conclusion, and the chapter supplies no experimental proof that every intervention is futile.
- **Next:** immediately ensuing Theon/Bran chapters for raids and Reek, later Winterfell episodes for exact casualties, survivor fates and crypts; later evidence on nature of greenseers and dream imagery.
