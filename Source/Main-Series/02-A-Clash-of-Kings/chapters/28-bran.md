## Causal narrative and major developments

1. **VERIFIED:** The Reed siblings remain at Winterfell after the feast. **Meera**, now nearly sixteen, demonstrates skilled **net and frog-spear** fighting by ensnaring **Summer** after he appears to knock her down. Jojen notes the technically decisive restraint while Bran initially thinks Summer won. The moment establishes Meera's non-knightly martial competence and Bran's tendency to misread what success requires. Bran's **ninth nameday** has passed; he resists Meera's offer to lift him and moves himself to a tree using his arms, reaffirming a desire for control and dignity.
2. **MEERA'S FAMILY INFORMATION:** Meera says **Howland Reed taught her to fight**, and Greywater Watch has **no knights, master-at-arms or maester**. She says ravens and enemies cannot find **Greywater Watch because it moves**. This is family testimony of an unusual moving stronghold, not a detailed verified mechanism. The Reeds invite Bran to visit, even immediately.
3. **VERIFIED / CRITICAL NORTHERN UPDATE:** Ser **Rodrik Cassel** is away attending trouble in the east. **Roose Bolton's bastard** seized **Donella Hornwood** on her return from Winterfell's harvest feast, **married her that night against the political expectations of other claimants**, and **Wyman Manderly occupied Hornwood Castle**, explaining he meant to shield the lands from Bolton. Rodrik is angry with both. The dramatic escalation from `ACOK 16` is **now narrated as Bran's knowledge**, though the abduction and marriage are reported retrospectively rather than witnessed directly. Avoid assigning the bastard's legal naming, bride's consent, completed succession or ultimate fate without later evidence.
4. **JOJEN'S CLAIM:** Jojen urges Bran to **leave Winterfell soon**. Meera says his **green dreams** reveal future events; Jojen rejects her qualification that they work only sometimes. Jojen describes a **winged wolf bound by grey stone chains**, with a **three-eyed crow** attempting to chip the chains. He identifies Bran as the chained winged wolf and claims he and Meera were sent by **Howland Reed** after Jojen described the dream. This independently ties the Reed mission to a prophetic *claim* rather than only the formal fealty visit in `ACOK 21`.
5. **JOJEN'S PERSONAL CLAIM / CROSS-POV MATCH:** Jojen says he nearly died of **greywater fever** as a child when the crow visited him, and that the crow resides **beyond the Wall**, not at Greywater Watch. He tells Bran to **open a third eye** that supposedly can see history and distant places and the future of an oak from its acorn. His depiction is a character's explanation of supernatural power; it is not yet an objectively mapped location for the crow or full definition of the ability.
6. **STRONGER CORROBORATION:** Jojen specifically tells Bran that at the harvest feast he dreamed **he was Summer** in the godswood, and Jojen felt **Bran inside Summer** when he physically touched the direwolf. `ACOK 21` had directly narrated this **wolf-perspective experience**, the Reeds' approach and the sudden feeling of falling on contact. The match is strong independent evidence within the fiction for a **wolf-dream / mind-sharing phenomenon**, not just a generic vague prediction. Yet the precise causal agent of the falling remains undetermined.
7. **VERIFIED / THREAT:** Bran refuses to discuss the **golden man** and terrifying fall that echo Jaime's shove (cross-reference `AGOT 8 Bran`, `ACOK 16 Bran`). When Jojen insists Bran's wolf fear and anger are linked, **Summer lunges toward Jojen**, then **Shaggydog appears**, placing Meera and Jojen between both wolves. Meera defends with spear and net and calls for the wolves to be recalled. Bran says he **cannot control them**; Jojen repeats that this is not the day he will die, and Meera makes them climb the **weirwood**. **Hodor** rushes from the hot pools at Bran's summons and drives the wolves away. The incident is a real safety failure, even if the source of the wolves' rage is disputed.
8. **LUWIN'S EXPLANATION:** Bran seeks Maester **Luwin**, who identifies ancient **greenseers**, supposed powers of perceiving through weirwoods and influencing woodland beasts, and the reason First Men felled sacred trees in war. Luwin shows his **Valyrian-steel maester-chain link**, symbolizing formal study of the **higher mysteries**—uncommon, roughly one maester in a hundred. He says he tried spells without success and argues magic has dwindled/vanished, that dragons and giants are gone and no living man has true greensight. **The reader already knows living dragons exist** (ACOK 12/27) and has Bran's narrated wolf perception, so Luwin's universal negative is **demonstrably incomplete**, though his methodological caution about cherry-picked dreams is valuable.
9. **THE NEW GREEN DREAM TEST:** Meera relays another green dream: at a supper **Luwin serves Bran juicy rare meat** but serves the **Walder Frey wards rotten grey flesh**, which they strangely enjoy more than Bran likes his own. At the next real supper **all receive ordinary pigeon pie**, prompting Bran to dismiss the warning. Jojen, via Meera, says Bran will understand **later**; the dream is **symbolic, not an asserted literal supper that failed that night**. Do not count this as disproven solely because the first meal differs.
10. **BRAN'S PERSONAL CONSEQUENCE:** Bran's hope that magic might permit a different life is crushed by Luwin's certainty. His denial that he is a wolf and his thought that he will never walk or fly record his **fear and disability grief**, not a settled limit on abilities or future. The chapter ends without explaining the Frey image or moving Bran from Winterfell.

## Theory tests / next references

- **Bran as warg:** More evidence than mere metaphor: the Reed witnesses correspond to what Bran experienced during `ACOK 21`. Mechanism and control remain incomplete; a dangerous moment shows proximity is not full mastery.
- **Jojen prophecy:** The winged-wolf dream has a meaningful match, but could represent accurate intuition about Bran rather than proof all future images are literally true. The **Walders' supper** should be tracked as a distinct pending prediction and challenged by later events.
- **Hornwood conflict:** Bolton and Manderly each move unilaterally while Rodrik is away. A marriage obtained during seizure cannot be simply counted as legitimate free assent; later testimony on force, title and Lady Hornwood's condition matters.
- **Luwin:** His certainty is not reliable given dragons, but his statistical challenge to matching dreams remains valuable.
