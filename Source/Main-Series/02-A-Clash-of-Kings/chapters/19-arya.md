## Main narrative, choices and outcomes

1. **VERIFIED — PRIOR UNCERTAINTY RESOLVED:** Arya, Gendry and others revisit the burned holdfast after Lorch's departure. They find **Yoren dead**, recognizable by his beard and garments, with his skull split by an **axe**. Four Lannister corpses lie near his body. Arya insists on burying Yoren. **ACOK 14's unknown fate is now confirmed**, but his personal killer is not identified.
2. **VERIFIED:** Yoren's three towerhouse lookouts initially survived the Lannister assault by pulling up the ladder; **Cutjack** admits Gendry after the enemy goes. **Kurz** later dies of an arrow wound that festers despite treatment with mud and moss, and **Tarber and Cutjack** vanish taking his weapons and supplies. Likely desertion is **inferred**, not directly observed, and their later location is unknown.
3. **VERIFIED:** Five children—**Arya, Gendry, Hot Pie, the wounded Lommy and the traumatized toddler renamed Weasel**—survive together along the Gods Eye shore. Most buildings are abandoned or burned. They avoid roads and flames, eat **insects, worms, acorn paste, berries** and whatever little game they can obtain. Arya's feet toughen after her shoes rot away. Weasel has stopped constant sobbing but sometimes eats mud; she clings to Arya.
4. **VERIFIED:** Arya scouts a small lakeside settlement where smoke and horses imply occupants; she counts **26 thatched roofs and one slate roof**, sees swans and a wooded island and detects crows. **Gendry** refuses a fire to avoid attention. Lommy, unable to walk after the calf wound, repeatedly advocates surrender. Gendry argues nobility may be ransomed whereas common boys may simply be killed and privately suggests Lommy's death or abandonment would relieve a cruel burden. Arya rejects writing Weasel off as useless.
5. **VERIFIED:** While walking toward the settlement, Gendry reveals he inferred Arya is a girl by her behavior; she admits her actual name and descent: **Arya Stark, daughter of Ned**. He promises confidentiality but suddenly adopts the awkward formal courtesies he learned for lordly daughters at his forge apprenticeship. **His knowledge changes; neither Lommy nor Hot Pie receives this disclosure**, and Gendry still does not know his own royal descent.
6. **VERIFIED:** Arya reconnoiters a settlement occupied by armed men: **six bodies** hang from a lakeside gibbet, extensively consumed by crows and other scavengers, and screams emanate from a guarded **stone storehouse**. Gendry is captured while scouting separately, struck, stripped of his recognizable **bull helmet**, and locked inside. A small boy attempts to run out and is thrown back; civilian prisoners have not been identified.
7. **VERIFIED:** The banners are a **gold lion on red** and **three black dogs on yellow**. Arya eventually recognizes the second as the arms of **Gregor Clegane**, not Sandor personally (cf. Sansa 18). She watches guards but is too far away to free Gendry alone. She returns to persuade Hot Pie to help, while Lommy guards Weasel.
8. **VERIFIED:** As Arya and Hot Pie crawl beneath the gibbet after dark, a **crow lands on Hot Pie's back** and he panics, shouting surrender. The guards capture both children. Arya attempts to fight with **Needle**, is hit and loses her sword. Gregor, present as an enormous commanding knight, orders them led to the other fugitives, but it is **not stated he personally kills every prisoner**.
9. **VERIFIED:** **Weasel flees**, according to Lommy, before armed men arrive. Arya hopes she escapes; **no safe destination or further survival is shown**. Lommy expressly **yields**, states he cannot walk and asks to be carried. An **unnamed spearman** stabs him through the throat while making a cruel joke. His death concretely refutes Hot Pie and Lommy's hope of safety through surrender. The chapter ends with Arya/Hot Pie in the captors' hands and Gendry already confined.

## Agency, competing readings and next tests

Arya values mutual aid and a riskier rescue of Gendry; Gendry sees brutal constraints and no realistic method to carry Lommy indefinitely. Both perspectives have evidentiary force without making abandonment a moral necessity. In a world of ransom privileges for nobles, the death of a yielding lowborn boy confirms why safety assurances are unreliable. Gregor's men are observed committing these specific crimes; do **not** retroactively assign them to the anonymous perpetrators of the earlier destroyed village (`ACOK 9`).
