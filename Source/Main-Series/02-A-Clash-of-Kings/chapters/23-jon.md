## Whole-chapter narrative and implications

1. **VERIFIED:** The Great Ranging's **200 men** have ridden through **six days of driving rain**, swollen streams and mud, and have now found **seven abandoned villages**—expanding the four seen by `ACOK 13`. **Jarman Buckwell** reports that **Craster's Keep is still occupied**, a relief for the party. Mormont orders men not to touch or trouble Craster's wives. Smallwood considers Craster a valuable if troublesome friend; **Dywen** associates him with sexual crimes, slavery and demons, which at this point are **ranger accusations**, not direct verification of every allegation.
2. **VERIFIED:** Jon carries Mormont's warning down the extended column, passing Sam and **Ottyn Wythers**; **Dywen and Grenn** patrol together as flank outriders. Ghost hunts apart from the column, and other horses remain disturbed by the direwolf. The keep itself is **poorly fortified**, with a muddy dike, earthen slope, skulls of **bear and ram** at the gate, sod-and-wattle hall, leaking roof, few animals, pigs and a small garden. At most a few dozen can sleep under cover; the rest camp in wet mud.
3. **VERIFIED:** Craster is older, strong and missing an ear; only he enjoys a chair. He has numerous **wives/daughters and girls**, and no other adult men or male children are visible. Craster says **Benjen Stark has not passed for three years** and remembers the prior expedition of **Waymar Royce, Will and Gared**, speaking disparagingly of Waymar. This establishes that he claims to have encountered the vanished Waymar patrol but does not know Benjen's more recent movements. His history is **interested testimony**, not omniscient proof of Benjen's route.
4. **VERIFIED / CHARACTER CLAIMS:** Mormont tells Craster directly about two dead rangers who reanimated at Castle Black, including the deaths of **Jaremy Rykker** and the attempted attack on Mormont. Craster professes no such trouble at his homestead and claims 'gods' keep him safe. He admits **Mance Rayder ordered him to leave to join the gathering**, says he **cut out and nailed the messenger's tongue** to the wall, and boasts that empty villages are **Mance's work**. The claimed mutilation has a visible claimed trophy, but the scene does not independently confirm the victim's precise history.
5. **VERIFIED:** Craster trades information and one night's hospitality for **wine and an axe**, and threatens to take the hand of any Watchman touching his wives. Mormont agrees and later adds a **crossbow** according to Gilly's account. Jon sends **Sam Tarly** to draw a map from Craster's intelligence and **Dolorous Edd** for the ceremonial axe with gold inlay. Such gifts show a concrete transactional relationship and why the Watch tolerates the man's rule.
6. **VERIFIED:** **Ghost breaks into a rabbit hutch**, kills one rabbit for food and takes another to Jon, alarming a **pregnant girl** of around fifteen or sixteen. She says she is both **Craster's daughter and wife** and intended to breed rabbits because sheep are gone. Jon promises recompense, though he has no personal coin, and protects her from cruel ridicule by **Chett and Lark the Sisterman**, who mock Jon's bastardy and frighten her.
7. **VERIFIED:** Jon shares Ghost's rabbit with Sam in a sheltered woodland fire and describes the lack of sheep and men at Craster's. Sam spends the night at the hall to draw the map. The weather changes sharply from rain to hard **overnight frost**, encasing leaves, tents and cloaks in ice. Jon briefly sees natural ice as magic; nothing in the chapter **proves a supernatural cold front or attack** caused the freezing.
8. **VERIFIED / GILLY'S DIRECT APPEAL:** At morning, the pregnant girl returns wearing **Sam's lent black cloak**, which he deliberately gave her to permit a safer approach. She names herself **Gilly**, says Craster has **nineteen wives in total**, apparently counting her and asks Jon as a king's half brother to take her with the Watch. A female child will become another of Craster's wives, she says, but **male infants are given away**. This is **Gilly's firsthand household testimony**, later substantially supported by Mormont's recognition, but the text does not depict an infant's actual handover this night.
9. **GILLY'S REPORTED SUPERNATURAL EXPERIENCE:** She says Craster sacrifices the boys to the **cold gods**, who come with the white cold, have **blue eyes**, and are the 'white shadows.' As those visits grew more frequent he reportedly offered sheep too, explaining the depleted sheepfold. Jon recalls the **blue-eyed wight** he fought; he concludes she has seen Others and Craster lied about never knowing such beings. This inference is grounded in her specific description but **does not directly show the beings at Craster's Keep, their precise nature, or whether Gilly distinguishes wights from Others**.
10. **VERIFIED / UNFULFILLED RESCUE:** Jon initially says he cannot take Gilly, since the party is going **north after Mance** and has been ordered to respect Craster's wives; guest-right and the Watch's vows constrain him. Gilly counters that **Jon himself never ate beneath Craster's roof**, so the host never extended individual guest-right to him. Jon does not accept that as authority to take her. Sam had promised to try finding a way on a **return trip** without permission and hoped Jon could help; neither actual rescue nor a definite return route is secured. Jon feels ashamed, despite insisting the request is impossible.
11. **VERIFIED / COMMANDER'S ACKNOWLEDGMENT:** When Jon quietly reports that Craster has neither sheep nor sons and gives his boys to the woods, **Mormont says he already knew**: **Smallwood and the rangers have long known**, and the Watch tolerates Craster because his keep has repeatedly saved rangers' lives. Mormont argues they cannot make the whole world just and must reserve force for larger threats. He speculates that wives could kill Craster with the gift axe, but does not organize a rescue; Jon recalls Ned's principle that unjust retainers dishonor their liege. Craster is **not legally Mormont's bannerman**, yet this does not erase the moral cost of sustaining the bargain.
12. **MORMONT'S INTELLIGENCE / FINAL DECISION:** Craster locates **Mance Rayder's gathered wildlings in the Frostfangs**, consistent with a separate captive's report to **Denys Mallister** from the Gorge. This largely explains the empty settlements. **Mormont** interprets the concentration as an impending invasion of the weakened Seven Kingdoms, given the mountains' low food capacity, the Watch's decline and Robb's army gone south. Alternative explanations include **migration/flight from supernatural dangers**; the chapter has not established Mance's own stated motive. Mormont's operational decision is to **find, fight and stop Mance**.
13. **VERIFIED:** The column departs Craster's Keep north and west after one night. Jon flexes his burned sword-hand and privately compares the small ranging force to the possible mass beyond the mountains. **No clash with Mance** takes place in this chapter.

## Contradictions and next tests

**Mormont's duty dilemma:** Craster's information and shelter aid the Watch, and Mormont correctly fears wider catastrophe; tolerating abuse and offerings is nevertheless a real choice. Jon's concern conflicts with institutional priorities. **Countercase:** immediate attack on Craster might strand rangers, endanger wives, or cost crucial intelligence; it is not guaranteed to rescue Gilly.

**What Gilly actually proves:** She provides a specific firsthand account that Craster gives sons to blue-eyed nocturnal entities, corroborated as known conduct by Mormont. This is **stronger than rumor**, but not a direct scene of supernatural delivery or proof the cold gods are a single known category of being.

**Mance's purpose:** Mormont infers offensive invasion from mass and terrain. Later wildling testimony could show the migration was motivated by *fleeing danger* even if an attack on the Wall becomes necessary. Do not record 'Mance gathering for conquest' as verified intent.
