## Narrative sequence and significant changes

1. **VERIFIED:** At Winterfell's autumn **harvest feast**, Bran ceremonially rides his specially trained **Dancer** into the Great Hall in Stark's white-and-grey colors, wearing a **silver-and-jet wolf-head brooch**. The lordly cheers make him proud despite his awareness that they are for the house, Robb's victories and the harvest more than for him. **Hodor and Osha** transfer him from the horse to the high seat, exposing both his adaptive ability and dependence on others' assistance.
2. **VERIFIED:** Bran formally welcomes vassals in Robb's name and thanks gods old and new for the harvest. The high table includes **Rickon, Ser Rodrik, Beth Cassel, Wyman Manderly, Donella Hornwood, the Umbers and the Frey wards**. Rickon refuses haircuts since Catelyn's departure. **Luwin** reminds Bran that Robb is contracted to marry a Frey girl and **Arya** a Frey boy; Bran immediately objects that Arya would never consent. These are *existing political promises*, not marriages that have occurred.
3. **VERIFIED:** A spectacular succession of meat, fish, bread, vegetables, fruit, wine and desserts is served; **Wyman Manderly** supplied twenty casks of seafood from White Harbor. Bran, who has been taught how to distribute the lord's portions, sends good dishes to **Lady Hornwood, the Umbers, Cley Cerwyn, Joseth** (who trained Dancer) and sweets to **Hodor and Old Nan**. He teases Big and Little Walder by sending them less favored vegetables. The feast demonstrates actual food stocks and status performance, especially contrasted with starving **King's Landing** (`ACOK 17 and 20`), but does not measure all northern future food reserves.
4. **VERIFIED / SOCIAL CONTRADICTIONS:** Bran is surrounded by a huge public celebration but feels isolated, missing both physical activity and family. He remembers Robert's welcoming feast and the many dead or departed Stark household members, including **Jory, Fat Tom, Septa Mordane, Vayon Poole**, and others he fears may never return. **Donella Hornwood** remains visibly desolate amid the festivities. **Osha** strikes a man with her ale flagon when he touches her without permission; the surrounding crowd laughs. Ordinary festive sociability coexists with coercion and grief.
5. **VERIFIED:** **Meera and Jojen Reed**, children of **Howland Reed of Greywater Watch**, arrive unexpectedly during the feast. Meera wears practical traveling clothes with **bronze-scaled jerkin, net, knife, frog spear and shield**; the younger, green-eyed Jojen wears green and carries no weapon. The Freys voice derogatory stereotypes about crannogmen. **Howland himself remains absent**, despite the request for northern lordly attendance in `ACOK 16`.
6. **VERIFIED / CULTURAL PLEDGE:** In a formal ceremony Meera and Jojen renew ancestral fealty on behalf of the Neck, pledging support, harvest and protection subject to reciprocal mercy and justice. They conclude with a distinctive **ice-and-fire oath**. Bran improvises a suitable welcome. The siblings explicitly want to meet **Summer and Shaggydog**; Jojen asks where the wolves are, and Rickon says they are in the godswood; the actual nighttime meeting comes later.
7. **VERIFIED:** At the dancing, a song about **the Long Night and Night's Watch riding against the Others** is accompanied by **Hother Umber's large warhorn**. Bran feels sad that he cannot dance as he once dreamed of knighthood; he departs early with Hodor. This is an in-world remembered song, not a precise historical eyewitness account of the first Battle for the Dawn.
8. **BRAN'S RECOLLECTION OF NED'S WORDS:** Before sleep Bran remembers asking Ned about great Kingsguard knights. Ned named **Ser Arthur Dayne**, wielder of **Dawn**, a weapon said to be **forged from the heart of a fallen star**, as the greatest fighter he had witnessed. Ned had told Bran that Arthur **would have killed him but for Howland Reed**. **Bran recalls this attributed statement; exact events, means of rescue, survival and the full Tower of Joy story are not depicted here.** It is a material lead for later Dayne, Reed and Jon-parentage theories but **proves no specific hidden parentage theory**.
9. **VERIFIED AS DREAM / NOT EXTERNALLY VERIFIED:** Bran sleeps and experiences the godswood from a **wolf-body perspective**, accompanied by Shaggydog. The two Reeds approach without evident fear. **Jojen** says the wolves will grow larger, calls Shaggydog fearful/angry and Summer more powerful than he knows, then declares this is not his day to die. When he touches the wolf, Bran experiences the world disappearing and himself **falling**. This is a compelling wolf-dream and possible green-dream interaction; the passage alone does not independently verify Jojen's prophetic accuracy or a physical waking-world transfer.

## Agency, rival readings and next research tests

- **Bran** actively performs lordly duties while longing for knighthood and fearing estrangement. Serving as Stark representative is genuine growth, not a replacement of his disability's practical consequences.
- **Reed siblings** bring explicit fealty, not a sworn statement about Tower of Joy secrets. They may hold knowledge Bran lacks; neither the oath nor Ned's memory authorizes inventing the details of Arthur's defeat.
- **Wolf dream:** coincidence, ordinary symbolic dreaming, an emerging capacity to experience Summer, or an externally influenced contact with Jojen are competing explanations. Later independently matched waking details could discriminate among them.
- **Reed/Dayne:** the **minimal warranted conclusion** is that Howland played a decisive protective role according to Ned's remembered words. Howland having killed Arthur, used magic, witnessed a secret birth, or conspired about parentage each needs additional evidence.
- **Next:** `ACOK 28/35 (Bran)` for Reed accounts of dreams and actual wolf contact; `AGOT 39 (Eddard)` for Tower of Joy recollection; later Dayne/Howland sources for corroboration.
