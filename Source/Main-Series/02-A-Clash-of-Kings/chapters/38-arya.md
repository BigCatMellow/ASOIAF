# ACOK 38 — Arya: two deaths spent, Tywin departs, and the wrong target

## Main narrative and causal record

1. **VERIFIED:** Harrenhal is preparing for **Tywin Lannister's departure**: wagonloads of provisions, fletched arrows, cleaned armor, horses, dismantled encampments and overworked captive servants. **Addam Marbrand** leads an advance contingent out a day earlier; Tywin's main host will leave shortly. Soldiers discuss Robb's success in the west in mutually contradictory variants, including rumors he burned Lannisport or took Casterly Rock. Only the broad fact of a western victory is independently corroborated by `ACOK 32/35`; Arya's hearsay is not reliable detail.
2. **VERIFIED:** Weese sends Arya running errands beyond Harrenhal's walls, creating tangible **escape opportunities**, but threatens to hand fugitives to **Vargo Hoat**, who he says cuts off feet. Arya is literate; **Weese thinks she cannot read** and therefore leaves his work orders unsealed. She reads a petty gambling-debt note and avoids a knight who tries to strike her when she reads it aloud. Taking a drinking horn from him, she brings it to Weese, who promises to share a **capon** as a reward. Neither her literacy nor the promised reward generates secure protection.
3. **VERIFIED:** A gate guard says **Amory Lorch is the new castellan** while **Vargo Hoat's Bloody Mummers** stay to forage despite mutual dislike. **Gregor Clegane** and his men, including **Polliver, Dunsen and Raff**, will ride with Tywin; Arya fears she will lose the chance to name one of them to **Jaqen H'ghar**, who still owes her **two deaths** after the reported killing of Chiswyck in `ACOK 30`.
4. **VERIFIED:** In the smithy Arya speaks with **Gendry**, who reports **Hot Pie** remembers her battle cry of **Winterfell** at the burning holdfast. Gendry has told him he misheard a different shout, protecting her hidden **Stark identity**. Her **sex** is known to Gendry, but she needs both secrets managed. She delivers a request to **Lucan** for a replacement sword on **Ser Lyonel's mark**, realizing the same unread paper could serve as pretext to **take a horse and sword and ride through the gate**. Her fear that she cannot answer questions about Lyonel or evade Weese prevents the attempt.
5. **VERIFIED / TALE TRANSMISSION:** Marching archers repeat tales of Robb's soldiers aided by **twenty-foot giants**, nightly speed and inhuman Stark wolf power. Other archers dismiss the stories; the rumors demonstrate that a battlefield surprise is translated into supernatural propaganda, not that Robb commands literal giants. Arya briefly feels renewed Stark courage until **Weese slaps her** and orders another errand, an immediate reminder of the power imbalance.
6. **VERIFIED:** Arya encounters **Rorge**, who has become an Amory-affiliated man and sexually threatens her while recalling her rescue of the three wagon prisoners. She locates **Jaqen at the bathhouse**. Jaqen hears her approach and explains it by the ordinary **scuff of leather on stone**, recommending barefoot stealth; extraordinary powers are **not needed** to explain that particular perception.
7. **VERIFIED — SECOND NAME:** She whispers **Weese** to Jaqen, seeking to end the immediate abuse. This is a **conscious decision** rather than an accidental death request. Back at supper Weese hoards the capon, beats and threatens her again, and gives bones to his **ugly spotted dog**. Arya repeats her enemies' names at bedtime and anticipates Weese's death, but he remains alive at the next morning's breakfast.
8. **VERIFIED:** Tywin and **Kevan** ride out in splendor, accompanied by named banners and soldiers. **Gregor is last**, Polliver carrying the **three-dog Clegane standard** and described in this chapter as **wearing Gendry's horned helmet**. Earlier `ACOK 26` attributed the helmet to **Dunsen**: this chapter shows different custody or a mistaken earlier attribution, without a witnessed transfer. **Do not silently collapse those two specific on-page descriptions**.
9. **VERIFIED — CHOICE REGRET:** Arya suddenly recognizes that she has spent a precious opportunity on **Weese rather than Tywin**, whose departure gives her no realistic immediate chance to name him before he rides away. Her remorse is about **strategic scale** as well as her lived fear. The scene is not proof Jaqen could have reached or killed Tywin merely because his services are feared.
10. **VERIFIED — SECOND DEATH:** As Arya runs toward the departing host to find Jaqen, screams draw her to **Weese's body** in the castle yard, his throat torn open while his formerly obedient spotted dog attacks and consumes him. A man with a **crossbow shoots the dog dead**. Onlookers ascribe the attack to Harren's curse or ghost. **Jaqen stands watching** near the Wailing Tower and touches **two fingers to his cheek** when Arya looks at him, matching the second name fulfilled and leaving **one death owed**. The story does not show **how the dog was induced to turn on its master**; magical animal manipulation, poison, bait, training or chance are competing possibilities, not verified methods.

## Rival interpretations and next tests

- **Jaqen's killings:** Chiswyck's fall and Weese's dog attack follow Arya's choices closely, but **no murder technique** is directly narrated. Assassination skill is better supported than any exact method of magical influence.
- **Arya's agency:** The abusive Weese is a genuine danger, so her choice is not trivial; her later recognition that Tywin commands wider violence is also morally and strategically substantial.
- **Tywin's campaign:** Departure is confirmed, not destination/arrival or fate. See `ACOK 39 (Catelyn)` for reports he marches west, and consider those reports' limits.
