## Main chronological record

1. **VERIFIED:** **Lancel** appears at Tyrion's solar long after midnight, sent by **Cersei** with a sealed command to release **Pycelle**, whom Tyrion imprisoned in `ACOK 25`. He also demands that **Jacelyn Bywater** be dismissed and arrested after defying an earlier release order attributed to the king. Tyrion infers Cersei already tried Bywater and was refused. Tyrion has been working through **Varys's reports and Littlefinger's accounts** instead of sleeping, showing continuing dependence on both dubious councilors.
2. **VERIFIED:** Lancel describes the **regent as superior to the Hand**, and threatens Tyrion by touching his sword. Tyrion knows from Varys that Lancel is sexually involved with Cersei, and uses this plus Lancel's part in **Robert's death** to blackmail him. Lancel admits **Cersei gave him strongwine for Robert's hunting trip** and invokes Tywin's earlier instruction to obey her. This is consequential **participant testimony** about facilitation, not a direct confession that Lancel secretly administered a separately named toxin.
3. **VERIFIED / BLACKMAIL:** Tyrion threatens to tell **Joffrey** that Lancel helped cause his father's death and has been sleeping with his mother. Frightened, Lancel falls to his knees, agrees to stay close to Cersei and report **her movements, visitors, conversations and plans** directly to Tyrion. Tyrion explicitly asks Lancel to **continue Cersei's intimacy** as part of maintaining her trust and warns against producing another child. This recruits a source through coercion; Lancel's future reliability and survival are **unknown**, and his apparent submission is not affection or autonomous political allegiance.
4. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion gives Lancel a cover story: tell Cersei he frightened the Hand into backing down, that Tyrion regrets conflict and will henceforth seek her approval. Tyrion agrees to **release Pycelle on the morrow**, but **not restore him to the council**, and asks Lancel to claim he might win Bywater from Tyrion's side. The chapter does not actually depict Pycelle walking out of his cell, so **planned release** must not be marked **completed release**.
5. **TYRION'S INTERIOR MONOLOGUE:** Tyrion feels some sympathy for the boy and anticipates either Cersei's retaliation if she discovers betrayal or Jaime's rage if Jaime discovers the relationship. This predicts **a possible lethal outcome** from a secret triangle; it does not establish a later death. Tyrion regards his own ruthless maneuver as more effective than the honor-bound paths of Ned and Jon Arryn; his assessment of why they died is self-flattering, not a controlled comparison.
6. **VERIFIED:** Unable to sleep, Tyrion takes **Bronn** through the dangerous city streets to **Chataya's brothel**, where **Alayaya** plays his ostensible partner while he accesses the secret **wardrobe-and-tunnel route** to a different residence. Customers, including **Dancy**, assume he favors Alayaya; **Marei** is reportedly teaching other women to read. A wager about whether Tyrion will select Dancy illustrates that the disguise requires repeated, conspicuous behavior, not a one-time cover.
7. **VERIFIED / CITY STRAIN:** Chataya cannot obtain her usual fine wine at any price, and a woman encountered outside carries a **dead cat**, apparently valued as food. These are small direct signs of scarcity amid ongoing city unrest; the woman's intended use for the cat is **Tyrion's supposition**, not her reported explanation. Bronn's violence toward an earlier spitting bystander shows how Tyrion's protection contributes to fear.
8. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion visits **Shae** at a secret manse protected by **mercenaries Varys recruited**: among them an injured-looking Braavosi, a eunuch strangler and two Ibbenese. He favors visibly unattractive men to avoid rivals and excludes his recognizably loyal clansmen because their appearance would reveal his private arrangements. This is not a secure independence from Varys: **Varys selected and knows the location of her guards**.
9. **VERIFIED / ETHICAL CAUTION:** He finds Shae asleep and initiates intimacy before she is fully awake, afterward experiencing affectionate reassurance and excitement at his own political power. The scene is narrated from **Tyrion's** perspective, not Shae's inner account; it should not establish her wholly unqualified consent or her sincerity in loving him. Tyrion thinks the entire earlier Tysha marriage was a staged prostitute's deception, based on **Jaime's old claim**; this is his belief, not fresh proof in the current chapter.
10. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion concludes he loves both his place at the center of the game and Shae. His pride and emotional dependency produce a potential vulnerability, not proof she will later betray him.

## Interpretation and future tests

- **Lancel**: His role in Robert's last hunt is now better substantiated but its precise causal effect must be distinguished from the fatal boar wound. Blackmail gives Tyrion information but increases danger to a subordinate.
- **Cersei**: Trying to control Bywater and reclaim Pycelle shows continued counter-power after losing her red cloaks. Tyrion's apparent concession is deliberately deceptive.
- **Tyrion/Shae:** Attachment is real in his internal POV, but his sexual access, control over guards, money and secrecy complicate his certainty about mutuality. The Varys-provided household may be easier for enemies to discover than Tyrion imagines.
- **Next:** Subsequent Tyrion/Cersei for whether Lancel actually informs and Pycelle is released; later Tysha revelations; security breaches around Chataya's entrance and Shae's manse.
