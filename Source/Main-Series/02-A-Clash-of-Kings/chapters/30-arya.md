## Narrative and consequences

1. **VERIFIED:** Arya, now using the **alias Weasel**, works at Harrenhal for **Weese** under the Wailing Tower. Her bedding is a straw niche in the vaults; food and washing water are more reliable than during the forced march, but she is not free. **Hot Pie** serves in the kitchens, while **Gendry** works in the forge. Hot Pie continues calling her **Arry**, an identity risk, and is struck for trying to smuggle her a tart. The chapter distinguishes adequate rations from basic safety.
2. **VERIFIED:** Harrenhal is ruinously oversized: five **named towers** (Dread, Widow's, Wailing, Ghosts, Kingspyre); Lady Whent had occupied only the low floors of two; Tywin orders once-empty Wailing Tower levels repaired to house his troops and captives. Arya removes refuse and bat nests, scrubs floors, runs messages and serves soldiers. The top floor's gigantic **Whent sigil bats** are ordinary animals, not confirmed spectral signs. Rumors Tywin intends to make Harrenhal his eventual seat are **camp gossip**, not an enacted grant.
3. **VERIFIED:** Arya explains the Wailing Tower's noises as **wind through heat-cracked masonry**, not haunting by Harren's ghosts; she correctly fears present overseers more than legendary dead. Harrenhal covers roughly **three times Winterfell's ground**, with stables for **1,000 horses**, a **20-acre godswood**, kitchens comparable to Winterfell's great hall, and the Hall of a Hundred Hearths in fact appearing to contain **33 or 35** fireplaces by Arya's inconsistent count. The giant scale allows a 'little mouse' to **overhear information** that powerful figures dismiss as unimportant.
4. **VERIFIED:** **Weese** terrorizes his servants, has a vicious spotted dog and uses it against a latrine boy; he hits and later canes Arya. Within **three days** she moves **Weese** to the first place in her hatred litany. Her self-concept shifts from helpless *sheep* on the road to **concealed mouse** inside the castle. Her claims to hear all secrets should not make every overheard rumor reliable.
5. **OVERHEARD / GOSSIP:** Arya hears that a letter accusing **Joffrey of illegitimacy** reached **Maester Tothmure**, who was told by **Tywin** to burn it. This is a serving girl's account of a message and an order, not firsthand proof Arya witnessed the letter burn. The chapter separately reports confusion about the competing Baratheon kings and discussion of whether King's Landing can hold. Lannister soldiers tell conflicting stories that **Beric Dondarrion has already died multiple times**. This is testimony and mockery; death and resurrection are **not established by Arya's direct sight**.
6. **VERIFIED:** Roughly **a fortnight later**, **Vargo Hoat** arrives with the multinational sellsword company **Brave Companions** (called **Bloody Mummers** and other slurs by Lannister men), bearing a **black goat with bloody horns**. The riders' distinctive weaponry, languages, appearance and Hoat's coin-chain/goat helm are observed; Tywin has hired them as armed agents. After a violent dispute kills a **Harys Swyft squire** and injures two mercenaries, **Tywin has the two wounded men and an archer from Lord Lydden hanged**, followed by forced outward reconciliation between Hoat and Harys. The detailed claim that the archer started it is **Weese's report**.
7. **RUMOR / MILITARY INTELLIGENCE:** A Brave Companion reports **Roose Bolton's northern army occupying the ruby ford of the Trident**. Lannister fighters dispute whether Bolton can cross before Robb joins him. Arya realizes **Riverrun is far closer than Winterfell**, and seeks a way to reach Robb. None of the many competing camp reports about Tywin marching to Riverrun, Highgarden, King's Landing, the Vale, or making peace is validated in this chapter. The rumor Tywin intends to marry Lysa or forge silver anti-warg swords is particularly speculative.
8. **VERIFIED / FAILED RESCUE POSSIBILITY:** Captive northern lords and Freys reside in the **Tower of Dread** on pledges not to flee. Four Frey brothers (three legitimate, one bastard) are soon ransomed when other Frey kin arrive with gold. The **northmen** remain. Arya knows **Lord Cerwyn** and hopes he will recognize her and fund escape, but cannot reach him as he is injured and guarded. **Silent sisters carry his shrouded body out**; his death is confirmed by a guard, closing this particular rescue route. This should update `ACOK 25 Tyrion` where Cerwyn was publicly offered in exchange for **Tion**: the man is now dead, though King's Landing might **not yet know**.
9. **VERIFIED:** **Ser Amory Lorch** returns after hunting Beric with fewer and wounded troops, bringing **Rorge, Biter and Jaqen H'ghar** with him. Arya rescued these three from their burning prison wagon at the earlier holdfast (`ACOK 14`), but she initially believes **Jaqen does not recognize her** because she is disguised as a small castle servant. These three have visibly survived; their captivity/service relation to Lorch is not yet fully explained.
10. **VERIFIED / SUPERNATURAL OR SECRET-CRAFT CLAIM:** Jaqen visits Arya's bed in darkness, explicitly recognizes she was once Arry and is now a girl, and says he owes **three deaths for the three lives she took from the Red God**. She requests **escape to Riverrun** instead; he refuses and offers precisely **three names**, to be acted on by him. The phrase **Red God** is Jaqen's explanation, not an independent religious law established by the POV. His stealth and recognition are witnessed, the precise mechanism of future deaths is not.
11. **CHARACTER REPORT / MORAL TRIGGER:** When Gregor's men return after another round of raiding, **Chiswyck** boasts before fellow soldiers of an earlier inn crime: he says **Gregor and men sexually assaulted an innkeeper's daughter**, **Raff killed her brother**, and the traumatized father was humiliated. One soldier disputes the girl's age. This is **self-incriminating boastful narration**, not a flashback witnessed directly by Arya, but consistent with violence Arya has independently seen. Weese beats Arya for failing the clothes errand after she overhears the story.
12. **VERIFIED / NAME ONE:** Arya eventually whispers **'Chiswyck'** to Jaqen at the soldiers' meal. He gives no visible assent. **Two days of no news pass**, then on **the third day** Arya hears from Weese that one of Gregor's men **fell from a wallwalk and broke his neck**; soldiers speculate about drink or Harren's ghost. **Arya concludes she caused Chiswyck's death through naming him**, and removes him from the night litany. The narrative's juxtaposition strongly supports that **Jaqen arranged it**, but **no witnessed killing method or eyewitness perpetrator** is shown. It leaves **two promised names**.
13. **VERIFIED:** Arya's death choice is consciously weighed against **Ned's injunction that executioners should look condemned men in the face**; she reasons she should kill Lannisters but hesitates to delegate murder. Her choice after hearing Chiswyck's crime is a real moral change, not an unambiguous endorsement of vengeance or proof each named victim will deserve death.

## Agency, alternative explanation and follow-up

- **Arya:** Low-status servant invisibility grants observation while her reliance on violent outsiders gives leverage at a moral cost. Choosing Chiswyck involves victim protection, revenge and the lure of power, but she has no independent control over how Jaqen acts.
- **Jaqen:** He knows Arya's earlier identity and claims three lives as divine accounting; at this stage his abilities could reflect extraordinary stealth and practical assassination rather than proof of miraculous shapechanging.
- **Tywin:** His forces are held together by mercenaries, public punishment and a huge occupied ruin; the servants are informants only if someone listens. His exact future target remains unverified.
