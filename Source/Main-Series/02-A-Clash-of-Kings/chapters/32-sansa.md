## Narrative and immediate consequences

1. **VERIFIED:** **Sandor Clegane** takes Sansa to Joffrey on urgent orders. Sansa fears her secret meetings with **Dontos** have been discovered, but Sandor clarifies that her **brother Robb**, not she, has angered Joffrey. Sansa repeats the rehearsed accusation that Robb is a traitor and fears harm to **Jaime** would bring retaliation against her. The Hound's contempt for her training shows the gap between her spoken survival formula and her real loyalties.
2. **VERIFIED:** At the archery ground a **yellow cat lies dying with a crossbow bolt through it**. Joffrey is brandishing his ornate crossbow and surrounded by **Boros Blount, Meryn Trant, Lancel, courtiers and servants**. Dontos appears on a **broomstick horse** (a punishment Joffrey imposed after his drinking at the tourney) and privately encourages Sansa to be brave.
3. **LANCEL'S DISTORTED ACCOUNT:** Lancel reports that **Robb Stark** destroyed Stafford Lannister's host in a surprise attack using supposed **warg sorcery**, after which northerners allegedly feasted on corpses. This is hostile propaganda; **Tyrion** later supplies a more plausible military explanation for the horse panic. The specific allegation of cannibalism is **not verified**.
4. **VERIFIED:** Sansa protests that **Lady**, unlike Arya's wolf, never harmed Joffrey, and Joffrey retorts that **Ned** killed Lady while he himself ordered Ned's execution. Joffrey **claims** he recently killed a hungry demonstrator with a crossbow through the throat and injured a stone-throwing woman. It is **Joffrey's self-report** of an earlier event, not directly witnessed by Sansa. He says Cersei forbids killing Sansa because that could provoke **Jaime's death**; Sansa's hostage value limits but does not prevent abuse.
5. **VERIFIED / DONTOS'S FAILED INTERVENTION:** Joffrey orders **Sandor** to strike her. **Dontos** volunteers instead, striking Sansa with his ludicrous **melon-headed morningstar**, trying to transform violent punishment into comedy and prevent worse injury. The crowd laughs; **Joffrey remains unsatisfied**, removes Dontos and orders Boros and Meryn to punish her. Dontos's willingness to intervene is directly shown, but the act does **not** demonstrate that he can safely extract her from King's Landing.
6. **VERIFIED:** **Boros punches Sansa in the belly**, strikes her thighs with the flat of his sword and tears open her bodice at Joffrey's command, exposing her before onlookers. The violence is sustained and humiliating. **Sandor protests 'Enough'**, but Joffrey continues. These are **directly witnessed assaults**, not symbolic metaphors for captivity.
7. **VERIFIED:** **Tyrion arrives with Bronn and Timett**, orders an end to the beating, challenges the Kingsguard's chivalry and invokes **Aerys's fate** as warning against unrestrained kingship. Sandor **throws Sansa his white cloak** so she can cover herself; when Boros challenges Tyrion, Tyrion threatens him with Bronn and Timett. Tyrion's intervention stops this assault but does not undo his broader participation in holding Sansa hostage.
8. **VERIFIED:** Joffrey repeats **Cersei's maxim that fear is better than love**. Tyrion points out coercing a defenseless girl is not a credible substitute for handling **Stannis and Renly**. Tyrion has Sansa removed to the **Tower of the Hand**, bathed and medically treated by **Maester Frenken** with salve and **dreamwine**. Sansa's internal response to the suggestion she will forget the beating is sharply skeptical.
9. **VERIFIED:** Sansa recognizes she has been placed in **Arya's former room** from Ned's tenure as Hand. A **Black Ears woman with an ear necklace** refuses to let her leave for the godswood, even though Sansa desperately wants to consult Dontos. The temporary 'protective' lodging also prevents her from pursuing her only apparent escape channel. The woman can guard her without being a conventional knight.
10. **TYRION'S RECONSTRUCTION OF OXCROSS:** Tyrion says **Robb attacked Stafford at Oxcross six nights earlier**, near **Lannisport**, and the capital learned of the defeat that morning. The northern attackers **cut horse lines** and sent **Grey Wind among the horses**, causing panic even among war-trained destriers. **Stafford was killed by Rickard Karstark's lance**; named Lannister losses include **Rubert Brax, Lymond Vikary, Crakehall and Jast**, while captives include **Martyn Lannister** and Jast's sons. Tyrion believes Stafford failed to set proper watches. This is a **military account received at court**, more specific than Lancel's rumor but not a firsthand battle scene.
11. **IMPORTANT ROUTE QUESTION:** Tyrion says his father's troops still hold the **Golden Tooth** and deny Robb passed there, leaving **how Robb reached the western host an open military mystery**. Do not fill the gap with a known secret route before another POV or reliable report supplies it.
12. **VERIFIED / UNFULFILLED PROMISE:** Tyrion tells Sansa he **does not intend her to wed Joffrey**, that no marriage can heal Stark–Lannister hostility, and that he plans to **send her home if Robb accepts the king's peace**. His intent is stated, not a completed annulment or release; consent of **Joffrey, Cersei and Robb** remains uncertain. Sansa dutifully claims love for Joffrey, concealing her actual prayers for Robb and his death.
13. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion offers his mountain-clan guards as protection in the Tower of the Hand. Sansa **refuses** because such guards would prevent **Dontos's planned extraction**; she uses a convincing lie that Ned's murdered household haunts the tower. Tyrion agrees to escort her back to **Maegor's Holdfast**. Her decision is tactical, not proof she prefers Joffrey's violence.

## Competing readings and next tests

- **Sansa** has accumulated real tactical skill at deception. She sustains humiliation to survive, distinguishes a temporary protector from a potentially lasting escape strategy, and correctly withholds her feelings.
- **Tyrion** genuinely prevents further immediate violence, yet regards her as political collateral and links her release to Robb's surrender. His concern coexists with the hostage system he operates.
- **Sandor/Dontos** both offer partial protections, one with a cloak and a verbal protest, one through deliberately less damaging comic blows. Neither is guaranteed reliable or safe.
- **Oxcross:** Grey Wind materially contributes to horse panic. That does not prove northern troops are supernatural armies, nor show Robb used mind control. The route through the western defenses needs later proof.
- **Next:** later Sansa/Dontos for escape planning and safety, Theon/Bran for Robb's wider campaign consequences, and Tyrion intelligence on Tywin's reaction.
