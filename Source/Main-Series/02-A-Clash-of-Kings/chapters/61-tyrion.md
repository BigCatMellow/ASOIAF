## Narrative sequence and agency

1. **SORTIE ACTUALLY LAUNCHES:** Tyrion leads mounted defenders from the **King's Gate sally port** in a wedge. **Ser Mandon Moore** rides immediately to his right with the royal **stag-and-lion banner**; **Podrick Payne** unexpectedly rides at his left with a sword. Tyrion objects that Pod is too young, then reluctantly allows him to remain nearby. This **confirms the rally in `ACOK 59` generated an actual attack**, not merely inspirational speech.
2. **FIRST SUCCESS / GATE RELIEVED:** Against a **black-oak battering ram**, archers and soldiers gathered beneath the King's Gate, Tyrion orders a lance charge over wet mud and corpses. Mandon **impales a man with the banner lance**, while Tyrion **kills a knight with Florent-like fox-and-flowers heraldry** and several other combatants. The attack **breaks the ram crew** and scatters the immediate attackers; this is an achieved tactical success, not the final rout of Stannis's army.
3. **CITY IDENTITY AGAIN:** Tyrion's force shouts **King's Landing** and some men call him **Halfman**, connecting the local-defense appeal in `ACOK 59` to a real, if transient, battle identity. Tyrion knows his galloping force is **fragmenting**, and men who lag behind may be dead, scattered or pursuing foes; avoid inventing a tally.
4. **TO THE MUD GATE:** Tyrion redirects the pursuit **toward the Mud Gate**, seeing that other defenders likely under **Balon Swann or Lancel** continue fighting river landings. **The wildfire has not secured the north bank**. His own horse and men must move through burning wreckage; he understands better why **Sandor fears fire**, though he judges his horse easier to force onward.
5. **BATTLE FEVER / UNRELIABLE PERCEPTION:** Tyrion enters a **frenzy of fighting**, hacking at mounted and unmounted opponents, laughing and feeling wounds only belatedly. He recalls **Jaime's description of battle fever**—collapsed perception of time, pain and consequences. This is **direct first-person experience** but a poor basis for counting enemy dead or reconstructing exact individual movements, particularly with his narrow helm slit.
6. **HUMANIZED ENEMY:** One wounded enemy knight offers a **gauntlet as pledge of surrender**, but when Tyrion takes it he discovers the **man's severed hand remains inside**. The sight horrifies him and briefly interrupts the intoxication with slaughter. This is a direct detail of casualties, not a symbol to replace the scene's broader cruelty.
7. **UNINTENDED BRIDGE:** **Ser Balon Swann** points out a heap of **roughly twenty tangled galleys** spanning portions of the river, held by grappling lines, rigging, broken rams and lodged hulls. **Hundreds of Stannis's men cross from wreck to wreck** above water and wildfire. Tyrion recognizes that **his chain/fire trap has accidentally created a makeshift bridge** for the invasion. This is a major strategic countereffect that can coexist with the fleet's destruction in `ACOK 58–59`.
8. **SECOND SORTIE INTO WRECKS:** Tyrion leads his remaining men and Balon's troops to the **foot of the bridge**, meeting enemies near the **wrecked Dragonsbane**. A **Celtigar crab-badged spearman** kills Balon's mount, spilling him; Tyrion strikes at the spearman while galloping past, then **his own horse leaps into a damaged ship**. Horse and rider fall; Tyrion's red stallion **breaks a leg** and must be **put down by Tyrion with a knife**. Tyrion loses his **axe** and later knife in the confusion.
9. **FIGHTING ON SHIPS:** Tyrion fights across unstable decks in a mix of **blood, water, collapsing rigging and wildfire**, at times with **a broken spear**. **Mandon and Balon**, in their white Kingsguard armor, are nearby and help keep opponents off. Tyrion sees a **naked man fall from above and burst on a deck**, a direct on-page payoff compatible with the Antler Men thrown by trebuchet in `ACOK 59`; the falling person's specific name and guilt are not shown.
10. **BRIDGE COLLAPSE / ORIENTATION LOST:** Catapult stones and damaged hulls break apart the temporary ship bridge. Tyrion lands in rising water and nearly drowns, clinging to broken lines. A brief illusion that **Stannis's army and the Red Keep have exchanged banks** is explained by the **wreck revolving beneath him**. This is a material perception error during battle, not magical relocation or time distortion. He eventually notices an **arrow lodged in his shoulder**.
11. **MANDON'S APPARENT RESCUE BECOMES ASSAULT:** **Mandon Moore**, standing on the adjacent ship, offers Tyrion **his left hand** across a widening gap. As Tyrion reaches, Mandon strikes with a sword **at Tyrion's face**, cutting him badly below the eyes and throwing him back toward the water. The presentation as a rescue attempt is a **direct assassination-like betrayal by a Kingsguard**. **No identity of a person who hired or instructed Mandon is provided**, and the chapter offers no certain reason for his left-handed approach.
12. **SECOND DIRECT ATTEMPT:** Tyrion manages to cling to an oar and pull himself back onto deck, bleeding heavily. **Mandon approaches and places sword point at Tyrion's throat**, holding the blade with both hands, preparing a finishing blow. This second action eliminates the interpretation that the facial cut was accidental collateral damage or a misread gesture; **his intent to kill Tyrion is strongly explicit**.
13. **SUDDEN INTERVENTION / POD LINK:** Before the killing stroke, Mandon **lurches toward the rail and falls overboard**, leaving Tyrion alive. A rescuer bends over Tyrion, with a young voice he thinks sounds like **Podrick**; Tyrion first deliriously thinks of **Jaime**. The immediate sequence strongly supports **Podrick's intervention**, but **the viewpoint does not directly narrate the physical push or identify its exact mechanics**. Future confirmation should be sought.
14. **CHAPTER END:** Tyrion survives the attack long enough to recognize a familiar young voice, with **severe facial wound, arrow injury and profound exhaustion**. There is **no direct on-page confirmation he recovers, who ordered Moore's attack, Joffrey's exact location, or Blackwater's final victor**.

## Theory tests and later evidence needed

- **Who sent Mandon?** A documented attempt to murder Tyrion in battle is far stronger evidence than his prior distrust of the White Sword. It is **not proof Cersei, Joffrey, Varys or Littlefinger ordered him**, even though each might have motives or opportunities. Test Moore's assignment, payment, changes in command, his history in Arryn's service and who profits from Tyrion's injury.
- **Was the chain successful?** It catastrophically annihilated much of the invading fleet and then accidentally **made a crossing possible** by trapping numerous wrecks together. Assess both effects rather than treating the new bridge as an implausible exception.
- **Tyrion as commander:** He inspires and delivers meaningful tactical relief. Yet he kills surrendered or defenseless-feeling enemies during frenzy and loses his control of the scattered force; neither heroic nor vicious labels alone suffice.
- **Podrick's role:** Tyrion's final identification is only sensory impression through pain and blood, but its proximity to Moore's fall is compelling; revisit subsequent chapters.
