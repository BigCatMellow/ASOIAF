## Main cause and effect

1. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion deliberately wines and flatters **Janos Slynt**, the City Watch's commander and new Lord of Harrenhal. Slynt proposes six replacements, especially cruel **Allar Deem**, while deriding **Jacelyn Bywater** as inflexible. Bywater lost his hand in the campaign against Balon Greyjoy and was knighted by Robert at Pyke.
2. **SLYNT'S ADMISSION:** Deem was chosen to murder a still-nursing daughter of **Robert Baratheon** in a brothel; the mother was killed trying to defend her baby. Slynt refuses to identify the person who issued the underlying order. Tyrion's disgust is sharpened by Slynt's contempt for sex workers, which reminds Tyrion of **Shae and Tysha**.
3. **SLYNT'S ADMISSION / LIMITED TEST:** Slynt ordered the spear thrust that assisted **Ned's arrest**, defending it as action against treason. He assigns the command to behead Ned to **Joffrey** and mocks Varys's supposed omniscience. Tyrion suspects the speed of the execution but has no verified alternate mastermind.
4. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion strips Slynt of real power and sends him to the Night's Watch; he promises Slynt's eldest son a lordly title and substitute land, the younger sons placements as squires and the daughter family marriage prospects. **Bywater and six gold cloaks** appear to escort Slynt, already demonstrating that Tyrion has won actual armed obedience.
5. **VERIFIED ORDER / UNVERIFIED OUTCOME:** Tyrion includes six of Slynt's associates aboard the **Summer's Dream**, scheduled via **Gulltown, Three Sisters, Skagos and Eastwatch**. He privately lets Bywater know that Deem's falling overboard would be welcome. The chapter establishes an instruction/intent, **not** that Deem died or the vessel arrived.
6. **TYRION'S INFERENCE / VARYS'S SUPPORT:** Tyrion concludes **Cersei** sent the gold cloaks to kill the infant. Varys supports that explanation while admitting he moved **an older royal bastard boy** to safety but failed to foresee the baby girl's danger. **Likely Gendry connection:** in `ACOK 5 (Arya)` gold cloaks specifically seek Gendry; however Varys does **not** name the saved boy or verify how he traveled.
7. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion finds his purge hollow because the alleged higher authority remains untouched. He has supplied Shae with a guarded, comfortable manse and a **white bird from the Summer Isles**. She asks to be allowed to help him more directly; he keeps her role sexual, despite her dissatisfaction. He questions whether a sex worker can love, exposing his biases and past wounds.
8. **VERIFIED CONVERSATION / CHARACTER THEORY:** Varys revisits his ruler/priest/rich man/swordsman riddle. He maintains power ultimately depends on **what people believe**. Tyrion's control of soldiers gives the idea practical force, but Varys's formulation is a political theory voiced by an interested manipulator, not objective narration.
9. **VARYS'S INTELLIGENCE / TYRION'S ORDERS:** The **White Hart** captain reportedly plans defection to **Stannis**; Tyrion chooses a show trial. **Horas and Hobber Redwyne** allegedly plan a postern escape via a **Pentoshi galley Moonrunner** disguised as oarsmen; Tyrion orders the bribed guard removed and ship watched. Comet-linked preachers multiply, but he lets them rant and declines to treat a jest at a supper table as treason.
10. **VERIFIED / UNSURE CONTROL:** Tyrion counts roughly **6,000 gold cloaks**, **100** royal household red cloaks under Vylarr, about **150** of his own clansmen, plus Bronn's recruiting. He suspects he has replaced Littlefinger's Slynt with **Varys's Bywater**; actual long-term control is **UNKNOWN**. Tyrion recalls Ned's misplaced sense of security after similar calculations.
11. **VERIFIED / MORAL CONTRADICTION:** Tyrion waves away **Timett** killing a gambler who cheated him. **Bronn** explains brutal recruiting and says he would ask only how much an employer paid before killing a baby. Tyrion realizes the criminals available to him may be no better than Slynt's; he ends aching for Shae.

## Who knows / rival explanations

- **Tyrion** knows Slynt selected a killer and has enough men to enforce a purge, but Cersei's direct orders and Bywater's private allegiance are not proved to him. His effort to distinguish his rule from hers is undercut by his own agents.
- **Varys** chooses which secrets to disclose. His older-bastard claim may be true and useful; the account also positions Tyrion against Cersei. Independent corroboration required.
- **Slynt** relies on royal favor and a lordly title while failing to secure the soldiers at the door. **Countercase:** Joffrey's order of Ned's execution can explain compliance without proving secret pre-planning.
- **Bronn** puts a price on lethal orders rather than rejecting them; the end of the chapter contradicts a simple reading of Tyrion as having purged cruelty.
