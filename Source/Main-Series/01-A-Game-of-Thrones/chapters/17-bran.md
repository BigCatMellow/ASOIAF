# AGOT 17 (Bran) — the three-eyed crow and Summer

## Major narrative

1. **VERIFIED as a dream:** Unconscious Bran experiences an endless fall and encounters a speaking crow demanding he fly. The crow sometimes seems to speak to him as though his dreaming and bodily survival are connected. The *dream's existence* is directly narrated; its geography and speakers are **not independent external observations**.
2. **VERIFIED as a dream:** A golden face repeating Jaime's line about love intrudes on Bran's consciousness. The crow pecks and orders the memory away, pushing Bran toward the necessity of flight.
3. **VERIFIED as a dream:** Bran looks across Winterfell, sees Luwin, Robb, Hodor and a responsive heart tree; his perspective sweeps to Catelyn at sea with a bloody dagger, Ned and the girls amid shadows, lands across the narrow sea, the Wall, Jon and a terrible northern darkness.
4. **VERIFIED as a dream:** The crow displays three eyes, warns winter is coming and demands a life-or-death choice. Bran spreads his arms and experiences flight; the crow pecks at his forehead.
5. **VERIFIED when awake:** A black-haired serving woman cries out that Bran has awakened; Bran cannot move his legs and feels no touch when the now-grown direwolf lands on them. He names the animal **Summer**.

## Knowledge, choice, and theory stress

**Waking knowledge:** Bran survives, is paralyzed and names Summer; he does not appear to tell anyone the contents of the vision. **DREAM-CONTENT:** offers broader information and an apparent source of instruction, but character knowledge after waking is uncertain.

**Theory A — Bloodraven is the crow:** plausible with later cave material; still requires comparing what Bloodraven actually claims and knows. **Theory B — a different agent is the crow:** underdetermined; differing dream imagery alone is insufficient proof. See [crow case](../../../../research/theories/cases/bloodraven-crow.md).

**Crucial dramatic contrast:** magical 'flying' does not reverse Bran's physical disability. His childhood dream of knighthood has been transformed into an unknown kind of agency, not given an instant, uncostly substitute.
