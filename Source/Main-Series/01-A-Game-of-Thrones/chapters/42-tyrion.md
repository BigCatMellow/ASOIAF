## Plot and consequences

1. **VERIFIED:** Freed from the Eyrie, Tyrion and Bronn take the dangerous high road; Bronn wants to move quietly by night. Tyrion deliberately builds a visible fire and moves slowly because the clans will find them regardless. **TYRION'S HOPE:** negotiate rather than die while hiding.
2. **VERIFIED:** Bronn openly admits he has no love for Tyrion and will serve his own survival. He previously killed his wounded companion Chiggen with a dirk rather than risk discovery while Chiggen groaned, then gave Catelyn a misleading explanation. Tyrion offers a continuing financial incentive and explicitly promises to outbid future employers.
3. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion pays Mord the **promised gold** before leaving the Eyrie. He wishes to investigate who supplied the dagger after reaching the west or capital. His debts are a matter of reputation and deliberate leverage.
4. **TYRION'S RECOLLECTION:** He describes marrying **Tysha**, a lowborn young woman, when he was thirteen, after Jaime helped him intervene in what looked like an attack. Tywin exposed and ended the marriage and forced sexual humiliation. Tyrion believes an account he says Jaime gave him: that the incident had been staged and Tysha was a paid sex worker. **This account is not independently verified here**—the distinction matters for later Jaime/Tyrion evidence.
5. **VERIFIED:** Bronn declares willingness to kill Tywin for such treatment, demonstrating his own response to Tyrion's disclosed trauma, without adopting Tyrion's stated Tysha explanation as established fact.
6. **VERIFIED:** Moon Mountains clans reach the fire: **Gunthor son of Gurn** leads Stone Crows, and **Shagga son of Dolf** stands among armed fighters including Conn. They initially claim everything Tyrion owns. Tyrion argues that food and superior iron arms may matter more than mere coin to under-equipped clans.
7. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion promises swords, lances, mail and, finally, the **Vale of Arryn itself** in exchange for protection. **UNKNOWN:** whether the mountain people accept and what Tyrion can deliver; he owns neither the Vale nor its lands.

## Rival theories and future evidence tests

**Tysha:** the chapter verifies Tyrion's belief and emotional injury, *not* the supposed financial arrangement. Compare later Jaime confession before labeling her; treating a coerced woman's status as established from Tywin's narrative repeats a major epistemic error. **Clans 'conquer' Vale:** offer is negotiation under duress, not proof they can seize impregnable castles. **Tyrion always buys loyalty:** his money works on different incentives, but material supply and real military risk alter bargains.
