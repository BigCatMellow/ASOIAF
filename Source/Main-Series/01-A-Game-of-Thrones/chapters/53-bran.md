## Narrative: obvious plot must remain visible

1. **VERIFIED:** Rickard Karstark arrives with roughly **300 horse and nearly 2,000 foot**; he brings sons **Harrion, Eddard and Torrhen**. They are the last great household to assemble at Winterfell. **Luwin's count:** Robb has about **12,000** gathered, including **~300–400 knights** and **~3,000 armored lances who are not knights**. Others are expected to join farther south; his in-castle muster is not his final campaign strength.
2. **VERIFIED:** Concentration of soldiers overwhelms the winter town and threatens supplies. Luwin argues the army must march soon or starve its environs. Robb protects Bran by forbidding more exposed town outings after an earlier kidnapping and violent tensions between bannermen.
3. **VERIFIED:** Bran has an **adaptive back basket** designed by Luwin for Hodor, permitting movement through castle spaces a horse cannot enter. He goes to the godswood while Hodor enjoys **three pools fed by an underground hot spring** near the guest house, concrete evidence of Winterfell's geothermal infrastructure.
4. **VERIFIED:** Rickon reacts to Robb's coming departure with rage, refusal of food and hiding in the crypts with a **rusted iron sword removed from a dead king**. Shaggydog injures Gage and Mikken and is chained in kennels. This is a family/animal problem affecting castle safety, not proof Rickon is under magical control.
5. **VERIFIED:** Robb's bannermen test the fifteen-year-old acting lord through demands for command, marriages, hunting, a holdfast, river-damming rights and precedence. **Greatjon Umber** threatens withdrawal and draws steel; **Grey Wind bites off two fingers**. Greatjon laughs afterward and becomes Robb's loud champion. Robb later privately admits he feared being killed and distrusts the stiller Roose Bolton.
6. **VERIFIED:** A **raven bearing a letter written by Sansa under the Stark seal** reaches Robb at Winterfell. It asserts Ned plotted treason and demands Catelyn/Robb pledge Joffrey allegiance, and **does not mention Arya**. Robb disbelieves the political account and is distressed by the missing sister. This is one **confirmed receipt** of the four letters coerced in `AGOT 51`; Lysa/Hoster/Catelyn receipts are not certified by this chapter.
7. **VERIFIED:** Bran recalls **Lady's bones actually returning to Winterfell**, accompanied by a household escort, and the grave in the old lichyard below the First Keep. Wolves begin howling before the bones reach the castle; **a symbolic or sensory bond is plausible**, mechanism unidentified.
8. **VERIFIED:** **Osha**, still shackled and forced to work kitchens, says she prays to the same old gods and tells Bran the south lacks weirwood 'eyes,' claiming the gods' reach diminishes there. She states that giants survive beyond the Wall and that people who leave northern fires sometimes return **as blue-eyed, black-handed wights**. She attributes these dangers to threats that Mance Rayder wrongly believes he can fight conventionally.
9. **VERIFIED:** Osha explicitly tells Bran to warn Robb he is marching **the wrong way**: his swords are needed **north rather than south**. This is an on-page warning, not an assertion that the Night's Watch's ancient builders designed the Wall to keep northmen inside. Luwin later dismisses Osha's warnings as wildling tales and instructs Bran not to trouble Robb, despite the reader's independent knowledge of supernatural peril from the prologue and `AGOT 52`.
10. **VERIFIED:** Robb nevertheless leads his assembled men toward the Riverlands **two days after** the godswood conversation. He leaves **Bran the acting lord in Winterfell**, with Luwin's counsel and responsibilities for Rickon. Theon and Greatjon ride beside Robb; Grey Wind goes with him.

## Theory tribunal: testable readings and rivals

**“Night's Watch looking the wrong direction” — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED IN A DIFFERENT SENSE.** The warning is a literal *reallocation of attention* from southern war to north-of-Wall survival, supported by earlier Other/wight sightings. **Not established:** that the Wall was originally built to imprison the North, that its ancient guards faced south, or that a pact reversed its mandate.

**Secret city under Winterfell — MATERIAL SUPPORT FOR SHELTER INFRASTRUCTURE, NOT A CITY.** The busy seasonal winter town, active underground hot-water source, castle cellars/crypts and adaptive routes make substantial refuge and infrastructure plausible. No character visits an extensive hidden underground population center.

**R+L=J and northern succession:** historic Stark–Karstark kinship plus old crypt ceremonies say little about Jon's actual parentage or future royal claimant. Robb's immediate known objective is rescuing Ned/helping Tullys, not altering succession.

**Operational tension:** Osha offers urgent intelligence that Luwin rejects. The chapter dramatizes how valid warnings can be disbelieved for understandable immediate reasons; the on-page source of the warnings remains Osha.
