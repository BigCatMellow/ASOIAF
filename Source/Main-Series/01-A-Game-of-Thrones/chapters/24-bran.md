## Plot spine

1. **VERIFIED:** Bran, paralyzed, watches Rickon play with three direwolves and mourns running, riding and the absent family. Robb exercises practical authority and has less time for his brothers; Rickon fears everyone will vanish.
2. **VERIFIED AS ATTRIBUTED STORY:** Old Nan recounts the Long Night, the Others attacking living beings and reanimating the dead, and the Last Hero seeking the children of the forest when armies fail. The tale ends mid-pursuit: **Luwin interrupts before Nan's version of any rescue or resolution is told in this chapter**.
3. **VERIFIED:** Hodor carries Bran downstairs to meet Tyrion, who is returning south from the Wall. Robb greets Tyrion with a drawn sword and suspicion, treating Night's Watchmen more hospitably.
4. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion asks Bran how he fell; Bran denies falling deliberately, and Luwin says Bran recalls neither the climb nor fall. Tyrion offers a custom saddle design and recommends starting with a green horse trained to voice and reins so Bran can ride despite paralysis.
5. **VERIFIED:** All three Stark direwolves converge threateningly on Tyrion despite his helpful proposal. Bran, Robb and Rickon can call their wolves off, though Shaggydog takes longest. Neither Tyrion's actual guilt nor the reason for this animal response is established.
6. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion declines Robb's belated hospitality and stays at the inn in the winter town. Later the Watch guests warn that Benjen has not returned from his ranging. Robb insists he lives, but his fate remains **UNKNOWN**.
7. **VERIFIED AS DREAM:** Bran fears climbing stonework, glimpses red-eyed gargoyles resembling lions, and wakes distressed when Hodor enters. That night Robb helps carry him to bed and, believing he is unseen, weeps. Bran reaches for his hand.

## Interpretive and theory challenges

**Others' forgotten treaty / 'Wall faces wrong way':** Old Nan describes a dreadful enemy and an attempt to seek aid from children of the forest, not a negotiated truce with Others. Her folklore is a **CHARACTER STORY** and the narrative interruption leaves open many possibilities; see [Others/Wall theory test](../../../../research/theories/cases/others-wall-pact.md).

**Bran's dream lion gargoyles:** a frightening and suggestive analogy, but independent waking recollection of the event is still absent. **Tyrion's kindness versus wolf hostility:** strong counterexample to any claim that direwolves always attack actual perpetrators and accept innocent allies.

**Practical hinge:** Tyrion's saddle may transform Bran's daily agency without healing his legs; the family cannot dismiss technology and dependence while projecting mystical power.
