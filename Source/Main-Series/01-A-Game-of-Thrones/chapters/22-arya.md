## Plot and decisions

1. **VERIFIED:** Ned joins a strained meal with Arya, Sansa and his retainers, reluctantly agrees to let both daughters attend the impending Hand's tourney. The sisters' conflict remains unresolved after Lady's death and Mycah's murder.
2. **VERIFIED:** Arya blames herself for Mycah because she invited him to practice. The guards' failure to intervene has undermined her former trust in Winterfell adults. She flees the table, barricades herself in her room and draws Needle.
3. **VERIFIED:** Ned privately visits. He discovers Needle is Mikken's work; Arya refuses to identify Jon as its giver, protecting him.
4. **VERIFIED:** Ned says Arya's willfulness and appearance remind him of Lyanna Stark, who might have wielded a sword if permitted. He warns that the same 'wolf blood' was associated in family memory with Brandon and Lyanna's early deaths.
5. **VERIFIED:** Ned explicitly tells Arya that Mycah's murder belongs to Sandor Clegane and the queen, not Arya. He also knows Arya did **not** merely lose Nymeria: she and Jory drove the direwolf off by throwing stones to save her from Cersei.
6. **VERIFIED:** Ned teaches that the Stark family must protect one another rather than quarrel in danger, returns Needle, and arranges personal instruction with **Syrio Forel**, former First Sword to the Sealord of Braavos. Arya begins formal left-handed water-dance instruction after three days.

## Character agency and alternative interpretations

**Ned:** shows more flexibility than a caricature of absolute rule-following, accepting protective deception and permitting sword training. **Arya:** learns physical technique but still carries guilt and anger; her family bond is under strain. **INFERRED:** her resilience may grow from real instruction and empathy for powerless people rather than innate 'chosen one' magic. **Counterevidence to easy prophecy:** resemblance to Lyanna is genealogical and thematic evidence, not proof she must reenact Lyanna's fate.
