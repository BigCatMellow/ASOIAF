## Causal sequence

1. **VERIFIED:** The royal party has occupied Ser Raymun Darry's castle during the search, four days after Arya disappears. Jory returns with Arya, but Lannister-controlled guards send her directly before Robert and Cersei without notifying Ned.
2. **VERIFIED:** Arya reports that Joffrey threatened Mycah; Joffrey counterclaims that Arya and Mycah assaulted him. Ned calls Sansa to corroborate, but she says she does not remember clearly. The reader has direct evidence from `AGOT 15` that Sansa witnessed the violence.
3. **VERIFIED:** Robert is initially willing to end the children's dispute with ordinary discipline. Cersei insists on punishing Nymeria; because the wolf has vanished, Cersei directs punishment onto innocent Lady instead. Robert permits this rather than confront his wife.
4. **VERIFIED:** Ned refuses to leave the killing to Ilyn Payne and personally executes Lady with Ice. He orders her body returned north for burial, refusing to let Cersei obtain her skin.
5. **VERIFIED:** Sandor Clegane returns with Mycah's corpse, which bears a devastating sword wound. He admits he pursued the fleeing boy and mocks his inability to escape. No judicial hearing for Mycah is shown.

## Information ledger and alternatives

**Reader:** knows Joffrey initiated violence against Mycah (AGOT 15); Mycah is dead and Lady was absent. **Ned:** believes Arya and has heard Sansa's earlier version, but cannot command the king. **Robert:** has conflicting reports yet approves a substitute death sentence. **Sansa:** sees her honest but incomplete testimony used in the collapse of her sister's credibility and the killing of her own wolf.

**Theory stress:** Lady's name and fate can support symbolic foreshadowing of Sansa losing an obedient feminine identity; **alternative:** direct moral tragedy and politically motivated scapegoating already explain the narrative force. Mycah's killing demonstrates Sandor's violence before any later redemptive trajectory; do not minimize it as an accidental consequence.
