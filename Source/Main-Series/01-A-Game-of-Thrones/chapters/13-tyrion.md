## Causal spine

1. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion travels north with Benjen, Jon, retainers and new Watch recruits. Yoren brings two imprisoned young men accused of rape. Jon sees how sharply this differs from his romantic picture of a noble brotherhood.
2. **VERIFIED:** The climate and geographic conditions become progressively harsher after leaving Winterfell, first near the wolfswood then beyond the farm settlements. The company carries ravens supplied by Luwin and extra horses for the Watch.
3. **VERIFIED:** On the eighteenth travel night, Tyrion reads a borrowed book on dragons and recalls the nineteen skulls previously kept beneath the Red Keep, including the small, deformed final hatchlings.
4. **VERIFIED:** Tyrion explains reading as his chief weapon and shares his early fascination with dragon power. He needles Jon about his family exclusion and the Watch's actual recruits until Jon becomes furious and Ghost knocks Tyrion down.
5. **VERIFIED:** Jon makes Tyrion ask politely for aid, then helps him; mutual humor reopens conversation. Jon accepts the harder truth about the Watch. Tyrion observes him alone on guard, staring into the campfire.

## POV contrasts and theory tests

**Tyrion knows:** Jon's status humiliation, his lack of realistic knowledge of Watch life, and books as possible self-empowerment. **Jon knows:** at last a painful approximation of the institution he is joining. **Reader knows:** supernatural danger north of the Wall from `AGOT Prologue`, making Tyrion's dismissal of grumkins/snarks and low danger dramatically ironic.

**Theory tests:** (a) Does a dragon's size track age, magic, husbandry, or policy? The skulls establish a pattern but no cause. (b) Is Ghost acting on Jon's feelings or under independent instinct? Compare later true wolf dreams. (c) Is Tyrion's attraction to dragonfire predictive of lineage? Ordinary psychological explanation is sufficient; lineage requires independent birth evidence.
