## Narrative with cause and effect

1. **VERIFIED:** After Littlefinger's offer Ned visits **Chataya's brothel** with Jory, Wyl and Heward. He meets a very young woman who is nursing Robert's infant daughter **Barra**. She describes Robert as attentive and asks Ned to deliver her loyalty and request for his return; Ned promises to provide for the child.
2. **VERIFIED/CHARACTER REPORT:** Littlefinger lists another known Baratheon bastard fostered at Storm's End, reportedly born to a Florent servant of Selyse at Stannis's wedding. He also relays an **unverified rumor** that Cersei killed twin bastards born at Casterly Rock and sold their mother into slavery.
3. **VERIFIED:** Ned sees the same black hair in infant Barra that he recalls in Robert's first daughter, connecting the original body of evidence: Gendry, royal bastards and the genealogy book. His thought of Jon Snow and the price of keeping Lyanna's promises recurs.
4. **VERIFIED:** Jaime Lannister ambushes Ned's escort in a city street, responding to Tyrion's arrest. Ned says Catelyn took Tyrion at his command. Littlefinger rides away saying he will fetch City Watch.
5. **VERIFIED:** Jaime chooses **not** to kill Ned because of risk to Tyrion's life but orders his men killed. Jory, Wyl and Heward die; Ned's horse slips, his leg breaks and he is left in the rain until Littlefinger and City Watch arrive. Pycelle treats him with milk of the poppy.

## Interpretation and opposing explanations

**Is Robert paternity proven for every black-haired bastard?** Gendry and Barra make a coherent pattern, yet parentage remains an inference from resemblance, mother's testimony and personal history; a genealogy strengthens it but is not genetics. **Is Petyr directly behind Jaime's assault?** The direct perpetrator/order is Jaime; any Petyr orchestration is **THEORY** without chain of orders. **Jaime's protective love:** he spares Ned, then kills Ned's men; both acts are central to later character assessment.
