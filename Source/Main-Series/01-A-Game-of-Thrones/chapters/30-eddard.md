## Major causal sequence

1. **VERIFIED:** Ned sees Ser Hugh of the Vale's body after the tourney. Hugh was Jon Arryn's squire for four years; Robert knighted him after Arryn's death, but the young man died before Ned could question him. Barristan personally kept vigil. **NED'S SUSPICION:** Gregor's lethal lance thrust may not have been accidental; proof is missing.
2. **VERIFIED:** Ned and Barristan persuade Robert not to enter the melee by pointing out that opponents would allow him to win. Robert's desire for genuine combat clashes with his power as king.
3. **VERIFIED/ROBERT'S ACCOUNT:** He regrets marrying Cersei at Jon Arryn's advice, admits Joffrey's lie about Arya and Mycah, fears Joffrey succeeding him, and dreams of abandoning his throne to sell his sword. His occasional insight does not remedy his inaction.
4. **VERIFIED:** Sandor Clegane defeats Jaime; Loras Tyrell then unhorses Gregor, whose stallion is visibly distressed by Loras's mare. Gregor kills his own horse and attacks Loras with live steel. Sandor intervenes, defends Loras and does not aim at Gregor's exposed face. Robert's shouted command and armed escort stop the fight.
5. **VERIFIED:** Loras yields the final match to Sandor, recognizing that he saved his life. Littlefinger **claims** Loras deliberately used a mare in heat to unsettle Gregor's stallion; whether Loras planned it is not established in his POV.
6. **VERIFIED:** Anguy, an unknown marcher, wins the archery purse but declines Ned's offer of service. Thoros of Myr wins the melee using a flaming sword that frightens horses. Arya reveals rigorous Syrio training, including eyes-covered movement and plans to catch cats.
7. **VERIFIED:** Varys visits Ned secretly and warns that Cersei deliberately provoked Robert toward the melee as a pretext for murder. **VARYS'S CLAIM:** Jon Arryn was given tears of Lys by someone close, probably Ser Hugh. Varys says the deadly secret was Arryn's habit of asking questions.

## Whose knowledge is limited?

Ned **witnesses** violent acts in the lists, but not Gregor's thoughts. He knows Robert's character well enough to prevent a disastrous melee yet places too much trust in the king's capacity to confront the queen. Varys has a plausible but unverified map of court plots. Ser Hugh is dead; no independent testimony from him on Arryn's illness exists.
