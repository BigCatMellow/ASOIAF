## Essential narrative

- **VERIFIED:** Jon attends Robert's feast below the high table, where he observes the royal family, Starks and Lannisters from an excluded position. **JON'S CLAIM:** Catelyn regarded seating a bastard among the royal children as an insult; the chapter does not give her private reasoning.
- **VERIFIED:** Jon quietly studies Cersei's disdain, Robert's changed body, Joffrey's hauteur and Jaime's regal appearance. His narration is observant but often colored by status anxiety and resentment.
- **VERIFIED:** Ghost remains unusually silent, protects food from a larger dog, and obeys Jon's early commands. These behaviors support a developing bond, not yet an on-page skinchanging event.
- **VERIFIED:** Benjen initially praises Jon's observation but warns that joining the Watch entails real sacrifice of relationships, family and possible children. Jon, fearing his future as a bastard, volunteers before understanding those costs.
- **VERIFIED:** Jon leaves in humiliation after declaring he will never father a bastard, and meets Tyrion outside. Tyrion offers an identity-based form of resilience while revealing his own sense of parental rejection.

## Specific signals

| Detail | Reason to preserve |

| Jon's separation from trueborn siblings | Visible foundation for longing for status and the attraction of the Watch; the hurt is genuine even if its political cause is narrated only by Jon |
| Jon watches faces | His skill at reading social signals makes him perceptive; it does not make every judgment correct |
| Jaime 'looks like a king' to Jon | A question about outward legitimacy and appearance, not an encoded claim that Jaime will be king |
| Joffrey's Lannister-like appearance | Narrative evidence to compare against the parentage investigation, but appearance alone does not prove incest |

| Young Dragon comparison | Jon likes Daeron's conquest; Benjen emphasizes the greater losses of trying to hold Dorne. Early critique of idealized martial heroism |
| Ghost's silent threat display | Wolf behavior becomes part of Jon's identity; later ADWD skinchanging rules supply context not yet available to Jon |
| Jon says he does not know his mother | Establishes a real informational absence, not a direct clue identifying her |
| Tyrion's sharp advice and final shadow | Both characters know how social judgments deform identity; the shadow's kingly size is symbolic, not destiny proof |
| Tyrion's improbable courtyard acrobatics | A concrete early physical depiction; avoid reconciling automatically with every later description of his disabilities |

## Interpretive stress test

**Simple reading:** Jon is an inherently dutiful, destined leader. **Challenge:** He is also proud, adolescent, reactive and tempted by an institution whose oath cost he minimizes. Benjen is not simply refusing him out of cruelty: he gives a concrete warning.

**Theory:** Ghost is a sign of hidden royal parentage. **Alternative:** ghostly coloration and silence primarily dramatize Jon's family exclusion. Later magic can add a different level without erasing the first.
