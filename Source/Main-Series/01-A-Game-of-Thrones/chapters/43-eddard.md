## Full political and causal sequence

1. **VERIFIED:** With Robert and many lords hunting a white hart, Ned sits the Iron Throne despite his broken leg. Petitioners from **Sherrer, Wendish Town and the Mummer's Ford** describe burning, rape and killing by mounted raiders. Knights Raymun Darry, Karyl Vance and Marq Piper bring testimony.
2. **VERIFIED AS WITNESS ACCOUNTS:** Survivors agree raiders were well-mounted, armed with martial steel, and burned crops and people rather than merely stealing supplies. Counts range from roughly fifty to a hundred or several hundred. A conspicuously enormous leader is described, leading Ned and Riverlords to identify **Gregor Clegane**; **the culprit is not narratively observed unmasked**.
3. **VERIFIED:** Edmure has called his banners, fortified the Golden Tooth approaches and dispersed responders to border villages; Hoster insisted Riverlords seek formal royal permission rather than immediately retaliate. Ned suspects the raiding is calculated to scatter Edmure's forces and make Riverlands look like aggressors if they retaliate.
4. **VERIFIED:** Pycelle urges the victims to appeal to Tywin, Gregor's liege, rather than ask the king to punish his wife's father's bannerman. Ned rejects that narrow jurisdiction and invokes Robert's broad justice.
5. **VERIFIED:** Young Loras volunteers to take revenge on Gregor after the Hand's tourney fight. Ned declines, distinguishes punitive **justice from personal vengeance**, and names **Beric Dondarrion** with **Thoros of Myr, Ser Gladden, Lord Lothar** and twenty men apiece, plus **twenty Stark guards**—a planned force of one hundred—to pursue Gregor under the king's banners.
6. **VERIFIED:** Ned publicly strips Gregor of titles and holdings and sentences him to death, commissioning Beric to enforce it. **NOT VERIFIED:** Gregor's capture, the force assembling in full or receipt of royal ratification.
7. **VERIFIED:** Varys privately points out Ned missed a political chance to make Tyrell allies by choosing Loras, and may have offended **Ilyn Payne**, the royal executioner. Ned confirms he deliberately avoided Payne because the Paynes are Lannister bannermen.

## Theory and strategic alternatives

**Tywin masterminded the raid:** Ned firmly believes so; material evidence strongly implicates Gregor, who serves Tywin. **Countertest:** need direct command, later admissions or observed logistics before identifying Tywin as orderer rather than permissive superior. **Loras as best leader?** His skill and Tyrell connection are valuable, but fury against Gregor may distort the assignment. **Beric as religious destiny:** here he receives an ordinary royal commission; later supernatural developments should not erase the secular chain of orders.
