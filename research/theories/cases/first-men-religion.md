# Drowned God worship as a survival of pre-Pact First Men beliefs

- Origin: [u/LothorBrune, original theory](https://www.reddit.com/r/asoiaf/comments/1oysxdx/)
- Recognition: **2025 Best New Theory joint winner**
- Status: `SOURCE AUDITED (FIVE NOVELS) / SUPPLEMENTARY HISTORY PENDING`
- Primary checked: ACOK 11; AFFC 1 and 19
- Other required source: [The World of Ice & Fire](../../../Source/The-World-of-Ice-and-Fire/NOTES.md) — **not audited yet**

## Claim

The Ironborn Drowned God / Storm God religious pairing may preserve an older coastal system shared by early First Men, before the Pact and the diffusion of weirwood worship. Parallel sea/storm divinities in the Sisters and Stormlands are proposed as fossil traces of that older faith.

## Verified five-book passages

| Locator | Excerpt | Narrowly supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ACOK 11 (Theon)** | “the flame the Drowned God brought from the sea” | A god-centered myth rooted in seawater and conquest, not weirwood practice. |
| **AFFC 1 (The Prophet / Aeron)** | “For a thousand thousand years sea and sky had been at war.” | The Ironborn themselves frame natural antagonism as sacred ancient history. |
| **AFFC 19 (The Drowned Man / Aeron)** | “We were born from the sea” | Mythic continuity in Ironborn self-description. |

## Proposed causal history (theory only)

Early First Men carry sea/sky religious traditions; mainland branches merge with the children of the forest's tree worship; the isolated Iron Islands retain a stronger coastal pantheon. Similar gods among the Sistermen or Stormlanders might be survivals, not independent inventions.

## Strong objections

1. Gods of sea and storm occur independently in many cultures; motifs alone cannot establish a single origin.
2. The Ironborn's own written history is fragmentary and often self-mythologizing.
3. The strongest mainland parallels belong in *The World of Ice & Fire*; **until that source receives its own audit, the cross-regional linkage is unverified here**.
4. Even if the historical derivation is correct, it says nothing about whether the gods literally exist.

## Relevance and disposition

**Worldbuilding payoff:** Offers several competing versions of the Long Night and Old Gods' emergence without revealing one supposedly infallible origin story. Use Sam, Bran, maesters and oral traditions as **separate narrators**, not as a single explanatory lecture.
