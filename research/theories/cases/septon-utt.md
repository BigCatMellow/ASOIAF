# Septon Utt may have killed the unnamed Harrenhal boy

- Origin: [u/dblack246, 2025 original discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/asoiaf/comments/1n5yog5/)
- Recognition: **2025 Best New Theory joint winner**; Citadel nominee; Old Nan nominee
- Status: `SOURCE AUDITED / PLAUSIBLE, UNPROVEN`
- Evidence: Five-book EPUB checked 2026-10-14, ACOK 47 & 64; ASOS 39

## Claim and mechanism

An otherwise unexplained young servant's death during the Bloody Mummers' capture of Harrenhal was not an accidental battle casualty: Septon Utt may have exploited the confusion to abduct and kill the boy. This is a small-scale hidden-crime theory, **not** a prediction of the final books.

## Passage evidence

| Primary passage | Short original text (verified) | Inference and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ACOK 47 (Arya)** | “one boy who'd been killed in the fighting for no reason” | Text draws attention to an otherwise unexplained death; battle chaos could explain it without murder. |
| **ACOK 64 (Arya)** | “Septon Utt likes little boys” | A character reports Utt's predilection; he is present among the Mummers, but the line doesn't identify this boy. |
| **ASOS 39 (Arya)** | “he'd killed his latest boy” | Arya remembers a repeated pattern of killing; the Brotherhood tries and hangs Utt. |

## Challenge

Victims were killed indiscriminately during the takeover, and the story offers no eyewitness connecting Utt to this specific death. No narrated confession or unique physical clue survives. The hypothesis neatly explains a conspicuous line, but it must not be upgraded to `VERIFIED`.

## Gardening relevance

A precedent for perpetrators using wartime cover rather than acting on clear military orders. Valuable for interpreting the Brotherhood and Stoneheart: a just cause does not reliably produce just agents.

**Disposition:** `PLAUSIBLE / NO PLOT DEPENDENCY`. If used, preserve the original ambiguity.
