## Competing hypotheses

**H1:** Aegon is the genuine infant son of Rhaegar and Elia, smuggled away and raised by allies. **H2:** Aegon descends from the rival Blackfyre line, with Illyrio and Varys promoting him as a Targaryen. **H3:** Aegon is neither, but his political identity becomes effective through belief, institutional support and conquest.

## Why it can work

The Blackfyre history explains why a company once associated with rival royal claimants might abruptly return to the Seven Kingdoms. Illyrio's unusual investment in Aegon and Varys's manipulations invite scrutiny. A public 'mummer's dragon' could plausibly gain acceptance despite disputed origin.

## Against certainty

- The text never shows a verified pedigree or confession.
- Golden Company officers express ordinary ambitions—lands, pay, returning home—as well as dynastic rhetoric.
- Connington believes Aegon to be Rhaegar's son, and his belief has narrative consequences regardless of biology.
- Prophetic imagery is underdetermined; 'cloth' need not equal 'Blackfyre'.

## Gardening outcome

Make **legitimacy** a question of conflicting evidence, affection and public perception. Do not precommit to an expositional reveal or an intrinsically evil Aegon. A sympathetic claimant can still provoke a disastrous dynastic war.

**Disposition:** `PLAUSIBLE / IDENTITY UNKNOWN`.
