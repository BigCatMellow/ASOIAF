## Three **different** possible historical claims

**H1 — Defensive Wall, hostile Others:** Traditional story: the Others threatened mankind and the children of the forest; humans and children cooperated to resist and later built a barrier. Supported **as a legend**, not through direct observation of construction.

**H2 — Mutual boundary/treaty:** The Wall was part of an arrangement after the Long Night that divided domains or regulated contact. Offers a possible reason why magical passages and rites exist. **No five-book direct treaty or Other representative confirms it.**

**H3 — Wall built by Others to pen people north for sacrificial reproduction:** Stronger 2026 subreddit proposal. It infers from Craster's sons and Watch history; **no five-book account says the Others built the Wall**. Must explain why the Black Gate responds to Watch oaths and why Craster's apparent bargain is local rather than universal.

## Arguments **against** making a pact the automatic endgame

1. The Others attack and kill living humans in scenes; no dialogue or negotiation is shown. An agreement requires establishing **contact, incentives and enforcement**.
2. Craster's grotesque transaction may be an individual accommodation with a dangerous power. It cannot be assumed to govern the entire north.
3. “The sons become Others” relies on one old wife's explanation; the five books never directly depict the transformation.
4. The children's pact with the **First Men** is an older and distinct tradition. It is not evidence of a treaty **with the Others**.
5. The Black Gate appears responsive to *Night's Watch* vows, a challenge to a simple “Others built prison” thesis.
6. The idea that the Watch's *only* purpose was to prevent northern people escaping does not fit its demonstrated efforts to fight supernatural incursions, though institutions' duties could have changed.
7. The TV show depicts the Others differently. Those revelations do not settle book origins.

## Alternative story experiments (no branch selected)

- **Military defense plus incomplete history:** Sam/Bran recover better tools and forgotten events while Jon's political diplomacy among the living matters; the Others may remain implacable.
- **Diplomatic possibility with terrible cost:** Bran finds evidence of an earlier compromise; a potential negotiation is tested against the Others' actual destructive behavior and the wishes of wildlings who would be sacrificed or displaced.
- **False treaty revelation:** A character mistakes a **First Men–children** treaty, or Craster's local arrangement, for an Others pact; later evidence contradicts it. This can be a truthful character error rather than a writerly trick.

## New direct-source stress test: AGOT 48, 52 and 53 (2026-10-15)

The [sequential close-reading files](../../../Source/Main-Series/CLOSE-READING-STATUS.md) now give chapter-local checks for an especially important distinction: **Osha says Robb is marching the wrong way; she does not say the Night's Watch was historically created to guard the wrong direction.**

- **[AGOT 48 (Jon)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/48-jon.md) — VERIFIED:** Jon and Sam swear vows after passing through a triple-barred ice tunnel to a grove of nine weirwoods **north of the Wall**. They explicitly pledge to be the shield protecting the realms of men. This documents the *current Watch's vowed function*, without answering who constructed the Wall or what its earliest rules were.
- **[AGOT 52 (Jon)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/52-jon.md) — VERIFIED:** Othor and Jafer, missing from Benjen's ranging, are found with unusual corpse characteristics; **Othor later moves and attacks inside Castle Black despite fatal wounds**. His severed arm also moves. **An animated dead human is directly witnessed**, independently confirming that the north-of-Wall threat extends to the Watch itself. Who animated him remains **UNKNOWN**. Earlier rangers' choice to bring unburned bodies through the gate created a defensive breach.
- **[AGOT 53 (Bran)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/53-bran.md) — CHARACTER CLAIM:** Osha says the old gods have no eyes in the south where weirwoods are gone; says giants and wights exist north of the Wall; and explicitly asks Bran to tell Robb to march **north instead of south**. Bran reports her warning to Luwin, who discounts it. The reader has strong independent corroboration of *wights*, but not Osha's broader geography of divine power, giants or her description of Mance's precise calculations at this stage.

**Updated disposition for the 'wrong direction' phrase:** `STRONG SOURCE SUPPORT` for the **strategic attention critique** (war in the south distracts from an urgent northern supernatural threat). `NO NEW DIRECT SUPPORT` for an ancient **south-facing Watch mandate**, a **Wall built by Others**, or a comprehensive **Others-human treaty**. The latter remain distinct historical conjectures with demanding counterevidence requirements.

**Disconfirming tests:** Look for primary-text historical records of the Watch's founding; inspect the Black Gate and oath mechanics; find an independent witness/vision of Wall construction and actual Others' motives. Do not infer original design from which direction Robb should march *now*.

## Additional full-AGOT evidence: the *actual* Pact vs the Wall's origin (2026-10-15)

- **[AGOT 66 (Bran)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/66-bran.md):** Maester Luwin expressly describes a **First Men–children of the forest Pact** on the **Isle of Faces**, a division of land, an end to cutting weirwoods, and green men to watch the site. This is `CHARACTER HISTORICAL TESTIMONY`, **not** evidence of an agreed boundary with Others. He narrates the pact *before* the later Long Night; equating those two traditions is a source-level conflation.
- **[AGOT 70 (Jon)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/70-jon.md):** Mormont says **First Men built the Wall** and reminds Jon that dead men beyond it make southern crown disputes a secondary threat. Both are `CHARACTER CLAIM / HISTORICAL TRADITION` and current strategic argument, respectively. The First Men attribution is not an eyewitness construction record.
- **[AGOT 52 (Jon)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/52-jon.md)** plus **[AGOT 60 (Jon)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/60-jon.md)** establishes **two reanimated rangers**, not one: Othor directly attacks Mormont, and **Jafer Flowers is reported to kill Ser Jaremy Rykker and four more**. This is difficult for any theory insisting the northern undead danger is a misunderstanding of ordinary human threat.
- **[AGOT 53 (Bran)](../../../Source/Main-Series/01-A-Game-of-Thrones/chapters/53-bran.md)** adds Osha's **different** proposition: Robb should march north rather than south; it concerns political priorities now, **not** the founding oath's direction.

**Disposition unchanged:** the strategic warning is well supported; a negotiated pact with Others remains open conjecture, and an Others-built south-facing prison Wall is weak absent an independently grounded origin account.

## Disposition

`VERIFIED`: the threat and specific magical interactions. `STRONG HISTORICAL INFERENCE`: humanity and children shared ancient danger, but this is preserved in legend. `OPEN`: Others' origins and negotiability. `WEAK AS CURRENT CLAIM`: Others-built-human-prison Wall. Do not let the emotionally attractive 'balance of ice and fire' idea override contrary scenes.
