# Rhaegar + Lyanna = Jon (R+L=J)

- Origin: classic community theory, initial proposer `UNKNOWN`
- Status: `SOURCE AUDITED / VERY STRONG INFERENCE, NOT FIVE-BOOK REVELATION`
- Checked: AGOT 4, 12, 47, 58; ACOK 48; ASOS 24

## Claim

Jon Snow may be the son of Lyanna Stark and Rhaegar Targaryen; Ned concealed the child's birth and accepted the dishonor of being seen as Jon's father to protect him.

## Primary source anchors

| Locator | Short verified fragment | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AGOT 4 (Eddard)** | “Promise me, Ned.” | Ned's memory of Lyanna's death is burdened by a mysterious promise. |
| **AGOT 58 (Eddard)** | “bed of blood” | The setting of Lyanna's death suggests childbirth to many readers; not explicit proof. |
| **ACOK 48 (Daenerys)** | “A blue flower grew from a chink in a wall of ice.” | Blue flowers evocative of Lyanna are juxtaposed with the Wall and Jon's setting. |
| **ASOS 24 (Bran)** | The Knight of the Laughing Tree tale points back to Lyanna and Harrenhal. | Supports re-reading the political/romantic events around the tourney. |

## Contra and uncertainty

No fifth-book POV definitively identifies Jon's biological parents. Jon himself does not know. Lyanna's promise might involve other responsibilities; prophetic symbols can be interpreted many ways. Jon's *legal legitimacy* also does not follow from biological descent without proof of a valid marriage, annulment or comparable political recognition.

## Story relevance

The revelation should challenge Jon's lived loyalties, not grant instant kingly competence or erase his identity as a Stark. His status must interact with Aegon's competing claim and northern political needs.
