## Three major causal chains

### 1. Bran sees a secret → crime → concern over his survival → second attack

| Source | What is actually shown | Who knows / what remains unproven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AGOT 8 (Bran)** | Bran sees Cersei and Jaime together, hears discussion of Ned/Lysa and the succession; Jaime **deliberately** pushes him from the tower after pulling him up from the ledge. | Reader directly witnesses Jaime's act; Ned, Catelyn, Tyrion, Joffrey and Robert are **not** shown witnessing it. No separate catspaw order is issued in this scene. |
| **AGOT 9 (Tyrion)** | Tyrion hears that Bran might live and notices a telling glance between the twins. He discusses a maester's report that Bran's heartbeat changes when the wolf can be heard through an open window. | Tyrion has **grounds to suspect** a secret, not direct proof of the push or its motive. The heartbeat report is **mediated testimony**, not a controlled magical experiment. |
| **AGOT 10 (Jon)** | Jon visits the still-comatose Bran; Catelyn, exhausted with grief, cruelly wishes Jon had been injured instead. | Jon knows pain, not the cause of Bran's fall; Jon also **conceals** Catelyn's words from Robb, creating a small but real knowledge gap. |
| **AGOT 14 (Catelyn)** | A library fire draws Robb/guards away; a paid-looking stranger attacks Bran, is surprised Catelyn is still there and claims death would be a mercy; Bran's wolf kills him. Ninety silver stags and a Valyrian-steel/dragonbone dagger are recovered. | **Catelyn infers** the fire was intentionally set as a diversion and fears Bran knows a dangerous secret. The stranger's actual **employer and the dagger's previous owners are UNKNOWN**. No witness sees him ignite the library fire. |
| **AGOT 18 (Catelyn)** | Varys knows Rodrik approached Aron Santagar about a knife. Littlefinger takes it and asserts it was once his, lost in a jousting bet which Tyrion won. | The statement establishes **Littlefinger's story**, not authenticated ownership or a contract with the killer. Varys's access can be explained by men watching Santagar and Rodrik; supernatural omniscience not required. |

**Important distinction:** *Reader knowledge that Jaime pushed Bran* and *Catelyn's later guess that Jaime pushed Bran* must not be confused with independent physical proof **of who hired the catspaw**. The particular dagger can pass through multiple hands. Neither Jaime's AGOT 9 remarks about mercy nor Littlefinger's AGOT 18 tale fills the missing procurement chain.

### 2. A child is threatened → wolves intervene → competing testimony → unequal punishment

| Source | Verified incident | Reliability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AGOT 7 (Arya)** | Joffrey seeks **live steel** against Robb, Rodrik refuses, and Sandor backs the prince's bravado. Theon restrains angry Robb. | Early evidence of Joffrey's prestige-based aggression, not the Trident event itself. |
| **AGOT 15 (Sansa)** | Sansa narrates Joffrey threatening unarmed Mycah with steel. Arya's defense leads to Joffrey pursuing her with a live blade; Nymeria bites him; Arya calls Nymeria away and throws **Lion's Tooth** into the river. | Sansa saw the encounter; she had drunk more wine than usual and is frightened, but it is incorrect to say she saw nothing. Joffrey's wound is real without making his version accurate. |
| **AGOT 16 (Eddard)** | Arya is found after four days; at the king's hearing Sansa says she cannot remember, so Ned loses corroboration. Cersei demands Nymeria, then targets **Lady**, who was not at the fight. Robert permits the punishment; Ned kills Lady himself and sends her remains north. Sandor brings back Mycah's mutilated body. | What Sansa says in the hearing is demonstrably **incomplete relative to AGOT 15**; her exact interior reasoning at that moment is not given by Ned's POV. The Hound killed Mycah, but the actual chase is not witnessed. |

The actor-level choices, not a single mastermind, generate the catastrophe: Sansa and Joffrey leave both protective animals behind; Joffrey abuses a common child; Arya and Nymeria intervene; Sansa withholds useful testimony; Robert avoids resisting Cersei; Ned performs an unjust execution while choosing its least humiliating means; Sandor follows the hunt for Mycah.

**Trident worldbuilding:** Sansa records a **twelve-day transit of the Neck** and recalls Arya's rough outdoor friendships. Arya and Mycah plan to search for **Rhaegar's scattered rubies**; the chapter does **not** show any found. Joffrey's sword *Lion's Tooth* disappears into the Trident. These are separate material trails, not proof the ruby legend is a future treasure map.

### 3. Political information moves through agents, sea lanes, and self-interested memories

- **AGOT 11 (Daenerys):** Illyrio's supposed petrified eggs are given to Dany; Jorah acts as a sworn exile adviser; Drogo's pledged invasion has no guaranteed schedule. Dany's initial control over her silver filly is genuine but doesn't eliminate forced marriage, slavery or Viserys's threats.
- **AGOT 12 (Eddard):** Robert identifies **Jorah → Varys → royal rider → Robert** as the intelligence route reporting Daenerys's marriage. He contemplates child assassination but **does not issue an order in this narrated conversation**. Ned gives Robert *Wylla* as the name of Jon's mother; this is **Ned's claim**, not direct biological verification.
- **AGOT 14 (Catelyn):** Catelyn orders an alternate route by **White Knife → White Harbor → sea**, choosing secrecy over an obvious large escort. The true urgency is a dagger-and-assassin investigation, not official Crown business.
- **AGOT 18 (Catelyn):** Captain Moreo Tumitis brings them aboard the **Storm Dancer**, a sixty-oared Tyroshi galley. Despite travel precautions, Varys's watchers learn enough from Rodrik's visit to Santagar to identify the investigation. Catelyn's initial guess that the captain sold her location is plausible but **not the only established information route**. Littlefinger recounts his earlier injury in Brandon Stark's duel *through Catelyn's recollection*, providing personal incentives without proving a specific present plot.

## Geography / information hazards

| Claim a future writer might make | Current evidence status |
| --- | --- |
| Hidden tunnels and ancient passages cross Winterfell | **VERIFIED narrow fact:** AGOT 8 describes a route within the inner wall and non-level tower bridges. The claim of an extensive inhabited underground city remains **THEORY**. |
| The wolf warned Bran before the fall | **VERIFIED howling** in AGOT 8. Precognition or greenseer intervention **UNKNOWN**. |
| The fire was an effort to destroy a specific rare scroll | AGOT 9 names fragile Valyrian scrolls and *Ayrmidon's Engines of War*; AGOT 14 shows the **library burning**. The idea of targeted knowledge destruction is **UNPROVEN**; diversion is Cat's stronger inference. |
| Arya understood the Faceless Men's purpose from the beginning | AGOT 7/10/15 show ordinary childhood interests, sibling loyalty, practical learning and resistance to injustice. Later Braavos training shouldn't retroactively make these scenes covert initiation. |
| The true owner of the catspaw dagger is Tyrion | **CHARACTER CLAIM only** by Littlefinger at AGOT 18. Need independent chain of possession, wagers and motive. |
| Bran's dream in AGOT 17 proves the crow is Bloodraven | **NOT VERIFIED**; the crow claims to teach him flight and the dream contains seemingly prophetic images. Later identification must be separately checked. |
| Every image Bran sees while falling is contemporary reality | **NOT VERIFIED.** AGOT 17's Catelyn, Stark children, Jon, eastern dragons and shadowed figures require later confirmation; a dream can mix memory, distance, symbolism and possible foresight. |
| Bran's dream shows no lasting injury | **DISPROVED:** he wakes, feels no sensation in his legs, and names his wolf **Summer**. |
| Lysa's coded accusation and Jaime/Cersei's fear prove Jon Arryn's killer | **UNPROVEN**; AGOT 6/Lysa's message and AGOT 8/twins' dialogue are accounts about different knowledge and dangers. |

## One-scene agency tests: THINK before PLAN

1. **Catelyn + a dagger:** What evidence could she honestly give Ned *without* importing the reader's direct knowledge of Jaime? What does she risk by entrusting it to Littlefinger and Varys?
2. **Sansa + a prince:** Can the mismatch between his private cruelty and public gallantry be recognized without a supportive adult or control of the narrative? Which later act forces her to reconsider?
3. **Bran + extraordinary sight:** If a dream seems to reveal distant events, what can the waking, paralyzed child test? Don't assume the visions give him actionable proofs or permission to possess Hodor later.
4. **Jon and Arya's independent loyalties:** Needle, their farewell and their exclusion by status/gender are grounded. Their later reunification is neither guaranteed nor precluded.
