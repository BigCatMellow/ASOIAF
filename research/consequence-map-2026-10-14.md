## Rule

For each pending decision, ask **what the character knows**, **what they want**, **who bears the cost**, and **what new problem their choice creates**. A future chapter can invalidate a branch. Do not travel faster, share information automatically or assert prophecy fulfillment just to make a preferred theory work.

## THINK: major risks to the architecture

1. **Dorne timing shortcut:** Quentyn's fate cannot instantly change Arianne's diplomacy unless the news has a plausible courier route.
2. **North legal shortcut:** A signed will cannot decide a kingdom while claimant survival, legal authority and surviving supporters remain contested.
3. **Oldtown conspiracy shortcut:** Marwyn's declaration doesn't entitle us to turn every maester into a secret antagonist.
4. **Supernatural shortcut:** Naming a pact doesn't invent an Other who can honor it; any negotiation must earn communication, incentives and enforcement.

## Two book-sized pressures, not endings

**Winds:** information diverges further. Military crises produce contradictory claimant news, the Citadel initially receives warnings through Sam, and the Others' attacks continue regardless of how scholars interpret ancient legends. Political recovery remains local, provisional and uneven.

**Dream:** only after characters' decisions accumulate should the story test whether alliances, succession settlements and ancient knowledge permit coordinated survival. The destination (treaty, elimination, retreat, or renewed defense) is explicitly **open**.
