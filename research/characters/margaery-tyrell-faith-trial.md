## Final five-book condition

**Margaery is accused under the Faith's custody/jurisdiction, with a powerful father seeking a public vindication, but the text does not narrate her own testimony, a trial, or a verdict.** Her legal position is inseparable from the safety of Tommen and the Tyrell/Lannister balance, yet it cannot be collapsed into either Cersei's account of the accusations or Mace's confidence in his daughter's innocence.

## What the principal actors know and want

- **Margaery:** Her direct private reasoning is withheld in the endpoint material. Her declared procedural preference reaches the reader through Mace, so retain it as a character report. Her practical stakes include liberty, reputation, her cousins, marriage to Tommen, and the political value of a public acquittal.
- **Mace Tyrell:** Wants a visible vindication and resists any settlement that leaves lasting public suspicion. His leverage includes Tyrell guards and broader family power, but he does not control the Faith's court or the murder investigation that follows.
- **High Sparrow / Faith:** Holds the accused and has armed/institutional authority. Its final evidentiary judgment, internal divisions, and response to the court crisis are not shown.
- **Cersei:** Created a false allegation against Margaery but is then imprisoned on her own charges. Her fear that Margaery benefits from a Faith trial is interpretation, not evidence of Margaery's guilt or secret influence.

## Immediate decision gates

1. Someone must set or preserve the trial procedure after Kevan's death; a Mace preference is not a completed hearing.
2. Witness testimony must be tested despite the Blue Bard's coercion and Osney's conflicting incentives.
3. The Faith, Tyrells, and provisional royal authority must decide whether security, the murders, Tommen's position, or the trials take priority.
4. A public result requires a credible audience and political acceptance; even a legal result need not erase rumor or settle the Lannister-Tyrell conflict.

## Guardrails for theory and adaptation comparison

- Do not use Margaery's presumed innocence as proof that every Tyrell actor is reliable, or use Cersei's manufactured case as proof of any later plot against the Faith.
- Do not assume a television trial, Sept catastrophe, imprisonment outcome, or death supplies the book result. Any comparable book sequence must first cross custody, procedure, security, and causation gates.
- A plausible continuation can give Margaery agency, but must not invent a private decision, secret bargain, or command of Tyrell forces without a source-grounded opportunity for it.
