## Personal obligations and contradictions

**Sam:** A frightened but resourceful observer who has killed an Other with dragonglass, protected Gilly, and brought information others urgently need. He promises help and schooling while worried about the baby he helped take away from its mother. Service to the Watch and service to particular people may become incompatible.

**Gilly:** Caring for the baby she is holding (Dalla's son after Jon's exchange), while her own son is at the Wall. Her grief is not merely Sam's supporting motivation. Any continuation should preserve her actual choices about family and displacement.

**Alleras:** Guides Sam toward a sympathetic archmaester and may be Sarella Sand; the identity is a strong reader inference but no five-book scene unmasks Alleras. Sam cannot assume a confirmed Dornish spy mission.

**Marwyn:** Possesses knowledge and a burning candle; rejects many colleagues' views. He is neither a guaranteed honest guide nor an all-powerful mage. His decision to leave abandons Sam to negotiate with the institution he distrusts.

## Near-term branching decisions

1. **Present Jon/Aemon's warning to the formal Citadel:** Sam can pursue official action at risk of disbelief or exposure to hostile internal politics.
2. **Work through Alleras/Marwyn's trusted circle:** Protects some secrets but may delay help to the Watch and increase dependence on an uncertain ally.
3. **Prioritize threatened civilians, including Gilly, as Oldtown faces naval danger:** The duty to gather lore could collide with immediate survival; neither option cleanly absolves Sam.

**Counterexample to magical omnipotence:** One burning glass candle does not yield a verified, safe long-range communication service usable by Sam on demand. The city faces concrete naval logistics regardless of sorcery.
