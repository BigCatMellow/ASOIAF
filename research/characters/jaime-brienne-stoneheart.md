## Central problem

Brienne swore to protect Catelyn's daughters and has come to trust Jaime, but Catelyn has returned as a vengeful and physically altered Lady Stoneheart. Jaime's deliberate efforts to honor some promises coexist with threats and moral compromise. Neither a clean redemption nor an immediate execution is established.

## Character dilemmas

**Brienne:** If she refuses Stoneheart, Podrick and Hyle may die; if she lures Jaime, she betrays his trust and her own sense of honor. The original Catelyn's oath is being weaponized by the resurrected Catelyn.

**Jaime:** Can he honor his promise to Catelyn without denying the wrongs his family profited from? His threat to Edmure is direct evidence that his methods are still morally compromised.

**Stoneheart:** She retains Catelyn's memories and targets Frey/Lannister participants, but is not the same acting mother whom Brienne once swore to serve. Revenge may destroy the ethical basis of her former cause.

## Three gardener branches

1. **Testimony before execution:** Stoneheart forces Jaime to confront his and the Lannisters' roles; Brienne's divided testimony matters.
2. **Coerced raid:** Brotherhood demands Jaime/Brienne assist in a Frey operation, forcing choices about bystanders.
3. **Brotherhood fracture:** Thoros or other companions challenge Stoneheart's punishments; Brienne must decide whom to protect without any unbroken oath.

**Do not select a branch from show events.** Establish who has physical control of Podrick, Hyle, Jaime and Brienne after ADWD 48.

## Counterevidence

- Jaime has improved in some relationships but still uses threats against innocents.
- Brienne's cry does not itself prove she accepted any full assassination plan.
- Stoneheart need not be universally obeyed by every Brotherhood member forever.
- The 'second Red Wedding' is an interesting possible operation, not an announced plan in the five books.
