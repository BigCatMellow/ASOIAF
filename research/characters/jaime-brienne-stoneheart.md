## Central problem

Brienne swore to protect Catelyn's daughters and has come to trust Jaime, but Catelyn has returned as a vengeful and physically altered Lady Stoneheart. Jaime's deliberate efforts to honor some promises coexist with threats and moral compromise. Neither a clean redemption nor an immediate execution is established.

### Cross-book correction: Jaime has a name, not the identity (2026-10-17)

The [specialist Jaime/Stoneheart rumor and Hound-identity dossier](../synthesis/jaime-stoneheart-rumor-to-brienne-pennytree-information-gates.md) distinguishes **three actual Jaime receptions**: **AFFC 30 (Jaime)** Mariya reports a scarred hooded woman leading Oldstones outlaws; **AFFC 44** Walder Rivers offers **Stoneheart** as a possible Ryman killer; **ADWD 48** Jaime himself invokes **Stoneheart** when threatening Tytos Blackwood over sheltering rebels. **Jaime is not shown knowing she is Catelyn Stark**. Conversely **AFFC 42 (Brienne)** Brienne sees Catelyn alive and hears Thoros's Beric explanation, and she sees **Lem wearing Rorge's inherited Hound helm**. The **ADWD 48** line that “the Hound” will kill “the girl” is therefore **not authenticated proof that Sandor holds Sansa**, nor proof Stoneheart authored those precise words. Brienne's **unidentified** scream at the gallows does not certify a particular negotiated bargain; **Jaime's agreement, departure or captivity are not narrated**. These constraints refine the existing character branches without choosing one.

### Stoneheart's exact accusation rests on a taunt with a separately witnessed origin

The [Stoneheart hearing and Jaime/Red Wedding evidence comparison](../synthesis/stoneheart-jaime-red-wedding-attribution-evidence-and-hearing-audit.md) traces **Catelyn directly hearing Jaime's name spoken by Robb's killer (ASOS 51)** versus **Jaime uttering an ordinary reciprocal farewell to Roose (ASOS 44)** and **hearing Red Wedding news for the first time in his narrated journey at Brindlewood with Brienne (ASOS 62)**. **ASOS Epilogue Merrett** is useful but he **only speaks** Walder/Ryman/Roose/Lothar/Bastard Walder's roles to the outlaws and Stoneheart; his private recollection of planning is **not itself conveyed** and he doesn't mention Jaime. **AFFC 42 Brienne already defends Jaime**, with no visible success. **ASOS 72 (Jaime)** is the strongest omitted corroboration: Jaime **expressly tells Brienne to rescue Sansa before Cersei kills her**, refuting Lem's claim that a Lannister twin would never hide Sansa from her, but that private conversation **is not proven reported to Stoneheart**. Guilt for direct massacre planning, Lannister political gains from the killings, and Jaime's incomplete daughter-return oath are **different allegations**. Any next choice branch must preserve the still-unanswered ADWD 48 Pennytree invitation.

## Character dilemmas

**Brienne:** If she refuses Stoneheart, Podrick and Hyle may die; if she lures Jaime, she betrays his trust and her own sense of honor. The original Catelyn's oath is being weaponized by the resurrected Catelyn.

**Jaime:** Can he honor his promise to Catelyn without denying the wrongs his family profited from? His threat to Edmure is direct evidence that his methods are still morally compromised.

**Stoneheart:** She retains Catelyn's memories and targets Frey/Lannister participants, but is not the same acting mother whom Brienne once swore to serve. Revenge may destroy the ethical basis of her former cause.

## Three gardener branches

1. **Testimony before execution:** Stoneheart forces Jaime to confront his and the Lannisters' roles; Brienne's divided testimony matters.
2. **Coerced raid:** Brotherhood demands Jaime/Brienne assist in a Frey operation, forcing choices about bystanders.
3. **Brotherhood fracture:** Thoros or other companions challenge Stoneheart's punishments; Brienne must decide whom to protect without any unbroken oath.

**Do not select a branch from show events.** Establish who has physical control of Podrick, Hyle, Jaime and Brienne after ADWD 48.

## Counterevidence

- Jaime has improved in some relationships but still uses threats against innocents.
- Brienne's cry does not itself prove she accepted any full assassination plan.
- Stoneheart need not be universally obeyed by every Brotherhood member forever.
- The 'second Red Wedding' is an interesting possible operation, not an announced plan in the five books.

### The exact point where the continuation begins

**Last narrated moment:** Pennytree, nighttime, Jaime and his escort present, Brienne newly returned, injured and offering a specific private journey. **We do not know whether Jaime accepts the invitation.**

- **Route A — He trusts Brienne:** Jaime risks ambush and his soldiers' safety. Her conflicting loyalties may force a confession or an act of self-sacrifice, but no surrender into Stoneheart's power has yet occurred in the novels.
- **Route B — He questions the story:** Jaime notices the implausible claim about Sansa, Brienne's bite, and the sudden need for isolation. He can demand proof, offer escorts, insist on Podrick's safety or refuse. These are feasible actions, not proof he detects a lie.
- **Route C — He tries to help without accompanying her:** This could protect Jaime but might condemn the hostages Brienne sought to save. She faces the renewed cost of misleading him.

**Brienne's 'Hound' statement is doubly uncertain:** She has encountered Lem using Sandor's helm; the name is now transferable. She may be lying about Sansa to satisfy Stoneheart's ultimatum, yet that does not mean every part of the warning is necessarily invented.

### Jaime's deeper contradiction

**AFFC 44 ↔ ADWD 48:** Jaime aims to end destructive conflict and refuses an unnecessary assault on villagers, yet he threatens innocents, collects noble children as hostages, and invokes his father's annihilation of rebellious houses during a conversation with Hoster Blackwood. Hoster observes that slaughter creates new generations of enemies. A Stoneheart encounter should **test this incomplete moral change**, not simply erase his past because he once saved Brienne.

### Brotherhood is a faction, not a single mind

- Stoneheart explicitly orders executions.
- Lem eagerly enforces brutal punishment under a stolen terror-symbol.
- Thoros expresses disillusionment with their loss of mercy.
- Brienne's awareness of Catelyn's former identity collides with a changed leader who regards Jaime as an enemy.

These differing perspectives create room for negotiation, dissent, escape, sacrifice, or division. They do **not** establish an impending rebellion, a 'Red Wedding 2.0' plan or the Brotherhood's exact future ranks.

### Immediate cross-story leverage

**Red Ronnet Connington** is connected to Jaime through former Gregor Clegane retainers (ADWD Epilogue), while Jon Connington holds Ronnet's relatives at Griffin's Roost (ADWD 61). This provides a real but slow communication/hostage bridge to Aegon's invasion, *not* evidence that Jaime knows about those prisoners while standing in Pennytree.
