## Verified vs. reported vs. unknown

**VERIFIED in narrative:** Jon receives and reads the Pink Letter; Tormund explicitly doubts its veracity; Jon takes personal responsibility for riding south; some brothers depart his hall; Jon suffers repeated stabbings, including from Bowen Marsh.

**CHARACTER CLAIM:** The Pink Letter asserts Stannis was defeated, Mance captive, and Ramsay victorious. Jon chooses to believe it contains at least some truth. We must not upgrade this to confirmed Northern military history.

**INFERRED:** His desire for belonging and his moral commitment to threatened innocents increasingly compete with institutional neutrality; the assassination grows from concrete tensions, not a generic 'betrayed hero' trope.

**UNKNOWN:** Jon's final biological state, any second life in Ghost, resurrection mechanism, post-attack command structure, whether his proposed expedition proceeds, parentage, long-term place in northern succession.

## Current decision knot

**What does Jon's act of breaking institutional neutrality ultimately cost?** This is a question to be developed *after* establishing what happens immediately when he is stabbed. The next active viewpoint may be Melisandre, Tormund, Ghost through another narrator, or a Watchman; choosing POV is part of the research, not a settled answer.

- **Route A — Death is final for a time:** Watch and free folk confront a leaderless emergency; other characters' choices determine Jon's legacy before any possible magical return.
- **Route B — He survives severely wounded:** His attempted ride south and the Watch murderers must be adjudicated; avoiding an instant restoration of authority is essential.
- **Route C — He dies and later returns changed:** Test against Beric/Catelyn resurrection examples, Ghost's bond, and Melisandre's limited knowledge; do not assume exact mechanics or personality outcome.

None is promoted. The novel does not resolve the branch.

## THINK counterexample / challenge

**Tempting shorthand:** "Jon is simply honorable and the Watch betrayed him."

**Counterevidence:** The Watchmen have an oath against participating in southern political wars; Jon's public proposal knowingly approaches or crosses that line. Their killing of him is an extreme response, but they have a real institutional conflict, not merely a dislike of wildlings. See ADWD 69 alongside AGOT 70.

**Alternative mistake:** "Jon's title frees him from every competing obligation." His chapters repeatedly demonstrate the opposite.

## Provisional creative seed (NOT adopted)

A restored Jon might find that neither the Watch nor the northern lords agrees about what he is owed. That conflict should not be skipped via instant coronation, automatic rule at Winterfell, or a miraculous absolution of his oaths.
