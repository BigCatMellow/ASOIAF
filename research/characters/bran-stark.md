## Central ethical question

**When Bran can do what other people cannot, does necessity or suffering give him the right to use them?** This is not hypothetical: the five-book text depicts the repeated, secret violation of Hodor's bodily autonomy. It also shows his longing to walk, his compassion for Meera and the real danger posed by the dead.

## Three mechanisms: distinct, not interchangeable

1. **Skinchanging Summer/ravens:** Direct sensory participation; Summer's desires can overwhelm Bran's intent, and experienced ravens can be easier hosts (ASOS 9; ADWD 34). This is not equivalent to looking at any modern place on demand.
2. **Human skinchanging:** Hodor's fearful, involuntary compliance (ASOS 56; ADWD 34). The fact Bran 'returns' Hodor's body does **not** make ongoing occupation consensual.
3. **Weirwood memory:** Through rooted trees, he sees episodes separated by years/centuries and attempts to call to Ned (ADWD 34). Bloodraven states the past is fixed. **One apparent reaction by Ned is a reason to investigate, not proof Bran can remake past events.**

## Limits on the teacher / cave

- **Brynden/ Bloodraven is a narrator with his own wounds and memories**, not a guaranteed source of truth. He has a physical, root-bound existence and admits limits on predicting how fast Bran will master the gift (ADWD 34).
- **Jojen and Meera** have lost choices. Jojen expects death (from his green dreams) and Meera increasingly doubts the journey, but neither the specific circumstances nor date of his death are established.
- **Leaf's people** have substantial memories and a different relationship to time; Bran receives explanations from them, not a certified history of the Others or the First Men.
- **The cave's defenses** work against approaching dead things; beyond the protected threshold, people are not safe.
- **The weirwood paste** is given to Bran as a preparation for visions; the text calls it a paste of seeds. `Jojen Paste` is a **theory**, not a depicted death or ingredient list.

## THINK — immediate next decisions

**A. Stop using Hodor without consent:** This would cost Bran the exhilarating autonomy of walking and potentially some ability to protect the group. The sacrifice must have genuine practical cost.

**B. Continue using Hodor for 'good' reasons:** It may become easier and morally worse, because Hodor's inward retreat makes him appear compliant. An eventual crisis can expose the deception to Meera and Jojen.

**C. Test Brynden's claim about past communication:** Bran may attempt to call to historical figures or question the old sacrifices. The ethical and practical risk is misreading visions, assuming ghosts can act, or ignoring the living friends beside him.

These are *research branches*, not final-scene instructions. A genuinely character-led outcome may take another path.

## Cross-Stark and supernatural hooks, separated by evidence

- Arya experiences wolf dreams across the sea and skinchanges a Braavosi cat (AFFC 34, ADWD 45); Bran has been explicitly trained with Summer, ravens and weirwood vision. **No five-book scene shows Bran and Arya communicating mentally.**
- Robb's will (ASOS 45) was formulated under the mistaken belief Bran and Rickon were dead; neither Bran's physical location nor his knowledge is available to most northern houses.
- The Black Gate responds to sworn Watch words (ASOS 56). This is evidence of ancient supernatural rules, **not** proof of a treaty with Others.
- Historical human sacrifice at Winterfell does not tell us whether the Others required it, endorsed it or knew of it. Keep ancient-origin explanations open.
