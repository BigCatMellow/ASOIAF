## Core dynamic

**Cersei trades future sovereign control for an immediate political and financial advantage.** Having deferred royal debt to the Faith, she authorizes the revival of the Faith Militant to win debt forgiveness and a blessing for Tommen. She later discovers that religious authority empowered by the crown can investigate its benefactors.

## Three minds: difference between their goals

**Cersei:** Protect Tommen and her family's power, defeat Margaery, restore personal control and evade the prophecy she fears. Her strategy repeatedly relies on people she assumes she can purchase, threaten or replace. She has harmed herself institutionally: armed Faith, untrusted bank, empowered Qyburn, manipulated testimony.

**High Sparrow:** Gains real policing and military leverage through religious legitimacy, the poor's mobilization and a crown-issued legal concession. He is not a merely decorative obstacle or Cersei's obedient priest. His precise political ambitions are inferred from what he does, not a hidden POV.

**Margaery / Tyrells:** Their legitimacy and armies complicate both Faith trials. Margaery's sexual guilt is not established by coerced accounts; Kevan's council sees the trials as live state-security problems, while Mace seeks influence.

## Evidence gaps and serious counterexamples

1. **All confessions are not equal:** Osney/Blue Bard testimony may be engineered or tortured; Cersei's self-serving narratives are also unreliable. Track independently narrated conduct.
2. **Ser Robert Strong:** Extraordinary silence and physicality are a clue, not a confirmed forensic identification as Gregor's reanimated corpse in the five published chapters.
3. **Trial of Seven:** Has not been ordered by the Faith at ADWD's end. Cersei requests ordinary trial by battle; alternate trial format belongs to a separate [fan-theory file](../theories/cases/trial-of-seven.md).
4. **Faith = villain:** The sparrows respond to widespread war-caused suffering and state collapse. Their religious coercion can still be oppressive. Avoid replacing civic reality with caricature.
5. **Cersei's forced incompetence:** Her plan to divide enemies can yield short-lived tactical success; the narrative must allow her to adapt or choose worse methods, not simply fail on schedule.

## Immediate character choices: no fixed resolution

- **Defend herself in trial by combat:** Her hope depends on the Faith recognizing Robert Strong as acceptable and on circumstances not changing; a champion is not a verdict.
- **Punish suspected betrayers / reassert power:** Can increase elite support or alienate Tyrells and religious factions further; Kevan's death creates a destabilizing vacancy without appointing her sole regent.
- **Protect Tommen by accepting compromise:** A less domineering move could cost Cersei perceived authority while limiting the kingdom's fracture. Whether she can choose it is an open character question.
