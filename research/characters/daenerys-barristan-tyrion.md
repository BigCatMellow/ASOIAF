## Core insight

**The faction holding the title 'Daenerys's side' contains incompatible objectives.** Daenerys's attempt to make durable peace, Barristan's belief that he must save her government by overthrowing her husband, and Tyrion's separate plan to manipulate mercenary allegiances can each be rational **from the person's limited perspective** while producing an unstable outcome together.

### THINK: hard internal contradiction

Daenerys identifies enslavement as intolerable but accepts an agreement that preserves slavery beyond her city walls to keep people alive. Her later revulsion is **evidence of conflict**, not proof she will burn cities. Equally, Drogon's killing a child is evidence of a cost to her own supernatural power, not proof that every later dragon attack is inevitable.

### Near-term decisions, not endings

- **A. Return as a ruler:** seek to return to Meereen and protect the existing settlement even as some concessions violate her ideals; needs Dothraki cooperation and control of Drogon.
- **B. Break with the settlement:** interpret external slaving and Yunkish betrayal as evidence compromise has failed; this risks terrible consequences for hostages and freedmen.
- **C. Reframe her priorities:** prioritize stability, evacuations, a limited intervention or an Essosi political coalition before considering Westeros.

Do not select a branch without developing Dothraki politics and the Meereen battle independently.

## Barristan: order through an uncertain coup

- **ADWD 67 (The Kingbreaker): VERIFIED** Barristan joins Skahaz mo Kandaq in a plot to remove Hizdahr. He reasons from suspicious behavior surrounding poisoned locusts and a feared Harpy conspiracy. He also explicitly recognizes that the act would be treason if the king is innocent.
- **ADWD 67: UNKNOWN** The actual poisoner, identity of the Harpy's central command, the precise scope of Skahaz's private designs, and Hizdahr's guilt. Barristan's sincerity does not establish the truth of his suspicions.
- **ADWD 70 (The Queen's Hand): VERIFIED** Barristan exercises executive power after the seizure, considers the situation of captives and hostages, and prepares a sortie against Yunkai. Quentyn's disastrous dragon expedition has created further consequences, and the Yunkish later use plague victims' bodies in their bombardment.
- **ADWD 70: UNKNOWN** Battle result, durability of the coup, or whether Dany will forgive Barristan. **Do not** assume her eventual return validates his decision.

**Key narrative conflict:** Barristan serves Daenerys by removing the ruler she married to secure peace. He has honorable intentions but limited information, and his choice may overthrow her preferred policy without her consent.

## Tyrion: an opportunist outside the queen's circle

- **ADWD 66 (Tyrion): VERIFIED** Tyrion and Penny are inside Brown Ben Plumm's Second Sons camp as contingent recruits. Tyrion signs mercenary obligations, recognizes the precariousness of the Yunkish position, and plots to persuade Brown Ben to switch allegiance again. Jorah also believes Meereen has stronger military assets.
- **ADWD 66: UNKNOWN** Whether Brown Ben agrees, whether Tyrion survives the battle, or whether he can meet Daenerys. He has **not** become her advisor by the fifth book's end.

**Key narrative conflict:** Tyrion understands power but is struggling with anger, grief and a desire to regain influence. A chance to become useful to Daenerys would not automatically make his political advice wise or benevolent.

## Counterexamples and unearned shortcuts

1. **Barristan = truth:** He believes Hizdahr is corrupt; he cannot prove who ordered the poisoned locusts. A heroic knight's interpretation can be wrong.
2. **Peace = success:** The agreement permits slavery outside Meereen, embeds hostages, and relies on a hostile external army's restraint.
3. **War = liberation:** Winning a battle won't answer who administers the city, feeds former slaves, or limits reprisals.
4. **Tyrion = Dany's advisor:** This is a possible future development, **not** an already established relationship.
5. **Drogon = obedient cavalry:** Daenerys's closing chapter shows she cannot govern her dragon's movement simply by command.
6. **Show-end fate = book necessity:** No fifth-book chapter requires Daenerys to burn King's Landing or abandon Essos.
