## Real decision knot

Aegon begins asserting his own will; Connington seeks to repair his perceived failure to Rhaegar and privately carries a worsening disease; the Golden Company contains competing strategic and personal incentives. They share an invasion but do not necessarily share the same objective.

## Character motivations

**Aegon — agency and pride.** Educated to rule and influenced by Tyrion, he chooses to press west himself. This could demonstrate courage, dangerous impatience, or both. `UNKNOWN` whether he's truly Rhaegar's son.

**Connington — grief with a deadline.** His guilt over the Battle of the Bells and loyalty to Rhaegar are narrated in his own POV. Greyscale makes delay costly; concealment risks harming the people he means to protect.

**Golden Company — home vs. risk.** Bittersteel's Blackfyre history and members' exiled forebears do not prove Aegon's bloodline. Some officers want lost Westerosi lands; Strickland also needs a viable contract and assembled forces.

## THINK alternatives

1. **Rapid siege/assault:** Aegon and Connington capitalize on momentum; may outrun political and logistical support.
2. **Political coalition first:** Approach Stormlords and Dorne; may lose surprise and expose Connington's condition.
3. **Internal dissension:** Company leaders challenge exposure and reward promises; forcing Aegon to learn compromise.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Popularity and ruling skill are not yet tested; Varys's praise is self-interested.
- Connington's illness is a catalyst, not sufficient proof of planned atrocity.
- The Golden Company accepted a chance to return home, but we cannot infer all members secretly know Aegon to be Blackfyre.
- No biological test or direct childhood witness establishes Young Griff's identity in this corpus.

## Dependencies

Arianne's journey, Tyrion/Daenerys delay, the Tyrell field army, Jon Connington's secrecy, the Golden Company's scattered landing forces, Varys/Illyrio motives.

## Disposition

`OPEN`. Aegon's first major decision has already occurred. Later success or catastrophe should grow from how the coalition responds, not a compulsory King's Landing victory.

### Character-specific conflicts, newly sharpened

**Aegon wants to be treated as a sovereign now, not a safeguarded ward.** Duckfield's white cloak is a decision made partly from friendship; Connington would prefer to reserve Kingsguard honors for politically useful noble supporters (ADWD 61). Aegon and Connington disagree not merely over risk but over what it means to be a good king: demonstrated personal loyalty vs. the appearance of statecraft.

**Connington faces an ethical test independent of the disease.** He remembers Myles Toyne saying that Tywin Lannister would have burned Stoney Sept to deny Robert any sanctuary (ADWD 61). He now regrets his earlier refusal to be branded a butcher, but he has **not yet carried out the proposed massacre**. His greyscale creates urgency without predetermining atrocity.

**Strickland is strategically cautious for reasons larger than cowardice.** The force is dispersed, its essential horses/elephants have been delayed, and expected Daenerys support has failed to materialize. His caution may save men whom Aegon and Connington are gambling with. Other officers rationally prefer immediate return to ancestral lands. The conflict is genuine.

### Crucial cross-plot link: Red Ronnet Connington

- **ADWD 61:** Jon Connington holds relatives of Ser Ronnet Connington at Griffin's Roost, including Ronnet's younger brother, sister and natural son. This may become leverage; it is *not yet a narrated exchange*.
- **ADWD Epilogue:** Ronnet is at the Lannister/Tyrell court and publicly offers to kill his uncle and the supposed prince. Kevan nevertheless considers him suspect because the supposed invader is kin; Ronnet is accompanied by former Gregor Clegane retainers assigned by **Jaime**.
- **Consequence:** The Stormlands and Riverlands strands are already connected by family, prisoners and Jaime's personnel decisions. An order to Ronnet could affect innocent Connington prisoners as well as the battle. A future cross-POV message requires a proven channel, not instant shared knowledge.

### Immediate gardening experiments (not adopted)

1. **Aegon insists on leadership:** Connington can deny him, compromise with a visible-but-protected symbolic command, or permit actual battlefield risk. Each response affects Aegon's legitimacy **and** the trust between prince, guardian and captains.
2. **Dorne remains undecided:** A successful raid may cause Doran to consider Aegon, but his first obligation is Dornish security and his incomplete information about Quentyn/Daenerys. Do not insert his support simply because Connington says it is inevitable.
3. **Connington chooses speed over logistics:** Losses or a hostage confrontation can compel a retreat, harsher measures, or politically costly negotiation. Greyscale and guilt make choices harder; they do not dictate one outcome.
