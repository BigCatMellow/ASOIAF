## Real decision knot

Aegon begins asserting his own will; Connington seeks to repair his perceived failure to Rhaegar and privately carries a worsening disease; the Golden Company contains competing strategic and personal incentives. They share an invasion but do not necessarily share the same objective.

## Character motivations

**Aegon — agency and pride.** Educated to rule and influenced by Tyrion, he chooses to press west himself. This could demonstrate courage, dangerous impatience, or both. `UNKNOWN` whether he's truly Rhaegar's son.

**Connington — grief with a deadline.** His guilt over the Battle of the Bells and loyalty to Rhaegar are narrated in his own POV. Greyscale makes delay costly; concealment risks harming the people he means to protect.

**Golden Company — home vs. risk.** Bittersteel's Blackfyre history and members' exiled forebears do not prove Aegon's bloodline. Some officers want lost Westerosi lands; Strickland also needs a viable contract and assembled forces.

## THINK alternatives

1. **Rapid siege/assault:** Aegon and Connington capitalize on momentum; may outrun political and logistical support.
2. **Political coalition first:** Approach Stormlords and Dorne; may lose surprise and expose Connington's condition.
3. **Internal dissension:** Company leaders challenge exposure and reward promises; forcing Aegon to learn compromise.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Popularity and ruling skill are not yet tested; Varys's praise is self-interested.
- Connington's illness is a catalyst, not sufficient proof of planned atrocity.
- The Golden Company accepted a chance to return home, but we cannot infer all members secretly know Aegon to be Blackfyre.
- No biological test or direct childhood witness establishes Young Griff's identity in this corpus.

## Dependencies

Arianne's journey, Tyrion/Daenerys delay, the Tyrell field army, Jon Connington's secrecy, the Golden Company's scattered landing forces, Varys/Illyrio motives.

## Disposition

`OPEN`. Aegon's first major decision has already occurred. Later success or catastrophe should grow from how the coalition responds, not a compulsory King's Landing victory.
