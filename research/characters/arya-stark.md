## Central choice, not an ending

**Can Arya accept the Faceless Men's discipline and relinquish personal vengeance, when the very skills they teach also make her better able to pursue it?** She also wants belonging and care. She has been Arya, Arry, Weasel, Nan, Salty, Cat, Beth and the ugly girl, yet continues to remember Jon, Sansa, her parents and Needle.

A useful *Winds* continuation begins with her apprenticeship to **Izembaro in Braavos**, not with an unsourced instant return to Westeros or an assignment to kill a major lord.

## Two moral systems in unresolved conflict

**Arya's earlier justice:** She remembers Ned's values, condemns perpetrators who harm powerless people, keeps lists of particular enemies, and sometimes refuses to kill when confronted with a human being rather than a name. Trauma has hardened her, but her compassion remains real.

**The Faceless Men's doctrine (CHARACTER / INSTITUTIONAL CLAIM):** Their god allegedly gives death without weighing virtue; servants do not choose victims or kill for pleasure, pride or personal revenge. Arya's assignment comes from a system that accepts clients' petitions and keeps their motives hidden from her. That claim to impartiality is **not an independent moral endorsement by the novels**.

**Conflict on the page:** Arya murders Dareon from her own judgment, then kills an assigned target without establishing that he personally deserved execution. Obedience and personal justice can each produce grave harm. Do not solve this by declaring all her targets villains.

## Hidden capacities and knowledge: what she actually has

- **Needle** still lies concealed within the House of Black and White (AFFC 22); retrieving it would be an additional choice, not an automatic identity reveal.
- **Nymeria** leads wolves in the Riverlands in Arya's dreams. ASOS 65 links one dream to Catelyn's body; Arya never encounters Stoneheart in person.
- **Cat skinchanging** occurs during blindness in Braavos (ADWD 45). It supports a *wider* ability than wolf dreams but not omniscient control of distant human affairs.
- **Hardhome news** comes from overheard Lyseni sailors in Braavos (ADWD 45). She can potentially recognize what other factions might miss, but has no established outgoing reliable messenger to the Watch.
- **Stark identities** are deliberately concealed in rival plots: Jeyne Poole is being used as 'Arya' at Winterfell, while the real Arya remains far away. Arya has not seen that imposture.

## THINK: three adjacent paths

1. **Accept the new apprenticeship provisionally:** Arya builds contacts among mummers while continuing to conceal Needle, her wolf dreams and perhaps her true loyalties. The cost is complicity in further assignments she may not regard as just.
2. **Refuse a future morally troubling mission:** She may question the servants' claimed impartiality or protect an innocent; the institution can withdraw safety and training, and she has no guaranteed passage home.
3. **Try to repurpose her skills:** Her own list or Stark memories may pull her toward an unauthorized killing or escape. This would repeat the Dareon violation at greater cost, not cleanly 'free' her of trauma.

**Strong counterexample to a preselected revenge arc:** She shows sustained curiosity and affection for Braavos and its people. The Cat identity is a life she grieves losing. Going home is not necessarily her next immediate desire even if she keeps Needle.

## Character-led continuation constraints

- No automatic meeting with Stoneheart, Jon or Sansa; no teleporting from Braavos to the Riverlands.
- No claim that the House of Black and White ordered her to kill a figure from her private list. Her last contract was assigned independently.
- No certainty that Nymeria's every action is consciously directed by Arya; the relevant scenes occur as dreams.
- No claim the mummers have already recruited Arya or Izembaro is leading her to Westeros.
- Separate Arya's *inferred* connection to Catelyn's recovered body from verified knowledge of her resurrection.
