## Three incompatible aims

**Euron:** Wins the kingsmoot by promises of wealth and dragons, uses a strange horn, and redirects his brother's fleet to seek Daenerys. He trades on occult prestige and political spectacle, but his claimed Valyria experience, control of dragons, and future sacrifices are **not independently demonstrated**.

**Victarion:** Carries Euron's horn east but plans to take Daenerys and her dragons for himself. He distrusts his brother and increasingly relies on Moqorro's religious magic. His personal brutality (including mutilating captives) must not be softened into innocent victimhood.

**Aeron:** Seeks to preserve the Drowned God's traditional authority and resists Euron's accession. His personal history of abuse gives his revulsion force, but the five published books do not follow his later captivity or `The Forsaken` sample events.

## Decision forks, not story outcomes

1. Victarion trusts Moqorro and risks another blower's life; the horn may work, fail, or serve a different claimant.
2. Victarion delays the horn and chooses conventional naval strategy, possibly alienating his crews and losing the advantage he craves.
3. Euron gambles on escalating Reach raids, risking isolation from captains who want loot rather than dangerous magic.
4. Aeron's old religious coalition may resist Euron, but neither its current reach nor Aeron's fate has been narrated after his AFFC chapters.

**Key challenge:** Do not join Oldtown's Pate/Faceless Men mystery, Marwyn's candle and Euron's coastal campaign into a unified proven operation. Co-location and suggestive motifs are insufficient.
