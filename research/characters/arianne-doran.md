## Central question

**Can Arianne exercise real political agency when her father insists that secrecy is necessary to protect Dorne?** She correctly detects that Doran once intended Quentyn to follow him, but constructs a false explanation of his purpose. Her attempt to gain agency through Myrcella endangers the very child she wishes to elevate. Doran's subsequent disclosure does not erase her reasons to distrust him.

## Three different strategic agendas

**Arianne — recognition and self-rule.** She wants her birthright taken seriously, resents exclusion and wishes not to be treated as a bargaining piece. Yet her friend circle and use of Arys exposed others to punishment. She could become a more perceptive political actor, but she has not yet built independent broad-based loyalty or proven strategic competence.

**Doran — containment and delayed revenge.** He protects Trystane and Myrcella from an immediate war he believes Dorne cannot win, while retaining dangerous agents at court and hoping to join Daenerys through Quentyn. His caution is understandable **and** has made his family desperate for information.

**Nymeria/Tyene/Obara — different motives.** Their desire to avenge Oberyn does not automatically match Doran's plans. Sending them into court, the Faith and a punitive search for Darkstar exposes the Martells to autonomous action he may not control.

## The actual known information gap

- **Doran:** Knows Quentyn was sent toward Daenerys, hears an elephant-fleet *rumor*, assigns Sand Snake missions; **does not know on-page** that Quentyn has died.
- **Arianne:** Has heard the Viserys pact, Quentyn's true purpose, and Doran's planned positions at the capital; there is **no five-book scene** of her receiving verified Young Griff/Aegon identification or Quentyn's fate.
- **Connington:** Knows his invasion and claimed Aegon parentage; **does not know** Doran's current answer. Orders a letter, not a concluded Dornish alliance.
- **Balon Swann:** Hears a cover story about Arys and Darkstar; the text gives him reason to be wary, but not proven access to Hotah's first-person observation.

## THINK — three viable next decisions

1. **Question the new claimant:** When reliable news of Aegon arrives, Arianne may demand independent confirmation and seek to protect her own succession. A hasty commitment would risk repeating her Myrcella failure.
2. **Back Doran's conditional diplomacy:** She may negotiate limited recognition without binding all Dorne, but her father must tolerate independent judgment rather than another unread letter.
3. **Test the limits of the family alliance:** If the court or Sand Snakes trigger unexpected retaliation, Arianne may have to oppose her father's agents to prevent harm to Trystane, Myrcella or Dornish civilians.

**Counterexample to the expected 'Arianne marries Aegon' route:** Even a legitimate Aegon need not want a Dornish marriage; Arianne is heir to Dorne in her own right; the Company has limited forces and questionable assurances. No AFFC/ADWD chapter shows a marriage negotiation or a committed proposal.
