## Why this is more than a marriage setup

The last published Vale movement makes Robert Arryn's physical care, access to the mountain seat, Littlefinger's authority, and Sansa's disguise part of the same practical problem. It does **not** establish a murder, a completed feast, a Harry Hardyng engagement, a reveal, or a Vale army’s political decision.

## The immediate causal chain

1. **VERIFIED:** Winter weather makes the Eyrie progressively inaccessible; servants and soldiers have already descended, creating a genuine timing/safety pressure.
2. **VERIFIED:** Robert initially refuses to leave bed, complains of headache and a prior medicinal taste, and fears both mules and the route. Alayne persuades rather than compels him because rough handling can precipitate a fit.
3. **VERIFIED / CHARACTER JUDGMENT:** Colemon says milk of the poppy and a secured mule *might* be safest; Alayne rejects that proposed public presentation and orders sweetmilk despite the maester’s cumulative-risk warning. Colemon agrees, but the chapter does not depict the requested cup being administered.
4. **INFERRED:** Alayne is balancing immediate fall/fit danger and the political requirement that the Lord of the Eyrie not look helpless. This is an inference from her stated concerns, not a finding that she accepts a long-term plan against him.
5. **VERIFIED:** At the exposed saddle Robert begins shaking, but Alayne and Myranda help him cross. The party later passes Stone and reaches the Gates of the Moon; Robert sleeps on his mule en route. This shows safe completion of the descent, not a shown pharmacological cause, durable recovery, or a completed feast.

## Separate gates before any inheritance or political deployment

1. **Medical-observation gate:** Someone must determine whether either requested sweetmilk cup was actually given, observe Robert thereafter, distinguish symptoms from baseline illness, and decide whether to listen to Colemon, Petyr, or another caretaker. No published aftermath supplies this.
2. **Household/feast gate:** The party has reached the Gates, but the planned feast, Robert’s reception by the household, and the wider Vale’s reaction have not been narrated.
3. **Household-access gate:** Once down, named household figures must decide who sees Robert, who controls medicine, and whether Sansa is left alone with him. Petyr is physically present at the Gates, but no subsequent order or access arrangement is shown.
4. **Succession gate:** Robert would need actually to die or legally cease to hold the title; then the relevant inheritance, custody, and lordly acceptance steps must occur. Harry’s described status does not skip any of them.
5. **Marriage/identity gate:** Harry must meet or accept Alayne/Sansa, and an identified audience must accept a Stark reveal. This requires protection from Cersei’s search and a choice by Sansa, not merely Petyr’s forecast.
6. **Mobilization gate:** A Vale intervention elsewhere needs a legitimate command, lords’ assent, supplies, route, and reason to expose the Vale during its own succession issue.

## Competing readings of the medication issue

### A. Littlefinger is arranging Robert’s death

**Support:** Petyr’s plan economically benefits if Robert dies; he favors sweetmilk for a public descent; Colemon warns of a cumulative danger.

**Counterweight:** No passage narrates an order to overdose, a tampering plan, or a death. The immediate descent has a real fall/fit risk, and Alayne’s on-page motive is to get an already frightened child safely off an increasingly isolated mountain.

**Future test:** A traceable dose/order, a medical observation tied to a named actor, or a deliberate choice after the risk is understood would differentiate a lethal scheme from negligent/politicized care.

### B. Sansa knowingly chooses Robert’s political removal

**Support:** She recognizes a conflict between the boy’s needs and the lord’s public appearance and repeats Petyr’s language about larger concerns.

**Counterweight:** She refuses rough transport, spends the chapter calming and physically protecting Robert, and has not been told the full pharmacological risk. Concern, dependence, and compromised choices are not informed homicidal intent.

**Future test:** A scene in which she receives, understands, and responds to Colemon’s warning or an actual proposal affecting Robert’s life.

### C. Harry’s inheritance makes the Vale politically ready

**Support:** Petyr expects Harry to become heir upon Robert’s death; Harry has public standing and a Waynwood connection.

**Counterweight:** Robert lives, Harry has not consented to marry Sansa, other houses retain independent interests, and no public announcement or force commitment occurs.

**Future test:** A verified succession event followed by named lords accepting or challenging the result.
