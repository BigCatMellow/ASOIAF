## Why the order matters

- **Aegon first:** Doran can assess a claimant while still hoping Quentyn’s Daenerys mission has a future; his existing contingency planning and caution remain relevant.
- **Quentyn first:** grief, mission failure, and the lack of Daenerys can alter both Doran’s tolerance for risk and Arianne’s view of being excluded.
- **Simultaneous/misreported:** either report can be delayed, incomplete, forged, or treated as strategically interested; authentication becomes a scene-level decision rather than exposition.

None of these orders is narrated. The books provide only the separate source-side facts and Doran’s earlier uncertainty.

## Minimum evidence before adopting a branch

1. Name the carrier and departure point.
2. Specify whether it is a raven, ship, merchant relay, diplomatic letter, or personal witness.
3. State the recipient and whether the recipient trusts it.
4. Separate Doran’s private receipt from Arianne’s receipt and public Dornish knowledge.
5. Preserve the possibility that one message arrives while the other remains missing.

## Theory boundary

No received message proves Aegon’s parentage, commits Dorne to a marriage, or establishes a pre-arranged Martell response. Quentyn’s death would prove a failed mission only to people who receive credible evidence; it cannot retroactively authorize a theory about Doran’s intent. Independent challenge remains open.
