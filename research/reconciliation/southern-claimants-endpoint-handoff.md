## Theory and identity boundaries

1. **Aegon’s parentage:** Connington’s belief and Varys’s advocacy are evidence of a political project, not biological verification. Golden Company history supplies a serious alternative context, not proof of Blackfyre descent.
2. **Robert Strong:** remarkable size, silence, and Qyburn’s circumstances invite an inference; the five books do not narrate a forensic identification as Gregor.
3. **Dornish alliance / marriage:** Doran’s Targaryen contingency, Arianne’s inheritance, and a prepared letter do not equal a received proposal or chosen match.
4. **Cersei’s trial:** atonement releases her from confinement under terms; it does not determine legal guilt, Faith approval of her champion, or a trial format.
5. **Varys’s purpose:** the murder and stated design are narrated, but every laudatory assertion about Aegon remains Varys’s own testimony.

## Decision constraints for a continuation

- Let Doran and Arianne decide from actual received reports, not from a reader’s knowledge of Aegon or Quentyn.
- Do not treat the capital’s first response to Kevan/Pycelle as predetermined; plausible suspects and institutional vacancies matter.
- Keep Aegon’s need for visible sovereignty in tension with Connington’s secrecy, disease, captive relatives, and incomplete force.
- Keep Cersei’s personal return distinct from the crown’s capacity to control the Faith, Tyrells, or Iron Bank.
