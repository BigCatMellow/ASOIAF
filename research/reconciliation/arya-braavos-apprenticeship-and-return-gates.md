- Scope: Arya's final published-book position in the House of Black and White, especially **ADWD 45 (The Blind Girl)** and **ADWD 64 (The Ugly Little Girl)**.

## Identity and target-selection limits

The House's doctrinal challenge is explicit: its servants are not supposed to judge souls, kill for personal pleasure, or choose their own victims. Arya's responses are not a clean conversion. She calls the target evil from his appearance, imagines revenge names in the sanctum, and thinks the abused face-donor's father should have died despite being told that the donor requested death for herself rather than him.

| Evidence | Supports | Does not support |
|---|---|---|
| Arya accepts a target she says she has never known and works around the guards rather than killing them. | She can execute a specific, externally selected commission with restraint when supervised/tested. | That she will never choose a personal target, that she believes the institution's moral account, or that she has renounced Needle and her list. |
| The kindly man offers to remove the task or restore prior life options if she cannot comply. | She has at least some depicted opportunity to refuse/leave at this stage. | That departure would be safe, cost-free, available after every future task, or desired by Arya. |
| The priest demands the price of name, family roles, future, and face; Arya says she can pay it. | The order's requirement is unambiguous, and Arya hears it. | That a spoken assent has achieved emotional or ethical surrender. |
| Arya keeps parts of her sensory/skinchanging experience from the kindly man and thinks he may keep secrets too (**ADWD 45**). | Her information relationship with the House is incomplete and she can withhold something. | That she controls the House, knows all its purposes, or can safely conceal any future choice. |

An eventual personal act against someone on Arya's private list would therefore need an additional decision point: whether she remains subject to House doctrine, leaves or is expelled, deceives the House, receives a coincidentally overlapping petition, or undergoes some other source-shown change. It may not be treated as an automatic benefit of her current apprenticeship.

## Braavos and Hardhome: a local intelligence channel, not a message already at the Wall

As Blind Beth, Arya overhears three Lyseni sailors from the storm-damaged *Goodheart*. They say the Sealord's guards seized the vessel for carrying enslaved people. Their account is **CHARACTER TESTIMONY** overheard by Arya, although the seizure is supported by the ship's presence in Braavos and local enforcement context rather than Arya boarding it herself.

| Reported fact | Arya's knowledge status at ADWD 45 | Non-receiver boundary |
|---|---|---|
| *Goodheart* and *Elephant* encountered starving wildlings at Hardhome and took women and children before enslaving them. | Arya hears the sailors' account. | Jon, the Night's Watch, Tormund, and the trapped people are not shown receiving Arya's version of this information. |
| *Goodheart* was separated in a storm, damaged, and reached Braavos, where it was seized. | Arya hears this account; the sailors are physically in Braavos. | This does not establish the later position, cargo, or fate of *Elephant*. |
| The sailors believe *Elephant* may have reached Lys and may return for more captives because slave prices are rising. | A rumor/prediction by the sailors, not confirmation. | It is not evidence that a rescue, slaving return, or notification occurs. |
| Hardhome still contains thousands of vulnerable people. | Arya hears the sailors' claim, consistent in broad outline with the Wall crisis. | It does not give Arya current command information, a ship, or a way to alter the Watch plan instantly. |

Arya's cat-mediated perception is strong evidence that she can obtain information beyond her own senses, but she tells the kindly man only enough to satisfy his "three things" test and keeps the cat-warging implication private. The episode makes her a potential holder of useful Hardhome information; it does not make her a messenger or establish the House's policy about sharing it.

## What a Westeros return would require

Returning is not a single movement. It would need a chain of choices and logistics that the five-book endpoint has not shown.

1. **Motive and institutional-status gate:** Arya must choose to go, be sent, be released, flee, or otherwise cease/alter the current Izembaro apprenticeship. The books show no such decision.
2. **Identity and equipment gate:** She must decide whether to travel as Arya, as another face, or anonymously; whether to retrieve Needle; and what contact with the House continues. A face can help movement but complicates recognition by kin/allies.
3. **Passage gate:** A particular ship, captain, destination, season, funding, and safe landing must be supplied. Arya's prior route to Braavos does not establish a reverse passage on demand.
4. **Destination and recipient gate:** "Westeros" contains distinct possible fronts. The Wall, Winterfell, the Riverlands, the Vale, and King’s Landing require different routes and different people capable of recognizing or protecting her.
5. **Recognition and consent gate:** Arya's identity must be credibly established to a recipient who does not use her name merely as a political instrument. The false "Arya" marriage remains a separate northern problem; exposure of Jeyne Poole would not place the real Arya beside the relevant actors.
6. **Information-transfer gate:** Arya's Hardhome report, House knowledge, and personal observations change any Westerosi actor's choices only after she or a named intermediary chooses to communicate them, survives the route, and is believed.

No step can be replaced by reader knowledge that Arya is a Stark. No published scene gives Jon, Sansa, Bran, Rickon, Theon, Jeyne, Stannis, or a northern house Arya's current Braavosi location; conversely, Arya has not received their end-of-ADWD developments.

## Branch discipline

- A Braavos-centered branch can begin with Izembaro's apprenticeship, House oversight, local consequences of the insurer's death, or Braavosi responses to the slaving report. It cannot begin by assuming a specific Westerosi assassination or a completed homeward voyage.
- A return branch needs to name the first break/permission decision, transport, landing, disguise/identity choice, and first recipient. It must then distinguish private recognition from public use of the name "Arya Stark."
- A Hardhome-intersection branch requires a carrier and timely receipt. Arya's knowledge is valuable precisely because it is presently unshared with the people making rescue decisions.
- The House's apparent willingness to test, punish, or offer exit should be treated as a live constraint, not an automatic villainous or benevolent interpretation.

## Future verification tests

2. Does the House discover Arya's retained Stark attachments, Needle, cat-warging, or private revenge list, and what consequence follows?

## Non-adoption rule

Do not treat Arya as a fully authorized Faceless assassin, a person without Stark attachments, a recipient of future Westerosi news, or a returning Westerosi actor until the missing apprenticeship, choice, travel, and receipt events are actually supplied. Independent challenge remains open.
