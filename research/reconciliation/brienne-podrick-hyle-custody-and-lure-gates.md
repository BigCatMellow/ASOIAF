# Brienne, Podrick, and Hyle — custody, coercion, and the unspoken lure

- Record role: `RESEARCH / RECONCILIATION`.
- Status: `WORKING — THE GALLOWS INTERRUPTION AND ITS TERMS ARE WITHHELD`.
- Scope: the five published novels only; principally [ASOS 72 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/073-jaime.md), [AFFC 9 (Brienne)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/010-brienne.md), [AFFC 25 (Brienne)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/026-brienne.md), [AFFC 42 (Brienne)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/043-brienne.md), and [ADWD 48 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/049-jaime.md).
- Relationship to other records: the [Riverlands endpoint handoff](riverlands-jaime-brienne-stoneheart-endpoint-handoff.md) owns the broader Jaime–Stoneheart situation. This audit owns only the narrower **Brienne/Podrick/Hyle custody and communication problem**; it does not select a future confrontation or repeat the overview.

## Finding in one sentence

**VERIFIED:** Stoneheart orders Brienne, Podrick, and Hyle hanged after Brienne refuses the stated sword-or-noose choice; the text cuts away when Brienne screams one unrevealed word while Podrick is choking. Brienne later reaches Jaime alive, wounded, and carrying Oathkeeper, but the novels never show what she said, whether Podrick or Hyle survived, who holds them, or what terms make her Pennytree claim a coercive lure rather than a free report.

The distinction matters: **Brienne's own restraint, exhaustion, fever, broken arm/ribs, and face wound make a voluntary return to Jaime after the cave possible but not sufficient evidence of freedom from coercion.** She is physically alive at Pennytree; her consent to any intervening plan is not narrated.

## The hearing: evidence, not a reliable trial

**VERIFIED in AFFC 42:** the Brotherhood presents Oathkeeper, with its lion pommel, and Tommen's sealed authorization as evidence against Brienne. Brienne explains that Jaime gave the sword for a promise to Catelyn and sent her to protect Sansa. The captors then produce Podrick and Hyle alive. Hyle is badly beaten; Podrick supports him. Brienne expressly says neither man was party to any supposed betrayal.

That evidence gives Stoneheart's group reasons to suspect an association with Lannisters, but it does not settle the accusations:

- **CHARACTER CLAIM:** Lem/the other outlaws call the men "lions" and cite Podrick's former service to Tyrion and Hyle's service to Randyll Tarly. Those descriptions identify associations, not a demonstrated act of treachery in the cave.
- **VERIFIED:** Brienne identifies the revived woman as Catelyn; Thoros supplies an account of Catelyn's resurrection. Brienne consequently knows the speaker who once received her oath, whereas Jaime is not present to receive that information.
- **CHARACTER CLAIM:** Stoneheart, speaking through a northman, calls Brienne false and offers a binary: kill Jaime with Oathkeeper or be hanged as a betrayer. Her demand shows her present priority and power over the captives; it does not prove that Jaime participated in the Red Wedding or that Brienne has betrayed Catelyn.
- **VERIFIED:** Brienne refuses the binary. Stoneheart then says, “Hang them.” The sequence is an order for all three to die, not a witnessed deal to spare the two companions in exchange for Jaime.

Thoros has already described the group as a cave where mercy and justice have diminished under war and hardship. That is an important **CHARACTER CLAIM from a participating witness**, not an exoneration of the group or a guarantee that every member will execute Stoneheart's words identically.

## The gallows cut: the exact evidence limit

At the willow/elms, **VERIFIED**, the three are noosed separately. Lem silences Hyle when he shouts that he will kill Jaime. Brienne tries to ransom Podrick; Lem rejects the offer. The ropes are drawn tight. Brienne sees Podrick's feet leave the ground and him choke; Hyle curses, while Podrick does not speak. The chapter then ends after: **“She screamed a word.”**

The word, its listener, and any response are deliberately not supplied. Therefore all of the following remain `UNKNOWN`:

1. Whether the word is an acceptance, a name, a warning, a plea, or something else.
2. Whether Stoneheart, Lem, or any other follower heard/accepted it before any death occurred.
3. Whether Podrick and Hyle were cut down alive, killed, moved, released, or divided between keepers.
4. Whether Brienne promised murder, agreed only to lead Jaime, was given a deadline, or accepted another condition.
5. Whether Oathkeeper was restored immediately, later, voluntarily, or under a conditional instruction.

**INFERENCE, not fact:** Brienne's later Pennytree statement is likely connected to this interruption because she survived, possesses Oathkeeper, and requests that Jaime come alone in a manner that would meet Stoneheart's earlier wish to obtain him. The source does not convert that strong narrative inference into a recoverable bargain. A continuation must not write the unseen word as a quoted promise.

## Pennytree: what Jaime receives—and what he does not

**VERIFIED in ADWD 48:** two scouts bring Jaime a woman whom they took into custody after she rode up demanding words; she is Brienne. Jaime sees that she looks much older and has a bandaged face. She gives only “a bite” as the explanation, touches Oathkeeper, invokes his prior quest, says she found “the girl,” places her a day's ride away, and says he must come alone or “the Hound” will kill her. Jaime asks where the girl is; the published narrative ends with Brienne's answer that she can take him.

This creates four distinct communication gates:

1. **Brienne → Jaime:** Jaime has received an urgent assertion, not proof of Sansa's location, the captive's identity, or an actual Hound threat. He does not receive Stoneheart's identity, the cave hearing, Podrick's or Hyle's position, or the terms of any alleged reprieve.
2. **Jaime → Brienne:** Jaime has not yet accepted, rejected, interrogated, or modified the demand to travel alone. His reaction cannot be substituted from reader knowledge of Brienne's danger.
3. **Jaime → his own party:** His escort, scouts, household, and the Blackwood hostage are physically present at Pennytree. The text does not show who hears the conversation, who receives a command, or how Jaime could depart without choosing a command/custody arrangement.
4. **Brienne/Jaime → Stoneheart's network:** No location, rendezvous signal, timed deadline, observer, or demonstrated messenger chain is narrated. Brienne's ability to lead Jaime somewhere is asserted to Jaime; neither the readers nor Jaime are shown the route, current camp, or who will receive them.

The `Hound` language cannot close the gate. **VERIFIED in AFFC 42:** Brienne recognizes that Lem is wearing the helm taken from Rorge and Lem admits the transfer; **VERIFIED in ADWD 48:** Jaime hears only Brienne's label. Thus a frightening name has a concrete source for Brienne but does not establish Sandor Clegane, Lem, or any other person holds Sansa. See the detailed [Jaime Stoneheart intelligence audit](../synthesis/jaime-stoneheart-rumor-to-brienne-pennytree-information-gates.md#3-there-are-several-different-men-and-stories-called-the-hound) for that identity problem.

## Planning constraints and falsifiers

- Do not begin a Riverlands storyline with “Jaime is captured” or “Brienne has betrayed Jaime.” The last Jaime POV ends before he even gives an answer.
- Do not use Podrick/Hyle as live hostage leverage, corpses, rescuers, or informants without first making their post-gallows status visible. Their pre-cut presence is not a later custody receipt.
- Do not make Stoneheart omniscient about Jaime's private thoughts, or Jaime omniscient about Catelyn's survival and the cave. Those facts have not crossed the relevant communication gate.
- A concrete subsequent scene can falsify this audit's cautious branch structure: an identified survivor, an exact term, a route or messenger, Jaime's explicit decision, or a named captor can replace `UNKNOWN` with evidence. Until then, the safest conclusion is a coercive crisis with unknown leverage—not a completed assassination scheme.
