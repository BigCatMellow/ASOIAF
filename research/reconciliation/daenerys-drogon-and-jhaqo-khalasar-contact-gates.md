## The last depicted contact

**VERIFIED:** After leaving Daznak’s Pit on Drogon, Daenerys survives for an unspecified number of days at his grassland lair and then travels on foot beside a south/southeast-running stream. At the endpoint she is hungry, dehydrated, feverish or fever-feeling, barefoot, in a torn undertunic, with blistered feet and scraped/healing hands. After eating unfamiliar berries, she suffers severe vomiting and diarrhea; she later has heavy menstrual bleeding that alarms her. These conditions constrain what she can choose before political intention does (ADWD 71).

**VERIFIED:** Drogon returns to the rocky lair on his own, often ignores Daenerys’s words and whip, and repeatedly flies past without responding to her. He nevertheless comes when she calls after a Dothraki scout flees, carries her in pursuit, hunts a horse, and remains beside her as riders approach. This establishes dangerous proximity and occasional cooperation, not stable mount-control or a demonstrated command mechanism (ADWD 71).

**VERIFIED:** A lone scout sees Drogon, freezes, and flees. Daenerys follows his route on Drogon; a larger group of riders and horses also flees at the dragon’s approach. After Drogon kills and eats a horse, about fifty mounted warriors emerge from the smoke. The narrative identifies their leader as Khal Jhaqo, but ends before speech, recognition, demand, or violence (ADWD 71).

## Dothraki custom: evidence boundary

**VERIFIED:** At Vaes Dothrak, the dosh khaleen are former khaleesis whose khal husbands died; the text describes them as being sent there when a new khal succeeds, and says even powerful khals respect their authority (AGOT 46). Daenerys’s remaining bloodriders, after Drogo’s death, describe escorting her to Vaes Dothrak and the crones as the limit of what their norms permit (AGOT 64). In ADWD 71, Daenerys herself expects that, at best, a scout would send her to the dosh khaleen.

**INFERENCE:** The custom supplies a real coercive/legitimating pressure, especially for an exposed widow encountered by a khalasar, but it does not by itself establish that Jhaqo must execute it, that every rider will agree, or that Daenerys cannot negotiate, flee, or use Drogon’s presence to change the immediate encounter. The published text has not yet shown Jhaqo make any choice.

**CHARACTER CLAIM / MEMORY:** Daenerys recalls that the dragons’ birth spared her from being taken to the dosh khaleen after Drogo’s death and recalls Jhaqo’s breakaway. Those memories are highly relevant to her fear and tactic, but they do not report Jhaqo’s current beliefs or command structure (ADWD 71).

**THEORY TO AVOID ADOPTING:** “A living dragon automatically makes Daenerys khal of Jhaqo’s people.” The scout’s and riders’ fear supports deterrent leverage. Counterevidence is Drogon’s unreliability and the absence of an on-page recognition or submission. The distinguishing event is Jhaqo’s first response after seeing Daenerys and Drogon together.

## Decision gates before a Meereen-facing policy

1. **Survival and custody.** Food, water, clothing, treatment, a horse, guards, or physical confinement must be established before Daenerys can plausibly travel or give sustained orders. Her last physical state makes a lone overland return an unresolved logistical problem.
2. **First speech and status claim.** Jhaqo must either hear and answer her identity/status claim or act before such an exchange. A dragon at her side makes neither reception nor subjection predetermined.
3. **Drogon’s practical availability.** A proposed escape, return flight, or threat must show the particular moment of contact and compliance. Past occasional response cannot be generalized into a controllable air force.
4. **Khalasar decision.** Riders’ fear is not an institutional decision. Any escort, detention, alliance, command transfer, or split needs the relevant people to witness/accept it and needs an immediate means of feeding and moving them.
5. **News from Meereen.** Daenerys cannot choose an informed response to Hizdahr’s custody, Quentyn’s death, released dragons, siege developments, or a naval arrival until a credible carrier bridges the grass sea/city separation. Conversely, Meereen cannot premise its choices on her Jhaqo contact.
6. **Route and time.** A return to Slaver’s Bay requires either a demonstrated ride/escort, a chosen river route, or a specific flight. Each reaches a Meereen whose government and military situation may have changed independently.

## Reconciliation result

The book leaves Daenerys at a three-way contact boundary: an exhausted individual, an autonomous dragon, and a khalasar led by a former subordinate who has not yet spoken. Her political identity and dragon association create possibilities unavailable to an ordinary captive, but neither is a completed command relationship. Meereen’s crisis must continue on its own information horizon until a physical contact or message closes the gap. Independent challenge remains open.
