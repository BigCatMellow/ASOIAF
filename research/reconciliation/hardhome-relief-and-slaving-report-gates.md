No single row can be silently substituted for another. A ship leaving Eastwatch is not a ship returning; a field report is not a rescue; a Braavos tavern account is not news received at the Wall; and an announced land ranging is not a party on the road.

## 1. Why people are at Hardhome, and why its condition is already uncertain

**VERIFIED:** The Prologue places the post-Wall dispersal before the present Hardhome crisis. Varamyr remembers that hundreds went with Mother Mole after she urged survivors to seek the sea because ships would carry them south. He does not accompany them, and the scene does not follow their arrival or welfare (`ADWD Prologue`).

**CHARACTER CLAIM:** Interrogated free folk tell Jon that Mother Mole had a vision of ships arriving to carry people across the narrow sea. Jon calls their trust desperate rather than proving the vision (`ADWD 39, Jon`).

**VERIFIED:** Jon’s map-based case for a rescue is material rather than prophetic: Hardhome has a sheltered bay/natural harbor and nearby wood, stone, fish, seals, and sea cows. Those assets do not erase the ruin’s fearful history, winter weather, hunger, or the distance from Wall resources (`ADWD 39, Jon`).

**INFERRED:** Hardhome is therefore both an intelligible rendezvous point and a severe bottleneck. Its population may be concentrated enough to find, but ships, food, weather, and security limit what any rescue can actually move.

## 2. The sea operation: observed dispatches, not a completed rescue

### Preparation and departure

1. **VERIFIED:** Jon negotiates use of Tycho Nestoris’s three ships. Combined with the Eastwatch vessels, an impressed Ibbenese whaler, an impressed Pentoshi trading galley, and three repaired Lysene warships, he counts eleven ships; he acknowledges that this is inadequate for the people at Hardhome (`ADWD 44, Jon`).
2. **VERIFIED:** He plans orders for Cotter: take the eleven ships to Hardhome and bring back as many people as possible, with women and children first. The same thought records that Jon is deciding whether he should personally go; a draft order is not yet a sailing scene (`ADWD 44, Jon`).
3. **VERIFIED:** Bad weather initially confines the rescue fleet to Eastwatch. Jon knows a storm is raging on the Bay of Seals (`ADWD 49, Jon`).
4. **VERIFIED:** An Eastwatch message later says calm seas allowed departure that morning: Cotter commands from Talon, Tattersalt is second on Blackbird, Ser Glendon Hewett remains at Eastwatch, and twenty ravens plus Maester Harmune sail with the fleet. The message identifies three Braavosi, four Lyseni, and four Watch ships, while warning that two Lyseni vessels are barely seaworthy (`ADWD 49, Jon`).

The letters and personnel matter. Cotter cannot read or write, so Harmune writes Pyke’s words; Cotter’s mark establishes how he assents to the later dispatch, not that every fact in it is independently verified. Aboard ravens offer possible communication, not a guaranteed, timely, or surviving channel.

### The later Talon report

**VERIFIED:** Clydas gives Jon a parchment which reports six ships at Hardhome, Blackbird lost with all hands, two Lyseni ships aground on Skane, and Talon taking water. The text directly depicts receipt of the parchment, not the events at sea. Its stated authorial chain is “From Talon, by hand of Maester Harmune,” with Cotter’s angry mark (`ADWD 58, Jon`).

**CHARACTER CLAIM (the message’s field content):** The report says wildlings are eating their dead; dead things are in woods and water; Braavosi captains will take only women and children; people call them slavers; an attempted taking of Storm Crow was defeated at a cost of six crew and many wildlings; and eight ravens remain. It asks for land help because storms have wrecked the seas (`ADWD 58, Jon`).

**VERIFIED:** Jon himself calculates the reported reduction from eleven vessels to six and treats the message as grievous. His reaction establishes decision pressure at Castle Black, not the objective accuracy of every reported casualty, ship position, cause, or supernatural observation.

### Immediate sea limits

| Boundary | Consequence that remains required |
| --- | --- |
| Capacity | A stated preference for women and children does not tell us who boards, how captains choose, whether families agree, or whether any ship has room after crew, damage, and provisions. |
| Weather and damage | “At Hardhome” is neither a safe anchorage nor a return voyage. Talon is taking water; ships are lost/aground; another storm can change every available route. |
| Security | The report’s dead things and the Storm Crow clash make embarkation a security problem. Neither proves that all free folk or sailors share one account of the violence. |
| Communications | Eight reported ravens are a remaining resource, not eight delivered messages. A later sender, content, bird, weather route, recipient, and credited receipt must be shown before another theater can know more. |

## 3. The land rescue is a second plan, not a consequence already underway

**VERIFIED:** In the final Jon chapter, Selyse refuses aid, and Jon says the Watch will handle the crisis. He initially means to lead the ranging himself (`ADWD 69, Jon`).

**VERIFIED:** Jon considers concrete constraints before he calls the meeting: a large escort may protect people but delay movement; food for starving thousands requires carts/wagons and draft animals; sick and starved evacuees would slow the return; coastal travel from Eastwatch is quicker/less wooded but exposed to sleet, hail, freezing rain, and autumn storms, while a route from Castle Black crosses the haunted forest through deep snow. He has not chosen one plan in the narrated scene (`ADWD 69, Jon`).

**VERIFIED:** After reading the Pink Letter, Jon tells the Shieldhall that the Watch will go to Hardhome while he will go south. He names Tormund to lead the ranging and says he has promised him as many men as needed (`ADWD 69, Jon`).

**UNKNOWN:** No page supplies Tormund’s final route, Watch/free-folk roster, wagons, animals, rations, guards, instructions to Eastwatch, start time, or departure. The subsequent violence occurs before either Jon’s southern ride or the Hardhome ranging is shown to leave. See the linked Watch-command audit for the distinct custody and command implications of that violence.

**INFERRED:** Tormund’s familiarity with the free folk can make him a plausible leader, but it cannot solve food weight, injured people, weather, the distance, or authority over Watch resources. Treating “Tormund will lead” as a completed rescue skips every material constraint Jon identifies.

## 4. Braavos receives a different, contested slaving account

**VERIFIED:** In Braavos, three Lyseni sailors from the storm-wracked Goodheart tell their story at Pynto’s. Arya hears them while acting as the blind girl; the next day she tells the Kindly Man what she says she learned. The Goodheart has limped into Braavos and been seized by Sealord guards (`ADWD 45, The Blind Girl`).

**CHARACTER CLAIM, twice mediated:** Arya says Goodheart carried hundreds of enslaved women and children. Her account attributes the underlying information to the sailors, who say two storm-driven Lyseni pirates, Goodheart and Elephant, anchored off Hardhome for repairs; accepted women and children while free-folk men were hungry; then confined and bound those passengers to sell them in Lys. The sailors say a later storm separated the ships, Goodheart was forced into Braavos, Elephant may have reached Lys, and Lyseni believe it may return with more ships because slave prices are rising (`ADWD 45, The Blind Girl`).

The source therefore gives the report substantial narrative importance but a deliberately constrained evidentiary chain: alleged victims → unnamed/partially described sailors → Arya’s listening and summary → the Kindly Man. The seizure is observed/reported in the scene; the Hardhome conduct, captives’ number, Elephant’s course, and future slaving expedition remain claims within that chain.

## 5. Receipt gates before any continuation may connect the theatres

1. **Field-to-Wall gate:** A Talon survivor or raven must physically bring a report; Castle Black must receive and credit it. `ADWD 58` clears this gate once, for the particular Harmune/Cotter message only.
2. **Wall-to-field gate:** A named sender must choose a response, then get a raven/carrier through weather to a named ship or party. No post-dispatch response or receipt is narrated.
3. **Land-ranging gate:** Tormund needs a concrete force, food, transport, route, weather window, and departure. No announced leadership substitutes for them.
4. **Embarkation/custody gate:** Each prospective evacuee must reach a particular ship, be admitted or refused, and survive the immediate crossing. “Women and children first” is an intended triage rule, not a manifest or a moral resolution.
5. **Braavos custody gate:** Sealord guards’ seizure of Goodheart must become an examined cargo, identified victims, a legal/political choice, and a subsequent carrier before the reported crime can produce reliable external news.
6. **Cross-theater gate:** Braavos and the Wall require a specific messenger or maritime/raven chain. Arya’s private knowledge cannot arrive at Castle Black by reader inference.

## Competing readings held open

### A. The Watch’s sea mission can still deliver a meaningful evacuation

**Support:** Eleven ships actually sail; the later message places six at Hardhome, and Jon’s original order prioritizes people least able to travel overland.

**Counterweight:** The report itself describes losses, leaks, storm damage, conflict, and capacity limits. It does not show a single passenger safely landed at Eastwatch.

**Test:** A later observed manifest, arrival, eyewitness, or reliable vessel report specifying people and destination.

### B. The land ranging is now the only realistic rescue

**Support:** Cotter explicitly asks for land help, and Jon identifies the sea as storm-wracked; Tormund is assigned to lead a ranging.

**Counterweight:** Jon’s own logistics analysis makes a mass return slow, provision-heavy, and exposed; the text does not show the ranging beginning.

**Test:** A mustered force with supplies and a route, followed by an observed departure and contact.

### C. The Goodheart report proves a coordinated slaving operation that will determine Hardhome’s fate

**Support:** It offers a coherent profit motive and two named vessels; the reported captives’ composition resembles the vulnerable people both Jon and Cotter prioritize.

**Counterweight:** Its Hardhome narrative comes from sailors with direct interest in their own position after a seizure and reaches the reader through Arya’s summary. Elephant’s fate and any return are expressly uncertain.

**Test:** Cargo testimony, Braavosi investigation, an authenticated ship record, a direct sighting, or a message joining Braavos to the Wall.

## Prohibited shortcuts

1. Do not say Hardhome has been rescued, abandoned, reached overland, or evacuated; none of those outcomes is narrated.
2. Do not convert the Talon dispatch into omniscient fact about every casualty, ship, wight, or free folk decision.
3. Do not make Tormund’s ranging depart merely because Jon names him leader.
4. Do not treat the Goodheart’s seizure as a rescue, a trial, a liberation, or a warning that has reached Jon, Cotter, Stannis, or the free folk.
5. Do not identify Goodheart or Elephant with a named ship in Cotter’s force without evidence.
6. Do not treat a prophecy, Melisandre’s assertion that all ships are lost, or Mother Mole’s reported vision as a verified logistical outcome. Melisandre’s statement is a **CHARACTER CLAIM** from fire-reading, and Jon explicitly notes prior interpretive mistakes (`ADWD 69, Jon`).
