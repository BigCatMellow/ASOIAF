# Stark survivors — recognition and consent gates

## Core constraint

At the five-book endpoint, “a Stark is alive” is not one transferable fact. Each proposed restoration requires separate proof of identity, a safe recipient, a route, and the survivor’s own willing participation. A claimant can be politically useful without being physically safe or consenting to the role assigned.

## Three recognition sequences that should remain independent

1. **Bolton imposture:** Jeyne’s testimony can undermine a marriage-based claim before anyone learns where real Arya is. It produces a survivor-protection problem first, not automatic Stark unity.
2. **Manderly/Rickon mission:** A genuine boy’s recovery, if it occurs, is separate from Stannis, Jon’s status, and Petyr’s Sansa design. It may create new rivalry rather than solve it.
3. **Vale disclosure:** A public Sansa can change northern legitimacy only after Vale safety, Petyr’s leverage, Harry/Robert conditions, and travel are addressed. Her identity need not be revealed at the same time as any northern development.

## Tests for a credible Stark political convergence

- Who saw the person and can distinguish them from a pretender, rumor, glamour, or desired symbol?
- How does the news travel, and who sees it first?
- What does the survivor know about siblings, deaths, impostors, and current claimants?
- Is the proposed role freely accepted, negotiated, or coerced?
- What material resources—food, escort, castle, ships, guardians—make the claim more than a declaration?

## Provisional consequence

The likely early dramatic use of Stark survival is not a synchronized reunion but **competing partial proofs**: a false identity exposed, a child sought, a hidden girl considered for marriage, a wounded Watch commander, and a remote magical witness. Those proofs may strengthen anti-Bolton or anti-Lannister politics while also making succession and guardianship harder. Independent challenge remains open.
