## Command is a live three-way problem

### 1. Aegon's claim and agency

**VERIFIED:** Tyrion tells the boy that Daenerys may not accept an unequal marriage; Aegon then proposes sailing west so she can come to him (ADWD 22, 24). At Griffin's Roost he supports Connington's fast operation, but rejects being merely protected and says he will lead the attack on Storm's End (ADWD 61).

**INFERRED:** The invasion is neither simply a passive Varys/Illyrio imposition nor an already independent royal government. Aegon has exercised consequential choice, while Connington, Lemore, Duckfield, the captains, and the company still supply the information, force, and protection that make that choice actionable.

**Decision gate:** Before a public victory can make his kingship credible, Connington must accept, modify, or refuse Aegon's request for battlefield command. Each has a distinct cost: refusal can preserve the claimant while confirming that he is a ward; permission can give him visible agency while risking the entire political project; a staged or divided command requires enough trust that the text has not yet demonstrated.

### 2. Connington and the Golden Company

**VERIFIED:** The company is disciplined enough to land and take Griffin's Roost by surprise, but the force there is incomplete. Fewer than half of the ten thousand who sailed have arrived; horses are scarce; elephant ships are missing when Connington assesses the position (ADWD 61). Other detachments move against Crow's Nest and Rain House, and the initial force relies on speed and suppressed ravens.

**VERIFIED:** Strickland argues for waiting for men, horses, elephants, lesser lords, and reconnaissance; Connington chooses an eleven-day departure for Storm's End and says he will take it "by guile" (ADWD 61). The mechanism is deliberately not supplied. The council at King's Landing receives a report of the movement, not confirmation of its success (ADWD Epilogue).

**INFERRED:** Strickland's caution is not disproved by the first surprise seizure. The same dispersed shipping and incomplete muster that make a quick stroke attractive also constrain garrisoning, supply, scouting, and recovery if the guile fails. Connington's momentum case is genuine, but so is the logistical objection.

**Decision gate:** A field plan needs an on-page means of gaining entrance, maintaining secrecy, securing prisoners, and responding to a failed ruse. Do not replace those missing links with an assumed assault, surrender, or local rising.

### 3. The concealed illness

**VERIFIED:** Connington knows he was exposed at the Sorrows, sees numb, discolored fingers in ADWD 24, later treats the advancing marks with vinegar, and privately rejects a politically useful marriage because he expects death and fears exposure (ADWD 24, 61). He tells neither Aegon nor the officers in the narrated endpoint.

**INFERRED:** The illness adds a private deadline to his existing guilt over Stoney Sept and loyalty to Rhaegar. It helps explain why speed appeals to him, but it does not prove a particular tactic, a future massacre, or that urgency alone defeats rational command concerns.

**Counterpressure:** His Stoney Sept memory includes Myles Toyne's claim that Tywin would have burned the town. Connington treats that counterfactual as a lesson in his own failure (ADWD 61), but he has not committed the imagined atrocity on-page; at Griffin's Roost he orders captives not needlessly killed and offers the garrison safety for fealty.

**Decision gate:** Any later hardening must show both what Connington has learned about his condition and why he chooses a particular coercive act. Disease, grief, and a remembered remark do not automatically supply either disclosure, consent from his force, or brutality.

## Identity theory: evidence, alternative, and test

**THEORY under test: Aegon is a Blackfyre rather than Elia's surviving son.** The substitute story, Connington's conviction, and Varys's final advocacy support the proposition that an organized restoration project exists. The Golden Company's Blackfyre origins, Bittersteel's revered skull, and the preexisting Toyne/Illyrio/Varys arrangement provide a serious alternative political context (ADWD 24). They do **not** identify the boy's parents. Conversely, Connington's attachment and Varys's speech can be sincere, strategic, or both; neither is biological verification.

**Ordinary explanation:** He is the surviving prince whom Connington believes he is, and the company joins because a Targaryen restoration offers land, legitimacy, and return to Westeros.

**What would discriminate:** independent evidence tied to infancy; a knowledgeable account with a motive and details that can be checked; or evidence inconsistent with the substitution narrative. Popularity, military success, dragon association, or a marriage alliance would not resolve parentage by themselves.

## Immediate continuation constraints

1. Begin the Storm's End sequence with the unresolved command answer, incomplete force, proposed-but-undescribed guile, and distinct local/capital information states.
2. Keep reported movement toward the castle separate from a verified capture. A later council response must be based on a specific carrier and report, not reader knowledge.
3. Let Dorne decide only after a documented receipt and credibility assessment; the prepared letter is not an alliance or marriage.
4. Treat greyscale disclosure as a decision with medical, political, and ethical consequences rather than a hidden timer that mechanically dictates conquest.
5. Keep Aegon's biological identity open even if characters choose to support, oppose, or exploit his claim.

Independent challenge remains open: the five books end before Connington answers Aegon's demand, before the Storm's End stratagem is performed, and before any recipient is shown testing the claim, the letter, or the illness.
