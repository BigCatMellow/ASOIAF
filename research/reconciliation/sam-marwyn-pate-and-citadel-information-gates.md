- Record role: `RESEARCH / RECONCILIATION`.
- Scope: the published-novel Oldtown endpoint only: AFFC Prologue and AFFC 45 (Samwell). It does not assign an outcome to Oldtown's coastal war or duplicate the force/contact records for it.
- Status: `WORKING — PRIVATE RECEIPT PRECEDES ANY INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE`.
- Related: [Oldtown information custody](oldtown-information-custody-and-warning-gates.md), [Oldtown contact gates](oldtown-ironborn-contact-gates.md), [Samwell at Oldtown](../characters/samwell-oldtown.md), and [the alchemist identity case](../theories/cases/oldtown-alchemist.md).

## The endpoint is a divided information system

**VERIFIED:** Sam reaches the Citadel carrying Aemon's letters and a first-hand account of the Wall; before he reaches the acting Seneschal, Alleras takes him to Marwyn. Sam then recounts much of the Wall crisis to Alleras, Marwyn, and a pasty-faced novice, but withholds Bran's journey and Jon's infant exchange (AFFC 45).

**VERIFIED:** Marwyn's black glass candle is alight with unusual visible properties, and he announces that he will sail with the *Cinnamon Wind* toward Slaver's Bay. The young man who offers Sam a cell near Walgrave's ravens identifies himself as Pate (AFFC 45).

**UNKNOWN:** An official Citadel officer receives Aemon's letters or Sam's warning; any raven is sent; Marwyn actually boards, departs, survives the voyage, or meets Daenerys; the young man is the prologue's Pate, the alchemist, or either person's agent; or a glass-candle communication is sent, received, and correctly understood.

This audit concerns **who possesses particular knowledge and what authorization, movement, and receipt must follow**. It does not treat access to a room, a candle, or ravens as a completed message.

## Sequential gates rather than a single "Citadel response"

### Aemon's letters and Sam's Wall warning

1. Sam must preserve the letters and decide how much oral testimony to offer. He cannot honestly offer the facts he is still concealing as though the whole Citadel received them.
2. A named recipient must take delivery: Theobald, Walgrave, another archmaester, a clerk, Marwyn, or a different defined actor. Alleras and Marwyn's private receipt cannot silently become an official record.
3. That recipient must assess source reliability, distinguish Sam's direct observations from Aemon's interpretations and Wall reports, and choose investigation, dismissal, secrecy, or action.
4. Any outward warning needs a written or spoken content, an authorized messenger or raven, an identifiable destination, dispatch, and later receipt. A Ravenry's physical existence skips none of those links.

**Interpretive constraint:** Alleras's prediction that Theobald will dismiss Sam is a `CHARACTER CLAIM` about likely reception. Marwyn's advice to hide dragons and prophecy may be prudent, self-interested, or paranoid; it is not proof an official murder or censorship decision already exists.

### Marwyn and the glass candle

1. The candle's odd flame is **VERIFIED**, as is Marwyn's unusual prior knowledge that Sam is coming.
2. The alleged capabilities—seeing across distance, entering dreams, and speaking from afar—are Marwyn's account of the old use of such candles (AFFC 45). Alleras's attribution supports an inference of some active means of foreknowledge, but does not demonstrate the precise mechanism.
3. Before the candle can bridge Oldtown to the Wall, Meereen, or another named point, the source would need to show a user, intended counterpart, intelligible content, receipt, and an opportunity for the recipient to act. A mysterious vision, if one occurs, is not automatically a trustworthy message.
4. Marwyn's decision to sail is a conventional second route, subject to a ship, crew, departure, Ironborn risk, destination, Daenerys's unknown location, and her willingness to receive a stranger. It cannot be collapsed into verified magical contact.

### Pate, the key, and ravenry security

1. The prologue establishes a breach: the original Pate voluntarily yields Walgrave's key because the alchemist offers money that would let him leave Oldtown with Rosey. Its immediate use is unshown.
2. The later Pate's name, setting, and timing support a **strong INFERENCE** that an impostor has replaced or appropriated the earlier acolyte's identity. The text does not show a transformation, identify the alchemist as Jaqen H'ghar, or prove that the original Pate is dead rather than merely apparently dead.
3. Even a proven impostor would still need physical access at a relevant time, a particular target (books, locks, messages, or people), and a successful action before the reader may treat the ravenry as compromised.
4. Conversely, a key's earlier theft does not establish that all keys, ravens, archives, or maesters are hostile. Ordinary negligence, limited access, competing patrons, or an unconnected objective remain serious alternatives.

## Continuation guardrails

- Do not give the Night's Watch, Daenerys, Dorne, or an official Citadel council information merely because the reader has seen Sam's chapter. Each needs an independently depicted channel and receipt.
- Do not force Sam to disclose Bran or the infant exchange; the chapter specifically preserves those compartments.
- Do not make Pate's proposed cell proof of either safety or sabotage. Sam lacks the prologue's reader-only context.
- Do not treat Marwyn's conspiracy history, glass-candle mechanics, or projected rival emissary as settled fact. They are useful hypotheses with defined tests, not a license for instant coordination.
