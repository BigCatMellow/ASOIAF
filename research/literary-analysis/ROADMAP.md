## Parent outcome

An accessible, evidence-led analysis explaining **how the five published novels tell their story**, including what techniques appear to accomplish, plausible competing readings, trade-offs, and limits. Results are reusable as literary criticism and as craft lessons *without copying Martin's voice*.

## Cross-book research questions (queue, not promises of conclusions)

1. **Architecture:** Which chapter transitions carry information, irony or emotional counterpoint? Does the *Feast/Dance* geographical split strengthen or weaken that pattern?
2. **Point of view:** How does selective access influence audience trust in Cersei, Jaime, Theon, Sansa and other narrators differently?
3. **Character:** When does an identity shift become legible through decisions rather than internal self-description?
4. **Ethics:** What differs between Ned's, Jon's, Davos's, Stannis's and Daenerys's constructions of duty? Find an example that resists the apparent pattern.
5. **Symbolic design:** Which recurring motifs visibly change meaning, and which apparent parallels are too flexible to test?
6. **Worldbuilding:** When do distance, winter, food, rank and institutions become drivers of plot and moral choice?
7. **Emotion:** How do anticipation, off-page violence and aftermath redistribute reader attention?
8. **Composition:** What can the novels themselves establish about writing method? Which claims need dated statements from Martin or scholarship?
