# Literary-analysis method and evidence rules

- Record role: `RESEARCH / METHOD`
- Status: `ACTIVE`
- Parent: [README.md](README.md)
- Governing source rules: [../../SOURCES.md](../../SOURCES.md)

## 1. Start with a question, not a presumed pattern

Examples: Why does the story move from Will to Bran? How does Cersei's focalization change the apparent trustworthiness of information? What work do Brienne's seemingly slow travel chapters do?

Record **what would count against** the proposed effect before accepting it. Favor a bounded scene cluster or sharply defined cross-book comparison over another undirected 344-chapter pass.

## 2. Three distinct questions

| Kind of claim | Evidence needed | Correct form |
|---|---|---|
| **What the text depicts** | Five-book original with book/chapter/POV locator | `VERIFIED (scene)`, `CHARACTER CLAIM`, or `UNKNOWN` |
| **What the arrangement or detail does for a reader** | Concrete formal feature plus explained effect, against competing accounts | `INTERPRETATION`; scope of effect and counterexample stated |
| **What Martin deliberately intended or how he composed it** | Sourced interview, essay, draft evidence or documented statement | `AUTHOR STATEMENT` with date/source; inference about intent remains `INFERRED` |

A reader effect can be persuasive without evidence of authorial intention. A character's inner belief is not automatically reliable narration. A later revelation may alter a reader's retrospective interpretation without turning every earlier detail into deliberate foreshadowing.

## 3. Source hierarchy

1. **Primary text:** the original, owner-supplied bundled EPUB, identified by [SOURCES.md](../../SOURCES.md). Record `AGOT Prologue`, `AGOT 1 (Bran)`, etc.; no edition-neutral page numbers assumed.
2. **Navigation and research aids:** [chapter index](../chapter-index.md), [first-pass notes](../../Source/Main-Series/), [clue index](../source-clue-index.md), character/theory/continuity files. Useful for discovery; verify serious formal claims against original passages when possible.
3. **Attributed context:** Martin's essays, interviews, production history and relevant scholarship. Cite stable links, author and date. Differentiate direct statements from a critic's description or fan attribution.
4. **Community/adaptation:** contrasting interpretations, *not* automatically primary evidence for the novels.

Do not silently substitute chapter notes for a fresh examination of sentence-level prose. Record whether the original scene was directly rechecked. No new full-text transcriptions or extended quotations in this directory.

## 4. Minimum study structure

- **Question and finding:** one bounded thesis, confidence, and review state.
- **Corpus and method:** exact books/chapters; primary scene check versus note-only comparison.
- **Observed construction:** POV, ordering, repetition, framing, language, juxtaposition, structure.
- **Mechanism → reader effect:** explicitly connect a formal decision to a plausible effect. Distinguish impact on first-time versus rereading audiences.
- **Counterreading / limits:** serious alternative explanation, disconfirming passage, and what would change the conclusion.
- **Cross-book implications:** comparison only where supported; avoid treating recurrence as proven influence or destiny.
- **Evidence trail:** links to source notes and book/chapter locators, with no copied passages beyond very brief illustrative fragments.
- **Next verification / reviewer questions:** strongest unresolved claim first.

## 5. Quality tests

1. **Causal and formal specificity:** Is the claim about *how* a scene operates, or merely a plot summary?
2. **POV discipline:** Does it separate reader knowledge, focal-character knowledge and actual observation?
3. **Rival explanation:** Could genre convention, coincidence, ordinary scene economy or a different reader response explain the same choice?
4. **Negative case:** Can we locate a comparable scene where the alleged technique is absent or works differently?
5. **Scale:** Does a one-scene observation unjustifiably become a universal rule about Martin?
6. **Authorship discipline:** Do not say “Martin intended” without attributed evidence; write “the text invites” or “one reading is” instead.
7. **Independence:** Substantive theses remain `PROVISIONAL` until another reviewer critically checks their evidence and counterexamples; the author should not certify their own review.
8. **Integrity:** Verify locators and Markdown links after publication. Do not overclaim an exhaustive prose audit from the 344 first-pass summaries.

## 6. Practical cadence

Proceed by comparative questions with a limited chapter set, write one canonical essay, check it against original passages, challenge it, then expand only if that check exposes a useful missing case. Larger synthesis must link to tested essays rather than silently treating every provisional observation as settled.
