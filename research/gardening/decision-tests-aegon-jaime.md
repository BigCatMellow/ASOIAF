## Experiment A — ADWD 61: a prince who insists on leading an assault

**Setting already shown:** Aegon has brought companions and some delayed elephants to Griffin's Roost. He objects to staying safe and insists he lead the intended Storm's End action.

**Dramatic interests:**
- Aegon wants to prove he is worthy of loyalty, not merely a figurehead.
- Connington wants to preserve the irreplaceable claimant, win decisively before his disease progresses, and avoid failure like the Battle of the Bells.
- Strickland wants the scattered company assembled and supplied before spending its troops.

| Plausible decision | Immediate consequence | New pressure a later scene must answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connington openly denies Aegon command | Preserves apparent safety | Does the prince submit, assert royal authority, or privately recruit Flowers/Rivers? |
| Connington offers a controlled symbolic leadership role | Saves face but requires stage-managing risk and secrecy | Could cautious captains accept it? Does Aegon understand it is symbolic? |
| Connington allows Aegon real tactical command | Validates Aegon's agency and inspires some men | What happens if a young ruler miscalculates under fire; who is morally responsible for exposing him? |

**Counterexample to our expected direction:** If Aegon's orders are sound, Connington's paternalism may be the greater weakness. Do not assume youth produces defeat.

**Open collateral issue:** Ronnet Connington's imprisoned kin are potential hostages, but exploiting them could move Jon closer to the very brutality he believes might have saved Rhaegar.

## Experiment B — ADWD 48: a knight hears an impossible invitation

**Setting already shown:** Pennytree, after negotiations with House Blackwood. Brienne returns visibly injured and asks Jaime to ride alone to an allegedly nearby Sansa, citing the 'Hound' as threat. Jaime's reaction has **not** been written.

**Dramatic interests:**
- Jaime wants to find/protect Sansa and honor his promise, but has learned to distrust coercive power and concealment.
- Brienne wants to honor Catelyn, Jaime and her protection of Podrick. Some of these aims may be incompatible.
- Stoneheart wants Jaime delivered; her agents need not agree how he should be judged.

| Plausible decision | Immediate consequence | Problem it creates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jaime trusts Brienne and follows alone | Places him within potential reach of Stoneheart | Brienne may be seen as betraying the person who trusted her most; what can she truthfully say? |
| Jaime probes the contradiction and asks for evidence | Preserves discretion and grants Brienne space to reveal danger | If captives are held, every delay may endanger them; can Jaime speak candidly within earshot of his men? |
| Jaime refuses to ride alone, offers protection and escorts | Defends his men and resists an implausible claim | His refusal may abandon Brienne, Podrick or Hyle to Stoneheart's ultimatum and undermine his oath. |

**Counterexample to automatic ambush:** Jaime has reasons to notice the mismatch between Sandor Clegane's reputation, the 'Hound' name and the claim Sansa is just a day away; nevertheless no narrated realization follows Brienne's last sentence.

## Shared moral test — when does efficiency become cruelty?

In ADWD 61, Connington wonders whether Tywin's mass violence at Stoney Sept would have prevented Rhaegar's death. In AFFC 44 / ADWD 48, Jaime ends sieges without storming while using terrible threats and remembering Tywin's suppression of rebellious houses. Both men inherit the temptation to call ruthless action 'necessary'. This parallel can be developed thematically **without requiring them to meet**.

## Stop rules for future drafts

- Don't skip over the final question each POV has yet to answer.
- Don't move a field army, prince, northern claimant, or hostage without an evidenced route and interval.
- If a selected branch makes a character suddenly omniscient, return to THINK.
- If both choices are plausible, draft *two short original scene experiments* to discover which produces richer consequences; don't retroactively falsify earlier characterization.
