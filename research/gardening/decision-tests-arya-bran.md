## A. ADWD 64: Arya's first apprenticeship with Izembaro

**Real starting condition:** Arya was given back her own face after fulfilling an assigned killing and told to attend Izembaro the next morning, wearing a fresh face. The exact training and the mummers' work have not yet happened in the five-book text.

**Opposed wants:** To become dangerous enough never to be powerless; to keep Jon, Needle and the Starks alive within her; to feel at home with her Braavosi friends; to do what she believes is just. The Faceless Men demand obedience and reject personal lists.

| First decision | Immediate cost | What a second scene must face |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work faithfully as the mummers' apprentice | New friends and staged identities offer genuine belonging | An assignment may ask her to observe, deceive or harm someone she regards as innocent |
| Protect an ordinary Braavosi person against the interests of an assignment | Arya chooses local compassion over invisible employers | The kindly man can question her fitness and the person she protects may not trust her |
| Explore the Hardhome story without exposing her own wolf sight | Could help endangered northerners through ships/merchants | Does she reveal why she cares about the faraway place, risking her claimed new identity? |

**Strong counterexample to an automatic homecoming:** Arya liked being Cat of the Canals and may value Braavosi relationships even while hiding Needle. A trip home must have a credible push or pull.

## B. ADWD 34: Bran using Hodor without consent

**Real starting condition:** The Hodor practice is ongoing and undisclosed. Bran can skinchange Summer/ravens; Meera mourns and Jojen expects death; Bran hopes the weirwoods will let him see Ned again. He has witnessed old human sacrifice.

| Next decision | Immediate cost | What a second scene must face |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confess Hodor possession to Meera and ask forgiveness | Exposes abuse, risks losing mobility and his friends' trust | Is he willing to **stop** even if Hodor cannot safely navigate a cave crisis without his help? |
| Continue and rationalize possession as 'helping' | Physical convenience and control continue | Does Hodor withdraw further; will someone notice, and what will Bran learn about trust? |
| Prioritize visions over living friends | May uncover useful forgotten history | Jojen and Meera have needs the trees cannot automatically satisfy; seeing more could cost the very family Bran longs for |

**Counterexample to 'Bran sees all':** Brynden says the trees remember different time from humans; the vision of Ned looking up is ambiguous, and Bran's own questions still exceed the power he has mastered.

## A joint theme with genuinely different outcomes

The Faceless Men ask Arya to erase herself **in order to use other people's deaths without personal motive**. Bloodraven teaches Bran to enter other consciousnesses and remembered time, while Bran uses Hodor's body **for personal desire and practical need**. Each system requires a claim about when extraordinary powers are permitted. The novel dramatizes contradictions in both claims.

That is not a reason to force a reunion or a battle between the siblings. Their **first adjacent decisions** can occur at opposite ends of the world; only later should credible paths transmit consequences.
