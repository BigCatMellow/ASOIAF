# Gardening test — Castle Black after Jon’s stabbing

- Record role: `THINK / PLAN EXPERIMENT`.
- Status: `REVERSIBLE / NO STORY BRANCH ADOPTED`.
- Evidence: ADWD 58–69; [North/Wall endpoint handoff](../reconciliation/north-wall-endpoint-handoff.md).

## Starting condition

Jon is stabbed amid Wun Wun’s violence after declaring a southward ride. The text does not narrate who immediately controls the yard, whether Jon dies, which witnesses can give a coherent account, or whether any message leaves Castle Black. Some free-folk boys have already been sent toward Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower; their individual custody is not confirmed.

## Experiment A — first report, not first verdict

| First decision | Immediate cost | Required second decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Watch officer announces Jon is dead and claims command | Rapid order may prevent panic | Who saw enough to authenticate death and the attackers’ account; does an announcement inflame free folk? |
| A neutral/uncertain figure secures the injured Jon and refuses public conclusion | Invites suspicion and power vacuum | Who guards doors, weapons, ravens, and prisoners while factions demand a story? |
| A wildling leader treats the attack as betrayal and mobilizes | Protects perceived ally but risks civil conflict | Are hostages safe, and does retaliation reproduce Jon’s coercive bargain? |

**Counterexample:** A fast Watch victory is not guaranteed simply because the attackers say “For the Watch”; the chapter leaves on-the-ground numbers, witnesses, and loyalists uncounted.

## Experiment B — the hostage registry as an ethical command test

| Choice | Immediate gain | Consequence to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve Jon’s dispersed hostage arrangement | Signals continuity and may limit panic | Receiving garrisons may not yet know of the attack or have confirmed each child. |
| Release/transfer children as a peace gesture | Reduces coercive leverage and may gain trust | Who can authorize transport in winter; do individual parents regard it as credible? |
| Threaten hostages to compel free-folk obedience | Offers apparent short-term control | It repeats the coercion that made the Wall unstable and cannot affect dead fathers. |

## Experiment C — messages create different wars

Before any raven or rider leaves, choose its author, exact claim, recipient, evidence, and desired response. “Jon is dead,” “Marsh commands,” “wildlings attacked,” and “the Lord Commander was assassinated” are politically different messages. Eastwatch, Shadow Tower, Stannis, Hardhome, and northern lords require separate delivery/receipt paths.

## Stop rules

1. Do not begin with resurrection, funeral, election, or a settled mutiny narrative; none is published.
2. Do not give a single character the reader’s view of every stabbing, letter, hostage, or distant garrison.
3. Do not use the Pink Letter’s military claims as the first report’s established facts.
4. Do not make child custody a simple tactical token; each transfer needs a named custodian and travel route.

The experiment’s value is the conflict among safety, truth, oath, and survival—not selecting a canon answer. Independent challenge remains open.
