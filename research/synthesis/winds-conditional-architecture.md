## The smallest useful governing proposition

*The Winds of Winter* might begin with **local and imperfect responses to collapsed institutions** rather than a synchronized march toward an inevitable final battle. The books already place several protagonists at the brink of independently consequential actions. A believable continuation lets them act **before** they learn every other storyline's events.

**Working hypothesis `PROVISIONAL / NARRATIVELY PROMISING`:** Partial military successes, disputed succession, and delayed news cause the political landscape to fragment faster than anyone can reunify it. Winter and the Others then raise the cost of that fragmentation. 

**Counter-hypothesis:** A few unexpected shared interests or military defeats force local actors to cooperate early. The proposition is a research tool, not an official ending or a command to make everyone fail.

### Layer 1 — three clusters for immediate next-scene testing

These are **possible next choices**, not an assigned chapter order. Each line can be tested without borrowing events from another front.

| Cluster | First decision to dramatize | Choice A | Choice B | Character-driven cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **North / Wall** | Stannis receives people who can expose Winterfell's false Arya while an embedded Karstark faction urges attack (ADWD 62). | Question/protect Theon and Jeyne; postpone action to verify | Prioritize an assault or controlled deception to survive | Delay increases hunger and danger; immediate attack risks lies, betrayal and lives. |
| **South / legitimacy** | Aegon insists on leading an assault (ADWD 61); Connington decides how much agency the claimant gets. | Shield him through a symbolic command | Trust him with real risk and responsibility | Protecting him may infantilize him; exposing him may endanger the entire cause. |
| **Meereen / sovereignty** | Barristan governs after deposing Hizdahr, while Yunkish military threats escalate (ADWD 67/70). | Negotiate or preserve hostages while defending | Strike first and seek military success | A victory can cost the queen's chosen settlement; restraint can cost freed people and the city's defenses. |

**Independent counterexample test:** A character's prudent course might actually be the dramatic one. Stannis may prefer delay *and* gain advantage; Aegon may command competently; Barristan's coup may be partly justified. Do not engineer stupidity merely to force a crisis.

Only *after* a layer-1 decision should any of these events affect other theaters:

- **Aegon → Dorne → capital:** Connington's message must be sent and received; Arianne may evaluate him before learning Quentyn's fate. Court rumor and direct first-person observation must stay separate. [Handoffs 4–6](information-handoffs.md).
- **Stannis/Theon → Northern claims → surviving Starks:** Jeyne's truth can destabilize Bolton's marriage basis, but the boy Davos seeks and Robb's will remain independent tracks. [Handoffs 2–3, 13](information-handoffs.md).
- **Barristan/Victarion → Daenerys:** A battle, horn test, or mercenary switch may precede Dany's return; the political problem changes with the order of events. [Handoffs 9–10](information-handoffs.md).
- **Sam/Citadel → Watch/Reach:** Sam's warning must pass through real recipients while coastal war interrupts ships and messages; the North cannot assume it receives help in time. [Handoffs 7–8](information-handoffs.md).
- **Brienne/Jaime → Riverlands justice:** Stoneheart's demands may collide with Jaime's efforts to limit bloodshed; other Brotherhood members need not agree. The true Sansa remains in the Vale. [Handoffs 11–12](information-handoffs.md).

### Layer 3 — possible convergence payoffs later in *Winds* (all conditional)

1. **Northern military fortunes become a succession and food crisis.** Success against a Bolton force would be the *start* of negotiations among surviving lords and claimants, not a ready-made Stark unity.
2. **Aegon gains a reputation before Daenerys can reach Westeros.** This would make the question of who is 'rightful' political and emotional, not solvable by disclosing lineage. **Counterexample:** Connington's campaign could fail before any broad popular acclaim.
3. **Daenerys returns to the consequences of government acting in her name.** She could defend, punish, or revise Barristan's choices; the city could be unsalvageable or its peace partially recoverable. **Counterexample:** Her first commitments to Dothraki politics may consume the entire early arc.
4. **Oldtown discovers useful history while danger interrupts its transmission.** Sam's learning can matter without an all-powerful anti-dragon maester conspiracy or a magical library that tells him every answer.
5. **Stark children grow individually before gathering.** Sansa's relationship to a vulnerable child, Arya's refusal of moral indifference, and Bran's abuse of Hodor can each shape future choices without an early reunion.

## Competing macro trajectories

| Hypothesis | What it explains | Strongest objection | Trigger to upgrade/downgrade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H1: Distributed partial victories; delayed convergence** (`WORKING`) | Every faction has local motives and nearly incompatible news. Victories create food/legal consequences. | Too many fronts may postpone meaningful narrative payoff; readers need some closure. | Upgrade if first battles change government without fixing legitimacy. Downgrade if durable coalitions immediately form on-page. |
| **H2: Rapid political consolidation around an apparent restorer** (`ALTERNATE`) | Aegon, a Stark heir, or another coalition might stabilize territory quickly under winter pressure. | Underestimates Dorne's divided interests, the Faith and unresolved Stark identities. | Upgrade if actual lords and captains demonstrate coordination and the means to maintain it. |
| **H3: Severe failures force survival coalitions before dynastic settlement** (`ALTERNATE`) | Hardhome, famine, plague, the Others and naval raids have costs kings cannot ignore. | Players repeatedly prioritize oaths, power and vengeance even under threat. | Upgrade if characters begin reallocating forces away from dynastic wars; downgrade if conflict intensifies despite the danger. |

**No numeric confidence:** The evidence supports these as meaningful explanatory structures, not probabilistic forecasts.

## Anti-shortcut rules and independent review gate

- No character learns a fact because *the reader* knows it. Each key fact needs a POV observation, reliable message, interception or admitted rumor.
- No assumption that the Night Lamp succeeds, the Pink Letter is authentic, the golden boy is Blackfyre, the Vale marches, the horn works, or the Others accept diplomacy.
- AFFC and ADWD overlap; a chapter appearing later in the EPUB doesn't timestamp an unrelated POV after every earlier chapter.
- No magical revelation may erase practical food, travel, injuries, medicine or political consent.
- No television resolution adopted automatically; this is literary continuation work.
- **Success test for a scene proposal:** Does the actor make a real choice with two viable options? Can we name what they know? Does the result create a nontrivial obligation that another POV can later encounter? Would an independent skeptical reader reject any presumed fact?
