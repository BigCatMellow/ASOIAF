## Executive result: a repeated **exposure** pattern, but not four verified engineered massacres

There are **three distinct evidentiary levels**:

1. `ROBERT/ROBB CHOSE RISK:` **AGOT 55/59** show **Robb's own intentional decision to send the predominantly infantry eastern column toward Tywin**, distracting him while horsemen crossed at the Twins for Riverrun. **Roose's appointment is Robb's order**, informed by Catelyn's advice that a prudent commander would be needed. A risky Green Fork fight is **part of Robb's acknowledged strategy**, not independently evidence Roose engineered the initial split.
2. `ROOSE CHOSE DESTINATION, THEN OMITTED IT:` **ACOK 64** visibly attributes the march toward **Duskendale to Roose**; **ASOS 35** shows a major defeat and **Robb's incredulity** at the destination; **ASOS 49** sees Roose accept blame directed at **Robett Glover** and offer Glover's grief as explanation without mentioning his earlier order. This is the **strongest authenticated incident** of problematic command accountability.
3. `ROOSE REPORTS LOSSES, THEN ORDERS NEW RISK:` **ASOS 49** records a **self-interested, not directly witnessed** version of the flooded Trident crossing; **ADWD 51** shows a **fresh order** to send two mutually hostile forces toward Stannis after fighting in Winterfell. The on-page **field result is absent**, and **ADWD 46's “dead northern rivals benefit the Dreadfort”** analysis is **Theon's internal explanation**, not Roose's confession.

**Notable comparison:** **Tywin** frankly explains to **Tyrion** in **AGOT 62** that he **expected his weaker left to break**, hoping to draw what he thought would be Robb's rash main force into a flanking trap. **No comparable Roose confession exists for his own Green Fork, Ruby Ford, Duskendale or Winterfell dispositions.** We can identify Tywin's actual planned attrition with higher confidence than Roose's hypothesized four-campaign plan. His willingness to risk his own left demonstrates that a commander can do this, **not that Roose necessarily did**.

## 1. The Green Fork: the decoy force was **Robb's** original idea

### Intent and theater design — AGOT 55/59, `POV-OBSERVED DIRECT STRATEGIC DISCUSSION`

Robb explains his planned **split** to Catelyn: a limited force holds Moat Cailin, most footmen advance south along the kingsroad, and horsemen cross at the Twins, leaving Tywin confronting the **apparent main army** while Robb moves rapidly to relieve Riverrun against Jaime. He wants **a cautious independent commander** for the foot and initially considers Greatjon, then appoints **Roose on Catelyn's prompting**. **AGOT 59** confirms Roose commands the mostly infantry host as a result of Robb's instruction to advance toward Tywin.

**Knowledge gates:** `ROBB AUTHORIZED DIVERSION` and `ROOSE EXECUTED/COMMANDED FOOT` are established. **Unknown** is whether Roose later used that risky mission opportunistically to favor some houses over others. No independent on-page Northern battle council gives his troop-by-troop disposition.

### The actual battle and aftermath — AGOT 62/69/71, `OPPOSING-POV OBSERVATIONS + REPORTS`

- **AGOT 62 (Tyrion)** hears Lannister alarm horns **before dawn**, as soldiers prepare to meet the approaching northern host **closer than expected**. Bronn says the northern army stole a march in the night. That means **Roose's force *did* achieve a significant approach before the Lannisters had assembled**, contradicting an absolute claim he made no effort to use surprise. It is **not enough to know whether his march was optimally conducted, or if an earlier chance to attack during sleep existed**.
- Tyrion sees **Hornwood, Karstark, Cerwyn, Glover and Frey standards** near his section of the battle; he does **not** identify a complete formation map or establish that no Dreadfort soldier fought. This is a **local view from one Lannister combatant**, later partly obscured by chaos and injury.
- Lannister arrows strike northern advance; the Karstark spears endure mounted attacks; Clegane and Kevan push back the Northern line; Tywin's reserve breaks the remnants. **The northern defeat is witnessed**, but not its complete casualty/placement accounting.
- **Ser Addam Marbrand reports** taking **Lord Cerwyn, Ser Wylis Manderly, Harrion Karstark, four Freys**; **Lord Hornwood dead**; **Roose escaped** (AGOT 62). These are **postbattle reports to Tywin** and do not establish *exactly which men Roose personally intended to expose*, nor Dreadfort-only casualty rates.
- **AGOT 69**: Tywin refers to Roose as wary and made more wary by defeat; expects **slow pursuit**. This is **Tywin's military prediction**, not testimony that he had made a secret deal with Roose then.
- **AGOT 71**: Catelyn's court receives news Roose **has reassembled remnant infantry** at the causeway. **Roose did not defect on-page to Tywin at this stage**, and his force remains a potential Lannister strategic concern.

### What the records cannot calculate

There is **no house-by-house Green Fork muster and casualty roster** in these passages. The names of captured or dead northern commanders make severe non-Bolton losses apparent, while **Roose's escape alone is not evidence his Dreadfort men escaped without loss**. Nor can one compare casualties per house while ignoring Robb's deliberate force composition (many foot, few horses), different armor/position and what was happening off Tyrion's field of view.

The **strongest counterargument** to `GREEN FORK WAS ENTIRELY ROOSE'S TRAP` is Robb's own plan: he intentionally wanted Tywin diverted toward that force. **A flawed or treasonous tactical execution remains possible** but requires Roose's actual subcommand orders, remaining force composition or a reliable witness.

## 2. Duskendale: one directly seen Roose order, major losses and a new public explanation

- **ACOK 64 (Arya), `DIRECT ORDER:`** At Harrenhal, Roose tells Qyburn to compose a letter to **Helman Tallhart**: punish the Darry Lannisters, link up with **Robett Glover**, then **strike east toward Duskendale**. At least the violent Darry reprisal is presented as a command from the king; Qyburn is to prepare the message for **Roose's seal**. **Arya does not personally follow the courier or see Tallhart acknowledge receipt**.
- **ASOS 35 (Catelyn), `REPORTED OUTCOME:`** **Randyll Tarly** shatters the force at Duskendale. **Robb** responds with disbelief about why Glover/Tallhart went so far east, estimating that **one third of his foot** was lost. **Robett survived the battle but was captured later near the kingsroad**.
- **ASOS 37 (Jaime), `ROOSE SELF-REPORT:`** Roose says he added **Harrion Karstark and remaining Karhold soldiers** to Glover's troops. His deliberately casual hope that Harrion survived cannot by itself establish that Roose intentionally killed/targeted Harrion, or that Harrion actually died there.
- **ASOS 45 (Catelyn), `REPORTED:`** Galbart subsequently hears Robett was placed on a ship at Duskendale, connected to proposed Lannister prisoner exchange. Do **not** carry Robett forward as an irrevocable battlefield death.
- **ASOS 49 (Catelyn), `DIRECT SPEECH:`** Robb says Glover will be made to answer. Roose agrees Duskendale was folly and attributes it to Glover's grief over Deepwood, **without giving Robb the previously observed directive**.

The inference that Roose **shifted public blame** is considerably stronger than the inference that he **selected Duskendale to arrange exactly the resulting losses**. The latter requires prebattle intelligence about Tarly or an explicit reason to place those houses in danger *beyond the military assignment itself*. The lost force includes people Roose had actual command over, but whether his intention was conventional raiding, betrayal or opportunistic sacrifice remains **UNKNOWN**.

## 3. The Trident crossing: reported selective exposure, with a survival arithmetic trap

**ASOS 49 (Catelyn), `ROOSE CHARACTER ACCOUNT, NOT AN INDEPENDENT BATTLE POV:`**

Roose tells Robb that he **delayed at Harrenhal**, allowing **Aenys Frey** to get across the Trident days earlier. When Roose's own column attempted to cross **the river was swollen**, and Roose had too few boats, forcing a piecemeal ferry. **Two thirds** of his troops were already on the north bank when **Gregor Clegane** attacked those still waiting to cross. He identifies them chiefly as **Norrey, Locke and Burley**, with **Ser Wylis Manderly and White Harbor knights** serving as rear guard. **Wylis rallied survivors** but was overwhelmed and captured; many drowned, fled or were taken. Roose says he was **across the river and unable to assist**.

Roose says he left **600 men** defending the crossing, mainly river/mountain/White Knife spearmen, **100 Hornwood longbows**, Stout and Cerwyn men, with **Ronnel Stout and Ser Kyle Condon** in command. Robb **praises** that decision. The same discussion says Roose's returned core consists of approximately **500 mounted and 3,000 foot**, mainly **Dreadfort men**, plus some **Karhold**.

**What this does suggest:** an **important imbalance in who survives to accompany Roose**: non-Dreadfort names dominate his narrative of the drowned, cut down, captured and rear-guarded, while the returned army is described as principally Dreadfort. This is a **strong source-backed comparative clue** consistent with him **privileging troops loyal to himself** in positioning and withdrawal.

**What it cannot yet prove:** Roose planned a rising river, deliberately held rivals on the south shore, instructed Aenys to depart early, falsified the rear-guard roster, or selected his crossing order **with attrition as the desired objective**. The passage gives **his own retrospective explanation**, not a confirmed independent chronology. Nor are the **600 left at the ford** automatically **600 killed or 600 non-Bolton losses**: they are **left to defend a river crossing**, with named commanders, and their subsequent fate is not narrated here.

### Do not add incompatible counts

- `~one third foot lost at Duskendale` is **Robb's estimate of losses in that theater**, not a fraction that can be applied again to remaining Trident soldiers.
- `two thirds across the river` describes a **moment during the crossing**, not a 2/3 survival rate or a full casualty roll.
- `500 horse + 3,000 foot` describes **the men Roose later presents to Robb**, not necessarily the original Green Fork infantry host, all Duskendale soldiers, or a mathematically exclusive Dreadfort survivor group.
- `600 left to hold the ford` are **a postcrossing assignment**, not demonstrated casualties or definitely additive to the report of other forces without a clear date-defined headcount.
- `three hundred White Harbor men / hundred knights at Winterfell` is **Aenys's later ADWD 46 hostile characterization**, not an independently verified muster and not equivalent to the White Harbor knights taken at the Trident.

The **unreconciled distribution** is itself useful for theory, but a precise **house attrition percentage** would be fabricated without initial strength and timing.

## 4. Winterfell: a direct risky order, no observed field result

**ADWD 32 (Reek)** establishes Roose is directly concerned about a surviving Stark claimant and grudging northern loyalty; he **chooses** the supposed Arya wedding **at Winterfell to draw Stannis** and exploit his mountain clans' dedication. This is **Roose's stated strategy**, not simply Theon's interpretation.

**ADWD 46 (Theon)** depicts overcrowding, snow, missing scouts, multiple killings and increasing Frey–Manderly distrust. Theon **privately thinks** Roose may want dubious northern banners sent out to die fighting Stannis, leaving the Dreadfort fewer potential enemies. This is **POV-INFERRED**, heavily influenced by his experience and fear; no private Roose order confirms the particular purpose.

**ADWD 51 (Theon)** does directly show Hosteen Frey badly wound Wyman Manderly, followed by **six White Harbor and two Frey dead** inside Winterfell. Roose commands **Hosteen's Freys to the main gates**, **Wyman's White Harbor men to the east gate**, both ostensibly against Stannis. Hosteen/Aenys lead horses from the Great Hall, **Wyman remains in medical care** and the actual White Harbor commander is never certified. The **Freys are not a disposable hostile northern house in the same sense as the clans; they are an important Bolton dynastic ally**. That adds a counterpressure against any simple plan to eliminate all unreliable auxiliaries indiscriminately.

No **field contact, battle, deliberate decoy, Frey defeat, Manderly defection, or Stannis victory** is narrated in these five novels. The orders give Roose an opportunity to send rival groups beyond his direct immediate control, not a completed operation.

## 6. Rival models, counters and grading

### H1. Deliberately consume rival northern houses across campaigns
**Supports:** Duskendale initial Roose directive plus later blame; Trident's unevenly described victims and predominantly Dreadfort returned host; Winterfell Theon interpretation and direct separate battle order; Bolton political incentives confirmed in ADWD 32.

**Costs:** Robb himself creates the first decoy army; Roose's private Green Fork deployments are unseen; river crossing lacks independent witnesses; he needs Frey military support; Winterfell results cannot yet be assessed. A continuous master plan requires **at least two undocumented intention/coordination links**. `PLAUSIBLE / NOT PROVEN`.

### H2. Conservative command, self-protection and opportunistic denial
**Supports:** Robb deliberately chooses Roose for caution; Bolton survives Green Fork, regroups and Tywin respects his wariness; swollen Trident and cavalry threat offer straightforward reasons to protect the force already crossed; Winterfell's sortie can lessen immediate dangerous overcrowding.

**Costs:** Duskendale blame omission is difficult to excuse; no proof military choices optimized protection of Robb's non-Dreadfort men; selective concern over who is caught at the ford. `PLAUSIBLE BUT ACCOUNTABILITY FAILURES REAL`.

### H3. Treason develops during, not before, the military series
**Supports:** Green Fork is early and features no witnessed Bolton-Lannister deal, while subsequent Harrenhal/Duskendale and Red Wedding context permits changed incentives. One should not assume the same degree of treason throughout.

**Costs:** exact time and content of any political negotiation with Tywin is not secured by the military passages examined. `CHRONOLOGY UNKNOWN`.

### H4. Command hierarchy and incomplete narrator vision create a misleading pattern
**Supports:** Green Fork Tyrion only sees portions of battlefield; Robb authorizes decoy; Catelyn gets Roose's own account of Trident; Winterfell is seen through fearful Theon before battle.

**Costs:** cannot erase **Arya's directly witnessed Duskendale order** and **Robb's contradictory reaction**, nor the direct Winterfell orders after casualties. `IMPORTANT COUNTERWEIGHT, INSUFFICIENT TO FULLY EXCULPATE`.

### H5. Roose intentionally reshapes other lords' *obedience and liability* rather than preferring deaths
**Supports:** Duskendale assignment under seemingly royal authority, later Glover culpability; in ADWD 32 he favors **courteous invitation** over visible coercion, and his Winterfell order pushes hostile men toward a common external target.

**Costs:** some commanders genuinely die and no direct internal monologue differentiates reputational advantage from casualty objectives. A lord can want both. `PROMISING NARROWER MODEL / NOT AUTHORITATIVELY SELECTED`.

**Prioritization:** The `DUSKENDALE PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY CONTRADICTION` is the strongest single piece of Roose-specific evidence. The `TRIDENT DIFFERENTIAL LOSSES` is suggestive but filtered through Roose. The `GREEN FORK ROBB-DECIDED DIVERSION` is a major falsifier of easy treason claims. `WINTERFELL RESULT` remains outside published observation.

## 7. Practical consequences for the speculative continuation

- A cautious northern lord who knows **Robb's war strategy** may remember Green Fork as a costly authorized diversion, not a uniquely Bolton choice; others may have independently observed dangerous postbattle casualty distributions. **No authenticated roster yet joins these views**.
- A person who possesses the **Qyburn dispatch** or Arya's memory of Roose's Duskendale instructions (Arya was a disguised servant with no current safe route to Northern war councils) could impeach Roose's story, but **Robb died without this direct order shown as received**. **Reader knowledge is not survivor knowledge**.
- **Robett Glover** survives Duskendale (ASOS 35/45) and is later present in Wyman/Davos's White Harbor plotting. He might be an **important living witness to whose letter he or Tallhart received**, but no quoted testimony in the audited novels confirms he saw the actual Roose-authored dispatch, or identified a deliberate trap.
- The **Trident rear-guard survivors and captives**—including **Wylis**, whose return is important to Manderly politics—might have first-person reasons to mistrust Roose, but the five-book project should not assign them a confession or identical interpretation by fiat.
- Roose's **self-justifying reputation as a cautious and polite ruler**, and his concern about surviving Starks, matter because **a genuine Stark claimant** may break the grudging coalition without a military battle. That is distinct from the battle-casualty mechanism.
- **Conditional episode structure:** `Glover/Tallhart order recovered → witnesses authenticate source and sender → northerners evaluate Roose's omission → current house leaders choose whether to remain in Bolton alignment → practical military action`. Each arrow is an event that must be written, not an assumption that someone reads the entire five-book narration.

## 8. Future falsifiers and the no-false-precision ledger

1. **Actual Green Fork field roster:** find a first-person Northern commander describing positions of Dreadfort versus Hornwood, Cerwyn, Karstark, Frey and Manderly soldiers. Tyrion's standards sighting is not a full roster.
2. **Roose's Green Fork tactic:** independent order/testimony as to night approach, attack timing and reasons for withdrawal; Lannister early surprise is a real datum against absolute incompetence.
3. **Original Duskendale letter:** recover who sealed, carried, received and read Qyburn's draft; clarify whether Tallhart passed the order to Glover and to what extent it claimed royal authority.
4. **Tarly intelligence:** did Roose know the enemy's current position/plan before dispatching Glover? No sufficient contemporary intelligence is in the directly observed command scene.
5. **Trident independent survivors:** get Wylis's or Norrey/Locke/Burley witnesses' versions of actual ferry order and timing; `ROOSE REPORTED` ≠ `INDEPENDENTLY WITNESSED`.
6. **Returned-force accounting:** establish original house-specific strengths before any percentage claim that Dreadfort men were protected disproportionately.
7. **Winterfell field report:** who led the wounded Wyman's White Harbor troops, who crossed which icebound gate, whether Freys met Manderlys/Stannis, and what happened? The ending is unshown.
8. **Attribution check:** whenever a later character says Glover “chose” Duskendale, ask if that character knows Arya's ACOK command scene. `LACK OF EVIDENCE` is not an invitation to invent their report.
