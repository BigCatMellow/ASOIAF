## Working thesis

**A battlefield success can generate a harder legitimacy, subsistence or ethical problem.** The key to an organic continuation is therefore not an automatic chain of winning battles. The convergence points are moments where *some* characters learn a fact, gain a resource, or lose a safeguard that other protagonists still lack.

This thesis is a **literary inference**, not a statement of Martin's intended ending.

## Five cross-theater collisions worth preparing (not declaring inevitable)

### Collision A — Aegon's invasion versus the capital's collapsing ability to respond

- **Verified anchors:** ADWD 61 Connington holds Griffin's Roost and seeks support; ADWD Epilogue court officials debate his claim while Varys murders the man trying to stabilize government.
- **Inference:** A claimant may gain acceptance because the regime becomes less credible, **not** because his parentage is proved. The Tyrells, Faith, Dorne and Lannister court need not react together.
- **Choice pressure:** Aegon decides whether immediate visible leadership is worth risk; Connington weighs speed and preservation; Cersei may choose retaliation that damages Tommen. 
- **Disconfirmers:** Stormlords could resist; Golden Company muster remains incomplete; royal forces and religious institutions may stabilize faster than Varys expects.
- **Prohibited shortcut:** No unsupported “Aegon has already taken Storm's End / King's Landing” or “Dorne joins as soon as it hears.”

### Collision B — Meereen's defense versus Daenerys's later political authority

- **Verified anchors:** ADWD 50 peace permits slaving beyond Meereen; ADWD 52 Dany flies away; ADWD 67 Barristan deposes Hizdahr; ADWD 70 he faces renewed war; ADWD 71 Dany is elsewhere.
- **Inference:** A loyal commander can undermine the policy his sovereign made. A military rescue may worsen the legitimacy of whichever faction next claims to speak for Daenerys.
- **Choice pressure:** Barristan faces hostage/supply decisions; Tyrion may seek a sell-sword defection; Daenerys must interpret the consequences if/when information reaches her.
- **Disconfirmers:** Dany could support Barristan's choice; a negotiated settlement could be salvageable; a defeat could prevent any useful restoration.
- **Prohibited shortcut:** Tyrion is not yet her adviser. Horn magic has not demonstrably bound a dragon.

### Collision C — The North's potential military victory versus a disputed Stark restoration

- **Verified anchors:** ADWD 29 Wyman conditions help on Davos retrieving Ned's boy; ADWD 37/51 Bolton marriage depends on false Arya; ADWD 62 Stannis receives Theon and Jeyne; ASOS 45 Robb's heirs were selected under false death reports.
- **Inference:** Anti-Bolton forces may win a battle and immediately argue over who may rule Winterfell. **Theon is a source of crucial identity evidence**, but has reason to fear how it will be used.
- **Choice pressure:** Stannis must choose whether to expose/protect Jeyne; Manderly must choose between a true Stark child and Stannis's own interests; Jon's fate and Watch oath remain unsettled.
- **Disconfirmers:** Northern houses could accept a temporary regency or one candidate; Davos may not retrieve Rickon at all.
- **Prohibited shortcut:** Robb's will cannot be silently produced from Howland Reed's pocket without an actual chain of custody.

### Collision D — Oldtown's endangered knowledge versus the Wall's collapsing command

- **Verified anchors:** AFFC 45 Sam reports Others/dragonglass to Alleras/Marwyn; ADWD 69 Jon is attacked and the Hardhome rescue remains unfinished; AFFC 45 raids threaten the Reach coast.
- **Inference:** Necessary warnings may arrive where the institution can study them but lacks the incentive or resources to act. A delay may be **bureaucratic and military**, not proof of a secret anti-magic cabal.
- **Choice pressure:** Sam decides whom to trust and whether to make the warning public. At the Wall, successors must allocate starving bodies to rescue, defense and internal policing.
- **Disconfirmers:** Some archmaesters may cooperate; the naval threat may be repelled; a credible messenger could reconnect the institutions quickly.
- **Prohibited shortcut:** Marwyn is only preparing to depart; Sam doesn't have a tested glass-candle emergency network.

### Collision E — Familial identity versus political ownership

- **Verified anchors:** ASOS 65 Nymeria retrieves a corpse strongly inferable as Catelyn's; AFFC 42 Stoneheart coerces Brienne; ADWD 48 Jaime hears a claimed Sansa lead; AFFC 41 the real Sansa lives as Alayne; ADWD 64 the real Arya is in Braavos.
- **Inference:** Multiple adults attempt to use absent Stark daughters' names for ends those daughters have not approved. Arya, Sansa and Stoneheart each have an incompatible image of what protection or justice means.
- **Choice pressure:** Jaime demands proof of the alleged Sansa; Brienne weighs Podrick against Jaime; Sansa weighs the Harry plan against Sweetrobin; Arya's training pits obedience against personal ethics.
- **Disconfirmers:** Brienne may tell Jaime the truth before travel; Sansa may reject Littlefinger without immediate retaliation; the Stark women may never meet one another during *Winds*.
- **Prohibited shortcut:** No instant telepathy among Stark children, no off-page Jaime acceptance, no assumed Stoneheart–Arya meeting.

## Strong global conclusion: two kinds of scarcity

**Material scarcity:** Winter food, horses, ships, plague treatment, hostages and manpower determine which military/political victories can be occupied and sustained.

**Verified knowledge scarcity:** Theon knows Jeyne, Arya knows parts of Hardhome, Bran has weirwood access, Marwyn knows Sam's warning, Connington has information that Doran may not, and Wyman's arrangement with Davos is incomplete. The reader has all these strands; **the characters emphatically do not**.

The convergence likely becomes more believable by treating scarce reliable communication as a major plot resource alongside dragons and armies. `INFERRED / MEDIUM-HIGH NARRATIVE UTILITY`. The *specific* events and winners remain open.
