## Executive finding — the very man used to make the fraud credible may be the strongest repudiating witness

**ADWD 37 (Theon):** Lady Dustin specifically picks **Theon Greyjoy**, Eddard Stark's former ward, to give Ramsay the hand of the supposed Arya. Theon privately recognizes the reason: he **grew up with Arya and her siblings**, so the northern lords at the wedding are invited to interpret **his participation and acceptance** as authentication. The woman is **Jeyne Poole**, whose brown eyes differ from the grey Theon remembers of the actual Arya. Theon is **physically present as participant**, aware of the false identity, and acts under threat of Bolton cruelty.

**Source-grounded inference:** If Theon later tells Stannis the bride was Jeyne, he is **recanting a public certification in which he was an instrument**, not offering a detached rumor. That improves the *relevance* of his account while creating a hard problem of *credibility*: a mutilated former betrayer of House Stark, previously observed publicly declaring the girl Arya, must explain why the court should now believe his reversal. **His actual recantation to Stannis is NOT shown**.

**ADWD 42 (Asha)**: Stannis's northern allies were pushing him toward Winterfell to reclaim the castle and save **Ned Stark's supposed daughter**. **ADWD 17 (Jon)**: Stannis had **expressed preference**, not granted an appointment, for **Arnolf Karstark** to hold Winterfell, although Arnolf is tied to Bolton-side plans in **ADWD 32 (Reek)**. The purported Arya identity is thus **not only genealogy**: it is bound to northern war legitimacy, a false Bolton marriage claim, Stannis's campaign rhetoric and the intended recipient of a conquered stronghold. A truthful revelation may **discredit Bolton's marriage** while depriving some allies of the **rescue purpose** they believed justified enormous winter hardship. **Neither political response is observed.**

### Why Jeyne is not a frictionless witness

**ADWD 37**: the bride struggles not to identify herself as someone other than Arya before Theon presses her to maintain her role. **ADWD 51**: she panics that her rescuers are a test arranged by Ramsay, fears severe punishment and only begins to say her true name before Theon stops her. **ADWD 62**: Asha first sees her shivering and frightened with **blackened nose from frostbite**. The trauma is **POV-OBSERVED**; any exact post-escape ability to deliver a coherent deposition is **UNKNOWN**.

A responsible proposed hearing would therefore distinguish **medical shelter, warmth and safety** from the military wish to obtain an answer quickly; an apparent continuation should not portray a torture victim as instantly free of conditioned fear or omniscient about the Boltons. This is both **character coherence** and **epistemic caution**, not a prediction Stannis will choose kindness.

## 3. Double-edged legitimacy: Bolton's marriage and Stannis's northern war justification

**Bolton's side:** Roose explicitly wants Stannis drawn toward Winterfell by the Northern clans' refusal to leave the presumed **Ned Stark's daughter** with Ramsay (**ADWD 32**). **Lady Dustin** insists Theon give away the bride precisely because he has the Stark-child recognition credential (**ADWD 37**). A believable witness who reverses that credential can undermine Bolton's claim to have married Stark inheritance.

**Stannis's side:** The anti-Bolton northern fighters urged rescue of Ned's girl as a reason to risk the frozen march (**ADWD 42**). Stannis's military campaign to defeat Roose remains a distinct objective, and earlier **ADWD 17** records his intention to allocate Winterfell to **Arnolf**, whose loyalty is independently in question. A disclosure can therefore simultaneously:

1. **Damage Bolton's genealogical claim and weaken his ability to rally northern lords around the bride.**
2. **Remove a particular rescue objective from Stannis's story** while leaving the Boltons in possession of Winterfell and leaving Northern anti-Frey grievances intact.
3. **Put Stannis under pressure to explain who would govern** — and whether his earlier preference for Arnolf is tenable after possible witness/warning corroboration.
4. **Create a new danger to Jeyne** as different factions try to silence, discredit, protect or use the person who can refute a public wedding.
5. **Leave separate Stark claims (Rickon, Bran, real Arya, Sansa) unresolved** because Theon's account cannot actually place them before the king.

**Important counterargument:** Not every northern lord personally depended on the bride's identity; the Red Wedding, ancestral Stark loyalty and hatred of Bolton/Frey rule survive exposure of the imposture. Likewise a single mutilated former captor/turncloak's recantation may fail to persuade anyone. **Do not set the coalition to disintegrate or flip universally on one speech.** The source supports differentiated responses, not automatic political collapse.

**Coalition evidence (2026-10-16):** [Comparative Manderly, Umber and clan ledger](northern-coalition-manderly-umber-clans-arya-revelation-gates.md) source-checks how each northern power could respond *differently* to a credible **Jeyne Poole** disclosure. **ADWD 29 (Davos)**: Wyman Manderly's Stark-first bargain requires Davos to bring him **Rickon and his direwolf** before Wyman promises to take Stannis as king; this is **not an accomplished alliance**. **ADWD 42 (Asha)**: Wull/Liddle and the mountain allies explicitly want Ned's girl *and* a fight with Bolton; revealing that the girl is not Arya could alter the rescue rationale without eliminating the anti-Bolton motive. **ADWD 17 (Jon)**: Mors Umber only conditionally offers part of his house and refuses to fight Whoresbane's Umber force, regardless of girl identity. His original price includes **Mance's skull**, a second conflict with Mance's secret rescue. **ADWD 62** puts rescued people in Tycho's party by Mors's account, but no meeting confirms that Mors or Stannis already knows Jeyne's name.

## 5. Three outbound channels, each with a concrete exposure problem

### A. Use Arnolf's camp maester and cage of ravens

**Source:** **ADWD 62 (Asha)** specifies the maester and cage **belong to Arnolf's arriving contingent**, not a neutral trusted Watch exchange. **ADWD 32 (Reek)** gives Roose's discussion of Arnolf's attempted entrapment; **ADWD 44/49 (Jon)** gives Alys's warning and uncertain delivery.

- `CAN ATTEMPT, CONDITIONAL:` ravens may carry messages if available/appropriate for the destination, with whoever writes and services them.
- `SECURITY COST:` an officer relying on Arnolf's maester without vetting would potentially **expose testimony about Arnolf himself to the very man under suspicion**.
- `NOT PROVEN:` Stannis already trusts that maester, cages include birds trained for Castle Black, the maester opens or diverts a note, or Arnolf has sent any battle/traitor report.
- `WRITER-TO-RECIPIENT`: `message drafted? NO`; `bird trained for specific destination? UNKNOWN`; `issued? NO`; `received? NO`. **Having a cage is not direct Castle Black connectivity**.

### B. Use a human rider through Deepwood Motte

**Source:** Tycho **traveled from Jon at the Wall toward Deepwood** (ADWD 44); afterward the banker, accompanied by six ransomed ironborn and others, **found Mors** and reached Stannis (ADWD 62). Meanwhile Clydas **dispatched** Jon's Arnolf warning to Deepwood (ADWD 49), not proven received by the king.

- `PLAUSIBLE ROUTE:` reverse or redeploy a trusted rider through Deepwood, seek authentication there, and have a message sent on via a maester or another rider.
- `SOURCE LIMIT:` no named rider is instructed **after ADWD 62** to travel from crofter's village to Deepwood; Tycho has not turned around, and the blizzard has made short journeys take many days.
- `SECURITY COST:` interception, pressure on the current Deepwood keeper, mistaken receipt or reliance on the same compromised route used by Arnolf's contacts. The **Asha-side Deepwood receiving maester** and **Jon's Clydas** do not create a witnessed continuous postal service.

### C. Use apparent Watch-clad members of Tycho's party

**Source:** **ADWD 62 (Asha)**: in the arriving group she sees two black-clad riders and **assumes** Night's Watch affiliation, but the narration **does not name them or disclose their authority/instructions**.

- `POSSIBLE COURIERS:` if they are sworn brothers and still able to travel, their acquaintance with the Wall could in principle help route a message.
- `SOURCE LIMIT:` no command, oath identification, letter inventory, promised return to Castle Black, or post-hearing travel is observed.
- `SECURITY COST:` revealing a fraught southern political matter to unidentified men may be as dangerous as sending it through a potentially disloyal retinue.
- `DO NOT CLAIM:` they are Jon's two missing traitor witnesses from Karhold, or that they **already bear a Pink Letter rebuttal**. The previous dossier appropriately tracks the Karhold escort testimony **at the Wall**, a distinct group.

**Non-route:** A **wet raven on Maester Rhodry's arm at Winterfell** in ADWD 51 is neither Stannis's courier nor a witnessed outbound Pink Letter; its actual landing/flight is not shown. Do not import it into Stannis's inventory.

## 6. Questions a credible first hearing can distinguish quickly

This is an **analyst's proposed interview order**, not dialogue already in the text:

1. **“Who are you in your own words?”** Separates Theon's name from the Bolton nickname and makes room for Jeyne to state Poole identity **without others prompting “Arya.”**
2. **“Why did you give the bride away as Arya at the wedding?”** Requires Theon to account for his prior public endorsement, fear and incentives, rather than treating a recantation as costless.
3. **“What did Roose Bolton say with you in the hall, and who else heard it?”** Retrieves Arnolf trap claims **as words overheard by Theon**, not a personal observation of Arnolf swearing treason.
4. **“Which Frey and Manderly movements did you see, rather than merely hear ordered?”** Keeps preparation orders separate from field dispositions. Theon can name two assembly gates but cannot identify real-time positions under snow.
5. **“Who rescued the girl and whom did you see survive?”** Establishes Abel and women's last-known roles without making Stannis or Theon omniscient about Mance's glamour or all six fates.
6. **“Who controls the messaging equipment, and who is allowed to hear these answers?”** Directly tests Arnolf's suspect maester/ravens and the still-unknown pair in Watch colors.
7. **“What is the next safe place for her if we move?”** Makes the witness's life, frostbite and the king's desperate supplies a real command constraint rather than a transparent story convenience.

**Proposed least-assumption initial sequence (`THEORY / NOT ADOPTED`):** Asha and/or Tycho secure a short private audience and warmth, Stannis separates Theon's privately important operational testimony from any public announcement about Jeyne, checks loyalty before choosing a messenger, and defers broad northern publicity until credibility and her safety improve. This still costs time/food, may offend anxious clansmen, and may be superseded by immediate armed threats. Another branch can be chosen only with equally explicit consequences.
