## Rule of use

The rows below do not establish elapsed days or a chapter sequence. They identify the **earliest causally valid order** in which one endpoint crisis can change another: local event → identifiable carrier/contact → recipient’s interpretation → a new choice. Any route can fail, be delayed, or be misread.

## Ordering principles for a source-grounded continuation

1. **Local choice outruns distant consequence.** The Wall, Meereen, Cape Wrath, and the capital can each generate a new political fact before a different theater hears of it.
2. **A first report is not a settlement.** A recipient must decide whether it is authentic, complete, and useful; rivals can receive different versions.
3. **Custody is information.** Theon, Jeyne, Hizdahr, hostages, children at the Wall, and Connington relatives cannot be used as plot tokens until a keeper, consent, and physical access are established.
4. **A military result changes rather than erases governance.** A castle, a battle, a dragon, or a claimant can create a new food, legitimacy, or revenge obligation before enabling a later convergence.
5. **Magic may add a route but cannot be presumed to erase distance.** A horn, vision, glass candle, or dragon needs a demonstrated operator, recipient, and cost.

## Independent challenge

The matrix assumes communication is scarce because that is the book-endpoint condition, not because delay is automatically dramatic. A future text may provide a swift, credible route for a particular message; if it does, revise that row rather than forcing the delay. Independent challenge remains open.
