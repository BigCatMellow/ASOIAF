## Executive finding: the reader knows two attackers, but has no named *surviving* knife witness on record

**POV-OBSERVED — ADWD 69 (Jon):** Jon personally sees **Wick Whittlestick slash him**, then **Bowen Marsh plunge a dagger into him**. A third stab from behind and a fourth wound follow; **their attackers are not individually identified**. Jon's narration stops as he feels cold. The book **does not confirm post-wound death, recovery, speaking testimony, lawful removal, a murder verdict, or who physically controls the body**.

**WITNESS PROBLEM:** **Horse and Rory** accompany Jon from the Shieldhall and race toward Hardin's Tower with him; **Leathers** is present at the giant confrontation, instructed to calm Wun Wun; and **men from multiple factions** rush from towers/keeps. These are named or collectively observed **nearby persons**, **not narrated as identifying the knives or later giving evidence**. A continuation may make a named witness see a knife; **the five books do not**.

**INSTITUTIONAL PROBLEM:** The Watch already survived **Jeor Mormont's murder by Ollo Lophand at Craster's Keep**, and then chose Jon via disputed Watch-wide voting, **not by acclaiming Mormont's killer**. A separate earlier episode sees **Janos Slynt** denounce Jon as an oathbreaker using Rattleshirt's adverse eyewitness-like account; **Aemon challenges the charge and writes Cotter Pyke**. This makes **testimony, physical proof and independent correspondence** more useful than simply calling the stabbers lawful “Watch” representatives. These precedents **do not create a newly demonstrated formal murder-investigation statute or guarantee a fair review**.

Any surviving character giving an accurate account has to **obtain and distinguish the details**.

## 2. The strongest analogous event: Mormont's death after a food dispute

**ASOS 33 (Samwell):** Surviving brothers at Craster's Keep argue about starvation and a supposed private larder. **Dirk cuts Craster's throat**. **Mormont explicitly condemns the killing under guest-right custom** and threatens disciplinary action. **Ollo Lophand stabs Mormont** when he tries to stop the armed men. In Sam's own POV, Mormont remains conscious long enough to instruct him to return to the Wall with news of the Fist, the Others/wildlings, dragonglass and the catastrophe. Sam subsequently sees he has died.

The mutineers immediately control enough of **Craster's hall and women/food** to prey on people, but **the Watch does not thereby elect Ollo as Lord Commander**. Sam and some loyal brothers flee, and the subsequent Watch-wide election occurs in **ASOS 75/78/79**, culminating in Jon's choosing.

**Source-tested parallels:**

- **Food/survival dispute + brother-on-commander violence** occurs in both episodes, but the Castle Black murders **do not yet follow a demonstrated run on stored food**.
- **Victim-witness separation:** Sam **directly observes** the knife blow and, before departure, receives the dying Mormont's instructions; at the later Castle Black stabbing, the reader gets **Jon's thoughts**, not an observed surviving reporter who has been entrusted with the story.
- **Protection while mutineers control space:** Gilly and her child escape with Sam after the Craster violence. That is a **precedent that vulnerable Free Folk can become focal to a loyalist escape**, not a promise that Tormund's dispersed wards will be released or that a second escape must occur.
- **Do not copy-paste actors:** Dirk kills Craster, Ollo stabs Mormont; Wick and Marsh stab Jon. **No verified mapping** of Gilly onto Val, Ollo onto Marsh, or Sam onto Satin should be promoted from analogy to foreshadowed fact.
- **Avoid claiming legal procedure:** At Craster's Keep there is **an attempted exercise of Mormont's command and a violent refusal**, followed by flight; the books show no judicial hearing for those killers before the later election.

**Counter-reading:** Castle Black is a fortified Watch seat with garrisons, hostages, royal guards and thousands of Free Folk. Craster's Keep was an isolated refuge for a battered ranging. The earlier episode is a **relevant narrative and institutional precedent**, not a reliable prediction of timing, casualty count or political factions.

## 3. The other precedent: Jon himself was accused on hostile evidence, and a letter mattered

**ASOS 69 (Jon):** **Janos Slynt**, functioning as Castle Black commander until Marsh's garrison returned, had Jon seized and questioned. **Alliser Thorne** accused Jon of oathbreaking and desertion. The captive **Rattleshirt** described Jon killing Qhorin Halfhand and maliciously portrayed it as begging for his own life, contrary to Jon's earlier orders and account. **Maester Aemon** challenged the charge, invoking Jon's defense of the Wall and the account Aemon and Donal Noye had heard when Jon first returned. This is an **in-universe example of politically useful, disputed testimony**, not a court deciding which speaker must be right.

**ASOS 73 (Jon):** After days in an ice cell, Jon is told by Slynt that **Aemon had written Cotter Pyke and shown Slynt the letter**, saying Jon was not a turncloak. Slynt responds by asserting he will not hang an innocent man but instead engineers a highly dangerous mission against Mance. **VERIFIED: written challenge disclosed to Slynt and his own admission that it constrains his public justification.** **UNKNOWN: independent confirmed arrival at Pyke or Pyke's response to that specific letter.** A written protest can matter **without proving an official review or preventing dangerous abuse of command**.

**ASOS 79 (Jon):** Even when Slynt again publicly demands Jon's death and calls him a turncloak, **Denys Mallister** insists a sworn brother may be nominated, **Yarwyck** publicly changes his proposed vote advice from Slynt toward Jon, the brethren cast tokens, and the outcome is Jon's election. The Watch's legitimacy is more complex than one forceful officer's allegations. **Do not infer everyone voted for Jon, that Yarwyck is immutable in loyalties, or that a new election will reproduce this one.**

**Causal implication / INFERRED:** A mailed statement by someone called “Lord Steward” could be challenged by a named brother or on-site witness **even if the dissenter does not physically control the rookery**. A receiving commander such as Mallister might distinguish **“Marsh says Jon broke his vows”** from **“Marsh was lawfully empowered to kill or depose him.”** Mallister is not guaranteed to do so, and Hewett and Pyke may react differently.

### Authentic report = multiple distinct tests

A **credible first notice** should separate the following, not conflate them: **(1) where/when someone *personally* saw blood or a knife; (2) who recognized which attacker; (3) whether Jon is alive, unconscious, or dead *at the time of examination*; (4) whose statement assigns motive, guilt or Watch authorization; (5) who signs or seals the notice; (6) whether the letter was truly dispatched; (7) whether the addressed garrison received and accepted it; (8) separately, whether a legitimate Watch choosing has occurred.** A seal proves a seal is present; it does not make a disputed political assertion true.

The relevant stage-gates are deliberately different: `DRAFTED`, `DISPATCHED`, `RECEIVED`, `CREDENTIALED / INDEPENDENTLY CORROBORATED`, `ACTED UPON`. A recipient can take **precautionary** protective steps with weaker evidence but should not be reported as having **certified** the disputed claims.

## 5. First news and hostage safeguards interact without proving collective punishment

**ADWD 58 (Jon)**: Tormund's hundred hostages are recorded at Castle Black; **sixty are reported sent toward Eastwatch and Shadow Tower**, the individual split and names per destination unknown. **Dryn** is given as Jon's personal page, but his present warder at the last Jon chapter is unshown. Two girls disguised as boys are to be reallocated to Long Barrow; delivery unverified. **The intake registers exist; confirmed onward receipt lists do not.**

A report to either fort might have several effects **depending on the actual recipient**, none observed in ADWD 69:

- **A. “Jon is wounded; guard all wards and hold current orders provisionally.”** `CONDITIONAL`. Can preserve immediate safety without endorsing an alleged successor; requires a real issuer, actual available guards and food, and receipt.
- **B. “Jon broke his Watch vows; Marsh is in charge.”** `CHARACTER CLAIM IF EVER SENT`. It could be supported by descriptions of the Shieldhall speech yet **lack proof** that Marsh became a lawful Lord Commander or that boys should be penalized for their fathers. A hostile respondent might accept policy arguments while refusing a murderer's authority.
- **C. “The Watch has killed Jon; free the hostages now.”** `CONDITIONAL`. Could travel among Free Folk by testimony from the crowd even before a written Watch message; it still does not show an Eastwatch or Shadow Tower officer has physically released any child.
- **D. “Patrek was killed by a giant; the Free Folk are attacking.”** `POSSIBLE DISTORTION`. Patrek's death is real, but **who began that fight is not shown**. Such news can reach the queen's men on a parallel line, distorting any Watch-wide understanding of the courtyard without proving Free Folk collective violence.

The **safe claim** is that these dispersed wards raise the cost of every disputed communiqué. Acknowledging receipt or offering a guard guarantee is **not** the same as selecting a candidate or surrendering the boys. Equally, no previous Watch command, hostage precedent or old anti-wildling prejudice establishes an actual post-stabbing execution order.

## 6. Competing causal branches, falsification checks, and disposition

| Model | How the first notice shapes responses | What evidence would disconfirm it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Limited emergency administrative pause** | Brothers who will not settle the office of Lord Commander agree to protect prisoners and children while gathering eyewitnesses. | Keys/guard refusals; direct orders to punish; credible death/attack messages that make compromise impossible. |
| **Marsh's faction asserts a lawful-sounding emergency** | A message stresses the public Winterfell march and calls stabbing an act for the Watch; a recipient may believe policy objection but inquire into legitimacy. | Authentic Watch-wide vote or compelling known custody/disciplinary authority — **none verified**; or witnesses show recipients explicitly reject the letter. |
| **External garrisons refuse to choose** | Mallister must defend the western Wall; Hewett commands Eastwatch while Pyke fights for survival at Hardhome. They maintain local routines pending verified news. | A real reply approving a claimant and complying with a request; a newly observed march or garrison ballot. |
| **Fast rumor, slow ward confirmation** | Free Folk chiefs hear an attack story while hostages are at two other castles; some demand proof of safety first. | Direct named-boys roster, witnessed returns and immediate affirmative replies; actual father-by-father choices. |
| **Violence escalates from rival explanations** | Watchmen blame Jon's Winterfell choice; queen's men react to Patrek; Free Folk fear harm to wards; none accepts the other's account. | Observed restraint/recognition by each faction or testimony that resolves the conflict before any orders. |

**Critical objection:** The Craster comparison and Aemon-to-Pyke letter show that **reporting and opposition are possible**, not that the Watch can investigate fairly or stop violence. **Slynt had power to imprison Jon despite Aemon's contrary account**, and Mormont's murderers controlled the immediate scene. The five books therefore support both **dangerous capture of a local narrative** and **later outside challenge**.
