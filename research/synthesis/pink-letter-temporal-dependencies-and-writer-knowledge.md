## Executive conclusion: the letter gives no verified address for Theon or Jeyne

**ADWD 69 (Jon)**: a scroll signed as Ramsay orders **the return of his bride and of “Reek.”** **ADWD 51 (Theon)** shows the supposed bride is **Jeyne Poole**, escaping with Theon from Winterfell. **ADWD 62 (Asha)** subsequently establishes both are alive and have been passed through **Mors Umber and Tycho Nestoris's party to Stannis's camp**.

**CRITICAL DISTINCTION:** The letter **does not say Jeyne or Theon is at Stannis's camp**. A reader knows more about their location than the letter writer demonstrates. Even a genuine Ramsay author **need only know or believe his captive and bride are missing**, not where they have gone, to demand they be returned.

The **most constrained other assertion** is the claim to have **Mance captive** and **the six women flayed**: the last observed Winterfell scene has **Abel free and performing**, **Squirrel still in Ramsay's room**, **Rowan/Willow/Myrtle returning to Abel**, **Frenya fighting**, and **Holly hit by two bolts**. This creates a mandatory **unshown subsequent resolution** if *every* captive/flaying detail is literally true. It does **not** make the statement demonstrably false: later discoveries and captures might occur off-page.

The alleged **seven days' victory over Stannis** likewise cannot be converted into a settled chronology. **Stannis is physically present and alive in Asha's crofter-village chapter before Tycho presents Theon**. There is **no on-page narrated defeat**. The fact that another POV chapter appears later in the book does not prove Jon's letter and Asha's observations share a particular same-day calendar.

### The strongest **negative** result

**Nothing in the final letter says “I know Theon and the girl were delivered to Stannis.”** Nor does it give Theon/Jeyne's current geographical location. Therefore `S2 → writer must have heard S2 → C1` is **not a valid required edge**.

A reader may draw a plausible conditional branch **if** an author has access to Tycho/Mors/Stannis intelligence, but that is additional speculation, not a datum demanded by the letter's words.

## 3. Conditional timing diagrams — do not collapse claims into a single timestamp

### A. If **every material allegation is true and known firsthand**

The strongest timeline this theory needs is:

`W1 (Mance mission) → W2/W3 (Abel at Winterfell) → W4 (rescue + party splits)`
`W4 → Bolton household discovers missing bride and Reek`
`W4 → all six women individually resolved into captive/dead status; Mance exposed and caged`
`S1 (Stannis alive) → claimed seven-day defeat + confirmed death + sword acquisition`
`all relevant information converges → author writes and sends → message travels → C1 (Jon receives)`

**Only** the first local edges and eventual Castle Black receipt are source-verified. Crucially, the middle lines would require multiple additional **off-page** events and a report route. Because the novel **does not bound the time between W4, S1 and C1 in days**, it is not possible from the audited chapters alone to prove this whole chain *chronologically impossible*. But the burden of missing evidence is **high**.

**Potential objection:** A writer can truthfully claim information on the six women **without each capture occurring after W4** if some new earlier movement went unreported. Here W4 explicitly last-sees multiple women alive and apart; **all-six flaying cannot be completed before those very last sightings** in that same local chain. A writer may claim it falsely earlier.

### B. If writer knows **only the failed rescue and disappearance**

`W4 (attempt) → report of bride/Reek missing → writer sends ultimatum → C1`.

All northern battlefield and Mance-captivity details can then be **false, outdated or exaggerated**. This requires far fewer actual outcomes and fits a coercive propaganda model, but **does not authenticate any particular writer**.

### C. If writer has **inside rescue knowledge before the attempted escape**

`W1/W2 (Mance/six/wedding knowledge) → proposed bluff or planned provocation → sends → C1`.

This is an allowed *theory of anticipatory language*, not the book's demonstrated sequence. It must still explain why the writer predicts the bride and Reek will leave and how the scroll later arrives. **Do not say the letter “could only” be written after W4 if it can contain an anticipatory lie**; say only a **genuinely informed report of their already accomplished disappearance** must follow that discovery.

### D. If Stannis really won or survived while the sender claimed otherwise

`S1 (living Stannis) → later hostilities undecided`, while `author boasts death of king → C1`.

This is compatible with the *known observations* because we are not forced to accept a letter claim as a verified event. It is **not proof** Stannis ultimately wins, just that he has not been killed in any on-page battle known to this audit.

## 4. Physically credible routes still need evidence

**Ramsay or Winterfell household.** The strongest **specific local information** would be the absence of the bride/Reek after the bedroom deception and their escape. Later interrogations could reveal Abel/Mance and the separated women, but **no interrogation or subsequent capture is shown**. **Maester Rhodry standing with a wet raven in ADWD 51** and Roose's parchment provide **communication capability**, not a proven outgoing Jon-addressed message. Separate staging of guards, weather and writing is missing.

**Mance or one of six.** The group knows their **own mission, Jon's intended beneficiary, six companions and Reek's role**, and may hope to draw Jon south. Yet Theon **withheld the real Jeyne identity** from Abel, and Abel's location after the women's escape remains unknown. The truth of **claimed later deaths** would be particularly tricky for a surviving woman to know firsthand. A messenger would need **actual access to a raven or courier** during or after a highly dangerous separation.

**Stannis camp / Tycho or northern intermediaries.** **ADWD 62** shows a real **human courier transfer** of Theon and Jeyne to Stannis. That creates the possibility of later communication **if** someone learns the mission details from them and **if** they choose to counterfeit a Bolton letter. Neither condition is shown. Asha recognizing Theon does not mean she or Tycho learns of a Mance glamour. The final letter **does not actually betray Stannis-camp-only knowledge** of their location.

**Castle Black or another outsider.** The Wall has direct secret knowledge in Jon/Melisandre's conversations and some operational recruitment details with Edd; older Bolton proclamations were available there. A forged letter writer might reuse names and wax conventions; but the **actual disappearance of bride and Reek** would need a post-W4 channel (unless mere prediction). **No proven Winterfell→Wall information leak to an alternative author, and no proved interception/forgery action, appears in the five books**.

### Material transit caution

Jon says he has received **“a raven in a storm”** when he reflects on the new letter (ADWD 69). That supports his **character understanding of correspondence**, but **the chapter does not show the bird landing, a dated dispatch log, or a flight-duration measurement**. Earlier Clydas letters illustrate how reports arrive; neither seal color nor severe snow uniquely fixes the origin or day of a particular flight.

**ADWD 51** shows a wet bird in Roose's hall: that bird **predates the specific witnessed rescue**, and Theon does **not explicitly watch it arrive**. It cannot be used as a demonstrated report of the **already completed later escape**, nor does it establish that Jon's eventual scroll was written there. The wet parchment is Theon's **inference**, not a laboratory analysis.

## 6. Five serious interpretations and explicit counterweights

- **Ramsay issued a coercive letter after learning the bedroom deception.** `PLAUSIBLE`: personal bride/Reek loss motivates him and earlier Ramsay messages leverage skin/torture and supposed bargains. **Counterweight:** no proof he caught Mance, knew Jon's private authorization, won Stannis's sword, or dispatched the final letter; bride/Reek mention shows *absence*, not omniscience.
- **Ramsay issued mixed propaganda after incomplete reports.** `PLAUSIBLE`: some genuine post-W4 information mingled with invented victory and captive claims. **Counterweight:** cannot assert which clauses are lies; a bad-faith writer may still report several true facts.
- **Mance wrote to provoke Jon by exploiting known mission.** `THEORY`: his information about the six women is strong. **Counterweight:** last scene leaves Abel at Winterfell; no escape, sending facility or writer’s intent observed; the letter claims female companions flayed.
- **Stannis/northern faction engineered disinformation.** `THEORY`: Stannis camp demonstrably receives Theon/Jeyne via Mors and Tycho; desperate strategic interests exist. **Counterweight:** no evidence any camp participant assembled Mance information, obtained Bolton pink wax and hand, or sent a taunt meant to make Jon violate vows; neither Theon nor girl at Stannis camp is mentioned in letter.
- **A pre-escape letter anticipated the rescue and provoked Jon.** `THEORY`: a true author need not actually witness every alleged event if many sentences are knowingly false. **Counterweight:** this must explain accurate prediction/knowledge of bride and Reek being absent and still provide a dispatch mechanism; none is verified.

**No probability percentages:** These are **unproven mechanisms**. Strongest novel-backed conclusions remain the **separate escape and delivery observations**, local command decisions, and the huge number of unverified post-escape claims.
