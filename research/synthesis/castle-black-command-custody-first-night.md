## Executive finding

**A man who can seize the courtyard is not thereby the elected Lord Commander; a woman who commands a royal household does not thereby command the Watch; a Free Folk chief who has warriors does not thereby acquire custody of Watch hostages.** At the end of ADWD 69, **Wick Whittlestick and Bowen Marsh have stabbed Jon, and an unidentified third and fourth knife follow**. The narrative ends before anyone reports a death, selects a successor, arrests an attacker, frees a captive, dispatches a raven or takes control of Castle Black.

The high-probability *pressure*, not a proven outcome, is a three-sided dispute among (1) officers who invoke Watch neutrality, (2) Free Folk who have trusted Jon's asylum and hostage bargain, and (3) Selyse's armed, religiously prescriptive court. An **immediate custodial and humanitarian decision** is more evidence-constrained than an instant election, an automatic wildling massacre, a coronation or a march on Karhold.

**UNKNOWN:** Neither the inspected election precedent nor ADWD 69 describes a binding automatic-acting-Lord-Commander order after a murder. An acting officer may coordinate defense, but **do not label Marsh the legitimate 999th Lord Commander by default**. Even Jon's biological death remains **unconfirmed at the chapter boundary**.

**Serious challenge:** In a siege or riot an experienced Lord Steward might effectively direct men, food and gates **before** a lawful election. The legal two-thirds rule does not prohibit de facto interim control; it forbids mistaking that control for an already completed election.

## 4. Geography and signal-control gates

- **At Castle Black:** King’s Tower houses the **queen's court**, Hardin's Tower houses **Val under Jon's own guards**, the partly ruined **Lord Commander's Tower undervault** houses transferred **Cregan**, while Jon had his own **rooms by the armory** (AFFC 5; ADWD 53, 69). These are **four different locations**, not interchangeable “the Lord Commander's room.”
- **Gate and Wall:** Mully is shown holding keys during Val's departure north of the Wall in **ADWD 39**; **ADWD 69** does not show who presently holds the keys or controls the winch/three interior gates. **Do not infer permanent custody of keys from a previous shift.**
- **Ravens:** Clydas has handled an outbound warning to Deepwood and the inbound Pink Letter (**ADWD 49, 69**). Knowledge of a message is not proof he still has uncontested rookery access during a putsch. Information to the **Eastwatch/Shadow Tower hostages** or **Stannis's lakes** is therefore **not sent, let alone received**, within the five published books.
- **External posts:** the 60 forwarded hostages are in *at least two destinations collectively*, Eastwatch and Shadow Tower; exactly how many each got is not established (**ADWD 58**). **Cotter is away at Hardhome, Glendon commands Eastwatch**, and Mallister remains separate at the Shadow Tower. There is no observed instant channel linking their responses.
- **Parallel security burden:** **ADWD 69** shows two chained corpses still stored in the ice cells, unburned, and the entrances vulnerable to fresh snow. Their reanimation is **not observed**; the need to protect the living and guard the Wall does not vanish when factions fight over Jon.

## 5. What was publicly announced immediately before the stabbing?

**ADWD 69 (Jon) partial order, all on-page until the knives:**

1. **Selyse** discusses Hardhome, insists Jon will answer to Stannis and announces further Free Folk marriages (including Patrek/Val); she and Jon disagree about Watch discretion and religious submission.
2. **Jon** hears Melisandre's warning, later meets his officers and Tormund; his direwolf **Ghost behaves aggressively**, and Jon **confines Ghost in the armory** before going to the Shieldhall. This is a documented immediate tactical separation—not proof a wolf/resurrection outcome is known.
3. **Pink Letter** arrives by Clydas; its writer's claims about Stannis, Mance and demands for royal/wildling persons remain unverified.
4. **Jon and Tormund** discuss next steps; Jon calls the Shieldhall. Jon says the **Watch** is to assist Hardhome while he will ride south against Ramsay and invites volunteers, without requiring his sworn brothers to accompany him.
5. **Several Free Folk chiefs applaud and volunteer**. **Marsh and Yarwyck withdraw with followers**. Melisandre and the two royal knights seen by Jon also leave the meeting; **their motives remain unobserved**.
6. Jon departs with **Horse and Rory**; hears a scream and rushes to **Hardin's Tower**. He sees **Patrek dead**, **Wun Wun bleeding from sword wounds** and multiple factions converging in the yard. He calls **Leathers** to talk to the giant, tries to prevent swords coming out, and is stabbed by **Wick, Marsh and two further blows of unidentified agency**.
7. **Narration ceases.** The scene does *not* establish formal impeachment, a new Watch election, a royal arrest warrant, a rescued Val, a freed Cregan, a messenger or any Free Folk counterattack.

**Important warning about causation:** Patrek's previously announced engagement to Val and the Hardin's Tower location make a botched attempt to reach her **plausible**. They do not prove motive or an agreed diversion with Marsh. Also, the Watch officers' disapproval of Jon's intended Winterfell ride supplies a credible grievance **without** verifying their full internal planning.

### Character-led pressure without fixed outcomes

- **Marsh:** Protective rationale for the Watch clashes with responsibility to feed and safeguard children living under its protection. His visible tears in ADWD 69 indicate emotion, **not** proof of remorse or a rescue motive.
- **Tormund:** To demand vengeance risks putting **Dryn and dozens of children** in the control of enemies at more than one castle; protecting them may be more urgent than fighting.
- **Selyse:** She can shelter Shireen and respond to a knight's death, but a decision to enforce royal marriages could collide with Free Folk consent and Watch electoral independence. **Her rumored widowhood is based on the letter, not proven history**.
- **Leathers:** A sworn Watch brother who formerly stood with Free Folk is exceptionally situated to explain why rescue and restraint serve both groups; **he is neither automatically obeyed nor shown stopping the fight**.
- **Alys/Sigorn:** If an actual report reaches them, they must choose how to protect their marriage and possible Karhold claim without assuming Jon's office, Stannis's ratification, a willing northern garrison or the Watch's resources. The earlier notes' **Harrion life/death, Arnolf betrayal receipts and 200-Thenn travel questions remain open**.

### D. Decision gate that discriminates the immediate branches

The **first verifiable division of power** is not “who is next Lord Commander” but **who can make an enforceable order at the place where the next vulnerable person is held**. The fastest useful new-scene tests are: (1) can Leathers and named guards prevent further fighting at Hardin's; (2) which Watch officers/gaolers control Cregan's **new** undervault; (3) who holds the 40 boys initially left at Castle Black, including whether Dryn is there; (4) who can authorize a raven to Hewett or Mallister; (5) what Tormund actually learns in Shieldhall; and (6) whether Alys/Sigorn are even accessible.

**Disposition:** `VERIFIED: scattered posts and differentiated arrangements; Alliser sent ranging, not shown back; Sigorn's grievance-root and witnessed marriage.` `UNKNOWN: post-stabbing command, all garrison reactions, absent Thorne fate, Sigorn's location and next choice.` Independent review remains outstanding.

## 8. An unexpected election constraint: distant votes, two hostile commanders and the absent broker

**Targeted direct source review (2026-10-15):** **ASOS 75 and 78 (Samwell), 79 (Jon); AFFC 45 (Samwell); ADWD 28, 49, 58 and 69 (Jon).** This strengthens the constitutional analysis without resolving the assassination's aftermath.

### A. The elective body was NOT restricted to brothers physically in Castle Black

- **ASOS 75 (Samwell):** The choosing used privately cast **tokens**, counted by Sam and Clydas with Maester Aemon adjudicating. A brother **on duty could ask another brother to vote on his behalf**. **Denys Mallister and Cotter Pyke voted for garrisons they had left behind**. These are narrated precedents for **proxy voting** and cross-garrison representation.
- The chapter also states the winning threshold was **two-thirds of the sworn brothers**, not a mere majority of one garrison or whoever survived a particular meeting. An earlier choosing was said by Aemon to have lasted nearly **two years and seven hundred votes**. There is no sourced statutory deadline for an instant successor.
- **ASOS 78 (Samwell) / 79 (Jon):** Although Stannis threatened to interfere, Mallister and Pyke insisted that only sworn brothers could elect their Lord Commander. Jon was **then chosen through the Night's Watch ballot**, not appointed by Stannis.
- **Correction to any implied new election mechanics:** A claimant does not necessarily need to assemble everyone at Castle Black; proxy voting has a demonstrated precedent. Conversely the text does **not** provide detailed rules for an emergency election after the current assassination or demonstrate that a fresh vote has been called. Neither “Marsh controls the Castle Black men” nor “the outside garrisons cannot be heard in time” is a proven voting outcome.

**A highly consequential information asymmetry within the election's own history:** In **ASOS 78 (Samwell)**, Sam privately tells **Mallister** that Stannis means to appoint **Pyke** and tells **Pyke** that Stannis means to appoint **Mallister** if no agreement occurs. Do not claim Stannis truly made either ultimatum, the two commanders subsequently discovered the deception, or that Sam's persuasion makes the formal vote null.

**Why a second compromise is harder:** The prior choosing benefited from **Sam's individual persuasion**, **Aemon's counted and adjudicated ballot**, and **Clydas's assistance** (ASOS 75/78). By **AFFC 45 (Samwell)**, **Sam is in Oldtown and Aemon has died**. Clydas remains at Castle Black as the message handler (ADWD 69). Those facts mean the **known previous political broker and respected election elder are absent**, not that no one else could convene a choosing or accurately count its votes.

### C. Two different chains of obedience at Eastwatch

**ADWD 49 (Jon):** Pyke's own communication states he commands from **Talon** on the Hardhome mission and **Ser Glendon holds Eastwatch**. **ADWD 58 (Jon)** places Pyke in deep trouble at Hardhome, reporting a wrecked rescue, losses, violence, and dead things on land and water. **ADWD 58** also reports that some of the sixty dispersed boy hostages went to Eastwatch (exact count and ward locations unknown).

Three questions must be kept separate in a future scene:
1. Would **Hewett**, physically commanding Eastwatch, immediately follow Marsh's disputed decree?
2. Would **Pyke**, if news reached his ships and he survived to answer, ratify Hewett's choice?
3. Would the **Eastwatch sworn brothers' represented electoral tokens**, however assembled, support anyone at all?

The source supports **no affirmative answer** to any of these. This is a specific alternative to the false shortcut “Pyke will recognize Marsh because Eastwatch is held by his deputy” or “Pyke will save Jon because he detests Marsh.” In the meantime, **the Hardhome rescue and scattered hostages** generate responsibilities neither rival candidate can wish away.

## 9. Which of Jon's orders can continue without Jon? — real delegation, sworn brothers, and unexecuted plans

**Direct source audit, 2026-10-15:** **ASOS 78 (Samwell); ADWD 35, 39, 53, 58, 69 (Jon)**. The narrow answer is **not** “all Jon's orders are binding” or “all die when Jon falls.” The five books give an earlier **temporary delegation**, distinguish men who have **already sworn the Watch's vows** from Free Folk guests, and show tasks in very different states of execution. **No general legal rule of automatic order survival was found in these passages.**

### A. A correction to the authority ledger: Marsh *has* held expressly delegated command

- **ADWD 35 (Jon):** When Jon rides out for the old-gods grove with new recruits, he tells Bowen Marsh, **“The Wall is yours, Lord Bowen.”** Marsh had just protested the dangers and Jon anticipated returning by midnight. **VERIFIED: personal short-term delegation for a specific absence**, not an automatic right to command permanently if the Lord Commander dies or is incapacitated.
- The same chapter says **Jon, Iron Emmett and Marsh personally assessed the six recruits and assigned them to Watch orders** before the new oaths: Leathers and Jax to the rangers; Horse to the builders; others to stewards/rangers. That participation matters. **Marsh is a witness and participant in formalizing at least two former Free Folk as brothers**, even though he opposes Jon's larger resettlement. This is **not** proof he accepted their proposed promotions or general Free Folk admission.
- **ADWD 39 (Jon):** Marsh protests the “savage” Leathers becoming master-at-arms. Jon replies that Leathers is now a **brother because he took the words**. This disagreement over **appointment/fitness** is different from a finding that Leathers never became a Night's Watch brother.
- **ASOS 78 (Samwell):** Maester Aemon declares as **Watch law** that a recruit's former crimes/transgressions are wiped clean when he takes the oath. This is strong textual precedent for the *membership status* of Leathers/Jax and their right to be treated as sworn brothers. It is **not a general pardon for future crimes**, proof they are acceptable leaders, or an extension of identical standing to thousands who **never took the black**.
- **Caveat:** Marsh stabs Jon at ADWD 69. His earlier delegated service is therefore a **real practical command precedent** and a **moral contradiction**, **not a legal ratification of the stabbing** or proof that all officers or garrisons would now obey him.

### C. One legal boundary is firmer than the child-protection claim

**ASOS 78 (Samwell):** Ser Denys Mallister declares the Watch does not fight in the kingdoms' wars; Stannis accepts in principle that he may not conscript Watch swords to fight his domestic enemies, while also pressuring the Watch over castles/the Gift. **ADWD 69:** Jon repeats the neutrality rule and names his march separately. This supplies a **credible institutional argument against the Winterfell march**, even if the reader supports Jon's motive.

**ADWD 53 (Jon):** Jon argues that admitting living humans fights the dead, consistent with the oath to defend the realms of men. Marsh answers that housing, training and feeding old enemies violates those same vows. **They disagree about what the Watch's mission requires; no neutral institutional adjudicator resolves that dispute**. Stabbing Jon is neither a demonstrated lawful deposition nor an implied vote on the legitimacy of every earlier act.

**Crucial limit:** The Watch's law about **sworn brothers** (ASOS 78) does not transform an **unsworn hostage boy** into a brother entitled to the same specific remission of crimes. The moral duty to protect children, practical obligations incurred by accepting them, and historical northern wardship precedent are **not identical to an explicit Watch rule prohibiting the execution of hostages**. The Norrey describes old northern hostage executions in ADWD 53 as an enforcement threat. Do not invent a statute of safe custody for all boys.

### D. A predecessor's safeguards could outlive Jon without becoming settled policy

**ADWD 39:** The Old Bear's surviving cautionary notes illustrate that at least some knowledge of dangerous Watchmen could be **handed from commander to commander**, even though all had sworn vows. Jon's already established response to dangerous male conduct around women was to put **Emmett and Edd** at Long Barrow; his discussion of Danny Flint and the two disguised girls in **ADWD 58** makes the protection problem explicit. There is therefore a real **documented precedent for risk-aware appointments**, not evidence the Watch as a whole has a standing child-protection office.

A feasible first-day safeguarding scene would need a **named brother who actually controls the stewards/guards**, orders to account for children (including girls not yet confirmed at Long Barrow), access to whichever of Mormont's notes remain, and cooperation from the separately commanded Eastwatch and Shadow Tower. **Tormund can seek these guarantees without first endorsing a candidate for Lord Commander; Marsh can grant limited protection without approving a Winterfell raid.** These are plausible *new choices*, **not events narrated at Jon's last POV**.

### E. Decision tests that could falsify a “compromise Watch” outline

1. **Marsh rejects every earlier order:** His explicit ADWD 53/69 anti-resettlement positions make wholesale repudiation a serious live outcome. That still leaves the practical question of thousands of living settlers and dispersed boys; it does **not** make the past passage and roll call disappear.
2. **Marsh condemns the Winterfell march but continues the wards/food transports:** **INFERRED**, not certain; tests whether he draws a different boundary between neutrality, promised logistics and rebellion.
3. **A sworn brother refuses Marsh's orders:** Leathers' demonstrated Watch membership matters for whether a command is framed as a brother's duty or outsider interference. His personal loyalty is **UNKNOWN**; Watch membership does not tell us whether he accepts the new claimed leader.
4. **Tormund demands nonretaliation but wants Hardhome support:** Both are consistent with a father trying to keep a son safe and other families alive; the Watch would have to decide whether the Hardhome mission is a continuing defense duty, an unauthorized expenditure or a security risk.
5. **An officer invokes Mormont's notes:** Requires actual possession and interpretation of notes; neither can be silently assumed from Jon's prior knowledge.

**Disposition:** `VERIFIED: Marsh's express prior short-term delegation, his direct participation in assessing former Free Folk as sworn brothers, Aemon's remission-of-prior-crimes rule, already executed admissions and transfers, Mormont's cautionary notes.` `CONTESTED: whether settlement fulfilled the Watch oath; whether Jon's southern plan truly avoided oathbreaking.` `UNKNOWN: post-stabbing interim command, survival of particular orders, new protection guarantees, authorized Hardhome ranging and elected successor.` Independent review still required.

## 10. Serious counterexamples, source corrections and verification

**Counterexample A — rapid restoration of Watch order without open civil war:** Marsh's faction might impose temporary control while Leathers or other brothers prevent wider bloodshed. The meeting's numbers do not prove every Free Folk fighter would rush to revenge. This could leave a credible, ugly contested authority without a full battle.

**Counterexample B — rapid Free Folk resistance:** A killing of their chief guarantor amid a wounded giant, hostile marriage claims and endangered hostages might trigger immediate armed reaction. **But** Tormund has promised men for Hardhome and entrusted Dryn to Jon; the Free Folk are not a single unit under Sigorn or Gerrick.

**Counterexample C — royal protective intervention:** Selyse's own guards might focus on keeping her and Shireen safe from escalating violence rather than taking over the Watch or enforcing marriages. Her actual choice after the knives is **UNSHOWN**.

**Proxy election correction:** ASOS 75 (Samwell) explicitly describes delegation of voting tokens for brothers on duty and votes cast by Mallister/Pyke for their distant garrisons. Physical assembly of the full Watch is **not a proven requirement** for a valid choice; neither are new proxy instructions or acceptance of any candidate known after ADWD 69.

**Explicit corrections to overly broad earlier formulations:**
1. “One hundred hostages at Castle Black” is false after **ADWD 58**; Marsh sends **sixty** toward Eastwatch and Shadow Tower, with later custody unobserved.
2. “Bowen becomes acting Lord Commander” is **unproved**; his **past castellanship and specific ADWD 35 delegation** do not establish an automatic succession rule.
3. “Mance's princely son at Castle Black” is **misidentified**: the actual child travels with Gilly (AFFC 45); the baby left at Castle Black is **Gilly's son** (ADWD 7; AFFC 5/45). The infants' social labels differ from genealogy.
4. “Queen's men and watch officers jointly assassinated Jon” is **unproved**; only Watchmen are shown stabbing him; the queen's attending knights depart but no attack by them on Jon is depicted.
5. “Sigorn immediately saves Jon or seizes Karhold” is **unsupported**; the magnar's location after ADWD 49 and his next decisions are not shown.
