## Executive result: a home garrison commander exists; a Winterfell expedition commander is not named

**ADWD 19 (Davos)** directly names **Ser Marlon Manderly**, **Wyman's cousin and commander of *his garrison***, at **White Harbor**, questioning Davos's claims about Stannis and northern military support. **ADWD 29** directly records **Wylis Manderly, Wyman's surviving heir, returned safely** to his father's household at White Harbor. Neither man is shown traveling to the **Winterfell** wedding or leading its White Harbor detachment. Do **not** promote **“commander of his garrison”** into **“Wyman's deputy at Winterfell.”**

**ADWD 51 (Theon)** directly depicts **four White Harbor knights shielding the wounded Wyman**, with the **eldest**, a **nameless greybeard**, answering Hosteen's continuing threats while **Maester Medrick** treats the lord. He has a **distinctive three-silver-mermaids-on-violet surcoat**, but is **not named, formally appointed to a field command, or seen ordering the three hundred** Manderly men that Aenys says came to Winterfell (ADWD 46). The source distinguishes **bodyguard authority in a room** from **command over a force outside the walls**.

**Additional missed gate:** **ADWD 29 (Davos)** has Wyman declare himself **too large to sit a horse**, hence his intention to attend the wedding **by barge and litter with a hundred knights**. Long **before Hosteen wounds him**, a plan for Wyman to personally head a mounted sortie was mechanically unlikely. Recovery from the chin wounds alone would **not** grant him the ability to ride. Roose's later order to Wyman to gather White Harbor men can be an instruction to a lord **to arrange and authorize his troops**, not proof that he is himself their mounted general.

**Highest-confidence endpoint:** `SER MARLON = HOME-GARRISON COMMANDER`; `WYLIS = RETURNED HEIR, LOCATION AT WINTERFELL UNVERIFIED`; `UNNAMED FOUR / ELDEST KNIGHT = WYMAN'S IMMEDIATE PROTECTORS`; `FREY HORSES MOVE`; `WHITE HARBOR SORTIE COMMANDER/ROUTE/OBEDIENCE = UNKNOWN`.

## 2. Why the old knight's arms matter, without naming him by wishful heraldry

**ADWD 51** describes the elder White Harbor defender with **three silver mermaids on a violet field**. **ADWD 15 (Davos)** presents White Harbor marked by House Manderly's **merman**, including the New Castle and city emblems; **ADWD 19** gives Marlon's distinctively marine armor. The old defender's arms are **not described as the ordinary single Manderly merman**.

- `VERIFIED:` Theon notices a distinctive surcoat worn by the **speaking knight** among **four White Harbor knights**.
- `INFERRED:` a separate personal or house device, or a variant/adapted emblem, **might** distinguish the knight from the lord's immediate family and suggest a bannerman or cadet's status. This possibility would make a newly named deputy plausible in a continuation.
- `UNKNOWN:` **what person, house, branch or military commission** those three mermaids identify. No named identification is made in the checked passage; matching sea imagery alone does not make this man Marlon, Wylis, a knight of House Manderly or a named castellan.
- `CONSTRAINT:` the four had authority **to shield the injured lord**, while ability to command the force **and control its objectives** is unshown. Even a named hereditary bannerman does not automatically inherit Manderly household command.

**Do not invent a “Ser [name]” for this knight from online fan lists or heraldry guesses** without a primary passage, attribution and correct status labeling. There is a genuine clue worth preserving, not a solved identification.

## 3. Wyman's mounted incapacity predates his wounding — the important logistical correction

The **sequence across ADWD 29 → 37 → 46 → 51** is:

1. **ADWD 29**: Wyman tells Davos he has become **too heavy to sit a horse**, and therefore will **travel to Winterfell by barge and litter with a hundred knights**. A subordinate leading mounted troops is plausible *even with Wyman alive and healthy*. There is **no named deputy in his statement**.
2. **ADWD 37**: At the wedding, Wyman is so drunk he **needs four men to support him out of the hall**. Those four men are *not certified as the same four named by their location* later when he is wounded. The chapter shows his practical dependency for movement, not a permanent absence of political control.
3. **ADWD 46**: Wyman threatens through banter to ride behind Freys; Hosteen doubts him. This may be bravado, a statement that his *knights* would ride, or simple theater. It **does not supersede** his prior direct acknowledgment of inability to mount. **Aenys** gives his force figure and suspects knights' nighttime involvement; both are character claims.
4. **ADWD 51**: Wyman is cut, collapses and lies bleeding; his knights protect him while **Medrick treats him**. Roose's order still addresses the **lord**, while active tactical performance now requires at least an **independently acting subordinate or physical recovery and transport**.
5. **ADWD 51**: Hosteen/Aenys and Frey horses visibly move from the hall; Wyman **never does so in the checked scene**. No alternate White Harbor commander is identified.

**Conclusion:** `RECOVERS WOUND` and `CAN COMMAND MOUNTED FORMATION` are two separate gates. Wyman might authorize a battle plan from a litter, act through an officer, refuse to send men, or become incapacitated. The text establishes none of these after the command.

**Counterargument:** Lordly tactical command does not *always* require mounted presence. A command sent from a litter, castle or sealed note could be valid, but the **note, subordinate, assent and receipt** are not narrated. It would be wrong to assert his disability means he cannot issue orders at all.

## 4. Medical custody is not a Manderly intelligence network

**ADWD 37** independently names **three Winterfell maesters**: Medrick (formerly Hornwood), Rhodry (formerly Cerwyn) and Henly (formerly Slate), working under Roose to manage Winterfell's restored ravens. **ADWD 51** puts **Rhodry** with a wet raven during Roose's command announcement, and **Medrick** tending wounded Wyman. This is a **real intersection of military decision-making, potential message handling and a lord's medical condition** under the Bolton household's roof.

- **What is shown:** Medrick sees the wounded lord; he **tries** to staunch blood; Rhodry attends Roose with a bird; the two are **different named maesters**, and the bird's earlier flight direction/sender are not explicitly observed.
- **What is not shown:** Medrick secretly favoring the Manderlys, sending a message to Marlon or Wylis, reporting Wyman's health to Freys, deliberately withholding treatment, deciding what commanders do, or even whether Wyman stabilizes.
- **Separate White Harbor channel:** **Maester Theomore** is the Manderly household's own counselor at White Harbor. Wyman privately expresses distrust of him because of possible Lannister family loyalties. The accusation is **Wyman's**, not proof Theomore is an actual infiltrator.
- **Potential double exposure:** If Wyman were to give an undercover order from his sickbed, both a **physician with access to him** and **White Harbor knights who may have been compromised** (Wyman alleges that Symond Frey bought two of his knights in ADWD 29) could create confidentiality problems. Neither leak nor collusion is depicted.
- **Method:** medicine `POV-OBSERVED`; political leakage `THEORY` until there is a messenger, testimony or actual receipt.

## 5. A coordinated defection requires more than Frey-hatred

A plausible `WHITE HARBOR FIELD DEFECTION` hypothesis, if pursued, requires the following **disjoint links**:

| Gate | What the published novels establish | Missing event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Intent** | Wyman privately reveals hatred of Freys/Boltons, describes staged Davos death and demands Rickon as his price (ADWD 29). | That he **specifically** wants the Winterfell cavalry to switch sides before Rickon returns. |
| **B. Trusted delegation** | Wyman arrives with knights; **an elder knight can speak for four bodyguards** (ADWD 51). | Named field commander, authority to instruct White Harbor’s full force and actual communication with Wyman. |
| **C. Message security** | Wyman says Frey spies bought two knights at White Harbor and distrusts Theomore (ADWD 29). | A documented secret briefing or a tested reliable courier within Winterfell. |
| **D. Medical continuity** | Medrick works on Wyman's wounds; protection ring remains around him (ADWD 51). | His subsequent consciousness, life status, ability to issue or revise orders, or chosen acting commander. |
| **E. Physical exit** | Roose orders rendezvous at east gate; earlier heavy ice and snow restrict gates (ADWD 46/51). | Gate cleared/opened, troops assembled, force moving through actual outer approach. |
| **F. Field allegiance** | Frey–Manderly casualties and open hatred are real (ADWD 46/51). | A captain chooses to fight Freys or Stannis; those under him follow, and separation of troops does not prevent action. |
| **G. Pro-Stannis coordination** | Stannis is opposed to Bolton; Tycho later delivers Theon/Jeyne near Stannis; Theon heard Roose's order (ADWD 62/51). | Messenger carries a promise/recognition, Stannis hears it and accepts any timely aid; he does not already know Wyman's Rickon terms. |

**Alternative paths are genuinely different:** A captain might **protect and evacuate Wyman** rather than attack; hold White Harbor men at the east gate until the Freys depart; feign compliance and retreat; attack Freys out of personal vengeance **without** adopting Stannis; or comply with Roose despite his lord's private politics. **Some followers may have lost comrades during the melee but lack the lord's Rickon intelligence.** Neither reaction can be assigned wholesale from their heraldic coats.

### The anti-Frey but not pro-Stannis distinction

Wyman's direct *conditional* promise to Davos is **bring Rickon, then I will recognize Stannis**. The anti-Frey blood feud can precede, outlast or contradict that diplomatic bargain. Therefore:

`ANTI-FREY ACTIVITY` **does not entail** `MANDERLY OATH TO STANNIS` and especially not `STANNIS HAS BEEN TOLD THE PLAN`.

A White Harbor commander might turn on Hosteen, delay action to preserve his own men, or fight Stannis under Bolton pressure, all without the leader's return. Wyman cannot be treated as a mind controlling unseen cavalry from a sickbed.

## 6. Candidate command paths and difficult disconfirmers

| Model | Supported premise | Specific falsifier / cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **M1 — Unnamed older knight leads under Wyman's authority** | His guard shows cohesion and he speaks first to Hosteen (ADWD 51). | He is neither named nor shown to know the muster orders or possess rank above other captains. Four-man protective leadership does **not** automatically scale to ~300 men. |
| **M2 — A different White Harbor captain from among the ~100 knights takes command** | Wyman planned to bring 100 knights (ADWD 29); Aenys reports a company of 300 (ADWD 46). | No named captain or delegation observed. Frey allegations about purchased knights create a speculative loyalty risk, **not proof of a particular traitor**. |
| **M3 — Wyman gives an order without personally riding** | He remains the lord even if too heavy to ride; pre-injury mounted incapacity is already openly acknowledged (ADWD 29). | In ADWD 51 he is collapsed and being treated; **no speech or dispatch from him after the wound** is narrated. |
| **M4 — Marlon or Wylis controls the wider House Manderly response from White Harbor** | Named garrison officer and returned heir actually exist at home (ADWD 19/29). | Neither is physically placed at Winterfell's east gate or shown directing its men; a future message and receipt would be needed. |
| **M5 — Bolton temporarily coerces White Harbor formation** | Roose controls Winterfell, commands sorties and uses **forty Dreadfort spearmen** to force a truce in the hall (ADWD 51). | No on-page assignment of Bolton overseers to east-gate White Harbor troops; he cannot assume their political loyalty once outside. |
| **M6 — East-gate force never becomes a viable column** | Wyman incapacitated, snow/ice problem and open Frey-Manderly hostility are narrated. | A battalion can use subordinate commanders and clear gates; no shown failure or abandonment of orders. |

**Least-assumption assessment:** **some officer must have practical responsibility for Wyman's men if they form and march without him**, but **no candidate can currently be named as the actual appointed field commander**. The presence of Marlon at White Harbor does **not** fill a winter-campaign gap simply because he has “commander” in his title.

## 7. Immediate tests for the next reader or future draft

1. If a later chapter names a **White Harbor knight with three silver mermaids on violet**, compare exact description and affiliation before assigning the old man.
2. If Wyman survives, **who treats him and who certifies he is able to speak?** Medrick's observed intervention is not a survival certificate.
3. If Wylis or Marlon gives an order, **how does it travel** between White Harbor and the Winterfell force? No demonstrated short-distance or immediate dispatch in ADWD 51.
4. If the White Harbor men show hostility to Freys, **does it result in a fight, delay or simply bodyguard protection?** These are not equivalent troop-scale actions.
5. If the White Harbor cavalry rides through the **east gate**, identify which physical Winterfell gate the account means and how snow/ice restrictions were cleared. No automatic identification with the Battlements Gate.
6. If Stannis hopes for Manderly assistance, **who told him Wyman had already demanded Rickon as the condition?** Davos does not report delivery in the five novels.
7. If a commander at the lakes employs the **Night Lamp** mechanism, demonstrate the Frey approach, Manderly position and the Stannis reception separately; no battlefield win is verified by this command study.
8. If an anonymous old knight is later formally revealed, do not back-project his orders to **ADWD 51** before that reveal.
