# House Hornwood: military losses, contested succession, and Bolton appropriation

- Record role: `RESEARCH / NORTHERN HOUSEHOLD ATTRITION, SUCCESSION AND FORCE-IDENTITY AUDIT`
- Status: `FIVE-NOVEL PRIMARY-TEXT AUDIT / NO HOUSE-BY-HOUSE CASUALTY LEDGER`
- Lifecycle: `ACTIVE`; authority: `ADVISORY / NONE`
- Scope boundary: this is a complementary House Hornwood case study for the [four-campaign attrition audit](roose-bolton-green-fork-duskendale-trident-attrition-audit.md), not a new claim that every Hornwood loss was deliberately engineered. The [Hornwood-fingers theory docket](../theories/cases/hornwood-fingers.md) owns the narrow question of Ramsay's exact treatment of Lady Donella.
- Primary scenes rechecked: **AGOT 53/55/62/63** (Bran, Catelyn, Tyrion, Catelyn); **ACOK 16/28/35/66** (Bran, Bran, Bran, Theon); **ASOS 49** (Catelyn); **ADWD 12/15/17/19/28/29/32/35/37/41/46** (Reek, Davos, Jon, Davos, Jon, Davos, Reek, Jon, The Prince of Winterfell, The Turncloak, A Ghost in Winterfell). File links below use the repository's chapter files, whose numeric prefixes include prologues.

## Executive result

House Hornwood supplies an important **counterweight** to a simple “Bolton killed off northern manpower” model. Its lord dies at the Green Fork and its only trueborn son dies at the Whispering Wood; this succession collapse is independently narrated through Lannister reports and Northern POVs. Ramsay then gains a purported legal foothold by coercing the widow into marriage and a will. But the books also keep showing **Hornwood men, banners, refugees, and a disputed Manderly claim**. The house is damaged, divided, and appropriated—not textually erased.

The most direct Trident datum is narrower than it is often made: in **ASOS 49 (Catelyn)**, **Roose's own account** says he left *one hundred Hornwood longbows* among a **later 600-man ford garrison** under Ronnel Stout and Ser Kyle Condon. This is a `CHARACTER CLAIM` about a post-attack defensive deployment. It is **not** evidence that those archers were among Wylis's rear guard, that they died, that they were handpicked for sacrifice, or that the named 600 were all later casualties. The later record does not establish their fate.

What *is* securely visible is a different Bolton advantage: the vacuum made by wartime deaths lets Ramsay present himself as **Lord of Hornwood** in letters and ceremonies, while Roose extracts from Wyman Manderly a demand to abandon *his* Hornwood claim. The political prize is land, castles, banners, and the right to call on survivors—not a documented transfer of every Hornwood soldier's loyalty.

## 2. The succession crisis is an independent mechanism of Bolton power

The Green Fork and Whispering Wood leave **Donella without husband or trueborn son**, creating a practical and legal contest before Ramsay enters the story. **ACOK 16** is unusually specific: Manderly, Umber, Tallhart, Flint, Karstark, Glover/Larence, and even possible Riverlands marriages are all proposed or discussed. Winterfell's temporary government concludes that Robb must decide. That is a `DIRECTLY OBSERVED DELIBERATION`, not a grant.

Ramsay exploits the interval while Robb is absent. **ACOK 28** gives the seizure/marriage report; **ACOK 35** makes the result materially consequential because Ser Rodrik says Ramsay forced vows before a septon and heart tree, consummated the union before witnesses, and got a signed/sealed will. Luwin counters that coercion voids the vows. The novels therefore preserve an actual **legal dispute**, not merely a retrospective horror story.

Later public usage shows political success of a kind:

- **ADWD 19 (Davos)**: Wyman says Roose requires him to surrender *his claim* to Hornwood lands and castles as a condition of settlement.
- **ADWD 28/32/37 (Jon, Reek, Theon)**: Ramsay repeatedly styles himself Lord of Hornwood, including in a notice with other northern marks and at the false-Arya wedding.
- **ADWD 19/29 (Davos)**: White Harbor speakers use Donella's treatment as an argument against accepting Ramsay as overlord.

This establishes `COERCIVE TITLE ASSERTION + POLITICAL RECOGNITION PRESSURE`. It does **not** establish that a northern legal consensus accepts Ramsay's inheritance, that Wyman surrendered his claim, or that Ramsay controls every castle, tenant, levy, or document belonging to Hornwood.

## 3. Castle possession is not the same as the surviving communications infrastructure

The five-book record contains an early **reported** castle occupation but no endpoint inspection. In **ACOK 28 (Bran)**, Bran learns that Wyman Manderly has taken Hornwood Castle, saying in a written explanation that he means to protect its holdings from the Boltons. In **ACOK 35 (Bran)**, Ser Rodrik reports Manderly knights and Dreadfort men fighting in Hornwood forests, while admitting he lacks the force to stop them. Neither passage identifies a castellan, garrison, maester, ravenry, or eventual winner. No later POV returns to the castle.

**ADWD 19 (Davos)** does not close that gap. Wyman says Roose's settlement demand requires him to relinquish *his claim* to Hornwood lands and castles. This is `CHARACTER CLAIM IN A NEGOTIATION`: it proves that Wyman asserts a claim and that Roose wants it surrendered, not present physical occupation by either man, a completed surrender, or a received deed.

The one direct institutional endpoint is elsewhere. In **ADWD 37 (The Prince of Winterfell)**, Theon sees **Maester Medrick**, who formerly served Lord Hornwood, at Winterfell with Rhodry and Henly. Theon says Roose brought all three to operate Luwin's ravens so that messages could again be sent and received there. This establishes:

- `POV-OBSERVED`: Hornwood's former household maester is physically at **Winterfell**, and Winterfell has a working Bolton-directed communications function.
- `UNKNOWN`: whether Medrick took Hornwood records, knows current castle control, retains access to Hornwood ravens, sent a Hornwood-specific letter, or favors Bolton, Ramsay, any claimant, or the house's tenants.
- `NOT SHOWN`: a message from Hornwood Castle to Winterfell, a reply, a named castle custodian, or a chain by which Hornwood fighters receive an order.

This matters for future construction: an authenticated message from **Winterfell** via Medrick could plausibly affect a Hornwood question only after a demonstrated dispatch, recipient, and credibility test. Medrick's former service does not make him a hidden Hornwood spokesman. Conversely, a change at Hornwood Castle cannot automatically reach Winterfell merely because the former maester is there.

## 4. Larence Snow: a named blood claimant whose later custody never becomes an on-page claim

**ACOK 16 (Bran)** gives the only narrated succession consideration of Larence Snow. Luwin says the Glovers foster Lord Hornwood's bastard; the Deepwood steward identifies the boy by name, says he is nearly twelve, and praises his wits and courage. Luwin says Bran's proposal that the bastard inherit has merit, while Ser Rodrik says the widow might reject him because he is not her blood. This establishes `NAMED BASTARD + PLAUSIBLE SUCCESSION ROUTE UNDER DISCUSSION`, not an investiture, legitimization, command, or final preference by Robb.

The later **ASOS appendix** labels Larence a thirteen-year-old ward of Galbart Glover and a captive of Asha at Deepwood Motte. That is useful status metadata, but it is **paratextual**, not a scene in which the reader sees his capture, hears his testimony, or learns who may negotiate for him. The **AFFC appendix** still lists him as Galbart's ward but does not repeat the captive description. That omission cannot be converted into a release: appendices are not a continuous event log. The ADWD appendix does not supply a narrated endpoint for him.

The necessary custody chain remains unshown:

`Glover ward / potential claimant → Asha's Deepwood captivity (appendix status) → possible later transfer, release, death, concealment, or continued captivity = UNKNOWN`.

No published narrative puts Larence at Hornwood Castle, with Stannis, at White Harbor, at Winterfell, or among Roose's 600 ford defenders. No named Hornwood captain receives an order from him; no surviving document invokes him; no reader sees him told of Donella's will or Ramsay's claimed title. ****ADWD 35 (Jon)** contains Stannis's received report that Deepwood has been retaken, but it names neither Larence nor any released captive. Deepwood's later military change therefore does not prove a child hostage's receipt or liberty.**

Larence therefore matters as a **contingent legitimacy lever**, not an available army commander. A future appearance could force the question whether a bastard who was considered by Winterfell before Ramsay's coercion has a better claim than a coerced widow's purported heir. It cannot, without additional evidence, retroactively establish who ruled Hornwood, how its men fought, or whether the ford archers survived.

## 5. Beren Tallhart: the maternal-nephew route remains a documented hostage gate

**ACOK 16 (Bran)** supplies a second, distinct claimant route. Leobald Tallhart proposes sending his younger son **Beren** to Lady Donella as foster child and possible heir; he says his wife **Berena is Lord Halys Hornwood's sister**, making Beren Donella's nephew through the dead lord. Luwin calls him potentially the best answer because he is half Hornwood by blood and might take his uncle's name. Ser Rodrik immediately identifies the counterweight: he is still a boy and could be unable to hold the land against stronger suitors.

This is stronger genealogy than a generic banner claim but remains an `UNEXECUTED PROPOSAL`:

- no scene shows Donella accepting Beren as foster son;
- no scene shows Robb approving a name change, inheritance, marriage, or regency;
- no will, seal, castle handover, or Hornwood levy recognizes him;
- Beren's older brother Brandon is named, but the ACOK discussion identifies **Beren**, not Brandon, as the proposed Hornwood successor.

The Tallhart war then makes Beren's status a custody problem, not a living political program. The **ASOS appendix** lists Berena, Brandon, and Beren after Leobald's death at Winterfell; the **AFFC** and **ADWD appendices** specifically list all three as captives at **Torrhen's Square**. The ADWD appendix also places Dagmer Cleftjaw's ironborn at that castle. These are `PARATEXTUAL STATUS ENTRIES`, not scenes of imprisonment, communication, ransom, or release, but they are the latest published status indication.

Thus the endpoint chain is:

`proposed maternal-nephew heir → unshown decision/recognition → Tallhart household captive at ironborn-held Torrhen's Square = NOT AN AVAILABLE HORNWOOD CLAIMANT ON-PAGE`.

This creates a different kind of future possibility from Larence. Larence's last specific captivity label occurs in the ASOS appendix and later status is unshown; Beren's captivity is still carried in the ADWD appendix. Neither boy can be assumed free, informed about Ramsay's title, or able to command a house. Nor does the phrase “captive at Torrhen's Square” prove what Dagmer knows of Hornwood law, that an exchange is underway, or that Beren's claim is legally superior to every other candidate.

## 6. The “hundred longbows” at the ford: what the number can and cannot bear

The Trident crossing record is one speech by Roose in **ASOS 49**. He says the river is high, Gregor is not across it, and he has left an assortment of troops at the ford: men from several regions, freeriders and hedge knights, **100 Hornwood longbows**, and a strong Stout/Cerwyn component, commanded by Ronnel Stout and Ser Kyle Condon. Robb accepts the report as prudent at that moment.

That creates four discrete propositions:

1. `CHARACTER CLAIM`: Roose ordered or left a 600-man rear defense.
2. `CHARACTER CLAIM`: 100 Hornwood longbowmen were in that force.
3. `UNKNOWN`: whether all named men actually remained, how long, and whether they could be supplied/relieved.
4. `UNKNOWN`: whether any particular Hornwood soldiers had been in the earlier exposed rear guard, drowned, were captured, or survived to later ADWD scenes.

The later **ASOS 60 (Tyrion)** statement that Gregor eventually crossed the Trident and seized the Ruby Ford proves a changed strategic result, not the fate of Stout, Condon, or the Hornwood archers. The [Trident witness audit](trident-crossing-wylis-manderly-captivity-witness-audit.md) tracks that distinction in full.

A conventional military reading remains strong: an archer contingent covering a ford during flood conditions is sensible terrain defense, and the mixed force under non-Bolton house officers can indicate a practical combined detachment rather than a list of intended victims. The adverse reading becomes plausible only as a **pattern question** because Roose controls the account and the later historical record is silent; it needs a missing order, survivor testimony, casualty return, or independent force report before becoming a finding.

## 7. Surviving Hornwood people are evidence against simple extermination—not proof of a united resistance

Several ADWD scenes matter because they make a total-destruction account untenable:

- **ADWD 15 (Davos), `REPORTED`:** displaced Hornwood smallfolk reach White Harbor. Their flight establishes insecurity under Ramsay, not a casualty total or factional allegiance.
- **ADWD 15, `REPORTED`:** a riverman says “some Hornwood men” joined Hother Umber with the Moat Cailin attack force. This is a local report, not an order or muster roll.
- **ADWD 17 (Jon), `POV-OBSERVED VISION / DIRECT SPEECH`:** Melisandre sees a Hornwood moose among banners at a wooden town; Clayton calls the banners Lannister lapdogs, while Jon says Hornwood and similar houses lost their lords and he does not know who leads them. A banner demonstrates a force identifying with the house; Jon's statement emphasizes leadership uncertainty.
- **ADWD 35 (Jon), `RECEIVED LETTER / CHARACTER CLAIM`:** Stannis's letter says his host includes people once sworn to Hornwood, Cerwyn and Tallhart. It supports a rival recruitment channel, while leaving authorship, exact numbers and current legal authority unverified.
- **ADWD 37/41 (Theon), `POV-OBSERVED`:** the Hornwood moose is present in Winterfell's visual political display, and Hornwood men are among listeners whose memories of Donella's death matter to Lady Dustin's argument.
- **ADWD 41, `THEON'S INTERNAL INFERENCE`:** Theon thinks a winter battle would conveniently reduce potentially disloyal Northmen, expressly including Hornwood men. This is analytically valuable because it identifies a structural incentive; it is **not Roose's admission** and not an observed order to kill Hornwoods.

The same house can produce refugees at White Harbor, men in a Bolton-aligned operation, men in Stannis's reported host, and banners at Winterfell. These may be different people and different moments. No five-book scene shows a Hornwood council choosing one side, a commander receiving a single house-wide instruction, or a message reconciling these fragmentary loyalties.

## 8. Implications for the Bolton strategy and a future North plot

**Supported political pattern:** Bolton power gains from depleted/leaderless houses without needing proof of a meticulously engineered fatality schedule. Loss of Lord Halys and Daryn creates a claim crisis; coercion turns it into a usable title; surviving retainers still provide banners and manpower, but their personal resentment makes that manpower unreliable. This is consistent with Roose's later need to assemble a broad, uneasy coalition around a false Stark marriage.

**Important limit on the attrition theory:** Hornwood is not evidence that Roose selected its men for destruction at the Green Fork—the initial infantry diversion was Robb's strategy—nor that the 100 archers died at the Trident. It instead shows why **post-loss appropriation** may be as consequential as battlefield attrition.

**Future-revelation value:** A named Hornwood captain, an archive of Donella's coerced will, a ford survivor, or an authenticated Raven from the house could do several different jobs. They could challenge Ramsay's title, clarify force distribution, or determine whether Hornwood men possess a usable independent command. One item cannot silently prove all three. In particular, proof that the marriage was invalid would not itself reveal where the ford archers went; a soldier's account of the ford would not by itself settle the inheritance.

## 9. Open questions and falsifiers

1. Who, if anyone, controls Hornwood Castle at the five-book endpoint? No POV visits it after the succession crisis; Medrick is at Winterfell, not demonstrated at Hornwood, and no Hornwood raven route is shown.
2. What became of the 100 longbowmen and the wider 600-man ford force? No casualty roll, relief order, prisoner list, or later commander testimony is narrated.
3. What exact claim did Wyman hold, and did he ever relinquish it? His settlement terms are a `CHARACTER CLAIM` in a negotiation, not a deed of surrender.
4. Is Ramsay's will available, genuine, or legally enforceable under northern custom? The text gives Rodrik's report of a sealed instrument but no later inspection.
5. Where is Larence Snow after the appendix's Deepwood-captive status? A later appendix omission is not a release scene; without a custody transition, he cannot be treated as an available claimant.
6. Does Beren Tallhart remain alive and captive at Torrhen's Square, and was the proposed Hornwood succession ever communicated beyond Winterfell's ACOK council? The appendices preserve status, not a current legal proceeding.
7. Which Hornwood survivors support Bolton, Stannis, a Stark restoration, or only local survival? Banners and reported recruitment demonstrate fragmentation, not a common preference.
8. Could a Hornwood grievance fuel a Winterfell break? `THEORY`: grievance and manpower make it narratively available. It fails without a named leader, a communicated decision, and an actual opportunity to act.

**Disposition:** `LORD AND TRUEBORN HEIR LOST / SUCCESSION CONTEST DIRECTLY SHOWN / RAMSAY'S COERCIVE TITLE CLAIM PUBLICLY USED / ROOSE-REPORTED 100 ARCHERS AT FORD ONLY / HORNWOOD SURVIVORS AND BANNERS MULTIPLY AT ENDPOINT / HOUSE-WIDE ALLEGIANCE, FORD CASUALTIES AND DELIBERATE BOLTON SACRIFICE UNKNOWN`.
