## Executive finding: the attacking faction has administrative leverage, not verified possession of Castle Black

**VERIFIED:** In **ADWD 69 (Jon)**, first builder **Othell Yarwyck** is surrounded by his builders in Shieldhall; lord steward **Bowen Marsh** stands with **Wick Whittlestick, Left Hand Lew and Alf of Runnymudd**. Marsh and Yarwyck leave with their followers as Jon's southward call is acclaimed. At Hardin's Tower, **Wick and Marsh** actually stab Jon; other wounds are not attributed to named individuals in the printed POV. The departing followers' later positions, the precise number of participants in the stabbing, and any obedience by other Watchmen remain **UNKNOWN**.

**INFERRED:** The attackers plausibly know how to reach the Watch's records, stores and builder work crews. They have **not thereby acquired the gates, archers, rangers, Free Folk chiefs, queen's men, Clydas's ravens, or the distant garrisons**. A garrison can suffer a successful assassination **without a successful succession or functional coup**. Equally, a small administrative faction could operate a caretaker regime **if guards and other brothers acquiesce**. Neither branch can be selected as five-book canon.

## 1. A split along Watch offices existed well before the knives

**ADWD 17 (Jon):** Jon thinks **roughly half of Castle Black supported Marsh's argument to close the Wall's gates and the other half rejected it**; he recalls **Dywen the forester** arguing that sealed gates are a disastrous answer because the Watch cannot man every mile. **Most rangers** generally supported Dywen's position, while **stewards and builders inclined toward Marsh**.

**What this DOES show:** a plausible **occupational fault line** around admission/security policy, independently of an angry crowd on assassination night. Controlling the stewards and builders is not equivalent to having the rangers' professional consent. This is a **past appraisal from Jon's POV**, not a named ballot, precise membership tally, oath to Jon personally, or proof all rangers will fight Marsh.

**Critical presence correction — ADWD 28 (Jon):** Jon then sends **Dywen** into the haunted forest leading one of three ranger parties of three; **Alliser Thorne** accompanies him. Black Jack Bulwer and Kedge Whiteye lead the other two patrols. Dywen's **return is not narrated before ADWD 69**, so he cannot be drafted as the immediate anti-Marsh ranger spokesman. **ADWD 21 (Jon)** also records Jon's impression that First Rangers were dying nearly as fast as appointed, a depleted leadership structure, not a clearly identified commander who can inherit Jon's tactical command. **Do not infer that Dywen, Thorne, Bulwer or Kedge is presently at Castle Black.**

**Counterweight:** The same rangers' resistance to **closing gates** is **not automatically** support for Jon riding south against Ramsay, agreeing to all new admissions, or endorsing Tormund as a commander. A ranger could regard the stabbing as a crime, the Winterfell march as oathbreaking, and continued Wall defense as essential — three independently separable positions.

## 3. Four separable choke points — a coup would need to resolve each

1. **Castle Black courtyard and immediate personal security.** Men respond to a bloody giant confrontation and an attempted or completed stabbing. **ADWD 69** shows mixed **Watchmen, northern guests, Free Folk, queen's men** racing into view. It does not say who arrests whom, who takes command of the bodies, or whether the giant is calmed. **Visible swords do not elect a Lord Commander.**
2. **Locked grain and ration distribution.** **Wick's former store keyring** and Marsh's inventory are unusually concrete, but the current keyholder, guard rota, actual reserves, kitchen staff and cooperation remain undemonstrated. [Food-control audit](wall-winter-food-credit-and-key-control.md) owns the full amounts and financing distinction.
3. **Wall passage and defenses.** The *tunnel's gate bars and keys* (Mully in ADWD 39) are a separate control chain from underground food vaults (Wick in ADWD 17); archers and sentries above the gate constitute another. **Closing a gate is not identical to controlling the whole Wall** — Dywen explicitly challenges that older doctrine, and far forts still have their own officers.
4. **Communications and credible legitimacy.** Ravens through Clydas, messages addressed to Hewett/Mallister, independent safety certification for hostage sons, and the Watch's two-thirds electoral tradition are **separate from same-night coercion**. [Election/custody dossier](castle-black-command-custody-first-night.md) and [information handoffs](information-handoffs.md) are the owners; do **not** invent an election, a dispatched letter or an acknowledged title.

**Material consequence:** **Marsh can plausibly administer stores with other stewards even without commanding all rangers**. **Rangers can plausibly reject the killers while refusing Jon's Winterfell march**. **Free Folk leaders can plausibly demand ward safety while refusing to surrender weapons.** These alliances would be issue-specific and unstable; none is established as already formed.

## 4. Counterexamples to “Marsh cannot possibly govern”

**Counterexample A: disciplined caretaker control.** If enough brothers obey Marsh/Yarwyck for practical tasks, the former officer network, ration records and builders could deliver a meal, hold gates and prevent a riot for a time. The previously recorded split **does not preclude nonaligned rangers from reluctantly accepting essential food/defense orders**. A legal succession dispute and functioning basic administration can coexist. **Unshown, conditional**.

**Counterexample B: a narrow anti-mutiny coalition, no Jon policy victory.** Horse/Rory, Leathers and other uncommitted Watchmen could demand investigation of the attack, while **still opposing a march against Bolton** and debating Free Folk admissions. A person can oppose both the stabbing *and* the disputed southern order. Requires witnessed statements and control points; **unshown**.

**Counterexample C: food available but delivery fails.** The physical supplies are real, but key/guard confusion, attempted seizure or rival factions might interrupt a common meal. That could trigger protective action by hostage fathers even without anyone choosing open war beforehand. **Do not assign sabotage to Wick or Marsh without an actual later event.**

**Counterexample D: Free Folk force but no legitimate command.** Tormund can rally a frightened Shieldhall audience with his horn and possesses fighters; that does not release the sixty dispersed boys, reopen food vaults, supply the flanking castles or persuade the Watch's independent commanders. **An immediate Free Folk military win could still become a winter governance loss**.

**Counterexample E: no conspiracy-scale inference from the hall.** Marsh has known followers; Wick and Marsh are known stabbing participants. Yarwyck and Left Hand Lew are **not named stabbing participants**, Horse and Rory are not shown reacting, and the third/fourth knife wielders remain unidentified. The source does not support declaring an exact number of mutineers or any “half the Watch joined the assassination” count.
