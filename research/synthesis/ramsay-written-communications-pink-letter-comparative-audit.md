## Executive discovery: the Jon wedding proclamation has an independently seen Asha counterpart

**ADWD 26 (Asha)** receives **a pink-sealed raven-borne report explicitly said to come from Barrowton**, with **brown, oversized spiky writing attributed to Ramsay**. Its account of Moat Cailin, the prospective marriage and northern-house countersignatures strongly matches **ADWD 28 (Jon)**. But Asha's version is **not identical in all details**: Asha reports **Ramsay styling himself Lord of Winterfell**, and **Lady Dustin** among appended supporters. Jon reports **Lord of the Hornwood**, and **Lord Dustin** among purported supporters. Record those differences; **do not silently normalize them**.

**ADWD 20 (Reek)** supplies a **third earlier Ramsay-associated communication**: a **pink-sealed safe-conduct scroll delivered to the ironborn garrison by Theon**, describing written terms allowing surrender and passage home. The seal is **not broken or read by a named ironborn in the scene**; the interpretation is **Theon's interested spoken assertion**, not independent proof of the parchment's contents. After the ironborn surrender, **Ramsay visibly breaks the pledged outcome** by slaughtering/exhibiting their bodies. One corpse has a parchment still sealed and wedged in its mouth. This gives a verified precedent of **Ramsay's manipulative *use* of a purported written promise**, though not a direct contemporaneous view of Ramsay composing it.

**ADWD 69 (Jon)** differs in being an explicit **personal ultimatum** to Jon, rather than the Moat's safe-conduct or the multiple-recipient northern proclamation. It uses pink wax and Ramsay's name, but **the chapter does not describe handwriting, ink color, independent seals or accomplice signatures**. The threat's **literary voice and coercive function** could be consistent with Ramsay **without identifying the writer**.

**Most consequential inference:** Even if Ramsay himself wrote the final ultimatum, the conditional promise not to harm the Watch if Jon complies cannot be treated as a reliable guarantee after his **actually observed breach at Moat Cailin**. Conversely, an imitator could copy Ramsay's distinctive public reputation, name and pink-wax convention. This is evidence of **plausibility, not uniqueness**.

## 2. The Moat Cailin test: a written amnesty fails despite successful compliance

**ADWD 20 (Reek):**

1. Theon's fear is acute and his loyalties are constrained by Ramsay's torture. Ramsay sends him to achieve surrender by a deadline; Theon brings the **pink-sealed written safe-conduct** with promises of sustenance and passage.
2. At Moat Cailin, the defenders are exhausted, ill and strategically abandoned. **Adrack Humble** helps force acceptance after a holdout is killed. The surviving garrison disarms and returns toward Ramsay with Theon. They do the **thing the letter's courier demands**.
3. **Ramsay welcomes the surrendered men and orders food and treatment**. This is a performed assurance, not the final disposition.
4. The following morning the former defenders' **bodies are displayed on stakes**, and one body has a parchment **with its pink wax still unbroken**. Theon counts sixty-three displayed corpses. They did **not** receive the promised safe passage. Ramsay sends **three riders south to his father** reporting that the causeway is open.

**VERIFIED:** promised mercy **as conveyed by Theon**, surrender, initial courteous reception, subsequent displayed slaughter, pink-sealed parchment among the corpses, Ramsay's other outbound riders **in this Moat episode**. **Do not falsely claim an ironborn actually read and confirmed every written clause**, Ramsay dictated those clauses on-page, or that the exact body carrying the parchment had physically held it the whole time.

**Analytical weight:** Ramsay is capable of using a written assurance for a tactical gain and breaking the corresponding promise when the target becomes helpless. So the final Pink Letter's “surrender these persons and I will not trouble your Watch” would be **strategically unsafe to take at face value even if authored by Ramsay**. It does **not prove every other factual sentence he could write must be false**; his Moat victory was real.

They demonstrate a **multi-recipient propaganda function**, not a verified single raven visiting both castles or a standardized printed master text.

**Two tempting but invalid leaps:**

- `Asha "Lord of Winterfell" vs Jon "Lord of Hornwood" + Lady/Lord Dustin = proof one copy is a forgery.` **NO.** Both arrive with close matching packages and physical signatures; the text never conducts an independent authorship audit. Recording literal variants is mandatory; assigning the cause to a forger is **THEORY**.
- `Earlier Ramsay writing looks spiky/brown, final pink letter has same hand.` **NO.** The final passage does **not provide handwriting or ink descriptions to match**. Its ominous phrasing and its signer cannot substitute for an observed graphological comparison.

## 4. The final message has a familiar coercive *shape*, but not exclusive diction

**Source-bound comparisons:**

- **Negotiation under fear:** Moat Cailin's safe-conduct says, as **Theon relays it**, surrender and live; otherwise die. The final letter offers no trouble to the Watch if Jon sends demanded persons; otherwise it threatens bodily violence. **Parallel form**, not identical legal conditions or unique authorship.
- **Flaying as communicative proof:** Asha receives a **physical skin remnant** and letter asserting brutality against ironborn. The final text **claims** bodily trophies from spearwives, but **does not include** them. The earlier use of threatened kinfolk makes Ramsay's voice **plausible**, while the lack of an attached trophy leaves the later claim **unconfirmed**.
- **Names and personal leverage:** An earlier proclamation broadcasts a supposed Arya marriage; final letter tries to leverage **missing bride and Reek**. Both may derive from Ramsay's genuine personal interests, yet those interests are **public within Winterfell** and potentially learnable by watchers.
- **Self-status is flexible:** Asha's earlier copy already presents Ramsay as **Lord of Winterfell**, before the final note says **Trueborn Lord of Winterfell**; Jon's parallel earlier copy says **Lord of Hornwood**. Do not treat the word **“Trueborn”** as proving or disproving Ramsay's hand.
- **Style contrast:** The Asha proclamation combines self-aggrandizement, public house endorsements and targeted terror; the final note is **shorter, intimate, insult-heavy and almost entirely imperatives/claims**. Different audience and genre can explain some contrast; an imitator or different writer remains feasible.
- **Seal form:** a **button of hard pink wax** in Asha's and Jon's earlier correspondence versus **a smear of hard pink wax** at the end. This is **verifiable wording**, but it gives no dimensions, signet impression, smell, instrument marks or comparison by a common observer. **Weak evidence on its own**.

The *most* valuable style-independent evidence would be a **dated dispatch record, matching signed physical hand authenticated against a known specimen, a messenger's eyewitness statement of composition or delivery, or a captured writer's credible account**. None exists after the ADWD 69 attack.

**One-way confidence calibration:**
- `HIGH:` physical earlier pink seals; ADWD 26/28 multiple-recipient Moat/wedding circular; two *literal* Asha/Jon differences; ADWD 20 actual broken surrender promise; final public effect on Jon; **no final-hand comparison**.
- `MODERATE / INFERRED:` matching visual conventions suggest common advertised Bolton communication practice, and the final threat's coercion is consistent with Ramsay's demonstrated tactics.
- `LOW / UNKNOWN:` whether the earlier letter used actual blood, whether the skin is demonstrably Theon's by forensic identity, whether Ramsay personally wrote each document, whether any claimed signer personally endorsed the circular, and all final-letter authorship, dispatch and military/captive claims.
- `THEORY / NOT ADOPTED:` forged Pink Letter proven by differences; Ramsay proven by sadism or pink wax; fixed seven-day battle result; conspiracy coordination with Marsh; an unpublished resolution.

## 6. Implications for a five-book-grounded continuation

A character receiving or evaluating the final letter has **good reasons to respond to the danger while withholding trust in its proposed terms**. The earlier Moat example proves a supposed protected surrender ended in massacre. Yet a character at Castle Black **has not necessarily seen ADWD 20's private scene**: the **reader knows** this precedent, but **Jon's individual information** is different. Jon does learn from his earlier wedding notice that Moat Cailin fell and that ironborn were flayed; he does **not on-page conduct our cross-recipient comparison with Asha**.

A future northern investigator could, *conditional on actual travel and surviving archives*, compare Asha's letter with Jon's. Nothing says either physical parchment reaches a common examiner. **This suggested scene is an analytic test, not a prediction**.

The most important conditional nodes: **(1) which earlier actual or copied letter an investigator could obtain; (2) whether the final letter survives with an actual seal/hand for comparison; (3) whose factual reports of Mance and Stannis can be independently checked; (4) whether a recipient believes the threat yet refuses the offered bargain; (5) what information about Theon's fake-identity bride escapes the North**.
