## Executive result: the political split is observed; battlefield behavior has not occurred on-page

**ADWD 51 (Theon)**: after accusations over the murdered Little Walder, **Hosteen Frey slashes Wyman Manderly through several of his chins**. Wyman rises, collapses and is surrounded by four protective White Harbor knights while **Maester Medrick attempts to staunch the bleeding**. The hall melee costs **six White Harbor men and two Freys**, with additional wounded. **Luton**, one of Ramsay's men, is wounded and then personally killed by Ramsay; he is *not* another Frey or Manderly casualty. **Forty Dreadfort spearmen** are needed to separate the combatants.

Only **after** this does **Roose** give the nominally unified military order: **Hosteen and his Freys to assemble at the main gates and strike the first blow**; **Wyman to gather his White Harbor men at the east gate so they also go out against Stannis**. Hosteen threatens to finish killing Wyman *after* killing Stannis. Four Manderly knights oppose him aloud. **ADWD 51 also actually shows Hosteen and Aenys leading Frey horses away from the hall, but Wyman remains under medical attention**.

**Proof boundary:** `DIRECT ORDER → LEADERS AND GUARDS REACT → FREY HORSES BEGIN MOVING` is not `TWO INTACT COLUMNS LEAVE WINTERFELL`, `MANDERLY COMMANDER RECOVERS`, `WHITE HARBOR HAS SECRETLY JOINED STANNIS`, `FREYS FALL THROUGH ICE`, or `ANYONE WINS`. Each further event needs new source evidence.

**Important narrative pressure:** Roose attempts to redirect an actual internal blood feud outward into a common foe. But **one commander has just been incapacitated**, and the king his soldiers are supposedly attacking cannot be presumed to know the orders until a messenger or Theon tells him. The combination creates multiple fragile command handoffs.

## 1. White Harbor's private position was already incompatible with loyal Frey cooperation

**ADWD 29 (Davos) — `DIRECT SECRET TESTIMONY`:**

1. Wyman explains why he publicly had an innocent substitute executed to simulate Davos's death: the Lannister/Frey custody of **his son Wylis** and potential devastation of White Harbor constrained him. Wylis has since returned. **Wyman's own explanation** of coercion fits his observable staged Davos deception; it is still a character's account of his motives.
2. Wyman openly describes his son **Wendel's murder at the Red Wedding**, bitter hostility toward the Freys, and surveillance inside White Harbor. He asserts a Frey spy **Symond** bought some servants and knights, and voices distrust of **Maester Theomore** because of his Lannister origins. **These accusations are attributed to Wyman**, not independently verified in every detail.
3. He expects to attend the Bolton wedding and to **give departing Frey guests palfreys**, invoking the protocol of **guest gifts**. That is *on-page language* relevant to the later missing-Frey mystery; the precise timing and identity of any assassin are not shown.
4. Wyman asserts significant naval, financial and mounted strength, **conditional** willingness to bring lands east of the White Knife behind Stannis, and **the actual price**: Davos must recover **Rickon Stark and Shaggydog**. Neither Davos's successful rescue nor Wyman's recognition of Stannis is established.
5. **Robett Glover**, explicitly, tells Davos that Stannis is Davos's king, not automatically *the North's*. `ANTI-BOLTON` and `PRO-STANNIS` are not interchangeable commitments.

**Implication:** Wyman's actual grievance against Freys and Boltons is **well supported and predates Winterfell's feast**. A theory that his troops might avoid or betray the Freys has a credible *motive basis*. But **secret motivation at White Harbor cannot be projected into every Manderly soldier or a battle pact with Stannis without testimony, instructions and a route**.

## 2. The public wedding is political cover, and the pie question has its own owner

**ADWD 37 (The Prince of Winterfell) — `POV-OBSERVED`:** Wyman physically attends the wedding, supplies the food and drink, and serves three substantial wedding pies to highborn guests, including Freys. He eats heartily himself. **Lady Barbrey Dustin** characterizes him as timid and outwardly cooperative, but also says she suspects he wants revenge and that **Roose watches him eat and drink before doing so himself**. This is **Dustin's assessment and reported observation**, not an independent omniscient judgment of Wyman's cowardice or Roose's exact intention.

**ADWD 46 (A Ghost in Winterfell) — `POV-OBSERVED DIRECT SPEECH`:** Wyman offers to ride behind Hosteen; Hosteen treats the suggestion as a possible rear attack and demands to know what became of the Frey guests. Wyman invokes their departure from White Harbor with guest gifts and dangerous roads. **Aenys Frey questions his explanation**, and Manderly knights stand ready. **Barbrey Dustin and Roger Ryswell intervene to quiet this dispute**; Theon believes Roose looks uneasy.

The **[Frey Pies theory](../theories/cases/frey-pies.md)** assembles the missing Freys, hospitality, wedding pies and Rat Cook imagery. The inference is meaningful but **no on-page ingredient test or direct confession identifies the meat**. This dossier therefore records the **effect** (Frey suspicion and the mounting conflict) rather than asserting the exact mechanism of their disappearance.

**Careful distinction:** Hosts and guests can publicly exchange gifts and promises while privately plotting harm; **ADWD 29 directly demonstrates Wyman staging one deception about Davos**. It does **not establish that every later suspicion about him is proven**.

### Distinguishing Little Walder's murder from its battlefield consequences

The [Twins succession and northern Frey-family audit](frey-twins-succession-edwyn-black-walder-brotherhood-north-fracture.md) checks the specific genealogical and evidentiary traps behind this sortie. **ACOK 4 (Bran)** says **Big Walder** (Jammos's son, from Lord Walder's fourth wife's branch) knows **Little Walder** (Merrett's son, third wife's branch) precedes him in the succession, and Big boasts that he will one day hold the Twins. **ADWD 51 (Theon)** observes Big **bloodied while accompanying Little's corpse**, offering a **dice-debt** account; this does **not identify the killer**. **Little is Fat Walda's full brother and therefore tied to Roose's wife**, so the incident is not an anonymous Frey casualty. **Hosteen accuses Manderly**, the immediate hall brawl kills Frey and White Harbor soldiers, and Roose gives separate march orders; **none proves Big, Wyman, Ramsay, or Abel's women planned the killing**, nor that the later field battle has happened in five-book canon. ## 4. Winterfell's gates and the snow: rally point is not an established escape route

**ADWD 46 (A Ghost in Winterfell)** provides an unusually detailed physical check: the **great main gates** and portcullis are **closed, barred and choked with snow/ice**; other gates are difficult to use; **the Battlements Gate reaches the outer works but not the countryside directly**. The **Hunter's Gate has seen recent use**, with scouts later leaving via it, though their return becomes uncertain. **ADWD 51 then speaks of a main-gates rendezvous for Freys and an east-gate rendezvous for White Harbor men**.

**Known:** rallying two commands in separate places might discourage their fighting in one hall and distribute the immediate siege sortie. **Unknown:** whether snow and chains have been cleared since ADWD 46, which precise gate system the term **“east gate”** denotes against other names, which postern/outer wall each command ultimately uses, their route through snow, their order of march or whether they leave together. **Do not silently map the east gate to a different named portal or show men riding through an explicitly still-frozen portcullis.**

**The logistical test for any proposed Battle of Ice account** must state:

`Roose command given → troops assembled under a named *functioning* officer → gates opened/cleared → enemy route/scouts determined → formation maintains purpose in storm → Stannis receives warning/intelligence → engagement happens`.

Only the first item, plus some Frey horse movement, is directly depicted. The force is not teleported from “east gate” to Stannis's lakes.

## 5. An immediate chain-of-command asymmetry: Frey leaders mobile, Wyman under treatment

**ADWD 51 source-backed last sighting**:

- **Hosteen Frey** can openly answer Roose and threaten Manderly, **physically remains active**, and later **turns with Aenys and Freys to lead horses from the hall**.
- **Aenys Frey** is named among the leaders of the horses leaving the Great Hall; **he is not narrated leading a completed field attack**.
- **Wyman Manderly** is **bleeding and collapsed, with Maester Medrick attempting to save him**; **four White Harbor knights** have formed a protective ring. **The text supplies no recovery, death certification, ability to ride, or appointment of a named acting commander** after Roose commands him to gather White Harbor men.
- **Roose** speaks as theater commander and orders both groups outward, but his ability to enforce tactical cooperation **once they leave** is untested.
- **Ramsay** threatens renewed intramural violence and kills his own wounded man Luton to end his crying; he is **not shown personally escorting or rallying the White Harbor force**.

**Novel payoff:** Wyman's status is not a mere medical footnote. Any theory requiring **Wyman personally in charge of a coordinated Manderly turncoat operation at the ice lakes** must cross the **post-wound recovery or delegation** gate. A theory requiring **him dead** must also cross a gate. Even if his retainers resent Hosteen, their choices and authority are not identical to their lord's secret agreement with Davos.

**Alternative reading / counterweight:** Wyman may recover quickly, could have established standing lieutenants off-page, and Roose could direct the rank-and-file through functioning subordinate officers. **The books have not excluded those options**. What they exclude is treating them as already *shown*.

**Named-officer and physical-command follow-up (2026-10-16):** [White Harbor officers and the unnamed Winterfell field deputy](white-harbor-officers-and-winterfell-delegation-gates.md) establishes **Ser Marlon Manderly** as **Wyman's cousin and commander of his *White Harbor garrison*** (ADWD 19, Davos), and **Wylis** as the **returned heir at White Harbor** (ADWD 29, Davos). Neither is depicted at the east gate. The four protective White Harbor knights at the wounded Wyman's side include an **unnamed elder** with **three silver mermaids on violet** (ADWD 51, Theon), distinctive from the normal Manderly merman, **without any stated family name or battlefield commission**. **Important correction:** Wyman said **before** his injury that he was **too large to ride** and would attend the wedding by barge/litter with **a hundred knights** (ADWD 29); recovering from the slash alone would not make him a mounted field leader. **Aenys Frey's report of 300 men, 100 knights** (ADWD 46) is a hostile character's headcount, not an independent official register. **Medrick**—Roose's Winterfell maester formerly of Hornwood—treats Wyman; **Theomore** is his mistrusted White Harbor maester. This narrows the missing **delegation, consent and actual field-officer** gates without selecting an answer.

**Longitudinal Roose-precedent audit (2026-10-16):** [Duskendale-to-Winterfell banner management](roose-bolton-rival-banner-management-duskendale-winterfell.md) adds **ACOK 64 (Arya)**, where Roose **personally directs Qyburn to write Helman Tallhart that he should join Robett Glover and strike east toward Duskendale**; **ASOS 35 (Catelyn)** shows Robb shocked that his men went there and lost a third of his foot; **ASOS 37 (Jaime)** records Roose saying he sent **Harrion Karstark with Karhold men**; **ASOS 49 (Catelyn)** shows Roose attributing Duskendale to **Glover's emotional folly**, without revealing the original order. This establishes a previous **order → disaster → selective attribution** chain, making deliberate Winterfell thinning a stronger testable hypothesis. **However, Roose's purpose in the original Duskendale order and the actual result of ADWD 51 Winterfell sorties are not narrated as intentional sacrifices.** ADWD 46's 'every northerner dead is one fewer opponent' explanation is **Theon's private interpretation**, not a Roose admission. The dossier also distinguishes Roose's explicit dependence on **Lady Dustin and her Ryswell relations** from a falsely assumed, homogeneous northern faction.

## 7. What could Theon actually tell Stannis?

**ADWD 51:** Theon **is in the Great Hall** for Roose's exact orders. He can credibly testify later, if willing, that **Hosteen was told to assemble Frey men at the main gates, Wyman was told to assemble the White Harbor men at the east gate**, that Hosteen attacked Wyman, and that Roose claimed Stannis was close and starving. He saw a grave intramural fight, Frey horses turn to leave and an impaired Manderly commander.

**ADWD 62 (Asha):** Theon and Jeyne reach **Asha in Stannis's camp** after Tycho says Mors Umber gave them over. **No direct audience with Stannis or intelligence briefing happens on-page**. Theon does not know that both armies passed gates, their whereabouts, how Stannis positioned himself, whether Manderly soldiers intend betrayal, or what the raven said verbatim.

Potential follow-up must separate:
1. `THEON HEARD DIRECT ORDER`;
2. `THEON ACTUALLY SAW PREPARATION` (Hosteen/Aenys movement, wounded Wyman's guard);
3. `STANNIS RECEIVED THEON'S TESTIMONY` (**NOT SHOWN**);
4. `STANNIS CHECKED CURRENT SCOUT REPORTS` (**NOT SHOWN**);
5. `FREY/MANDERLY ACTION AT LAKES` (**NOT SHOWN**).

**Three actors, three different horizons:** Wyman has a private Rickon loyalty bargain; his **White Harbor captains** may know portions of it or none; **Stannis** has not heard it from Davos nor seen this fight. One cannot give a commander the reader's or another POV's intelligence merely because it would be tactically useful.
