## Executive result: custody changed hands, but Jaime did **not** tell us where the other prisoners went

**AFFC 27 (Jaime)** is the only direct five-book sighting of the specific **post-Trident prisoner cohort**: **Ser Wylis Manderly and several additional, unnamed highborn Northern men**, identified in the text as captured by **Gregor Clegane during fighting at the Trident fords**. Jaime judges them valuable as ransom hostages. They are **alive when Jaime sees them**. Wounds and prior confinement are observed, but *precisely which man saw which decision at the crossing* is not reported.

Jaime promises **Wylis alone** a route from Harrenhal through Maidenpool to a ship for White Harbor, then assigns **Red Ronnet Connington** that particular escort (**AFFC 30**). [ADWD 29 (Davos)](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/030-davos.md) subsequently records Wyman's confirmation Wylis reached home. **No comparable release, ransom, escort, retained-custody or execution instruction is given for the unnamed men in AFFC 27/30.**

At the same visit, Jaime appoints **Ser Bonifer Hasty** castellan **for the Iron Throne**, holding until **Petyr Baelish**, the titled lord, comes to claim the castle. **It is plausible** other unreleased prisoners became Hasty's responsibility because he succeeded the former garrison; **it is not narrated as a named custody transfer, and their physical presence after Jaime's departure is UNKNOWN**. The label “Hasty's prisoners” is therefore an *investigation heading*, not a certified individual roster.

**Potential future witness** does not equal **testimony already obtained**: the named river-ford battle is corroborated, but no captive supplies a recorded statement of Roose's boat allocation, rear-guard instructions, possible alternatives or intent.

### Strictly bounded count

Jaime counts **his accompanying force** at fewer than a thousand before arriving at Harrenhal. He separately thinks Hasty's “Holy Hundred” should be called the **Holy Eighty-Six** after **fourteen** died at Blackwater. These are **different figures**. The **86** are the known disciplined core Hasty plans to use, *not* a certified total number of castle defenders or custodians; the text does not name every former garrison member who might stay, noncombatant staff or recruits.

## 2. What changed in the handover?

### Change A — an unresponsive garrison becomes an identified crown office

**AFFC 27:** Cersei complains that **no raven reply** has come from the men holding Harrenhal. Jaime speculates unflatteringly about Gregor's violent garrison; this is **Jaime's conjecture** about the missing replies, not a recovered intercepted bird.

He replaces practical control with a named castellan and a maester who **will remain with ravens**. The communication problem is therefore **capable of changing**: there is a planned resident officer, a known maester, and a way to send a raven. **No new evidence says it changed successfully**, no prisoner-custody report is read, and there is no verified letter from Hasty after Jaime leaves.

**Communications checkpoint:** to make a Northern hostage report matter elsewhere, a scene must first demonstrate (1) Bonifer or Gulian knows the person's identity/status, (2) a message is composed/sent, (3) its recipient and timing, and (4) a recipient who trusts it or seeks corroboration. A cage of ravens is **infrastructure**, not proof of delivery.

### Change B — Gregor's men leave, but his prisoners are not individually disposed of

Hasty rejects Gregor's followers as irredeemably unfit for his service. Jaime chooses to take them to Riverrun. He has also found **Lady Whent's surviving servant Pia**, whose abuse is evident; he removes **her** from Hasty's household after Hasty calls her morally corrupt. These are **specific named personnel decisions**, showing Jaime can distinguish people and relocate them.

But the reader gets **no equivalent specific disposition** for the other highborn northmen. The passage narrates neither a general amnesty nor continuing incarceration under Hasty. Even after the Mountain's henchmen depart, **the consequences of their earlier violence and possible coerced testimony remain**. Avoid inferring Hasty personally tortured, interrogated, exchanged, freed, fed, or executed any one of these men.

### Change C — authority is split between three levels

- **Legal title:** Baelish was granted Harrenhal and broad Trident overlordship in [ACOK 65 (Sansa)](../../Source/Main-Series/02-A-Clash-of-Kings/text/066-sansa.md). In [AFFC 33 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/034-jaime.md), Jaime reiterates his *Lord Paramount* role when discussing Riverrun.
- **Interim command:** Hasty holds Harrenhal **in the crown's name**, expressly **until Baelish comes to take it** (AFFC 27). He is not himself lord paramount.
- **Army and prison control:** Jaime's physical visit compels Clegane's garrison to recognize Hasty, and the parties make personnel decisions. Whether Hasty actually receives every prisoner and every record is not described. A later claim that “Littlefinger's men held the Trident witnesses” requires a real transfer or an agent acting on Baelish's orders; the nominal title is not enough.

Baelish's continued absence is relevant to any continuation linking Vale intrigue to a specific captive. A *legal claim* to a building must not substitute for demonstrated **access to its dungeon or residents**.

## 3. Hasty's actual moral and operational profile

**AFFC 27, `POV-OBSERVED`:** Hasty is strict, pious, claims willingness to forgive sincere repentance but still uphold punishment for crimes, rejects the Mountain's old soldiery, and describes a program of local security and eventual agricultural restoration with help from neighboring lords.

**Counterevidence to an unqualified “benevolent rescuer” hypothesis:** he refuses to retain **Pia**, a young woman whom Jaime has seen brutalized; Hasty instead condemns her as a source of temptation. His treatment of her reflects harsh sexual moralism inconsistent with an assumption that piety guarantees sympathetic care of vulnerable captives.

**Counterevidence to an unqualified “fanatical torturer” hypothesis:** the text calls his fighting men disciplined; Jaime considers him sober, just, and dutiful; Hasty opposes Clegane's men and insists that crimes require lawful penalties. No scene shows Hasty tormenting the Northern captives or ordering their death. His faith cannot be used as interchangeable evidence of cruelty to highborn hostages.

**Earlier allegiance caution — ACOK 65 (Sansa):** Bonifer is present among the noble *captives* taken after the Blackwater and processed before Joffrey. The same scene distinguishes those who switched sides in battle from those captured after fighting for Stannis; **it does not identify which category Bonifer individually occupied**. His later appointment by Cersei is nonetheless definite. A proposed “he would never change allegiances” backstory conflicts with his post-Blackwater situation; an opportunistic switching motive also remains unproved.

**Operational limitation:** Hasty's well-disciplined core of 86 is small compared with Harrenhal's vast footprint; Jaime questions whether he can hold the site, and advises real armor in addition to faith. An outlaws-versus-royal-castellan conflict is *possible*, not depicted after the appointment. The pious **Lancel** near Darry and powerful **Tarly** at Maidenpool are explicitly named possible allies, with no accepted mutual assistance pact shown beyond Hasty's plans.

## 4. What other highborn northmen might know

The plurality of captives creates **more possible first-person witnesses** than the single named Wylis. If the prisoners were taken by Gregor at the Trident fords, they might report troop composition and battlefield events within their personal sight or hearing. They **would not automatically know**:

1. whether Roose deliberately delayed the crossing (his claimed fault in [ASOS 49 (Catelyn)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/050-catelyn.md));
2. how many boats were available or whether he could requisition more;
3. how Aenys Frey's earlier crossing compared with the final river conditions;
4. why Wylis's White Harbor rear guard or Norrey/Locke/Burley men were exposed; or
5. where Ronnel Stout and Kyle Condon later went.

They may have been officers able to see some of this; **none is named**. Wylis, who reportedly commanded the endangered rear guard, is a *more directly identified potential witness*, but he is deeply traumatized by AFFC 27, and the novels give **no recorded statement from him** about the river. Multiple unknown witnesses are an *opportunity for future inquiry*, not proof of a coordinated accusation of Roose.

Also: Jaime suspects the prisoners were fed something appalling by Gregor's men, based on the Goat's torture and the captives' condition. **The exact food served to each Northern prisoner is not separately corroborated**. Their exhaustion, injury and loss of confidence can affect later consent or reliability, but should not be turned into fabricated confessions or explicit memories.

## 6. Five-book post-departure follow-up: the support network fragments, but Harrenhal's fate is *not* depicted

**Method and limit:** Rechecked the entire owner's bundled EPUB for subsequent narrative occurrences of `Bonifer`, `Hasty` and `Gulian`; checked later mentions of Harrenhal, Darry/Lancel, Merryweather, Baelish and Riverlands overlordship through the **ADWD epilogue**. `Bonifer` and `Maester Gulian` have **no new narrated Harrenhal scene after AFFC 27** (other occurrences are older narrative or appendices; `Gulian` is also used by unrelated names such as Gulian Swann). This verifies a **named-character narrative gap**, not the nonexistence of off-page letters or records.

### A. Lancel at Darry is not the stable alliance Hasty envisioned

1. In [AFFC 27 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/028-jaime.md), `DIRECT SPEECH`, Hasty announces a prospective anti-outlaw combination of **his Harrenhal force**, **Lancel at Darry**, and **Randyll Tarly at Maidenpool**, with **Maester Gulian and ravens** available at Harrenhal. No treaty, combined sortie or actual received warning is narrated.
2. In [AFFC 30 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/031-jaime.md), `POV-OBSERVED / HOUSEHOLD REPORT`, Darry still has newly repaired fortifications, **Maester Ottomore**, a garrison, and officers including **Ser Harwyn Plumm** (reported to be hunting outlaws beyond the Trident). The castle thus does not consist only of the personally devout Lancel. Lancel is spending much of his time praying; his family hoped a **Darry-descended wife** would strengthen a disputed new title.
3. In [AFFC 33 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/034-jaime.md), `DIRECT JAIME REPORT`, Lancel intends to **renounce wife, lands and lordship** and travel to King's Landing to join the High Septon's **Warrior's Sons**. His conversation with Jaime and Jaime's subsequent explanation to his aunt show this is more than gossip, but the exact legal disposition of Darry is not narrated.
4. In [ADWD 65 (Cersei)](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/066-cersei.md), `POV-OBSERVED`, **Lancel is physically present among the Warrior's Sons** escorting Cersei through King's Landing. The original named Darry partner in Hasty's plan has demonstrably departed and attached himself to a **different command structure**. In [ADWD Epilogue (Kevan)](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/073-epilogue.md), Kevan considers ways to redirect his son from the Faith to the Kingsguard; that is an aspiration, **not a completed reassignment**.

**Implication:** The **Hasty–Lancel–Tarly** proposed policing combination is not shown operating as planned through the end of the five books. One of its named lords renounces his seat and joins a force entangled in the crown's own disputes. **Do not infer** that Darry's *remaining garrison* abandoned the site, that Lancel's renunciation immediately cancelled all legal holdings, or that Hasty lost access to Maester Ottomore or every potential Darry messenger.

### B. Tarly is demonstrably at the capital, not continuously at Maidenpool

- In [AFFC 43 (Cersei)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/044-cersei.md), a court report says **Tarly is coming from Maidenpool** as the queen's crisis escalates. `REPORTED MOVEMENT`.
- In [ADWD Epilogue (Kevan)](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/073-epilogue.md), `POV-OBSERVED`, Tarly sits with the royal government and gives a detailed account of **Red Ronnet's former Mountain soldiers passing through Maidenpool**, including misconduct and punishment. This confirms Tarly was there to exercise authority over another *specific* group, **not** that he took control of the unnamed highborn Northern hostages at Harrenhal.
- Tarly's absence from Maidenpool by the epilogue removes the *person* Hasty named as his ready neighbor. There is **no evidence Tarly's whole army departs**, that an alternate Maidenpool governor cannot cooperate, or that Hasty learned about Tarly's movement in time.

### C. The potential Merryweather influence is weakened, but was never proven in the first place

- In [AFFC 27 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/028-jaime.md), Jaime says he **does not know** who convinced Cersei to appoint Hasty. He **suspects Orton Merryweather**, recalling Hasty may have served Merryweather's grandfather. His further assumption that an apparently “good” and outsider knight might appeal to Merryweather is `JAIME'S INFERENCE`, **not an admission or a documented appointment chain**.
- In [AFFC 43 (Cersei)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/044-cersei.md), Cersei learns Merryweather **has resigned from the small council and fled with his wife to Longtable**. That is an independently recorded loss of the *suspected* capital patron's position, not an explicit change in Hasty's royal commission.
- **No demonstrated consequence for Harrenhal:** nothing states Hasty received notice, lost his own appointment, changed policy towards prisoners, or appealed to Orton for reinforcement. If Merryweather was never the person who proposed Hasty, this line has no patronage effect at all.

### D. Baelish's lord paramount authority is still recognized *after* the turnover

- In [ACOK 65 (Sansa)](../../Source/Main-Series/02-A-Clash-of-Kings/text/066-sansa.md), Baelish is formally granted **Harrenhal** and lord paramount authority. In [AFFC 27 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/028-jaime.md), Jaime makes Hasty **crown castellan pending Baelish's arrival**. The two positions are not identical.
- In [AFFC 33 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/034-jaime.md), Jaime makes clear to Riverrun's new lord that Riverrun will owe allegiance to **Harrenhal under Baelish's lord paramount status**.
- **Important later corroboration:** in [ADWD 48 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/049-jaime.md), Jaime **states as terms** that **Lord Tytos Blackwood** must renounce Stark/Tully allegiance and swear loyalty to **Harrenhal and the Iron Throne**. This demonstrates that the crown still **demands recognition of Harrenhal's lord paramount hierarchy** well after Bonifer's appointment. **The exact solemn oath is not independently depicted in the scene**, nor does it show Petyr personally commanded the surrender or obtained access to the Trident captives.
- Jaime also **takes a Blackwood hostage**, ultimately accepting the son **Hoster** rather than the young daughter first suggested by Lord Jonos Bracken. `POV-OBSERVED SEPARATE HOSTAGE TRANSACTION`. **Hoster is not a Northern Trident prisoner or an identified resident of Harrenhal.** A decision in the name of Harrenhal's overlordship does not send this hostage there automatically.

**Institutional finding:** Harrenhal's *de jure* overlordship is invoked in Jaime's demands even while a crown castellan (rather than the titular lord) is its last observed military commander. One must still prove access to **this particular castle, its prisoner lists, and physical captives** before making Baelish a witness, prisoner broker or conspirator. The late Blackwood negotiation supplies *stronger evidence for the crown's enforcement of a legal claim* and **no independent evidence for prisoner administration**.

### E. Named local witnesses of castle operations are distinct from river eyewitnesses

**AFFC 27 (Jaime), `POV-OBSERVED`:** the three surviving people from Lady Whent's old castle household are described as **the cook** who had opened a postern to Gregor, **Ben Blackthumb**, the bent-backed armorer, and **Pia**, the abused servant. Jaime takes Pia with him after Hasty objects to her. The book does not give comparable on-page departures or later interviews for the cook and Ben.

The cook and Ben might provide **continuity of local observation** across changes of occupant, access to **kitchen and household practices**, or knowledge of *which officers were kept at the castle*; **no source says either heard the Trident battle orders**. Pia has firsthand experience of brutality at Harrenhal but is **removed with Jaime** and has **no verified ford command knowledge**. A general claim that “three surviving staff testified” would therefore be invented. The Mountain's former followers, also transferred into Jaime's escort, can speak about their own acts and may be adversarial sources; no one is recorded questioning them on the fords.

### Critical challenge to the “imminent second Harrenhal fall” scenario

The shifting personnel make Hasty's **anticipated support less dependable**, and his 86-rider core is modest for Harrenhal. However, **the five books do not narrate an attack that removes him**. Nor do they prove he receives no replacements, uses none of the castle's remaining servants, fails to exchange prisoners, or never writes a raven. A planned joint action might occur off-page, be modified, or be abandoned. Mark any collapse or rescue `THEORY` until a further source supplies an event.

**Most useful original-continuation branch:** information about northern captives can be **separated into three competing administrative channels**—crown/maester, nominal lord paramount, and local garrisons—which might each receive different news at different times. To put a witness into a later political scene, show **who knows which person was held, who can reach the current keeper, and how the message gets to a Northern recipient**. A map of titles cannot substitute for an itinerary.


## 7. Most consequential outstanding primary-source gap

**What happened to the other several highborn Northern Trident captives after Jaime inspected them?**

The strongest five-book facts stop at their being **alive in AFFC 27**. This research did **not** find a later named identification, prisoner roll, order of release, resettlement or reliable death report; nor a narrated Bonifer–prisoner conversation. These are useful open ends precisely because most rival-banner houses are already politically unstable.

**Next limited inquiry:** map whether **Maester Gulian, Baelish's Riverlands lieutenants, or the outlaws near Harrenhal** are independently mentioned in surviving late-book narrative chapters, and whether any named northern witness can be matched using *distinctive text* rather than shared clan surname. More useful than inventing captives is a **tested communication/contact graph**: alive at `AFFC 27` → custodial handoff `UNKNOWN` → identified speaker `UNKNOWN` → delivered testimony `UNKNOWN` → political effect `NOT CLAIMED`.

**Disposition:** `WYLIS RELEASE ROUTE NAMED / ENDPOINT SUPPORTED`; `SEVERAL OTHERS ALIVE DURING JAIME VISIT`; `HASTY APPOINTED TEMPORARY CROWN CASTELLAN`; `CLEGANE FOLLOWERS TRANSFERRED OUT`; `MAESTER GULIAN RAVEN CAPACITY PLANNED`; `NO CERTIFIED LATER PRISONER CUSTODY, RELEASE, DEATH OR TESTIMONY`. Independent critical challenge still open.
