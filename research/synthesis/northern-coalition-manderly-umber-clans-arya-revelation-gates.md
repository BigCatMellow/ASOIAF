## Executive finding: exposing Jeyne is anti-Bolton evidence, not an automatic pro-Stannis consensus

Three forces are too often conflated as “the North”:

1. **Wyman Manderly/White Harbor** — a **Rickon-first Stark restoration** under a **conditional** willingness to take Stannis as king, covertly hostile to Bolton/Frey rule (**ADWD 29, Davos**).
2. **The mountain-clan and allied northmen already marching with Stannis** — explicit **Ned Stark family loyalty**, hostility to the Boltons, and a willingness to fight despite deaths and impossible winter conditions (**ADWD 42, Asha**). They believe the girl in Bolton custody is Ned's daughter; their reaction if she is *not* is **unknown**.
3. **The two Umber household commands** — **Mors Crowfood** negotiates partial, conditional support to Stannis, while **Hother Whoresbane** is with Bolton; kinship, Greatjon's captivity and the **refusal to fight Umber against Umber** constrain action independently of the girl's identity (**ADWD 17, Jon**). **Mors holds or receives Theon and Jeyne after their jump, according to Tycho** (**ADWD 62, Asha**), but his own explanation and any discovery of the fake bride are not shown.

**Potential convergent interest:** a provable false Arya would damage **Roose's presentation of his son's Stark marriage** (ADWD 37) and may strengthen **Wyman's priority to find a real Stark** (ADWD 29). **Potential divergence:** it removes one publicly stated incentive for the mountain clans to continue a disastrously costly march (ADWD 42) and cannot on its own reconcile the Umber brothers or cause the Freys and Manderlys to switch allegiance. The reader's knowledge is greater than any one northern commander’s on-page knowledge.

## 1. Manderly: Rickon is the proposed price, not an oath already redeemed

**ADWD 29 (Davos) establishes two independent political signals.**

- **Robett Glover** openly tells Davos Stannis is **his king**, not automatically the northern lords' king. The debate is about legitimate overlordship after Robb, not merely tactics against Bolton.
- **Wyman Manderly** says the Boltons and Freys lie about the Red Wedding and Winterfell, and he describes bending the knee to their regime while **Wylis is held hostage**. He covertly saves Davos after orchestrating a public-seeming execution, and **Wylis's actual return** removes a prior immediate constraint.
- Wyman brings in **Wex Pyke**, whose report about the Winterfell escape yields a **possible Rickon/Skagos destination**, together with the presence of Rickon's direwolf as a potential later authentication mark. **A report and projected mission are not proof the child has already been recovered**.
- Wyman's *stated bargain* to Davos is **bring back Ned's son and direwolf, then Wyman will accept Stannis as king**. He does not tell Stannis directly that he already accepts his overlordship, and **Davos has not returned with Rickon by the five-book end**.
- Wyman emphasizes **warships, cavalry, economic strength, local follower lords** and political capacity (ADWD 29). These are **reported resources**, not proof all will immediately join Stannis in the snow.

**Unusual convergence:** Bolton claims possession of **Ned's daughter** while Wyman privately wants **Ned's son** to counter it. A testimony that the daughter is *actually Jeyne* makes the Bolton half of the public symbolic contest less credible and could make the Wyman plan comparatively more important. But **the actual Rickon mission is still unfinished**, and Stannis may have **an incompatible interest in giving Winterfell to Arnolf** (ADWD 17). Manderly's success would not compel him to accept Arnolf's appointment.

**ADWD 37/46/51 (Theon) adds anti-Bolton behavior, not a settled alliance with Stannis:**

- Manderly attends Ramsay's wedding, furnishes food and lavish pies, shares a court with Freys, and **seems publicly cooperative**. His private anti-Frey declarations to Davos make pure collaboration an implausible entire account; nevertheless **court attendance alone does not prove he has already flipped militarily**.
- **Lady Dustin thinks Wyman wants to betray Bolton but is too fearful**, and says Roose distrusts Manderly enough to watch him eat before touching his own meal (**ADWD 37**). These are **Dustin's character assessments**, not an objective finding that either man has committed a particular betrayal.
- **ADWD 46** shows openly hostile Frey–Manderly words: Wyman offers to ride behind Hosteen; Hosteen suspects a lance in his back and challenges him about missing Frey kin. The Freys' disappearance is suspicious but **does not conclusively prove the much-discussed “Frey pies” construction or all details of a murder**.
- **ADWD 51** turns the feud violent after Little Walder's death. **Hosteen Frey strikes Wyman and grievously wounds him**. Roose orders **Hosteen's Freys** and **Wyman's White Harbor contingent** to assemble at **different gates** for the sortie; Theon's hearing certifies the order, **not their future battlefield behavior, Wyman's recovery, or a coordinated defection**.

**Discriminating question:** Is any account reaching **Stannis** that Wyman's return to a Stark-led north is **conditional on Rickon**, not merely a sudden affection for Stannis or hatred of the Freys? **Answer at the five-book endpoint: no certified message.**

## 2. Mountain-clan fighting motive: Ned's girl **and** anti-Bolton war; no uniform answer to Jeyne

**ADWD 42 (Asha)** provides unusually direct **pro-war speech** and its critique:

- During preparations at Deepwood Motte, **Morgan Liddle, Brandon Norrey, Big Bucket Wull, the Flints and Alysane Mormont** insist Bolton cannot hold Winterfell and **Ned Stark's presumed daughter must be rescued**. **Justin Massey and many southron knights object** that Winterfell is too well fortified and the army lacks siege supplies. **Stannis chooses to march** despite this tension.
- Later in the starving march, **Peasebury** treats the human cost as unreasonable for “some girl”; **Morgan Liddle** and **Big Bucket** emphasize **Ned's girl**. Wull then explicitly says he would rather his men **die fighting** than starve abandoned in the snow, and his desired target is **Bolton blood**. His motivation is **at least two-pronged**: loyalty to Ned's household **and** revenge/meaningful resistance to the Bolton regime.
- **Asha is a prisoner and interpreter**. Her thoughts that the march could lead to mass deaths and that northerners would only desert after Bolton suffered a defeat are **her analysis**, not proof of what every clan elder would do.
- **ADWD 62 (Asha)** finds the expedition snowbound; the king's southern men and northern allies differ over another march, remaining at the frozen lakes, or withdrawal. **Arnolf and his Karstarks** publicly praise Ned and advocate immediate movement, garnering northern applause. **But Arnolf is independently linked by Roose's ADWD 32 testimony to a Bolton trap**, so vocally pro-Ned rhetoric **does not authenticate loyalty**.

**The false Arya revelation threatens a *belief* but may not defeat the *cause*.** If Theon and Jeyne explain the substitution, the clans' expectation of rescuing Ned's *daughter* is mistaken. It need not follow that the same men accept a Bolton–Frey Winterfell or abandon winter-season vengeance. On the other hand, the king cannot simply invoke the old rescue mission to make the same sacrifice seem worth it. **The clans must hear and judge the testimony; no narrated scene shows that judgment**.

**Test different clans instead of treating them as a single mechanical vote:**

| Actor/group | What the text actually supplies | Plausible reaction *if identity confirmed* | Serious competing outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Big Bucket Wull** | Speaks for rescuing Ned's girl but **also expressly wants a final fight with the Boltons** rather than freezing to death (ADWD 42). | Continue anti-Bolton fighting because the enemy and sacrifice remain. | Condemn Stannis for allowing a ruinous march under mistaken premises; demand a different next decision. |
| **Morgan Liddle** | Names Ned's girl, supports military action and endorses Wull's war speech (ADWD 42). | Insist the regime's imposture **strengthens** the charge against Bolton. | Resent war costs when the specifically promised Stark rescue no longer exists. |

**Material limit:** The desperate lake fish/food and horse conditions (ADWD 42/62) remain real after genealogical truth changes. `WE KNOW THE BRIDE IS FALSE` **cannot create supplies, siege equipment or intact cavalry**.

## 3. Umber division: the proposed price includes *Mance's skull* and pardon for Whoresbane

**ADWD 17 (Jon)** is more precise than “the Umbers join Stannis”:

- Stannis asks about **Mors “Crowfood” Umber** and Jon suggests making him swear by a heart tree. Stannis says **only half the Umbers** have offered themselves, **conditional** on his granting **Mance Rayder's skull for a drinking cup** and **pardon for Hother Whoresbane**, Mors's brother, who has **joined Bolton**.
- Stannis reports an explicit **Umber will not fight Umber** condition. Jon suggests deployment separated from Hother's banner, making geographic placement rather than instant mass allegiance the tactical solution. **The two halves are not demonstrated to plan to kill one another or to be secretly coordinated double agents.**
- Jon suggests **the Greatjon's captivity in Lannister hands** may compel Hother to serve Bolton. **Ser Godry proposes a self-serving Umber succession plot**, but Jon pushes back that the Greatjon has children who precede the uncles. The hostage leverage is **Jon's interpretation**, and murder for inheritance is **Godry's suspicion**—neither is a verified confession by Hother or Mors.
- **Hother/Whoresbane is present with the Bolton entourage** at Winterfell in Theon's narrative (**ADWD 37 and 46**), supporting the split as an actual political placement, not just Stannis's hearsay.

**Unrecognized collision:** **Mors's own negotiating price explicitly includes the skull of Mance**, yet Melisandre/Jon know the burn victim was not Mance and Mance is used covertly in the supposed Arya rescue (**ADWD 31/35**). That makes the **Umber price and the concealed Wall rescue potentially incompatible**, regardless of Jeyne's birth name. No narrated disclosure to Mors of the glamour or the rescue agent's identity resolves the conflict. This is an independent future hazard in any plan in which a living Mance meets Crowfood.

**Actual contact — ADWD 62 (Asha):** **Tycho** says he found **Mors Umber and green boys outside Winterfell** and **Mors handed over Theon and the girl**. This **supports custody/transfer of two escapees by Mors**, not a direct Mors POV of the jump, interrogation, identity recognition, or a statement from him that the girl is Jeyne. Asha sees the rescued pair afterward. **No evidence** says Mors has passed their true identity to Stannis.

**What a Jeyne disclosure changes:** Mors has **not made Ned's girl the one explicit condition of his oath**. His terms depend instead on Mance's skull, Hother's pardon, family noncombat and his own family losses. If Mors already regards the girl as Arya, proof otherwise might alter local rescue plans, but it does not automatically remove his **no-fratricide** limit, solve Greatjon's captivity or provide Stannis with an army of reunited Umbers.

## 4. Enemy-side alignments: the Bolton garrison is already divided, not safely flipped

**ADWD 37/46/51 (Theon)** establishes tension **inside Winterfell**:

- **Manderly–Frey hostility**: Wyman's Red Wedding grievances, missing Frey envoys, Frey accusations, then a **bloody fight in the Great Hall and Wyman's injury**. That can create a future opening for Stannis; it is **not a depiction of White Harbor troops already pledged to fight for him**.
- **Dustin and Ryswell**: Lady Barbrey Dustin is a **Ryswell by birth** and is presented as politically valuable to Roose in **ADWD 32**; in ADWD 37 she speculates about Bolton ambition, recognizes possible leverage, and comments upon Wyman's animosity. This is **political self-assessment**, not a formal Dustin/Ryswell defection. Her insistence that Theon give away the false Arya ties her own on-page conduct to the wedding's legitimacy theatre; her *awareness of the fraud* is not explicitly confessed.
- **Hother's Umber contingent** occupies the opposite side of Mors's bargain. A change in bride authenticity could change his choices, but no source shows Hother discarding the Greatjon dilemma.
- **Bolton winter logistics**: Roose's severe snow, closed gates and difficulty sending scouts in ADWD 46; ADWD 51 has **an observed wet raven** at Maester Rhodry's arm, *not witnessed landing*, and **an instruction to dispatch Frey/Manderly formations separately**. No actual allegiance reversal has occurred within the published observation.

**Avoid a classic theory trap:** The Freys' disappearance, Wyman's marriage/pie hospitality and his animosity **support serious suspicion** but do **not source-certify** the *contents* of the wedding pies; a “Frey pies” theory needs an evidentiary discussion distinct from an assumption that Wyman's whole force is in a hidden Stannis alliance.

### Two independent character-driven crossroads

**For Stannis:** Is the immediate goal to preserve a starving host, depose the Boltons, rescue the actual girl under threat, obey his own royal supremacy, or offer a Stark-led settlement attractive to northern chiefs? He may pursue several, but **they are not equivalent**; Wyman's condition and Mors's family restrictions constrain the choice. Stannis himself already refused a quick winter halt in ADWD 42 before the army became immobilized.

**For Theon and Jeyne:** A false Arya revelation can shatter Roose's stage-managed marriage, but **puts witnesses in danger**. Theon is the former public authenticator now recanting; Jeyne is physically traumatized and frostbitten. They may rationally want safety before serving a Stark restoration narrative. Treat protection as a genuine political act, not just a plot device.

## 7. Hard counterexamples, falsification needs and status

- `REJECT:` “Northern loyalty is synonymous with loyalty to Stannis.” **Robett Glover explicitly distinguishes the two** (ADWD 29); Wyman offers conditional recognition, not a completed oath to Stannis.
- `REJECT:` “Manderly has already recovered Rickon.” Wex's Skagos indication and Davos's assignment are a **plan** and possible route, not a demonstrated extraction.
- `REJECT:` “Theon can make the clans instantly abandon Stannis.” Identity proof may alter one justification while **anti-Bolton grievance persists**, and the effect is unobserved.
- `REJECT:` “Hother and Mors must fight.” **Stannis reports Mors's Umber-against-Umber refusal** and Jon discusses physical separation.
- `REJECT:` “All northmen in the Stannis camp are loyal.” **Arnolf's pro-Ned speeches** coexist with Roose's evidence of a Bolton relationship.
- `REJECT:` “Mors personally testified that the rescued girl is Jeyne.” Only **Tycho's report of the handoff** and **Asha's view of arrival** exist.
- `REJECT:` “Wyman's gallantry at the wedding proves he supports Bolton, and his animosity proves his troops already defected.” Both endpoints exceed the source.
- `REJECT:` “Wyman's pies are demonstrably made from three Freys.” Text gives clues and suspicions; no direct ingredient identification. Track as an independent theory if pursued.
- `REJECT:` “Dustin has confessed knowing Jeyne was fake.” She instructed Theon to present the bride, but her particular state of knowledge is not confessed in the audited dialogue.
- `REJECT:` “Mors knowing a fake Arya solves Mance's rescue.” The original **Mance skull** condition is separate and potentially opposed to that rescue.

### Unresolved information gates

1. **Which individual hears Theon/Jeyne's true-name testimony first**, and whether the king protects the girl and trusts a man who earlier authenticated a lie.
2. **Which mountain chiefs are present** for any revelation and whether each accepts a change in the war's declared purpose.
3. **Whether Davos finds Rickon at all**, and if so whether Wyman and Stannis consent to compatible northern governance.
4. **Whether Mors has discovered who Abel really was**, and whether his demand for Mance's skull can change as circumstances change.
5. **Whether Hother can leave Bolton without endangering Greatjon**, whose captivity is a reported political constraint.
6. **What Wyman/White Harbor men actually do upon leaving Winterfell**, not merely which gate Roose assigned.
7. **Who can send verified identity, military and hostage news** before false northern claims reach Castle Black.
