## Executive finding: one admission bargain, many leaders — and many captive sons

**VERIFIED:** In **ADWD 58 (Jon)**, the boys surrendered to the Watch include identified sons of **Tormund, Soren Shieldbreaker, Gerrick Kingsblood, Howd Wanderer, Brogg, Devyn Sealskinner, Kyleg of the Wooden Ear, Morna White Mask and the Great Walrus**; sons of other absent/dead captains also appear. Later, **sixty of the hundred** are dispatched toward **Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower**. We are **not told which named boy was sent where**, and should not quietly place every chief's son at Castle Black. It is not just **Tormund's** child whose fate could shape the future coalition.

**VERIFIED:** Tormund is a negotiator and influential captain but **not an uncontested king with a monolithic army**. The people he leads include old families, raiders, women, children, separate chiefs, regional rivals, and the Thenns who keep their own hereditary magnar. Several of those chiefs individually pledge themselves to Jon, and later support Jon's **southward expedition**, a decision distinct from rescuing him if attacked or respecting a new Watch authority.

**INFERRED:** The first rational collective dispute after the stabbing is **how to keep the Watch from harming the children while maintaining food, shelter and oaths**, not a simple binary vote between revenge and surrender. A limited mutual-protection pact, hostile reaction, or splintering into multiple local accommodations are all live. No Free Folk response to Jon's injuries is published.

## 3. Three competing political concepts of “king”

1. **Free Folk leadership as earned allegiance.** **ADWD 69 (Jon)** shows Tormund openly mocking the royal title Selyse gives Gerrick. The Free Folk's recognition of past kings, notably Mance, did not mean automatic hereditary succession. **CHARACTER INTERPRETATION:** Jon sees Gerrick's descent from Raymun Redbeard's **younger brother** as essentially worthless for an inherited throne. A Tormund/Gerrick civil split is **possible**, not observed.
2. **Selyse's dynastic project.** **ADWD 69:** the queen presents Gerrick as rightful wildling king, proposes marrying his three daughters to **Axell Florent, Brus Buckler and Malegorn of Redpool**, and assigns Val to **Ser Patrek** without consulting her. She is building court-managed relationships. **VERIFIED announcement; not witnessed successful royal coronation, daughter assent or completed marriage.** Gerrick agreeing about his daughters does not establish a mandate from all the Free Folk, especially those whose sons share his son's hostage status.
3. **Sigorn's inherited Thenn authority.** **ADWD 35/49:** the magnar rules a relatively hierarchical Free Folk society and vows to Alys; this is neither Tormund's elective prestige nor Selyse's fabricated pan-wildling royalty. **INFERRED:** Sigorn can have an independent, family-centered reason to preserve the marriage and seek northern recognition even if the Castle Black settlement collapses. **UNKNOWN:** whether he hears of the crisis in time.

**Disconfirming evidence for an easy royal coup:** Gerrick's own hostage son, Tormund's active leadership of substantial settlers, named chiefs' oaths to Jon, and Selyse's unverified claim of universal rule all work against the notion that the queen may simply rename Gerrick and command the whole coalition. Her household is militarily real; its accepted Free Folk jurisdiction is not.

## 4. Old grievances within the Free Folk survive admission

- **Personal loss, not caricature:** **ADWD 53** has Tormund recount that Dormund died fighting the Watch/Stannis side and Torwynd died and rose as a wight whom Tormund had to put down himself. His willingness to bargain is a **painful choice against a backdrop of grief**, not a promise he has forgotten the dead.
- **Preexisting intra-clan rivalries:** **ADWD 58** notes the two Harles hate one another despite fathering two cousin hostages; the Frozen Shore antlered and walrus-tusk groups do not like one another. The crossing already requires **Tormund and Toregg to stop disputes** over queue order and a jammed ox cart. This is direct evidence of both **fragility and ability to defuse conflict**.
- **The Weeper remains separate and dreaded:** **ADWD 53** makes the Weeper a **future potential admission** Jon discusses against Marsh, Yarwyck and northern chiefs' objections. He **has not appeared inside Castle Black** with Tormund in ADWD 58/69, nor is his feared force counted as currently under Tormund's command.
- **Armed but dependent:** **ADWD 58** has Jon estimate several hundred, perhaps up to a thousand, armed warriors entering ahead of many women, children, older people and sick. Bowen counts **3,119 total admitted people**. This is **not** 3,119 battle-ready swords; the Shieldhall's five-to-one Free Folk advantage is **only that room's audience**, not a castle-wide census.
- **Refuge and service, not a granted homeland:** Tormund's people are assigned **Oakenshield** and others to Watch castles. The watch maintains claim to its fortifications, royal visitors press contrary terms, and the northern elders object to wildlings reaching their lands. Food, work, wardship and individual political oaths are as relevant as troop numbers.

### A bounded, character-driven first-week test

1. **At Hardin's Tower, immediately:** Jon's condition and Wun Wun's injuries need witnesses; Leathers' attempted mediation may or may not succeed. **Nothing is resolved in print.**
2. **At Shieldhall and the Oakenshield connection:** Tormund and the identifiable chiefs need an actual report. **The moment they receive it is a missing event.**
3. **Before coercion:** The safest path for a Free Folk leader with a captive son is to seek a credible guarantee for all dispersed children. This is **INFERRED motivation**, not an already stated demand.
4. **If talks fail:** Some captains may demand action, others may avoid jeopardizing sons held elsewhere. The story's useful contradiction is *not* “Free Folk suddenly unite”; it is “the alliance that protected everyone now exposes each family to different risks.”
5. **Outside the Wall crisis:** Karhold, Stannis, the Weeper and Hardhome must retain their own information clocks. No simultaneous mobilization or instant royal authorization can be assumed.

**Analytical distinction:** An *active political hostage* deters a family whose decision-maker is alive and believes the captor can hurt the child. A *ward or dependent* may strengthen peace through care, education and mutual regard, **whether or not the father can be coerced**. A misplaced or misidentified hostage may instead spread rumor, lead to the wrong family being threatened, or be harmed without achieving any intended political outcome. None of this establishes a legal transformation of the Watch's hostage power into a custody agreement accepted by every parent.

### B. What the Watch can credibly account for — and what it cannot yet prove

- **ADWD 58 (Jon):** two stewards **write the children's names on long sheepskin records** at the entry gate. A third writes down valuables given as payment. **Record creation is VERIFIED**; *where the documents are held later, whether they list father's identity and destination, and who presently has access* are all **UNKNOWN**.
- **ADWD 53 (Jon):** Jon expressly orders **Bowen Marsh** to collect, count and send valuables toward Eastwatch; **Yarwyck** to prepare carts and wagons for distributing the newcomers; **Clydas** to supervise nursing of the sick. **ADWD 58:** Marsh is personally present with a tablet, has supervised the admission through his stewards and **reports sixty boys sent to Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower after food**. The precise split and name-to-fort allocation **are not revealed**.
- **Independent continuation opportunity — THEORETICAL:** Marsh, as administrator and a stabbing participant, may be the **most accessible person capable of helping reconstruct the records** of the very children he helped process. It would be significant if he used that knowledge to honor their safety, gain leverage, conceal a transfer, or refused to answer a request. **None of those choices is published.** The fact that he opposed *training* these hostages to fight (ADWD 53) does **not** prove he wanted children harmed; it also cannot prove his kindness.
- The Watch already has **separate local officers** at Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower, and Sixty boys are **reported sent toward** those garrisons. A Castle Black declaration is not an independent safety inspection in either remote castle. **Contact and affirmative reply from each actual keeper** are necessary for a credible dispersed guarantee. Refer to [first-night command ledger](castle-black-command-custody-first-night.md) for the actual command structure and [information handoffs](information-handoffs.md) for the unsent message gate.
- **Old Flint and the Norrey** in ADWD 53 recall the Kings of Winter's practice of calling some boys “wards” and holding them as hostages. The Norrey's example of harsh punishment for rebellious sires is **a historical threat argument made by a character**, **not** a Watch rule, Jon's declared execution policy, nor evidence that Marsh would copy it. The Free Folk and Watch might plausibly negotiate a **non-punishment guarantee**, but no text reports one.

### C. The smallest reversible safeguards a continuation could test

These are **proposed actions for a prospective opening scene, not events asserted about ADWD**. They avoid requiring all chiefs, their boys or distant officers to move instantly.

1. **Local ceasefire over children:** one Watch officer who actually controls the relevant guards tells all factions there will be **no harm to the children and no retaliatory seizure**, while a named Free Folk representative agrees not to assault barracks or prisoners. **Required evidence:** speaker, guard compliance, leader assent; a reassuring proclamation alone is insufficient.
2. **Produce the entry roster, not a fictitious complete custody list:** compare names with current physically checked Castle Black wards and record *where the remaining children were last sent*. Avoid announcing which named son is at Eastwatch or Shadow Tower unless the roster itself actually establishes that later.
3. **Separate custody from guilt:** boys chosen by lot, sons of deceased leaders, the two disguised girls and Dryn need guardianship regardless of fathers' decisions. A politically useful hostages rule is not a justification for neglect, abuse or assuming they all had an individual living commander to influence.
4. **Notify two distant commanders independently:** send a specific request for **boy-by-boy counts, immediate safety and named keeper** to Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower. **Required evidence:** outbound raven, exact letter substance, receipt and direct response; in ADWD 69 *none of these messages has been sent*.
5. **Keep food and winter shelter moving:** the admissions were obtained partly by taking valuable treasures and pledging shelter/provisioning. Interrupting supplies would endanger families and could make any child-security accord meaningless. **Required evidence:** actual stores, available labor, location of valuables and authority to release them; do not assert an Iron Bank or Eastwatch delivery has arrived.
6. **Delay punitive or permanent adjudication:** a provisional child-safety arrangement can function while the Watch works toward a lawful election. Marsh's office and administrative knowledge are potentially useful **without electing him Lord Commander**, and Tormund can act for followers without anointed kingship over all Free Folk.

### D. Three falsifiable interpretations of the same immediate crisis

- **H1: Truce through wards — plausible, UNSELECTED.** Tormund, a keeper and other chiefs agree that children are safer under existing arrangements with witnessed non-retaliation; future disagreement about food, marriages and oathbreaking continues. **Disconfirmation:** threats against named children, refusal to account for them, or an armed group beginning reprisals.
- **H2: A child-location failure causes a wider crisis — plausible, UNSELECTED.** A missing registry or delayed message makes a parent believe an outlying keeper has taken a child; inconsistent reports prompt independent chief reactions. **Disconfirmation:** complete shared rosters and timely, trusted confirmation from all actual garrisons.
- **H3: Coalition splinters over rival protections — plausible, UNSELECTED.** Gerrick accepts Selyse's royal guarantee, Tormund seeks Watch custody assurances, and another captain prioritizes local shelter. **Disconfirmation:** concrete agreement by the named chiefs *and* demonstrated custody safety. The fact that Gerrick has a hostage son makes both royal and Watch dependency viable, not proof he defects.

**Moral-political constraint from the novels:** Jon treated the children as both bargaining instruments and potential future Watch recruits, and understood the abuse danger of placing young girls with all-male brothers. That humane insight sits uneasily with the deliberately coercive hostage mechanism and the Kings of Winter's punitive precedent. A strong continuation should preserve this contradiction rather than credit Jon with a modern, non-coercive adoption scheme he did not propose.
