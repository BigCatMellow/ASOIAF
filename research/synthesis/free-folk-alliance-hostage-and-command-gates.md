## Executive finding: one admission bargain, many leaders — and many captive sons

**VERIFIED:** In **ADWD 58 (Jon)**, the boys surrendered to the Watch include identified sons of **Tormund, Soren Shieldbreaker, Gerrick Kingsblood, Howd Wanderer, Brogg, Devyn Sealskinner, Kyleg of the Wooden Ear, Morna White Mask and the Great Walrus**; sons of other absent/dead captains also appear. Later, **sixty of the hundred** are dispatched toward **Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower**. We are **not told which named boy was sent where**, and should not quietly place every chief's son at Castle Black. It is not just **Tormund's** child whose fate could shape the future coalition.

**VERIFIED:** Tormund is a negotiator and influential captain but **not an uncontested king with a monolithic army**. The people he leads include old families, raiders, women, children, separate chiefs, regional rivals, and the Thenns who keep their own hereditary magnar. Several of those chiefs individually pledge themselves to Jon, and later support Jon's **southward expedition**, a decision distinct from rescuing him if attacked or respecting a new Watch authority.

**INFERRED:** The first rational collective dispute after the stabbing is **how to keep the Watch from harming the children while maintaining food, shelter and oaths**, not a simple binary vote between revenge and surrender. A limited mutual-protection pact, hostile reaction, or splintering into multiple local accommodations are all live. No Free Folk response to Jon's injuries is published.

## 1. The actual bargain is not unconditional fealty to a new lord

| Bargain element | Narrowly verified fact | Where legitimacy or cohesion can fail |

His faction is distinct from the Weeper's followers and those trapped at Hardhome. | This is a conditional survival and service arrangement with **Jon as principal guarantor**. **Counterweight:** Tormund expressly says some of his own people will hate the terms (ADWD 53), yet **the admission happens** with many chiefs swearing and the armed groups actually crossing. They are neither guaranteed rebels nor docile dependents.

## 3. Three competing political concepts of “king”

1. **Free Folk leadership as earned allegiance.** **ADWD 69 (Jon)** shows Tormund openly mocking the royal title Selyse gives Gerrick. The Free Folk's recognition of past kings, notably Mance, did not mean automatic hereditary succession. **CHARACTER INTERPRETATION:** Jon sees Gerrick's descent from Raymun Redbeard's **younger brother** as essentially worthless for an inherited throne. A Tormund/Gerrick civil split is **possible**, not observed.
2. **Selyse's dynastic project.** **ADWD 69:** the queen presents Gerrick as rightful wildling king, proposes marrying his three daughters to **Axell Florent, Brus Buckler and Malegorn of Redpool**, and assigns Val to **Ser Patrek** without consulting her. She is building court-managed relationships. **VERIFIED announcement; not witnessed successful royal coronation, daughter assent or completed marriage.** Gerrick agreeing about his daughters does not establish a mandate from all the Free Folk, especially those whose sons share his son's hostage status.
3. **Sigorn's inherited Thenn authority.** **ADWD 35/49:** the magnar rules a relatively hierarchical Free Folk society and vows to Alys; this is neither Tormund's elective prestige nor Selyse's fabricated pan-wildling royalty. **INFERRED:** Sigorn can have an independent, family-centered reason to preserve the marriage and seek northern recognition even if the Castle Black settlement collapses. **UNKNOWN:** whether he hears of the crisis in time.

**Disconfirming evidence for an easy royal coup:** Gerrick's own hostage son, Tormund's active leadership of substantial settlers, named chiefs' oaths to Jon, and Selyse's unverified claim of universal rule all work against the notion that the queen may simply rename Gerrick and command the whole coalition. Her household is militarily real; its accepted Free Folk jurisdiction is not.

## 4. Old grievances within the Free Folk survive admission

- **Personal loss, not caricature:** **ADWD 53** has Tormund recount that Dormund died fighting the Watch/Stannis side and Torwynd died and rose as a wight whom Tormund had to put down himself. His willingness to bargain is a **painful choice against a backdrop of grief**, not a promise he has forgotten the dead.
- **Preexisting intra-clan rivalries:** **ADWD 58** notes the two Harles hate one another despite fathering two cousin hostages; the Frozen Shore antlered and walrus-tusk groups do not like one another. The crossing already requires **Tormund and Toregg to stop disputes** over queue order and a jammed ox cart. This is direct evidence of both **fragility and ability to defuse conflict**.
- **The Weeper remains separate and dreaded:** **ADWD 53** makes the Weeper a **future potential admission** Jon discusses against Marsh, Yarwyck and northern chiefs' objections. He **has not appeared inside Castle Black** with Tormund in ADWD 58/69, nor is his feared force counted as currently under Tormund's command.
- **Armed but dependent:** **ADWD 58** has Jon estimate several hundred, perhaps up to a thousand, armed warriors entering ahead of many women, children, older people and sick. Bowen counts **3,119 total admitted people**. This is **not** 3,119 battle-ready swords; the Shieldhall's five-to-one Free Folk advantage is **only that room's audience**, not a castle-wide census.
- **Refuge and service, not a granted homeland:** Tormund's people are assigned **Oakenshield** and others to Watch castles. The watch maintains claim to its fortifications, royal visitors press contrary terms, and the northern elders object to wildlings reaching their lands. Food, work, wardship and individual political oaths are as relevant as troop numbers.

**Important limitation:** Even chiefs visibly acclaimed Jon's planned march **south against Ramsay** (**ADWD 69**) only as an **invitation to volunteer**, not a binding commitment to fight the Night's Watch after Jon is stabbed. Supporting a living man’s campaign, honoring a dead man's promises and fighting his murderers are **three separate propositions**.

### A bounded, character-driven first-week test

1. **At Hardin's Tower, immediately:** Jon's condition and Wun Wun's injuries need witnesses; Leathers' attempted mediation may or may not succeed. **Nothing is resolved in print.**
2. **At Shieldhall and the Oakenshield connection:** Tormund and the identifiable chiefs need an actual report. **The moment they receive it is a missing event.**
3. **Before coercion:** The safest path for a Free Folk leader with a captive son is to seek a credible guarantee for all dispersed children. This is **INFERRED motivation**, not an already stated demand.
4. **If talks fail:** Some captains may demand action, others may avoid jeopardizing sons held elsewhere. The story's useful contradiction is *not* “Free Folk suddenly unite”; it is “the alliance that protected everyone now exposes each family to different risks.”
5. **Outside the Wall crisis:** Karhold, Stannis, the Weeper and Hardhome must retain their own information clocks. No simultaneous mobilization or instant royal authorization can be assumed.
