## 1. Who ruled Maidenpool in practice?

### A. Strategic occupation, not a prisoner manifest

- **ASOS 60 (Tyrion)** [source](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/061-tyrion.md): a Lannister-side operational summary describes **Randyll Tarly holding Maidenpool, Duskendale, and the kingsroad**. This supports control of the *area* after his Duskendale victory, not a list of his highborn prisoners.
- **AFFC 14 (Brienne)** [source](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/015-brienne.md): Brienne directly sees **Tarly judging cases** before a public gathering, while **Lord Mooton sits beside him**. The judge imposes physical punishments, resolves a dispute involving a man of Mooton's garrison, and orders more accused prisoners **returned to the dungeon** until the next day. Tarly's soldiers staff the town gates. `POV-OBSERVED PRACTICAL JURISDICTION`.
- At that same sitting, an archer wearing **Mooton's red salmon** faces a complaint; the accused archer looks toward **Mooton**, but **Tarly** issues the sentence. The local lord is present and has men, yet the military occupying lord is exercising authority. Do not describe Mooton as absent, dead, entirely dispossessed, or a proven co-author of Tarly's prisoner rules.
- **AFFC 25 (Brienne)** [source](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/026-brienne.md): Tarly receives Brienne in **Mooton's castle** and speaks as if it were his to use. Ser Hyle Hunt objects that the castle still belongs to Mooton; Tarly dismisses Mooton's courage. `DIRECT SPEECH` supports a public division between **nominal ownership** and **control on the ground**.
- **AFFC 14:** Brienne expects Mooton to be restored after the war; the farmer she meets mocks Mooton's passivity. The expectation and farmer's assessment are **not** a legal transfer deed or reliable forecast.

### B. The political marriage does not identify a prison warden

**AFFC 14 (Brienne)**: local talk identifies **Lord Mooton's daughter** as expected to marry **Dickon Tarly**, Randyll's young son; Brienne recalls Dickon from her days in Renly's camp. The [ADWD appendix](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/999-back-matter.md) lists **Eleanor Mooton**, daughter/heir of William Mooton, as married to Dickon. Mark the first as **REPORTED WEDDING PLAN** and the second as **PARATEXT STATUS**; there is no observed on-page ceremony in this audit.

`INFERRED`: the marriage offers a plausible way to sustain Tarly family leverage in a riverlands lordship when his field army departs. `UNKNOWN`: whether Dickon, Eleanor, William Mooton or a subordinate inherits *any* responsibility for Harrion, an established dungeon registry, or communications with Karhold. A dynastic link does not itself turn a supposed prisoner into a known witness.

## 2. The Lannister command pipeline: Jaime → Red Ronnet → Maidenpool → King's Landing

**AFFC 27 (Jaime), `POV-OBSERVED / DIRECT ORDER`:** At Harrenhal Jaime sees Wylis physically held with **unnamed highborn Northern men** captured by the Mountain at the Trident. These men are alive but ill-treated. Jaime tells Wylis of a coming transfer through **Maidenpool** to a **ship for White Harbor**. Jaime also assigns **Ser Bonifer Hasty** to hold Harrenhal for the crown and takes Gregor's surviving rough men away because Bonifer refuses to employ them. This establishes an **administrative turnover at Harrenhal**, not a confirmed fresh custody decision for every Northern prisoner.

**AFFC 30 (Jaime), `DIRECT APPOINTMENT / DISPATCHED`:** Jaime expressly assigns **Red Ronnet Connington** the duty to deliver **Wylis** to Maidenpool, allowing Jaime not to see Ronnet again. No scene depicts Ronnet handing over a prisoner to Tarly or naming a ship.

**ADWD Epilogue (Kevan), `DIRECT TARLY SPEECH`:** Tarly reports that **Ronnet's party, including former followers of Gregor Clegane, passed through Maidenpool; Tarly separately counts **twenty men accompanying Ronnet onward to the city****, where he personally dealt with at least two violent misconduct cases among the escort, hanging one man and mutilating another. Ronnet is now in King's Landing, being questioned about the rival **Jon Connington** and Aegon claim. This is **independent downstream evidence that Ronnet's escort company really passed through Maidenpool**. It is **not** a witnessed Wylis handover or an account of Harrion.

**ADWD 29 (Davos), `SEPARATE ENDPOINT`:** Wyman says Wylis has returned home, so this specific release ultimately led to the homecoming. The precise transfer sequence `Ronnet → named Maidenpool recipient → named ship captain → White Harbor` is **still partly unshown**. The new Kevan evidence bridges **the escort's presence** at Maidenpool, not every custody document.

**Valuable caution:** the same Tarly who controls Maidenpool considers Ronnet's men brutal and unfit. A commander's ability to impose punitive justice demonstrates jurisdiction; it **does not certify that every escort performed its custody obligations as instructed**. For Wylis, an independently known arrival reduces uncertainty. For Grell and Ryger, no such end state is observed in these passages.

## 3. Jaime → Rafford: a second, morally risky route to Maidenpool

**AFFC 44 (Jaime):** two Riverrun officers, **Ser Desmond Grell** (old master-at-arms) and **Ser Robin Ryger** (captain of guards), refuse ordinary discharge and instead request entry to the **Night's Watch**. Jaime allows them their arms and appoints **Rafford (“Raff the Sweetling”)**, with a dozen Gregor soldiers, to bring them to Maidenpool. He threatens severe punishment if the prisoners suffer harm.

**[ACOK 26 (Arya)](../../Source/Main-Series/02-A-Clash-of-Kings/text/027-arya.md)** explicitly recalls Raff killing the wounded **Lommy Greenhands** and portrays his participation in Gregor's abuse of captives. `POV-OBSERVED/ARYA TESTIMONY` gives the escort's leader a troubling history, even if it does not prove he disobeyed this particular Jaime order. The explicit `DELIVER UNHARMED` command reflects Jaime's recognition of the danger and illustrates the gap `ORDER GIVEN ≠ DESTINATION REACHED`.

**Do not conflate with the post-ADWD “Mercy” sample or TV**: no five-book narrated scene follows Grell/Ryger from Maidenpool to the Wall. Later published sample material is outside the project's present evidence boundary.

A second apparently routine prison transfer to the same port therefore provides a **built-in control case**: instructions may be real, escorts named, destination specified, yet later custody and witness availability are still unknown.

## 4. The custody gap *after* Tarly departs

- **AFFC 43 (Cersei)** [source](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/044-cersei.md): the court hears Tarly is marching down from Maidenpool as the capital's political crisis develops. `REPORTED MOVEMENT`, not yet an observed transfer of every guard or prisoner.
- **ADWD Epilogue (Kevan)** [source](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/073-epilogue.md): Tarly is personally at King's Landing, reports Ronnet's men passing through Maidenpool, and participates in the response to the Aegon/Connington threat. `POV-OBSERVED ARRIVAL OF OFFICER` with direct talk of the escort.
- **Who remains in charge? `UNKNOWN.`** Lord Mooton exists and has a garrison; the Mooton–Tarly marriage is reported and later listed; however, neither chapter states a *specific* post-departure master of the Maidenpool dungeon, its records, or any surviving Harrion. Tarly's physical move does not prove all his retainers left, Mooton took unrestricted control, or prisoners were automatically sent to King's Landing.
- **Tarly is a possible source, not an interviewed Harrion witness.** At the capital he can *potentially* be asked about battle captives, while Ronnet can be asked about the **Wylis escort**. Neither is shown giving a relevant Harrion answer. Their subsequent institutional/political positions may make questioning difficult: Kevan regards Ronnet as politically suspect due to his family tie to Jon Connington (ADWD Epilogue). This is not proof either man participated in an Arnolf scheme.

## 5. Countercheck of Alys's account: where would an execution order have to originate?

`Alys's CHARACTER CLAIM` in **ADWD 44 (Jon)**: Arnolf's pro-Stannis declaration was meant to provoke the Lannisters into executing Harrion; she alleges Arnolf/Bolton had bargained for his death. Compare the necessary **separate gates**:

| Gate | What the five novels actually give | Absent evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. **Arnolf's intent and supposed Bolton promise** | Alys's specific allegation (ADWD 44); Roose's private discussion of Arnolf's betrayal of Stannis (ADWD 32). | Bolton-side admission agreeing to Harrion's death, exact pact, exchange or date. |
| 2. **A publicly actionable declaration for Stannis** | Arnolf leads Karstark soldiers on Stannis's winter campaign; many treat him as loyal. | A dated authenticated Lannister-side receipt of his new allegiance, explaining why his **great-nephew** should pay. |
| 3. **A jailer still holding Harrion** | Alys's report places him at Maidenpool **almost a year earlier**; Tarly is later a documented authority there. | Current custody roll, living eyewitness, transfer from Duskendale, identity of a personally responsible gaoler. |
| 4. **A death decision and means** | No death direction in the Jaime/Cersei/Kevan/Tarly conversations audited. | Sender, warrant, recipient, prisoner match, execution report/body. |
| 5. **News from the South to the North** | Cregan says Harrion is dead (ADWD 49); Alys remains uncertain (ADWD 44). | Cregan's actual source, dated message, receipt at Karhold/Wall, independent confirmation. |

This chain **does not disprove** an off-page killing. It shows that **a coherent motive narrative is not yet an evidentiary chain**. Also, a living highborn hostage might be more useful to Lannister/Tarly rulers as leverage than a dead one; killing Harrion could strengthen Alys's rival claim instead of installing Arnolf directly. These are **countervailing incentives**, not evidence of mercy.

## 6. The larger cross-book significance

1. **Individual custody determines who can speak.** Wylis's actual homecoming gives White Harbor a possible Trident witness, but the other highborn men Jaime saw at Harrenhal do **not** receive equivalent narrated release orders. **Ser Bonifer Hasty** is an additional possible institutional custodian after Jaime, not proof he kept particular captives.
2. **Harrion's political importance outstrips documented custody.** Alys, Cregan and Arnolf dispute Karhold while no POV at Maidenpool gives a contemporaneous sighting. The gap can preserve uncertainty without requiring a secret master assassination plot.
3. **A port is not a message pipe by itself.** The possibility of sea passage to White Harbor (Wylis), a road to the Wall (Grell/Ryger), or a military road to King's Landing (Tarly/Ronnet) only matters if a *named messenger or captive* carries accurate information to a *named recipient*.
4. **Tarly's new alliance via Mooton may preserve local authority**, but the same scene shows Mooton's archer subordinated to Tarly's judgment. Any future Harrion rescue, disappearance, or confirmation must account for political and practical custody, not assume a single monolithic “Lannister prison.”
5. **Ronnet ties the Riverlands to a second theater.** He goes from a Wylis escort to being distrusted in the capital during the Aegon/Connington crisis. A possible testimony route could close through him, but he is **not** a confirmed Duskendale eyewitness and nothing says Kevan asked about Harrion.

### Hypotheses under challenge

- **H1: Tarly ordered Harrion killed for Arnolf.** Plausible *access* if Harrion remained in Maidenpool; **no witness, warrant or motive admissions**. `THEORY / VERY LOW EVIDENCE`.
- **H2: Harrion is still alive in southern custody.** Earlier prisoner report and no witnessed death permit it, but do not establish a current jail. `POSSIBLE / NOT VERIFIED`.
- **H3: Tarly took Harrion with him to the capital.** Tarly physically gets there, but no prisoner transport is mentioned; `SPECULATIVE TRANSFER`.
- **H4: Local Mooton–Tarly family interests determine his fate.** The marriage link is supported; connection to this *specific* prisoner is entirely absent. `POLITICAL POSSIBILITY / NO PRISONER LINK`.
- **C: Maidenpool was a functioning mixed military/legal administrative center where some prisoners changed hands while others' records are unshown.** Tarly's judgments, Mooton's household, two Jaime escort instructions and Tarly's Ronnet report all support this narrower explanation. `SOURCE-GROUNDED`.
