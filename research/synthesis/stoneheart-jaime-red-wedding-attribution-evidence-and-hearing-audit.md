## Executive result: her accusation has a real eyewitness basis, but not the meaning of the words

**[ASOS 51 (Catelyn)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/052-catelyn.md):** Catelyn sees an armored man wearing a blood-splattered **pale pink cloak** come up to Robb; she **hears him invoke Jaime Lannister's regards** immediately before he thrusts his sword through Robb's heart. Catelyn's witnessing of **the words and murder** is certain. That **Jaime sent a murder instruction** is *not* independently established by the declaration.

**[ASOS 44 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/045-jaime.md)** provides the cross-POV source of the phrase: Bolton asks Jaime to give warm regards to **Tywin** on returning south. Jaime responds by asking Bolton to convey *his regards to Robb*. The greeting is said in an ordinary escort farewell **before** the Red Wedding is narrated; **its later murderous use is the killer's decision, not a shown request from Jaime to murder Robb**. Catelyn was **not present** for the exchange, and Brienne remained behind at Harrenhal during the initial departure. Whether Bolton already planned to use the line at the moment he heard it is `UNKNOWN`.

**[ASOS 62 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/063-jaime.md)** supplies a separate and important constraint: while travelling with Brienne and their escort, Jaime **learns of the Red Wedding at Brindlewood from Ser Bertram Beesbury**. Brienne is shocked; Jaime questions Beesbury to get the story. `JAIME POV-OBSERVED LEARNING PUBLIC NEWS`. Jaime is **not at the Twins**, and **his narrative supplies no direct involvement in plotting the massacre**. This is **not mathematical proof he could not secretly have known something beforehand**—a conspirator can appear to learn publicly known news—but it is a strong contrary source to the literal interpretation `JAIME SENT HIS REGARDS AS A PREARRANGED ORDER TO KILL`.

**[ASOS Epilogue (Merrett)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/082-epilogue.md):** Merrett's **private thoughts** assign the overall murder decision to **Lord Walder** and detailed planning to **Lame Lothar with Roose**. When threatened by the Brotherhood in Stoneheart's physical presence, he **audibly names Walder, Ryman, Roose, Lothar, and Bastard Walder** in an attempt to minimize his own role. He **does not accuse Jaime of issuing the murder order** in this on-page exchange. This is **positive testimony pointing to other actors**, but **silence about Jaime is not a sworn exoneration**; nor can readers assume Stoneheart overheard Merrett's earlier *private thoughts*.

**[AFFC 42 (Brienne)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/043-brienne.md):** Brienne **expressly tells the woman she recognizes as Catelyn** that Jaime saved her from assault, returned for her at the bear pit, sent her after Sansa, and **could not have been party to the Red Wedding**. This exculpatory account has genuinely **already been offered inside Stoneheart's hearing**. Stoneheart nevertheless demands Brienne **kill Jaime with Oathkeeper or die as a betrayer**. Thus the challenge is **not simply to find someone willing to say Jaime is innocent**: Stoneheart and the men applying her will have to weigh evidence rather than simply enforce a predetermined punishment.

**Verdict within five novels:** `CATELYN REALLY HEARS JAIME NAMED AT HER SON'S MURDER`; `JAIME'S PERSONALLY UTTERED GREETING TO ROOSE IS A SEPARATE, INNOCUOUS SCENE`; `JAIME LEARNS OF MASSACRE AFTERWARD WHILE EN ROUTE SOUTH`; `MERRETT'S AUDIBLE TESTIMONY NAMES OTHER OPERATORS`; `BRIENNE'S DIRECT DEFENSE FAILS TO CHANGE STONEHEART'S DEMAND`. **Neither an earlier Jaime pact nor a future Stoneheart acquittal is demonstrated**.

**No single character possesses all these viewpoints:** Catelyn personally witnessed the fatal words, Jaime and Roose personally heard the original greeting, Jaime/Brienne encountered Beesbury later, Merrett knew Frey internal assignments, and Brienne confronted resurrected Catelyn. ### Does Merrett give Stoneheart more evidence than she already knows?

At [ASOS Epilogue](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/082-epilogue.md), the Brotherhood's **first direct interrogation topic** is **Sandor Clegane and a roughly ten-year-old child** who might have accompanied him to the Twins. They say **Thoros had information** that Sandor was approaching the crossing, and that ferrymen and a robbed traveler supplied intermediate sightings. Merrett **denies personal knowledge** of the pair. This is actual **investigative effort toward a Stark-child lead**, not simply a Frey hanging without any questions.

As Merrett begs for his life, he names **Lord Walder, Ryman, Bolton, Lothar and Bastard Walder** while asserting that he merely drank. Earlier he privately remembers Lothar **assigned him to intoxicate the Greatjon**, which readers know from his thoughts but which he **does not volunteer in the hearing**. The outlaws say **Lady Stoneheart is a witness**, reveal her identity and solicit her response regarding **Merrett**; she nods. **Her nod is not a finding about Jaime, nor a certificate of every detail Merrett named.**

**Critical anti-mind-reading rule:** Merrett's *internal* understanding that Lothar and Roose personally devised the entire operational plan **does not magically become Catelyn's knowledge** unless he speaks or another source tells her. What he **does** say within hearing is narrower: those leaders are responsible for the massacre, with Lothar and Bastard Walder as specific operational perpetrators. He does not say `Jaime was innocent` or `Jaime directed the operation`.

## 3. Chronological exculpation is stronger than a simple plea, but not an absolute alibi to every charge

**The strongest bounded defense** is not `Jaime is a good man now`—he has threatened innocents and used hostages in later chapters—but that **he was released by Roose, spoke a conventional farewell, traveled south, learned the massacre news at Brindlewood, and was not shown arranging the murders**. The *particular deadly phrase* has an independent innocent origin.

- **[ASOS 44 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/045-jaime.md)** confirms the original words occurred in Bolton's hearing, without an explicit murder commission.
- **[ASOS 62 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/063-jaime.md)** allows **Brienne** to describe their shared road journey after Jaime returned for her, and **Beesbury** could in principle corroborate that Jaime drew the first report from him at Brindlewood. **Brienne's AFFC 42 spoken defense does not actually recite this exact Beesbury conversation**. Beesbury's own present location, access, safety and willingness to testify after the encounter are unshown.
- **Other members of Jaime's party** (Walton/escorts and Qyburn) could potentially corroborate travel legs. A **Bolton loyalist** or politically compromised courtier is no neutral guarantee, and **Brienne was not present for Jaime's farewell to Roose**. Do not fake a complete witness affidavit.
- **[ASOS 53 (Tyrion)](../../Source/Main-Series/03-A-Storm-of-Swords/text/054-tyrion.md)** reveals Tywin's negotiations with Frey and Bolton for rewards; this cannot be imported as what Stoneheart knows. **Tywin's planning does not prove Jaime was briefed**. Nor does Jaime being surprised at public news **disprove all theoretical advance secret knowledge**; it supports a more limited and compelling contrary account.
- **[AFFC 44 (Jaime)](../../Source/Main-Series/04-A-Feast-for-Crows/text/045-jaime.md)** depicts Jaime making grave threats against Edmure's family to force Riverrun's surrender, while later at [ADWD 48](../../Source/Main-Series/05-A-Dance-with-Dragons/text/049-jaime.md) he threatens Blackwood with hostage Hoster's death. `JAIME NOT SHOWN IN RED WEDDING PLOT` **must not be conflated with** `JAIME NOW REFUSES ALL CRUELTY`. This is a real ethical difficulty that might prevent simple vindication even in a fairer hearing.

**Most important obstacle:** The reader knows the source of the fatal phrase. **Stoneheart may know the words only in their weaponized form**; the intermediary who could explain their origin—Roose—is also Robb's killer. There is no reason to assume she would trust him. Brienne's honest account is dismissed in the one hearing actually observed.

## 5. Three separate possible claims in a future confrontation (all unresolved)

1. **`JAIME ORDERED ROBB'S DEATH`:** Catelyn's strongest apparent evidence is the assassin's words. Rebuttal requires revealing the original Jaime–Roose farewell, distinguishing **Roose's purposeful use of words** from **Jaime's purpose**, and corroborating timing where possible. **No source shows a formal trial, a signed Jaime order or a final guilty/innocent ruling**.
2. **`JAIME ENABLED LANNISTER POWER WHICH REWARDED THE BETRAYERS`:** Tywin arranged rewards and Ja ime commands crown troops in Riverlands, so he is a beneficiary/agent of the regime that exploited the murders. This is a **different political/moral accusation** from direct planning. It is not rebutted simply by showing he did not send the fatal sword order.
3. **`JAIME FAILED HIS OATH TO RESTORE CATELYN'S DAUGHTERS`:** He sent Brienne with a Lannister sword and royal warrant in pursuit of Sansa, but Sansa and Arya remain separated from the mission. `INTENT TO SEARCH IS ATTESTED` while `DAUGHTERS SAFELY RETURNED` is **not**. Stoneheart can react to an unmet oath, though disappointment alone does not prove secret RW complicity.

**Ethical branch test:** an actual hearing might reveal Stoneheart has reasons to hold Jaime responsible for **different harms** even if the `RWB DIRECT MURDER` accusation weakens. A resolution that requires her to forget Catelyn's first-hand trauma, or requires Jaime to deny the cruel methods he still uses, would be less faithful than forcing both to distinguish intent, execution, and later political responsibility.

**No automatic mercy:** Stoneheart has **already rejected Brienne's defense and the group's Thoros admits mercy is scarce**. Alternative paths include coercive service, another witness, a trial that fails, refusal, division within the Brotherhood, flight, or punishment, but the story has not depicted any of those outcomes for Jaime. The last seen Jaime POV is **Brienne's invitation at Pennytree**, unanswered.

## Reversal tests and unresolved records

- What exactly did **Merrett say out loud** to Stoneheart? He names Walder/Ryman/Roose/Lothar/Bastard Walder but **not Jaime**; do not treat interior flashback planning details as a transcribed confession to her.
- What does **Catelyn actually see**? A pink-cloaked killer **speaking Jaime's name**, not an authenticated instruction from Jaime. No POV confirmation she recognizes his face explicitly in that sentence.
- Did Jaime learn of the wedding at **Brindlewood** only afterward? Yes from his POV; **knowledge of an earlier conspiracy nonetheless remains an unproven logical possibility**, not source fact.
- Can **Brienne give a timeline**? She can attest to the journey and Beesbury conversation, but **did not use that precise argument at the Stoneheart hearing**.
- Has **Stoneheart held or rejected a formal impartial hearing**? She orders Brienne to kill Jaime or hang, with no trial of Jaime depicted. The Brotherhood's question to Merrett about his own guilt is not a universal procedure.
- Was **Oathkeeper** given to find Sansa? Source-supported; **the meaning of Lannister heraldry** is ambiguous evidence of mission allegiance, not a lie detector.
- Does Stoneheart possess **Robb's bronze-and-iron sword-crown**? She holds a clearly matching object in AFFC 42; **how she got it** is unknown and does not implicate Jaime.
- Have **Podrick and Hyle been rescued/returned**? They were hanged at the gallows in progress; their final survival status and Brienne's bargain are unshown.
- Does **Jaime go alone**? No response, departure or meeting in the five-book endpoint.
- Will Jaime's testimony or Brienne's change Stoneheart's view? `UNKNOWN` and particularly not to be assumed given prior rejected defense.
