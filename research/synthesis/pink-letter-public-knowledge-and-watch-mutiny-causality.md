## Executive conclusion: the letter clearly changes Jon's conduct; it does not explain the entire plot against him

**Strongest fact: ADWD 69 (Jon).** Jon receives a threatening pink-sealed letter *signed as Ramsay*, privately reconsiders, talks with Tormund **for much of two hours**, reads the letter **before** a mixed Watch/Free Folk/royal audience, declares a personally led expedition to Winterfell, sees **Marsh and Yarwyck leave with followers**, and shortly afterward is attacked by **Wick Whittlestick and Marsh**, followed by two unidentified stabs.

**What this proves:** a **temporal and behavioral chain**: letter **received → Jon chooses different public action → Marsh and Yarwyck depart → stabbing follows**. The subsequent facts do **not** establish **letter author = murder planner**, prove the knife plot was first formed *after* the letter was read, establish who killed Ser Patrek or arranged the giant crisis, or reveal the third/fourth attacker. Saying this was `FOR THE WATCH` is an attacker's stated justification, not Watch-wide authorization.

**Previously underused contradiction:** The Watch's rebellion is **not attributable exclusively to an external letter**. Marsh/Yarwyck had spent multiple chapters disputing the gate, food, Free Folk command appointments, oaths and Hardhome. In **ADWD 31 (Melisandre)**, Mance, **still wearing Rattleshirt's appearance**, even **reports** a personal clash with Marsh over overhearing a defensive council at supper. That encounter is a **source-supported report of social proximity and tension**, not a verified transfer of the real Mance identity or a proven information leak.

The *first* letter provides the cleanest **previous public information**: Watchmen already had notice of Bolton's claimed Arya wedding, *without having to learn it through Mance's covert mission*.

## 2. The much smaller circle of actual Mance/glamour information

**ADWD 31 (Melisandre), `POV-OBSERVED`:** Melisandre privately demonstrates that the surviving “Rattleshirt” is actually **Mance Rayder** wearing a glamour; Jon sees the transformation when the ruby's seeming is lifted. The burning at the Wall consumed the **real Lord of Bones**, not Mance. **Melisandre, Mance and Jon** are direct source-backed possessors of the essential identity information. The novel depicts **Devan leaving before the reveal**, so being the woman's squire or a queen's man does **not** give him proven knowledge of the secret.

**ADWD 31, `CHARACTER TESTIMONY`:** While disguised, Mance recounts eating near **Bowen Marsh**, overhearing a council about high ground, and trading barbed words when Marsh objects to “murderers” listening. This is **Mance's account told to Melisandre**, not an independently seen conversation in that POV. It establishes plausible contact **between the undercover agent and the suspicious Lord Steward**. It does **not** establish:

- Marsh saw through the glamour;
- Mance knew that Marsh would later stab Jon;
- Marsh transmitted Mance's identity to Winterfell or authored the later letter;
- Rattleshirt's social visibility equals broad knowledge of the rescue mission.

**ADWD 31/35, `OBSERVED + JON'S PRIVATE RECOLLECTION`:** Mance asks for women to aid the rescue he proposes, and **Jon later thinks** he sent **Mance and six spearwives** north. Mance supplied some names; **Dolorous Edd** handled bringing selected women out of Mole's Town. **Edd's recruitment role is verified, but which concealed identities, true mission details or end destinations he understood is unshown.** Melisandre's initial vision of the grey girl is explicitly mistaken **in interpretation**: the arrival is **Alys Karstark**, not the Arya Jon imagines.

**ADWD 49 (Jon):** Mance and his spearwives have not returned; Jon worries Melisandre may be following her own purposes. **ADWD 69:** Jon still does not have their confirmed location or fate before the threatening letter arrives. A letter describing **Mance and six women** is therefore **evidence that its writer or source has specific information/rumors**, not proof **how** that knowledge traveled.

**ADWD 53 (Jon), `PUBLIC OFFICER KNOWLEDGE`:** Marsh publicly recalls **Mance as a former Night's Watch deserter** in debating how much to trust Free Folk; he does **not in that exchange establish knowledge that Mance survived the fire**. Do not back-project the reader's Melisandre POV into Marsh's knowledge.

**ADWD 69 (Jon), `PRIVATE RECIPIENT`:** Tormund asks how Mance could have been captured, given the public burning. **Jon's internal near-response identifies the glamour**, but he does **not simply narrate the entire transformation or personally certify the identity to Tormund** in that passage. Tormund's later suspicion that the letter could contain lies is not evidence he designed it.

## 3. Exposure at Shieldhall does not authenticate Winterfell and does change the stakes

**What Marsh and Yarwyck can credibly learn from Jon in the Shieldhall:**

1. **The letter's claims**, since Jon reads it aloud before they leave. The description of **Mance's survival and rescue** in the letter is therefore exposed to men who were **not independently proved to know the underlying secret**.
2. **Jon's proposed personal response**: travel south toward Ramsay/Winterfell, invite volunteers, and separate the Watch's proposed **Hardhome** ranging from his own political expedition.
3. **Who visibly supports the march**: named Free Folk captains are enthusiastic; the crowd contains both sworn brothers and Free Folk (approximately five Free Folk for each black cloak by Jon's glance, **not** a census of every Westerosi voter). Marsh/Yarwyck and their followers depart.
4. **The Watch's institutional problem**: Jon invokes Watch neutrality and says he will not compel sworn brothers to violate vows. **That is his asserted distinction**, while his own internal dialogue treats the choice as potentially treasonous and political.

**What the Shieldhall does NOT give them:** Independent proof Stannis lost, Mance was actually caged, six women were flayed, “Arya” is really Arya, Ramsay authored the document, or Jon holds an elected mandate to begin a southern war. Their reaction to the public *decision* may be causal **even if every military claim is fabricated**.

**ADWD 69 private interval:** Jon and Tormund discuss the changed plan for **nearly two hours** after he reads the message privately. The exact content is **not supplied**. It is **UNKNOWN** whether any representative of Marsh's faction hears, whether instructions or lists are sent during those hours, or whether an earlier knife conspiracy grows or crystallizes then. Do not retroactively fill those two hours with a described assassination council or detailed strategic pact.

## 6. Rival models that preserve character agency

**H1 — Local oath/security mutiny, precipitated by Jon's speech.** Marsh and associates already resent his Free Folk policy. They see a perceived final escalation when Jon reads hostile northern war claims aloud and offers a Free Folk-led southern march. The knife attack is locally motivated, whether or not the Pink Letter writer intended it. **Best supported local-cause model** because grievances and public conduct are observed. **Weakness:** no narrated planning, exact mutineer roster, or confession of timing; source does not tell whether murder was already planned.

**H2 — Long-brewing action that would happen without the Pink Letter.** Earlier food, resettlement, sworn office, Hardhome and castle-placement disputes might be enough to trigger conspirators. **Plausible**, since the conflict is visible before the last letter. **Weakness:** a counterfactual “they would have stabbed Jon anyway” is not observed; the specific knife event occurs after an unusually direct southern war announcement.

**H3 — Deliberate external bait to provoke a Watch internal breakdown.** A known or unknown writer might craft the letter to draw Jon south or bring Watch neutrality into doubt. **Plausible conspiracy theory only**. **Weakness:** no author is independently certified, no communication with Marsh/Wick proven, and Jon's unquoted two-hour talk with Tormund prevents us from attributing every planned next step to the sender's design.

**H4 — A real or partly real Bolton threat, independently acted on by local Watch officers.** The final letter could genuinely convey some northern facts and Jon could respond emotionally. The Watch's critics could still judge the new plan dangerous and act without coordinating with any letter sender. **A hybrid model cannot be excluded**, and does not require an omniscient mastermind. **Weakness:** each of the letter's claimed military/captive outcomes remains separately unverified.

**H5 — The Patrek/Wun Wun disturbance, not an external plan, creates the immediate opening.** The tower violence could be unrelated to the letter and still place Jon among crowded combatants at precisely the fatal instant; it could instead have been arranged. **Neither is confirmed**. **Weakness:** chronology proves concurrence but not accidental or planned coordination.

**Prioritization:** **H1** has the strongest *direct observations of grievances and decisions*. It is **not a verdict of motive** or an endorsed final-book plot. Every model still needs an independently narrated decision and actor to become an actual story.

**Winterfell counterpart audit (2026-10-16):** [Abel's six-woman mission, Jeyne's withheld name, and the independent raven routes](winterfell-abel-six-women-knowledge-and-raven-gates.md) now checks **ADWD 37, 41, 46, 51 (Theon)** and **ADWD 62 (Asha)** against the prior public/secret knowledge split. **Theon consciously declines to tell Abel that the bride is Jeyne**, and when the captive girl begins remembering her name among the washerwomen, he stops her. **Rowan calls her Arya and promises to bring her to Jon Snow**; a later unshown Mance identification is still possible but not source-proven. **At the escape, Rowan, Willow and Myrtle turn back toward Abel, Squirrel remains in the bedroom, Frenya holds the gate, and Holly is hit by two quarrels**; the book does not show all six captured/flayed. **Roose's Maester Rhodry has a wet incoming raven *before* this attempted escape**, not a verified outgoing Pink Letter. **Asha meets Theon** when the Tycho/Mors party delivers him and the girl to Stannis's camp. Neither the full fate of the rescue team nor a letter author/delivery route is thereby settled.

## 7. Specific future falsifiers, not a preselected answer

1. **Letter hand comparison**: a later source must **actually describe and compare handwriting/seals** from the earlier signed wedding announcement and the final threat; no described absence of extra signatures is sufficient.
2. **Mance identity leak**: obtain explicit testimony of **who knew or revealed the glamour**, and *when*. Mance's mention of listening to Marsh's supper council does not fill this requirement.
3. **Northern capture witness**: identify the actor who saw Abel/Mance exposed and the six women's fate, and separately determine if that actor had raven/courier access and time. The already held [Pink Letter theory docket](../theories/pink-letter.md) is the owner of detailed alternative-author tests.
4. **Watch pre-planning**: a named conspirator's account, witnessed preparation, or credible written instruction is required before claiming the assassination was set **before** the final message arrived.
5. **Patrek sequence**: recover who first confronted Wun Wun and why, with Val's actual account; absent that, no allegation about the queen's marriage plan proves the death's cause.
6. **Information received by Watch officers**: distinguish letter accusations from **verified** events in Winterfell and actual Watch oaths; receiving a public speech does not mean Marsh has independent access to northern secrets.
7. **Post-knife fallout**: before assigning success to any plan, obtain documented witness, life-status, garrison notice, hostage safety, and lawful Watch command recognition.
