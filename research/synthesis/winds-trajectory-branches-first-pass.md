# The Winds of Winter — first-pass trajectory branches

- Record role: `RESEARCH / GARDENING SYNTHESIS`.
- Status: `COMPETING BRANCHES / NOT ADOPTED`.
- Evidence base: completed five-book close reading and reconciliation ledgers. No television outcome is treated as canon.

## How to read these branches

Each branch begins from the same end-of-ADWD facts and varies only choices, message arrivals, and contingent results the books leave open. They are tools for testing character pressure. A later trajectory may mix elements only after it explains the causal bridge.

## Shared starting pressures

- Castle Black has an unreported command crisis after Jon’s stabbing.
- Stannis has Theon, Jeyne, Tycho, hunger, hostile weather, and possible but unconfirmed Karstark intelligence.
- Meereen has Barristan’s coup, hostages, loose dragons, disease, mercenary uncertainty, and an approaching Iron Fleet while Dany is absent.
- Aegon/Connington have early momentum but incomplete forces, a risky Storm’s End plan, and no received Dornish answer.
- King’s Landing has unshown discovery of Kevan/Pycelle’s murders, live Faith trials, and unstable authority.
- Oldtown faces naval threat and institutional conflict; the Vale remains politically local and conditional.

## Branch A — Local survival before grand convergence

**Initiating choices:** Castle Black actors contain violence rather than immediately unite; Stannis privileges survival/intelligence over a clean charge; Barristan’s first action limits hostage deaths; Doran receives delayed or incomplete news.

**Why it grows from the books:** hunger, weather, disease, damaged communications, disputed legitimacy, and traumatized witnesses all reward caution but make it costly. This branch treats the series’ many unresolved handoffs as active friction.

**Consequences to test:**

1. Theon’s testimony becomes politically useful only after humane treatment and corroboration, slowing a North resolution.
2. Jeyne’s identity cannot be public leverage without putting her at risk.
3. Tyrion’s usefulness begins with a precarious battlefield contact, not instant advisership.
4. Aegon’s rapid campaign gains ground faster than opponents understand it, but cannot purchase real legitimacy merely by surprise.
5. Cersei’s court response fragments before an external invasion produces consensus.

**Failure test:** If “caution” becomes stasis, force a character to choose whom they will endanger rather than letting logistics solve the plot.

## Branch B — Legitimacy contests create unstable coalitions

**Initiating choices:** A credible but incomplete report changes one court at a time: Stannis hears Theon; Doran receives Aegon’s claim before or apart from Quentyn news; the capital receives alarming southern reports; Sansa faces a local identity/medical decision.

**Why it grows from the books:** nearly every theater has a claimant whose public story differs from private truth—Jeyne/Arya, Aegon, Alayne/Sansa, Hizdahr’s kingship, Cersei’s public repentance, and Jon’s uncertain office/life.

**Consequences to test:**

1. A coalition can be militarily useful without resolving biological identity.
2. Arianne/Doran must choose whether new information reduces or intensifies the cost of secrecy.
3. Northern houses may prefer a living symbol while disagreeing on which Stark, king, or regent has lawful claim.
4. Cersei, Mace, and Faith can form temporary arrangements that worsen the later legitimacy crisis.
5. Bran’s potential political relevance remains blocked by physical location and unknown survival.

**Failure test:** No coalition may treat a reader-known identity as publicly available, or treat marriage/claim as consent.

## Branch C — Power escalation exposes the cost of instrumental people

**Initiating choices:** Leaders choose the apparently decisive tool: fire sacrifice, hostage coercion, Dragonbinder, uncontrolled dragons, mass naval violence, or public religious punishment.

**Why it grows from the books:** Bran uses Hodor, Stannis authorizes burning, Victarion sacrifices captives, Cersei weaponizes accusation, and Euron sells conquest as spectacle. These are real ethical fault lines, not imported darkness.

**Consequences to test:**

1. A magical or military gain damages trust, creates witnesses, or leaves an administrative burden.
2. Dany’s return confronts not only enemies but what others have done in her absence.
3. Aegon/Connington must face whether speed turns their restoration into the brutality Connington regrets.
4. Oldtown’s response must preserve civilians and knowledge rather than treating magic as a spectacle.
5. The Others crisis becomes harder because living factions have made cooperation less possible.

**Failure test:** Escalation must not become an adaptation replay; identify the book-specific actor, target, cost, and resulting information flow.

## Cross-branch discriminators

| Observation / choice | Branch pressure it most affects |
|---|---|
| Who reports Jon’s stabbing and how | North authority, wildling/Watch conflict, Stannis communication |
| Whether Stannis receives and trusts Theon | Battle planning, Jeyne protection, Bolton legitimacy |
| First surviving Meereen messenger | Dany’s response, Tyrion/Barristan meeting, Dorne’s Quentyn knowledge |
| Receipt order of Aegon and Quentyn news in Dorne | Arianne/Doran coalition choices |
| Public framing of Kevan/Pycelle deaths | Cersei/Faith/Tyrell balance and Aegon response |
| Any actual Dragonbinder response | Victarion/Euron power, dragon autonomy, Meereen survival |

## Non-adoption rule

Do not select a single branch until a second-pass continuity review has tested its travel, message, custody, and character-consent assumptions. The branches are deliberately designed to fail if they require omniscience or imported show causality.
