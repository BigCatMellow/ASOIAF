## Working conclusion

**The first political question after Jon's stabbing is who can uphold promises already made, not who immediately inherits Winterfell.** Alys Karstark and Sigorn of Thenn married with Jon as principal organizer; Queen Selyse's court separately claims power to arrange Free Folk marriages and impose kingship and R'hllor on all newcomers; the Watch officers contest Jon's decision-making. These authorities were **already in conflict before** the knives were drawn.

Alys/Sigorn's wedding **does not prove** the couple remain at Castle Black at the final scene, own Karhold, command northern clans, or can use the Watch's captive Cregan as their prisoner. The text's last depicted post-wedding status for the couple remains uncertain; do not put them at Hardin's Tower, the Shieldhall, or the Wall without a new narrated movement.

## 2. The Hardin's Tower puzzle — a higher-priority, unresolved micro-theory

**A verified location chain, not a verified motive:**

1. **ADWD 53:** Jon explicitly locates **Val** in Hardin's Tower and provides his own Watch guards and Wun Wun as protection from unwanted visitors. The giant sleeps in the entry hall.
2. **ADWD 53:** Patrek encounters Val while guarding Selyse; Val refuses to perform deference toward him.
3. **ADWD 69:** Selyse announces **Patrek as Val's intended husband**. Jon notes her consent has not been sought and the Free Folk do not follow the court's marriage conventions.
4. **ADWD 69:** After the Shieldhall meeting, a scream and Wun Wun's roars come from **Hardin's Tower**. Jon finds Patrek dead in the giant's grip; Wun Wun has sword wounds. Jon has **not** witnessed the confrontation's beginning.
5. **ADWD 69:** As Jon tries to hold queen's knights and other fighters back and calm the injured giant, **Wick Whittlestick attacks him**, then **Bowen Marsh** and others. What happened first and why Patrek was there remain **UNKNOWN**.

**Why this changes the Karstark question:** The same night that a planned **nonconsensual court marriage to Val** ends with a dead suitor and wounded protector, Jon—the person who arranged **Alys's chosen political marriage**—is stabbed. This creates possible distrust of crown marriage-making among Free Folk and possible retaliatory sentiment among queen's knights, without establishing how either group will actually act.

## 4. Misread prophecy already changed actual decisions: the grey girl is Alys

- **ADWD 31 (Melisandre)** — **VERIFIED internal belief:** Mel sees a grey-clad girl fleeing toward Jon and **infers** the girl is his sister Arya. She plans to gain Jon's trust by saving her and arranges for the glamoured **Mance** and spearwives to attempt the rescue. The girl-in-the-vision's identity is not directly revealed by magic in her POV.
- **ADWD 44 (Jon)** — **VERIFIED:** Alys arrives on a failing horse and asks Jon to protect her from a coerced marriage with Cregan. The encounter fits the visible signs of Mel's earlier report, but Mel's identification as Arya is not vindicated.
- **ADWD 49 (Jon)** — Jon explicitly tells Melisandre the girl was **Alys, not Arya**. Mel concedes her **interpretation was wrong** while defending the vision's truth. The Mance rescue operation and interest in the purported Arya at Winterfell remain separate events.
- **ADWD 69 (Jon)** — the letter **signed by Ramsay** makes assertions about Mance and his six women; Jon reacts by changing his announced course. Tormund warns that the letter may contain falsehoods. This is an **observed decision made under unverified claims**, not confirmation of the letter's authorship or the fate of those it names.

**Cross-plot inference:** A mistaken identification of an endangered noblewoman has already contributed to a real intervention in the Boltons' household, while the *actual* woman Mel glimpsed has secured a marriage Jon arranged at the Wall. **This is a character-driven information failure with consequences, not proof that every fire vision is reliable or that Mel controls the resulting events.**

**Serious alternative:** Even without the grey-girl prophecy, Jon hears other reports of a girl presented as Arya in Ramsay's control; a rescue might still have been attempted. Do **not** claim the whole Mance expedition was caused solely by an otherwise baseless prophecy.

## 5. Three immediate, falsifiable story experiments — none adopted

**Branch A — guarded continuation:** Trusted Watch guards, Val, the wounded giant and Tormund's captains prevent immediate reprisals while an identified authority establishes Jon's condition. Alys/Sigorn, if still reachable, must obtain news through a plausible messenger. A limited independent Karhold settlement remains possible. **Test:** Does a Watch leader issue an order protecting or releasing existing captives? Does anyone possess food and an enforceable truce?

**Branch B — political hostage crisis:** Marsh-aligned men or queen's knights try to take control of one or more protected persons (Val, the Free Folk boys, or Cregan), forcing Tormund and others to decide whether to bargain, resist or withdraw. The conflict can escalate **without** a magical solution. **Test:** An actual claimant to custodial authority, named guards and a shown response must appear; no one can be handed over by narrator fiat.

**Branch C — competing marriage legitimacy:** Selyse presses her other proposed marriages and her religious terms after Patrek's death; Alys and Sigorn, if they learn of it, must decide whether the crown's endorsement brings safety or another demand for subordination. Elders may support the specific Thenn match but reject coercive settlement. **Test:** Distinguish Alys's agency and genuine consent from the queen's announced matches; obtain a credible information route and evidence of who controls Karhold.

- **Reject:** "Jon's attack terminates the Karhold claim." Harrion's succession and Cregan's custody are unresolved political questions independently of Jon's fate.
