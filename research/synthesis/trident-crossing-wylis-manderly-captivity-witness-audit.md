## Executive finding: a surviving witness exists, but his eyewitness *testimony* does not

**Most consequential independent corroboration:** in **AFFC 27 (Jaime)**, Jaime **personally finds** Wylis Manderly at Harrenhal **among other highborn northern prisoners captured by Gregor Clegane at the fords of the Trident**. This corroborates an actual Mountain-versus-Northern-force action and Wylis's second captivity. It is **independent** of Roose's narration to Robb in **ASOS 49 (Catelyn)**.

It **does not** establish that Wylis received a particular crossing order, that Roose deliberately put the Norrey/Locke/Burley men last, that boats could have been obtained, or that Roose deliberately preserved Dreadfort soldiers. Jaime never interviews Wylis on these points on-page.

**Second important distinction:** the reader can reconstruct two separate captivities, and **ACOK 30 (Arya)** likely shows Wylis still held at Harrenhal under his description and heraldic clasp, but **the novels do not show the intermediary release or exchange**. A proposed `Wylis + Harrion → Willem` exchange occurs in dialogue in **ACOK 25 (Tyrion)**; the proposed hostage Willem remains with Robb's army and is killed in **ASOS 20 (Catelyn)**. The text cannot sustain an assertion that this **specific proposal was executed**. Nevertheless Wylis was later serving with Northern troops and was captured at the Trident; his first custody must have ended by some **unshown** means, barring an unproved identification error.

**Third important distinction:** in **AFFC 27/30 (Jaime)** it is **Jaime** who finds Wylis and directs that he be sent via Maidenpool to White Harbor, charging Red Ronnet with the escort. In **ADWD 29 (Davos)** Wyman confirms his son is home. At the Winterfell feast in **ADWD 46 (Theon)** Wyman publicly credits *Tywin* for returning his son. This distinguishes **documented operational action** from **public attribution**. He may be invoking Tywin's earlier guarantee, speaking strategically, or simply lacking details; no one motive is established and the line does **not** make Tywin the person shown issuing Jaime's order.

**Potential release window, with a decisive prisoner-group distinction:** in **ACOK 47 (Arya)** Arya helps free **Robett Glover** and a large group of northmen confined in the **Widow's Tower dungeon** during the fall of Harrenhal. In **ACOK 30**, the trident-clasp hostage is among the earlier **Tower of Dread** highborn prisoners, some of whom are given parole privileges. The text never identifies **Wylis** as part of the Widow's Tower rescue. Harrenhal's change of control is a plausible *opportunity* to end his first captivity, not a witnessed release. **ACOK 30** also has Arya overhear unverified camp talk that Tywin sent Gregor and Vargo against Roose. This is **REPORTED RUMOR**, not a direct order or proof of coordination between Roose and Gregor.

### ACOK 30's mini-roster: three Green Fork captives and unequal ransom

**ACOK 30 (Arya) is more than a resemblance clue**. In the same Harrenhal sequence, she describes: (1) the extremely fat hostage with a bushy moustache and a silver-and-sapphire **trident** clasp (strongly consistent with **Wylis Manderly**, see **AGOT 55**); (2) a young bearded hostage in a black cloak patterned with **white suns** (strongly consistent with **Harrion Karstark**, identified as captured in **AGOT 62**); and (3) **Lord Cerwyn**, whom Arya already knows from Winterfell and who **dies in Harrenhal captivity** after his wounds. These descriptions align with **three commanders Addam Marbrand lists as captured at the Green Fork** in AGOT 62. The inferred identifications of the first two men remain **not explicitly named in Arya's scene**, but the convergent roster, heraldry and individual descriptions considerably narrow the alternatives.

This also changes how to read **ASOS 49**'s description of **Ser Kyle Condon as the late Lord Cerwyn's right hand**: the relevant Lord Cerwyn's **death in captivity is actually depicted through Arya's discovery**, not merely an unspecified earlier death. It does **not** mean Kyle knew what happened to Lord Cerwyn or shared all his politics.

Arya **sees Frey hostages ransomed** while noting that no comparable ransom is paid for the northern captives. That is an observed **contrast in custody treatment at this time**, not proof no northern captive would ever be released, or proof Bolton later freed them all. In the same passage Arya glimpses Tywin walking with maesters and the fat prisoner; this is **observed proximity**, not an independently authenticated interrogation, bargain or confession.

**Do not import an apparent contrary state from an appendix as a later event:** the **ADWD appendix** still describes Wylis as a prisoner at Harrenhal, despite **ADWD 29**'s direct account of his return. Appendix listings are not a second witness or a chronology override.

### The hostage transaction and the release agency are separate

- **Early ACOK:** Tywin's no-ransom conditional demand would require Wyman's **military submission**; Wyman says no (**ACOK 16**).
- **Later ACOK:** Tyrion publicly makes a **different**, multi-captive proposal routed toward Robb (**ACOK 25**). Since Willem and Tion later die in Robb's custody (**ASOS 20**), **do not mark this bargain completed**.
- **Post-Red Wedding:** Wyman reports a new threat from Tywin conditioned on allegiance to Tommen and Roose, with White Harbor endangered; Wyman answers that he will submit **only after** Wylis's safe return (**ADWD 29**). This is Wyman's **account of correspondence**, not the letters themselves in the reader's hands.
- **After Tywin's death:** Jaime at Harrenhal orders the transfer and selects Red Ronnet as escort (**AFFC 27/30**). Wyman says negotiations with Freys continued and Wylis eventually returned (**ADWD 29**). We can establish **endpoints**, but not a complete sequence of Frey approvals, Lannister orders, transport and proof-of-delivery.
- **At Winterfell:** Wyman publicly says Tywin returned him (**ADWD 46**). Read alongside his private account and Jaime's actions, that statement is **public attribution**, not a verbatim movement ledger; whether it is rhetoric, conventional credit to Tywin's earlier guarantee, or incomplete information is **UNKNOWN**.

**Potentially consequential:** AFFC 27 depicts Wylis as a badly shaken prisoner, reduced to sobbing when Jaime announces release. Jaime also wonders about the food Gregor's captives had been given. The scene demonstrates **distress and coercive conditions**; it does **not** authenticate the exact contents of the food, a permanent mental incapacity, or any statement by Wylis accusing Roose.

### Separate Harrenhal custody/command investigation (2026-10-16)

[Harrenhal's Northern prisoners and the prisoner-to-command transfer](harrenhal-captive-command-reintegration-audit.md) establishes that **Roose specifically tells Jaime in ASOS 37** that **Harrion Karstark remained a prisoner when Bolton took Harrenhal**, and that Roose gave him the remaining Karhold soldiers and dispatched him with Robett Glover. The black-with-white-suns captive in **ACOK 30 (Arya)** is strongly consistent with Harrion. **That is not a Wylis release record**: the fat trident-clasp hostage was said to belong to **Tywin**, whereas Harrion was claimed by a ransom-seeking hedge knight. It also separates the earlier **Tower of Dread** highborn Green Fork captives from Vargo Hoat's later **Widow's Tower** prisoner convoy used during the castle takeover (**ACOK 47**). Thus a **specific Harrion command handoff is attributed**, while Wylis's first exit from captivity remains `UNKNOWN`.

### Maidenpool transit check and contrast with other northern prisoners

[Maidenpool escort/custody audit](maidenpool-prisoner-handoffs-tarly-mooton-connington.md) adds **ADWD Epilogue (Kevan)**: **Tarly speaks of Red Ronnet's escort passing through Maidenpool and the crimes of its Mountain men there**. Combined with Jaime's **AFFC 27/30** explicit Wylis→Ronnet→Maidenpool order and **ADWD 29** Wylis's reported homecoming, this **corroborates the escort's presence at the transit town**, **not** the exact Wylis-to-ship handover. **AFFC 27 also leaves several other unnamed highborn Trident captives present at Harrenhal** without individually narrated release orders when Bonifer Hasty takes over the castle. These possible witnesses must not be silently presumed to have joined Wylis in White Harbor or to have been moved to Maidenpool.

### Additional source test: what Hasty can and cannot testify to (2026-10-16)

[Harrenhal's post-Jaime custody and raven audit](harrenhal-hasty-unreleased-trident-prisoners-raven-audit.md) confirms **AFFC 27** directly shows **several unnamed highborn Trident captives** alive alongside Wylis, but **only Wylis** receives a named release/escort instruction. Jaime appoints **Bonifer Hasty** interim crown castellan and takes Gregor's men away; **Maester Gulian** is to remain with ravens. **This is not a proven prisoner-to-Hasty transfer**, and no subsequent five-book scene certifies the other men remain at the castle, are sent to Maidenpool, or tell Hasty anything about Roose's rear guard. Hasty's outgoing-raven capacity is an *unexercised pathway*, not a received testimony or royal inquest. Wylis's later homecoming remains the one individually identified end state.

## 4. Ronnel Stout, Kyle Condon, and the 600 men: three separate time slices

Roose's spoken sequence (**ASOS 49**) is:

1. `CHARACTER CLAIM` A river expedition is delayed and ferrying begins; many men transfer north.
2. `CHARACTER CLAIM` Gregor strikes men **waiting to cross** and Wylis rallies the exposed rear.
3. `CHARACTER CLAIM` Roose **leaves 600** to hold the ford, including Hornwood archers, men from the Rills/mountains/White Knife, Stouts and Cerwyns, with Ronnel and Kyle as commanders.

The **600 are a later posted security force**, not the precise roster of the attacked rear guard and **not 600 proven casualties**. The books do not name them again in a witnessed post-ford action. `ASSIGNED` does not mean `PERISHED`; `KNOWN OFFICER` does not mean `INTERVIEWED WITNESS`.

**Potential challenge to Roose if testimony surfaces:** Ronnel or Kyle might know whether the 600 actually received the order and whether ferry traffic, scouts and crossings were arranged as described. Yet their assigned position after the action does not guarantee either observed Wylis's placement or knew why it was chosen. The fact Kyle was close to **the late Lord Cerwyn**, not an identified Dreadfort household knight, means it is improper to class him automatically as Roose's loyal confidant.

## 6. Why this matters to White Harbor and the Battle of Ice

**What Wyman demonstrably knows:** his two sons were endangered in different ways. Wendel died as a guest at the Freys' wedding (**ASOS 51; ADWD 15/29**); Wylis was a hostage whose life constrained Wyman's public conduct (**ADWD 19, 29**). Wyman explicitly blames Roose for lying about the **Red Wedding**, not in recorded dialogue for the **Ruby Ford assignment** (**ADWD 29**). His anti-Bolton/frey motive therefore **does not require** knowledge of a river conspiracy.

**What Wylis *could* contribute:** first-person memory of Gregor's attack; his actual superior/order chain; knowledge of whether ships/boats were used to preferentially protect Bolton retainers. It is **unwarranted** to assign him this detailed knowledge or assume Wyman already heard it. He might have been unable or unwilling to discuss the trauma; no source establishes that either.

**Potential new political route:** `Wylis safely home → Wyman/Leona can speak without hostage threat → witness account voluntarily given and authenticated → Northern houses independently evaluate Roose's choices → current commands choose whether to obey Winterfell sortie orders`. Only the first and the independently attested hostile political atmosphere are shown. Every middle step is a possible **future narrative decision**, not five-book history.

This is particularly relevant because the **ADWD 51 (Theon)** sortie order sends Wyman's people separately from Hosteen Frey's, while Wyman is wounded and **his actual field deputy is unnamed**. Nothing shows Wylis at Winterfell, a Wylis-authored standing order to that deputy, or a Stannis–Manderly battle pact independent of Davos's unfinished Rickon condition. See [White Harbor officers](white-harbor-officers-and-winterfell-delegation-gates.md) and [sortie fracture](frey-manderly-sortie-command-fracture.md).

**A subtler information theme:** Roose offers Robb an operational explanation (**ASOS 49**); Jaime later finds operational consequences but does not know Roose's order (**AFFC 27**); Wyman's public account credits Tywin for Jaime's release order (**ADWD 46**) while his secret account explains the bargaining constraints (**ADWD 29**). The same episode creates **three different accounts that are not coextensive**. The novel's future political payoff could concern not only *who died*, but **who can document an order to make other lords act differently**.

## 7. Verification gaps and next bounded research

1. **First-captivity exit, highest priority:** **ACOK 30 (Arya)** likely identifies Wylis as a highborn trident-clasp hostage *after* ransom of some Freys; **ACOK 47** frees Robett Glover's separate dungeon group when Harrenhal falls. Neither scene names Wylis's release. Seek a letter, recollection, or order that completes `EARLIER HOSTAGE → RETURNED TO ARMY BEFORE RUBY FORD`. No name-explicit statement of the first release was located in the five-book audit.
2. **Lannister-side campaign order:** inspect Gregor's actual orders, movements, captives and the Harrenhal garrison's receipts; distinguish independent prisoner custody from a direct account of the engagement.

## 8. New cross-chapter check: Aenys's departure and the changing Ruby Ford

This pass tests the obvious comparator without upgrading it into testimony. **Aenys never gives an on-page account of his crossing**: no chapter records which banners crossed with him, his boat or ford choice, the water level he personally observed, or a conversation in which he disputes Roose. The point of comparison instead comes from two **separate POV checkpoints** that narrow the chronology and terrain claim.

| Checkpoint | Evidence | What it independently supports | What it does **not** establish |

| **Aenys at Harrenhal before withdrawal** | In **ACOK 64 (Arya)**, Arya personally hears Aenys urge departure because a huge castle, ruined country and absent harvest make a siege/logistics crisis; Roose answers that he does not mean to be besieged. Aenys is also visibly present when Roose assumes Harrenhal in **ACOK 47 (Arya)**. | `POV-OBSERVED DIRECT SPEECH`: Aenys had a conventional, stated reason to want the northern host out of Harrenhal. ### Aenys's incentives are visible; his river knowledge is not

Aenys is not merely a silent name in Roose's story. Arya hears him argue that Harrenhal cannot feed or withstand the army, and he warns that Lannister reach has undone commanders who thought Tywin distant (**ACOK 64**). He is also a Frey participant in the post-Harrenhal command environment and, later, a Bolton-aligned Frey at Winterfell (**ADWD 32, 46, 51**). These facts make him a **potentially interested** witness, not a neutral one.

The most defensible reading is narrow:

`AENYS LEFT HARRENHAL EARLIER / ROOSE WARNED JAIME OF A FLOODED, DIFFICULT FORD BEFORE THE REPORTED DISASTER / AENYS'S ACTUAL CROSSING ACCOUNT UNKNOWN.`

That result cuts two ways. It weakens the claim that flood difficulty was fabricated only after losses, because Roose gives Jaime the same broad warning in advance. It also leaves the central question untouched: an early party's ability to proceed under difficult conditions gives no evidence about which later companies were ordered to wait, whether boat shortages were avoidable, or whether Roose could have protected Wylis's rear guard.

### Revised five-book disposition

- `INDEPENDENT / LIMITED`: Aenys's earlier march and Roose's pre-disaster flood warning occur in Jaime's POV chronology (**ASOS 44**).
- `POV-OBSERVED / LIMITED`: Arya later sees that the river has subsided (**ASOS 74**).
- `CHARACTER CLAIM`: Aenys crossed successfully; Roose's exact departure delay, ferry numbers, two-thirds figure, rear-guard composition and defensive 600-man implementation (**ASOS 49**).
- `CHARACTER INFERENCE`: Sandor's conclusion that Gregor holds the ford from the presence of Gregor's men (**ASOS 74**).
- `UNKNOWN`: Aenys's own account; the operational fate of Ronnel Stout, Kyle Condon and their command; any independent witness to Roose's crossing order or intent.

This is a **corroboration boundary**, not an exoneration. It strengthens the ordinary-military explanation that flood conditions were genuinely difficult while preserving the unanswered allocation question on which a deliberate-sacrifice theory must stand.

## 9. False-positive witness excluded: Ser Bartimus's other Battle of the Trident

**ADWD 29 (Davos)** introduces Ser Bartimus, the one-legged chief gaoler of White Harbor's Wolf's Den. When drunk, he boasts that he **saved Lord Wyman's life at the capitalized “Battle of the Trident,”** for which the Den was his reward. This is a genuine link between a named White Harbor retainer, Wyman and a battle on the Trident—but it is **not evidence about Roose's later Ruby Ford operation**.

The contextual distinction matters:

- `CHARACTER CLAIM`: Bartimus's own boast is not independently narrated battle testimony.
- `HISTORICAL LABEL / INFERRED IDENTIFICATION`: “the Battle of the Trident” conventionally denotes Robert's Rebellion battle, rather than the later, separately described fighting at the **fords/Ruby Ford** in **ASOS 49**. The wording, Wyman's personal participation and the reward's long-established character all point to that earlier event.
- `NOT A RUBY-FORD WITNESS`: Bartimus is never placed with Wylis, Roose, Ronnel Stout, Kyle Condon or Gregor's attack; neither he nor Davos recounts a later river campaign memory.

Therefore Bartimus may help explain a durable personal tie between Wyman and an old White Harbor officer, but he cannot corroborate, contradict, or relay a first-hand account of the crossing under audit. It is precisely the sort of shared place-name that can create a false information path if the two Trident events are silently collapsed.
