## Executive finding: two old critics of Marsh were also critics of Jon's Free Folk placements

**VERIFIED OLD OPINIONS — ASOS 78 (Samwell):** Eastwatch commander **Cotter Pyke** derided Marsh's qualification for Lord Commander as a man who counts household objects; Shadow Tower commander **Ser Denys Mallister** also expressly said Marsh (and Yarwyck) were not up to the office. Pyke and Mallister were *rivals who distrusted each other*, yet each independently objected to Marsh as the Watch-wide choice.

**NEW CROSS-CHECK — ADWD 28 (Jon):** Jon sent **ten Mole's Town Free Folk workers/recruits each** to Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower. Both commanders complained to Jon by letter. Mallister wanted **trained, disciplined black brothers** rather than men of doubtful loyalty, and Pyke was openly contemptuous of the newcomers. The ten previously sent to each location **must not be conflated** with the **sixty later hostage boys reported sent toward Eastwatch and Shadow Tower** (ADWD 58). They are different movements and populations.

**Therefore:** Rejecting the *stabbing and/or Marsh's claim* does **not** mean that either officer ratifies all of Jon's admissions, follows Free Folk military leaders, or wants to protect arriving children out of personal affection. **A Watch-wide succession dispute may coexist with independent restrictions on refugee intake.** Conversely, opposing Jon's resettlement does **not** prove either would allow the murder of a sworn commander or execute wards.

## 2. The geography of legitimacy: a route that used to belong to the Watch alone

**ASOS 75 (Samwell) gives an actual land route:** Sam and Gilly walk **Nightfort → Deep Lake → Queensgate → toward Castle Black** alongside the Wall. They encounter **Ser Denys Mallister coming from the west** with Marsh and the survivors from the Bridge of Skulls, moving toward Castle Black. Sam reflects that **only the Watch uses the road** at that earlier time.

**ADWD 69 (Jon) changes the political environment:** Jon has since **conferred Queensgate on Morna White Mask** and **Oakenshield on Tormund**, which Marsh expressly worries could leave Castle Black surrounded or cut off from the Wall's other posts. Tormund has in fact **settled people at Oakenshield**, while arriving personally at Castle Black to discuss Hardhome. **ADWD 58** gives Morna a son among the Watch hostages.

**INFERRED, not observed:** Communication or reinforcement *on the western Wall road* now involves an environment in which a **Free Folk-held Watch outpost stands on an earlier demonstrated Watch route**. Morna might escort a frightened messenger, refuse a hostile armed column, wait for instructions, or not hear the crisis for days. **The specific location and settlement/guard strength of Queensgate at the moment of the stabbing are not narrated; this does not prove a roadblock, a toll, a siege or any Free Folk commander blocking a raven.**

**Transport distinction:** A horse party **along the Wall** might face a contested garrison on the road. A **raven** might bypass that road, provided an actual writer, bird, destination and receipt exist. Eastwatch's coast/port and Pyke's stranded fleet create a **third communication chain**. A paper demand from Castle Black does not physically control all three.

## 3. A difficult Watch precedent: older hostility does not translate to new ballots

- **ASOS 75 (Samwell):** the Lord Commander choice requires **two-thirds** of sworn brothers; voting by **tokens** permitted brothers on duty to have a friend cast their token. **Mallister and Pyke cast ballots on behalf of the garrisons they had left behind** when they were present for the *previous* choosing. That shows the franchise is not restricted to Castle Black's current courtyard.
- **ASOS 75:** Marsh **withdraws his candidacy** and endorses **Janos Slynt** in an *earlier* election. In ASOS 78 Sam sees Marsh holding the earlier **castellanship and Lord Stewardship**. **ADWD 35 (Jon)** separately shows Jon delegating Wall command for a short absence. Neither event elects Marsh to succeed Jon.
- **ASOS 78:** **both Pyke and Mallister** regard Marsh and Yarwyck as unfit for the highest post while their own contest blocks an easy consensus; **Sam privately misleads each about Stannis's threat to impose the other commander**. Their participation in electing Jon does **not imply unchanging loyalty to his later policies**.
- **After ADWD 69:** There is **no enacted new election, revised token count, legal command proclamation, renewed garrison proxy agreement, or authenticated mail to Hewett/Mallister/Pyke**. Because Pyke is away at Hardhome, **do not silently allocate all Eastwatch proxy votes to him, Hewett, or Marsh**. An older ballot custom is a precedent, not an already certified new proxy register.

### Personal complication: Mallister has dealt directly with Marsh

**ASOS 75** places Mallister and an injured **Marsh** in the same returning Bridge-of-Skulls company. This is **VERIFIED association**, not friendship or general trust. Mallister's later rejection of Marsh's competence for Lord Commander (ASOS 78) must therefore be read as an **institutional judgment about highest office**, not proof he would reflexively ignore any limited message from a familiar veteran officer.

The contrary possibility is just as live: a false claim of “Jon died in battle” or “all the brothers support me” could become damaging precisely because an experienced commander can ask what happened to **the actual named witnesses** and whether a Watch-wide choosing has occurred. **No specific deceptive letter is recorded.**

## 4. Three different Free Folk constituencies complicate remote compliance

1. **Ten earlier Mole's Town newcomers at each main outpost — ADWD 28.** Pyke and Mallister complained about the competence and loyalty of these men. Their eventual disposition, reassignment, oath-taking and fate are unshown.
2. **Sixty child hostages — ADWD 58.** Marsh **reports** that sixty of Tormund's group were **sent to Eastwatch and Shadow Tower**, but not the split, named destinations, proof each reached a fortress, or individual keeper. These are politically different from the recruits/workers dispatched earlier. A successor cannot authorize punishments against boys using an officer's earlier letter about dissatisfied adult recruits as supposed proof.
3. **Free Folk settlements in reopened castles — ADWD 53/69.** Tormund has family and followers at Oakenshield; Morna has been assigned Queensgate; Soren Stonedoor. They are not a single garrison, nor automatically subject to Gerrick Kingsblood, Selyse's court, or an unrecognized caretaker. **Do not convert each chief's Wall service oath into a Night's Watch election vote**.

The immediate political danger is **asymmetric information**: one local commander may see trained Watchmen, unreliable refugees and unknown hostages, while Castle Black fears an uprising and a Free Folk father fears a missing son. An urgent claim to authority might induce **limited protective cooperation** without obtaining political recognition. The converse—claiming a new Lord Commander and attempting to expel existing settlers—could lose the very transport/food/child-safety cooperation that keeps forts manned. Both are **CONDITIONAL**.

## 6. Counterarguments and disconfirmers

- **H1: Hewett accepts Marsh rapidly.** Some earlier associations with Thorne and Slynt and resentment of Jon might make him willing to help a Castle Black caretaker. **The attitude is possible, not observed.** Disconfirm with Hewett demanding election proof, rejecting the knives or protecting hostages despite his bias.
- **H2: Mallister insists on an independent choosing.** His ASOS defense of Watch voting traditions and rejection of Marsh as suitable command support a plausible demand. **His current response is unshown.** Disconfirm with explicit delegated provisional allegiance or a lawful later election.
- **H3: Pyke rejects both Marsh and new Free Folk arrivals.** His abrasive views make this plausible, while his rescue expedition demonstrates actual service to Jon's Hardhome initiative. His ability to act depends on survival and recovering a fleet. **Do not convert an anti-Marsh remark years/chapters earlier into a proven mutiny, or a rescue under Jon's orders into unconditional love for Free Folk.**
- **H4: A council preserves temporary provisioning while disputing legitimacy.** Local officers can attempt to enforce an emergency minimum and exchange boy-by-boy safeguards while postponing the title. **Must demonstrate** stores and keys, real guardians, communications and independent assent. A letter alone cannot prove it.
- **H5: Morna's Queensgate interrupts a land response.** Her strategic post and captive son create leverage; **nothing demonstrates she blocks roads**. A mounted courier may pass and/or ravens may move independently. A blockade is a speculative branch, not canon.
- **Competing priority — Bridge of Skulls:** Mallister's earlier command includes a **real western threat** from the Weeper and large camps north of his tower (ADWD 28/53). He could decline a dangerous trip to Castle Black **without recognizing Marsh**. His *presence* at the murder scene is unshown.
- **Historic ballots age badly:** No ASOS token count, prior withdrawal or former political alliance should be imported as the Watch's present actual electorate or mandate.
