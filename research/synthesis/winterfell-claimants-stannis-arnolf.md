## Executive finding: an attempted appointment, not a ratified succession

**Two strong pieces of direct evidence change the original research:**

1. **Stannis explicitly contemplates appointing Arnolf.** In **ADWD 17 (Jon)**, after Jon declines Winterfell, Stannis says he is **more inclined to bestow Winterfell on Arnolf Karstark**, instead of southron aspirants Richard Horpe or Justin Massey. He identifies Arnolf as a loyal northern man of distant Stark blood and considers his children an acceptable continuation. This is a **FIRST-PERSON STANNIS STATEMENT OF PREFERENCE**, not a signed grant, completed investiture, or promise given to Arnolf.
2. **The Bolton side independently treats Arnolf as an agent.** In **ADWD 32 (Reek/Theon)**, **Roose Bolton tells Ramsay** that Arnolf tried to draw Stannis toward the Dreadfort trap. Later Roose expects Arnolf to join Stannis as the Bolton army prepares Winterfell as bait; Ramsay anticipates trapping the invader. **ADWD 37 (Theon)** reports Theon's own understanding that Arnolf waits for a Bolton signal. The reader now has materially stronger evidence than **Alys's warning in ADWD 44** alone. Still no Stannis POV or confirmed warning receipt exists.

**The larger contradiction:** Stannis's ideal solution is **a Stark-blooded lord holding Winterfell as a vassal of the Iron Throne**. Robb's succession decision in **ASOS 45** concerns a **King in the North**, whose independence Stannis does not recognize. Even if Jon were the shared candidate, the two models conflict.

## 2. Four distinct legitimacies that the storyline cannot collapse

### A. Robb's elective/royal Stark continuity

**ASOS 45 (Catelyn):** Robb speaks of an heir as **King in the North** and favors Jon's legitimization. The reader knows the younger brothers survived; Robb does not. The actual document and witnesses' subsequent ability to testify remain under [separate custody audit](../continuity/robbs-will-witness-ledger.md).

**Stannis's potential problem:** A powerful northern house presenting Robb's will could demand independence under Jon, while Stannis would at most confer a subordinate North lordship. **This conflict is conditional on actual northern acknowledgment and discovery of the will**, neither of which has occurred.

### B. Stannis's sovereign royal appointments

**ASOS 79; ADWD 3/17:** Stannis can *propose* to legitimize Jon and grant him Winterfell. After Jon declines, Stannis considers Horpe, Massey and Arnolf. He wants northern allegiance and supply lines, not merely a symbolic Stark restoration.

**What does NOT follow:** A non-Stark appointment is necessarily legal, welcomed by northmen, or safely enforceable; he doesn't possess the castle. Stannis may change his preference when new information about Arnolf, Rickon or the Boltons arrives. **No executed appointment instrument is in the five-book corpus.**

### C. The surviving Stark children and their wishes

- **Sansa:** Jon calls her rightful heir in ADWD 3. She is actually Alayne at the Gates of the Moon (AFFC 41) and is not available to be installed. Stannis sees her legal marriage to Tyrion as disqualifying in practice. 
- **Bran:** alive north of the Wall (ADWD 34), unknown to Stannis/Robb when they discuss inheritance.
- **Rickon:** Manderly/Wex/Davos possess a credible but unverified Skagos lead (ADWD 29); Stannis hasn't received that intelligence.
- **Arya:** alive in Braavos (ADWD 64). Jeyne Poole is impersonating Arya at Winterfell (ADWD 37/51). Stannis's rescue rationale depends on a false identification, and Theon is now nearby as a possible witness.
- **Jon:** may be Robb's intended lawful legitimized heir if the will can be found; he's made no choice to accept Winterfell, and his physical survival following the ADWD 69 attack is unknown.

**Important uncertainty:** Do not automatically reorder all claims by ordinary primogeniture; Northmen, Stannis, Robb, Petyr, Wyman and the surviving children have different **knowledge, laws, incentives and capacities to enforce** their preferences.

### D. Political control, marriage and the Boltons' imposture

Roose/Ramsay control the ruin and use 'Arya' as a Stark legitimacy asset (**ADWD 32, 37**). The mountain clans support Stannis partly to rescue **Ned's daughter**. **Jeyne's true identity can dissolve one justification for Stannis's northern coalition** without necessarily causing his existing soldiers to support Bolton. It can also create a duty to protect an abused child whose consent has been disregarded. Theon/Jeyne's testimony must reach real listeners.

## 3. Karhold cannot be treated as Arnolf's hereditary prize either

**ADWD 44 (Alys):** Harrion (“Harry”) Karstark is the proper lord while alive; Alys asserts she succeeds him before her great-uncle Arnolf, and that the forced marriage to Cregan could transfer control to Arnolf's branch. **ADWD 19 (Wyman):** A separate powerful northern lord calls Arnolf only a castellan. This is a real succession weakness distinct from the question of **Winterfell**.

**What is most revealing:** Stannis contemplates putting an insecure regent of one northern castle in charge of another — **because the regent first publicly declared for him**. That is a believable political error and opportunity for a reversal, not proof Stannis is secretly a Bolton accomplice.

**Possible future development:** Jon's marriage arrangement for Alys and Sigorn (ADWD 49) gives her a new political base; it could invalidate Arnolf's hold on Karhold if Harrion dies. It does not itself give Alys troops at Stannis's camp or create instant acceptance of Thenn marriage among all northerners.

## 4. Was Arnolf targeting the Dreadfort? The trap changed under Stannis

- **ADWD 17 (Jon):** Stannis initially proposes an attack on the **Dreadfort** and takes Arnolf's submitted intelligence as helpful. Jon argues the move risks losing his army; the northern route and clans are discussed.
- **ADWD 32 (Theon):** Roose says Arnolf tried to bait Stannis into that Dreadfort trap, but he instead recovered **Deepwood** and gathered northern support. Roose adjusts, moving Ramsay's wedding to Winterfell and planning to force Stannis to advance because he cannot abandon the supposed Ned's daughter.
- **ADWD 35 (Jon):** Stannis's actual letter confirms the Deepwood victory and projected confrontation at Winterfell.
- **ADWD 62 (Asha):** Arnolf has joined Stannis's starving force and argues for a rapid march on Winterfell. The bait can change with terrain, hunger and enemy positioning.

**Strong, bounded inference:** Stannis escaped an **earlier intended Bolton trap** by taking an unexpected route; **the text does not identify his final tactical plan or credit Jon alone** with defeating the trap. A plot in which Arnolf is useful as a prearranged *exposed* double agent must first provide Stannis reliable warning and proof.

## 5. Decision tree: who should Stannis promise Winterfell *if* he survives?

| Provisional political choice | Immediate constituency gained | Immediate constituency at risk | Evidence and reversal condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Keep Arnolf as prospective holder** | His contingent and men already claiming Karstark loyalty; an established northern lineage | Alys/Harrion, loyal Starks, other clans and Manderly if treason emerges | Stannis's ADWD 17 preference is real; **reject** if credible Bolton collusion is exposed or Arnolf's soldiers revolt. |
| **Revisit Jon's declined offer** | Potentially much of Ned's northern following; the king initially believed Jon uniquely useful | Night's Watch, Sansa's right, any living Bran/Rickon, independence-minded Robb loyalists | **Not possible** to enact while Jon's vows and ADWD 69 condition are unresolved; Stannis's title remains a **vassal** grant. |
| **Back a verifiable living Stark child/regency** | Manderly and some northmen may prefer Rickon or another proven heir | Arnolf's party, Baratheon nominees, houses contesting guardianship | **Requires new proof** of survival, a safe regency arrangement, and Stannis's acceptance; Davos's mission isn't fulfilled. |
| **Reward Mors Umber / another northern ally** | Military northmen with direct reason to fight Bolton | Houses wanting an actual Stark, rival claimants, Arnolf's group | Jon recommends Mors (ADWD 17); **Stannis does not grant him Winterfell**. Loyalty and legitimacy remain distinct. |
| **Leave succession provisional / appoint caretaker** | Prevents premature alienation while witnesses are found | Ambitious captains seeking payment, hungry troops, sovereign credibility | Political feasibility depends on feeding a liberated North, not just winning a lake battle. No such caretaker is appointed on-page. |

**Counterargument to “the Stark child obviously wins”:** A minor can be exploited as a hostage, while wartime families value a leader who can actually defend/store food. **Counterargument to “Stannis can pick anyone”:** Holders need acceptance in a region that recently elected an independent king and may find surviving Starks.

## 6. Best-character first scene experiment — if a warning becomes credible

**Do NOT open with Stannis ceremonially stripping Arnolf of Winterfell.** He has never signed away the castle, and he doesn't possess it. The more source-grounded next move is for Stannis to decide **how to handle 450 armed followers whose supposed leader is now under suspicion**, while learning separately whether the rescued girl is Arya.

1. **Asha/Tycho arrive** (ADWD 62 ending). Audience not yet narrated. Tycho may or may not carry a letter from Deepwood.
2. **Theon/Jeyne may expose the marriage fraud**; Theon also heard Roose's separate Frey/Manderly sortie orders. This does *not* by itself expose Arnolf.
3. **If Jon's warning reaches Stannis**, it can be tested against Arnolf's written reports, camp maester/ravens, and military assertions. Stannis must distinguish Arnolf's *family* from hundreds of men who may be sincere.
4. **If Arnolf is exposed**, Stannis must revisit **ADWD 17's actual political preference**, while lacking news of Rickon and the original will. Publicly choosing another “lord” too soon would repeat the same mistake under more desperate conditions.
5. **If no warning arrives**, Stannis may still reject suicidal assault advice and demand credible scouting. Do not give him the reader's knowledge of Roose's ADWD 32 remarks.

**Best working thematic question:** Does Stannis learn to distinguish **the first lord who bends a knee** from **the lord capable of maintaining a legitimate and governable North**? His original interest in Jon shows he understands the latter problem, but his contingent preference for Arnolf shows how quickly his options degrade under lack of reliable news.

## The two Wall defectors are not the field force (2026-10-15)

The [Karstark household loyalty audit](karstark-household-split.md) corrects the assumption that Arnolf's retinue has no demonstrated cracks: **Jon reports two of Cregan's pursuit party sworn to Alys and corroborating the Bolton plot** (ADWD 49). This is crucial *household-level* evidence, but the novel does not transfer those men's allegiances, names, or testimony to Stannis's separate 452 fighting men (ADWD 62). Likewise, Alys's proposed claim depends on whether Harrion is alive, and Karhold's failed harvest makes civil survival an independent settlement problem.
