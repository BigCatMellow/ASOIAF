## Executive result

**A knowledgeable Theon might give Stannis genuine Bolton-side corroboration of Arnolf's treachery, but exposing a commander does not establish the guilt of his 452 fighting men.** Stannis would need to choose between speed, secrecy, justice, force cohesion and the protection of traumatized witnesses. His **previously stated preference to reward Arnolf with Winterfell (ADWD 17)** adds an especially costly political reversal.

**Important evidence correction:** ADWD 32 shows Theon/“Reek” still present **in the room with Roose and Ramsay after everyone else is dismissed**. He hears Roose discuss Arnolf's attempted bait toward the Dreadfort. In ADWD 37, Theon anticipates Arnolf turning on Bolton's signal. **Theon's later thought is consistent with—and may derive from—the ADWD 32 conversation; it is not necessarily a second independent source.** Alys Karstark's report to Jon (ADWD 44) provides a *separate* outside channel for the basic allegation, but details of her Karhold succession motive are her account.

## 3. What an Arnolf arrest would actually confront

**ADWD 62, Asha's eyewitness tally:** Arnolf brings **400 spearmen, 40 archers, 12 mounted lances**—approximately **452 fighting men**—plus **one son, three grandsons, a maester and a cage of ravens**. He supplies **only his own party**. The tally is a count of arms, **not evidence 452 men know the plan or can fight equally well in the snow**.

### Constraint matrix

| Proposed step | What makes it possible | What makes it dangerous / unshown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quietly question Arnolf alone** | Stannis has a guarded watchtower, and Horpe has already physically redirected Arnolf away from it after the burnings (ADWD 62). | The redirect does **not** prove a pre-existing counterintelligence plan or established surveillance. Arnolf might alert his son or demand access. |
| **Restrict the maester's messages** | The maester and raven cage physically accompany Arnolf (ADWD 62). | Training destinations, loyalties and any message traffic are **UNKNOWN**; seizing birds may signal distrust before troops can be reassured. |
| **Separate commanders from soldiers** | Arnolf's son Arthor and grandsons publicly offer aggressive siege ideas (ADWD 62), so some identifiable family leaders exist. | No independent Karstark lieutenant is shown prepared to take over. **Arthor's bellicosity is not direct proof he knows of Roose's pact.** Stannis cannot assume men obey a stranger. |
| **Reassign the contingent to a reserve/support role** | King can issue orders to his own coalition and need not accept suicidal siege proposals. | A sudden reassignment might be read as dishonor, could fail under blizzard conditions, and could leave a useful fresh force idle while Stannis's others starve. |
| **Publicly accuse Arnolf** | Theon and a delivered Alys warning could offer **partly independent channels** supporting the basic allegation. | Theon is branded turncloak; Alys has familial motives; Arnolf can appeal to northern pride. | **Execute or imprison the family** | If Stannis accepts proof of treachery, legal punishment fits aspects of his public ethos. | Punishing nonparticipating sons/grandsons or rank and file without evidence risks injustice and open armed defection. The source does **not** tell who signed what. |

**Internal camp constraint:** Asha sees northmen and southron knights divided along a central fire trench in the longhall. She also witnesses tension between Justin Massey and Richard Horpe, the latter questioning another knight's faith. Asha and Theon are vulnerable captives; Jeyne is a traumatized girl. A command split is not a painless administrative reassignment inside a harmonious host.

## 4. Three conditional Stannis decisions, with consequences and rejection tests

### Branch A — Quietly remove the senior plotters, preserve the men

**If sufficient proof is obtained**, Stannis confines Arnolf and requests an accounting from Arthor and the grandsons. He publicly states that men are not condemned for crimes they did not know of, but forbids the maester's dispatches pending review.

- **Plausibility:** Directly addresses a leader-level betrayal while protecting sorely needed northern arms. An actual kingly order may bind his closest guards; source does **not** guarantee Karstark obedience.
- **Human cost:** Sons and grandsons may be guilty, loyal to Arnolf, or merely frightened; the king risks punishing by blood association. Witnesses Theon/Asha/Jeyne may become targets for retaliation.
- **Military cost:** A badly timed confrontation could cause hundreds to seize their stores or ride away. Without trusted alternate officers, central orders might fail.
- **Rejection test:** Do not write an overnight bloodless disarmament or a newly invented independent Karstark officer already loyal to Stannis. Show **how** access, custody, announcements and food operate.

### Branch B — Know or suspect the plot, but keep Arnolf under controlled observation

**If evidence is plausible but operationally inconvenient**, Stannis declines a public accusation, rejects Arnolf's insistence on marching, quietly tests troop assignments and information channels, and waits for an opportunity to separate the family from combat-critical positions.

- **Plausibility:** Maintains nominal cohesion; does not require instant acceptance of one disgraced witness's statements.
- **Human cost:** Deliberately leaves a suspected plotter close to his own people; a leak may endanger Theon and Jeyne. Concealment from loyal captains could also undermine their trust if uncovered.
- **Military cost:** An embedded family can still strike, flee or relay camp movements, and the army is starving. Counterintelligence does not generate food or undo Boltons' sorties.
- **Rejection test:** This is **not** automatic confirmation of a masterfully designed Night Lamp. No five-book passage states Stannis has prebuilt decoy beacons or used the fishing holes for a trap.

### Branch C — Reject the accusation publicly as unproved; keep the coalition

**If only weak, inconsistent or late reports reach him**, Stannis regards Theon as compromised by years of Ramsay's coercion or treats reports as insufficient; he may challenge Arnolf's siege advice for practical reasons but keep the men intact.

- **Plausibility:** Does not demand that a rational king trust an erstwhile betrayer who previously killed Winterfell's boys (as Stannis may believe), or a distant Alys letter of uncertain provenance.
- **Human cost:** Theon may retreat into silence; Jeyne can be exploited as false Arya; Asha remains a prisoner with limited influence.
- **Military cost:** The original Bolton-side strategy may succeed if the right signal arrives. Conversely, Arnolf could be less able to betray than Roose anticipates, or other lords may independently detect problems.
- **Rejection test:** Do not give Stannis private access to Roose's dialogue unless Theon recounts it. Do not force him to comply with Arnolf's suicidal immediate assault merely because he doubts the accusation.

**Alternate challenge common to all:** Bolton could have overstated Arnolf's reliability even to Ramsay. Theon might be right about intent but mistaken about execution; a planned betrayal can fail because rank-and-file soldiers refuse it, communications fail, or snow buries the signal.

## 5. Sequence gates that prevent convenient authorial omniscience

1. **Audience gate:** In ADWD 62 Tycho, Theon and Jeyne arrive at **Asha**, not at a narrated Stannis interview. Theon must become safe enough and willing to speak; Asha's recognition may make access easier but doesn't mandate it.
2. **Knowledge gate:** Theon must specify **where he was when Roose spoke**. A deposition relying only on his ADWD 37 interpretation is weaker than testimony that he heard Roose himself (ADWD 32).
3. **Proof gate:** If the Alys warning arrived via Deepwood, its provenance must be shown. The two incoming black-clad riders' identities and letters are currently unknown.
4. **Identity gate:** Jeyne can refute Bolton's claim to Arya's marriage, but whether Stannis announces that truth and to whom is a separate ethical and military decision.
5. **Security gate:** A public accusation must account for Arnolf's relatives, armed household, maester and provisions; **neither 452 traitors nor 452 willing defectors is an observed fact**.
6. **Field gate:** Roose's orders in ADWD 51 send Freys and Manderlys out of separate gates. They are *orders*, not eyewitness battle positions. Stannis can't plan around guaranteed arrival times he has not learned.
7. **Political gate:** Stannis's **ADWD 17** preference to grant Arnolf Winterfell is not a completed award, and his claim to appoint a **Lord/Warden** remains incompatible with an independent **King in the North** under Robb's possible will.

## 6. Most defensible first-scene experiment (not adopted)

A **Theon POV** could start from the pain and terror of arriving with Jeyne, then require him to choose whether to reveal not only the Bolton girl's real name but **what Roose said about Arnolf**. He knows that naming one betrayal may open questions about his own. Asha could recognize and ask for his protection, but no guaranteed private audience exists yet.

A second scene, **seen through Asha**, could show the consequences of what a captor believed without giving us Stannis's inaccessible thoughts. Horpe's control of the watchtower and the longhall factions become visible; the reader can't know whether a quiet reassignment is caution or proof that the king already knew.

**Counterexample to this favored shape:** Theon may refuse to speak and a real Jon/Deepwood messenger may bring the warning first, or Stannis may act on practical objections to Arnolf's advice without accusing him. Both are equally legal branches in the evidence.

## Source correction — the Wall escort *has* split (ADWD 49)

Jon directly tells Cregan in ADWD 49 that two of the pursuer's men have **pledged to Alys** and corroborated the Arnolf–Ramsay arrangements. This is **reported testimony**, not a second POV scene, and the men remain unnamed. It establishes a *real, small-group fracture* and a possible source of future communication; it does **not** establish that even one of Arnolf's **452** has turned at Stannis's camp. See the [household loyalty and food-scarcity dossier](karstark-household-split.md). Alys's actual succession remains contingent on Harrion's status; her belief that Karhold's gates would open is not proof.

## 7. Character and structural payoff

- **Theon:** His knowledge becomes useful in a way that cannot erase his responsibility for the miller's boys or his assistance in Jeyne's escape. He must decide what truth to tell under fear.
- **Asha:** Distinguishes brotherly care from blind loyalty; may seek limited guarantees but still faces her own captors.
- **Stannis:** Must reconcile a preference for legal punishment with the political cost of losing fresh troops and the ethical problem of punishing ordinary men for their leader's choices.
- **Jeyne:** Her safety must matter even if her forged Stark identity is politically useful; Stannis must decide if his cause protects an exploited child or merely trades her between rulers.
- **Arnolf:** He is not an abstract traitor token; he stands at the intersection of Karhold inheritance, Winterfell patronage and troop command. Exposing one claim threatens the others.

**Working research preference:** **Branch B** is the highest-tension character experiment **if** Theon speaks or a warning reaches Stannis; it lets suspicion shape military behavior without asserting a flawless trap or a clean purge. **Branch A** is also plausible when sufficient proof/secure guards exist. **Branch C** must remain available if warnings are delayed or disbelieved. **No outcome chosen for *Winds*.**
