# The Winds of Winter — forecast confidence register

- Record role: `RESEARCH / GARDENING SYNTHESIS`.
- Status: `PROVISIONAL / NOT AN OUTLINE`.
- Purpose: say how far the five published books permit prediction without confusing a strong immediate pressure with a settled outcome.
- Related: [trajectory branches](winds-trajectory-branches-first-pass.md), [branch continuity tests](winds-trajectory-branch-continuity-tests.md), and [adaptation evidence](../adaptation/game-of-thrones-endpoint-evidence.md).

## Scale

`HIGH` means the next conflict/decision is directly staged in the published text, not that its result is known. `MEDIUM` means a causally supported development needs one or more unshown bridges. `LOW` means a familiar theory/adaptation destination lacks the necessary book-specific bridge. This is a confidence in **forecasting category**, never a numerical chance of an ending.

## Frequently proposed endpoints that remain low-confidence

| Proposed result | Confidence | Missing evidence bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A particular author of the Pink Letter | `LOW` | The book does not identify author, route, or truth of its military claims. |
| Aegon biologically proved Blackfyre or Targaryen | `LOW` | Political advocacy/history are not biological verification. |
| Stannis immediately sacrifices Shireen | `LOW` as timing/location forecast | A public Martin-reported possibility does not provide the book's command, custody, travel, or causal sequence. |
| Dragonbinder controls a dragon | `LOW` | No dragon response is depicted; its claimed rules are disputed. |
| Daenerys burns King's Landing / Jon kills her / Bran is king | `LOW` as book forecasts | These are television outcomes or reported endpoint discussions without the published-book mechanisms, choices, and aftermaths required to adopt them. |
| A quick Stark reunion or unified northern restoration | `LOW` | The children are separated, identities are concealed, and each information/travel path remains independent. |

## What would make a genuine forecast responsible

1. First, forecast the **next choice**, not the end state.
2. Name what the chooser knows, including contradictory reports.
3. Show a carrier and receiver for any cross-theater fact.
4. Give a serious ordinary explanation before crediting prophecy, secret identity, or a grand conspiracy.
5. Keep alternate outcomes alive until a new depicted observation distinguishes them.

## Current answer to “where is the story going?”

The books strongly support a *Winds* phase in which local institutions fail or improvise before reliable convergence: the Wall, a snowbound royal army, Meereen's wartime government, an understrength invasion, and a leaderless capital all reach decisions before their principals can share information. They do **not** yet support a single definitive endgame route. The most defensible next step is therefore a small set of character-led branches, tested against these handoffs—not one fixed prediction or a television-derived outline.

Independent challenge remains open.
