## Bottom line — the marriage is public, but the route to Stannis isn't

**ADWD 49 directly puts Queen Selyse, Melisandre, Old Flint, the Norrey and their escorts at Alys Karstark's wedding to Sigorn of Thenn.** Jon later tells Cregan that **two of his own small pursuit party** have sworn to Alys and corroborated her account of Arnolf's plans with Ramsay.

These facts create **plausible, distinguishable social and political paths** for news to reach Stannis. **No five-book scene depicts those specific post-wedding facts reaching him.** Jon's earlier (or already-dispatched) warning through Clydas to **Deepwood Motte** is verified as *sent* but neither its full text nor receipt is narrated. Because that text isn't shown, **do not assert that it did or did not contain the two men's later testimony** merely from the order of narrative paragraphs.

**New material complication:** The Karstarks have repeatedly fractured: almost 300 mounted men quit Robb in ASOS 20; Bolton separately commanded some Karhold men (ASOS 37, 49); Cregan's escort splits at the Wall (ADWD 49); Arnolf later brings an independently described ~452-man contingent to Stannis (ADWD 62). **These are not a single continuous, identifiable regiment.**

### How this changes the historical story

Rickard's **ASOS 20** bounty put his daughter into military politics as an **award for killing Jaime**. Years later Alys acts to escape a **coerced marriage** by Cregan and establishes a new alliance through marriage to Sigorn. The same family uses her marriageability for opposite purposes: **commodity for revenge / tool for seizure** versus **her chosen defensive coalition**. This is a character-level causal link, not merely a new genealogical claim.

Robb's decision to execute Rickard despite losing ~300 fighters makes loyalty to the young Stark king **costly and contested**. Later northern appeals to Ned/Robb by Arnolf are politically usable rhetoric (ADWD 62), **not evidence his entire force forgave Rickard's execution**.

The **Thenns' 200 fighters** are visibly at the Wall with Sigorn, and the wedding gives them an incentive to help Alys. For them to become militarily relevant beyond the Wall, the continuation needs a route, food, command arrangements, and acceptance by ordinary Karhold residents.

## 3. Who witnessed the wedding and what did that signify?

### Selyse / Melisandre

The queen participates in the rite; Melisandre officiates according to **R'hllor**. Jon is central in escorting Alys and forming the arrangement; the queen is not shown consulting Stannis first. **Public association with the Baratheon court** makes the marriage more than a clandestine escape, but **cannot turn the wedding into a signed Baratheon grant of Karhold**.

The ritual poses a problem for northerners who honor the **old gods**. Northern lords might recognize the couple's protective alliance without accepting a new faith, or reject both. The source does not show a binding ruling on which marriage law controls Karhold.

### Old Flint and the Norrey

Jon remarks that both are too old to march with Stannis and have sent younger kin; they attend and drink without throwing away wine. **Jon explicitly considers two interpretations:** tentative tolerance of the match, **or simply reluctance to waste good southron wine**. Do not transform their attendance into an oath of support for Alys, an endorsement of R'hllor, or a guaranteed route for letters to the army.

Their kin in Stannis's camp make the marriage a **possible intergenerational political message**, if and only if a traveler connects them. A credible scene could have one younger chief learn from a trusted elder and demand a private audience with Stannis; a scene in which *all* clans immediately know and acclaim Alys would skip several factual gates.

### Jon and Sigorn's visual legitimacy

The Thenns have no established noble-style heraldry; Jon arranges a **new bronze disk surrounded by red flames**, which echoes the **white Karstark sunburst** on Alys's maiden cloak (ADWD 49). The heraldic adaptation is **observable bridge-building**, not proof the Thenns become a recognized northern house by universal law.

Jon thus makes a political decision at the Wall while stating elsewhere that **marriages and inheritance are matters for the king** (ADWD 44). The tension matters: he protects a vulnerable person and creates a solution that **Stannis has not endorsed on-page**, while Stannis has expressed interest in awarding **Winterfell** to Arnolf (ADWD 17). Their separate powers can collide if/when news arrives.

## 5. Three practical, reversible continuation scenarios

**C1 — News arrives through a named witness or reliable relay.**

A traveler from Deepwood or a royal envoy shows Stannis that **Selyse witnessed Alys's marriage** and **Jon reported two men deserting Cregan**. The king can now demand verification of Arnolf's family arrangement and question who actually speaks for Karhold. 
- **Cost:** Selyse's apparent blessing of a Thenn union may upset Stannis's political bargain with old-gods northerners; threatening Arnolf risks losing his field men or food.
- **Rejection test:** No unnamed magically instantaneous messenger; the courier must have been in position to hear **these specific facts**, and Stannis must react to the actual message, not all reader-held facts.

**C2 — Theon reports Bolton evidence, but Alys's marriage remains unheard.**

Stannis can question Arnolf or guard him based on Theon's ADWD 32 firsthand account, while **having no reason to know a lawful claimant has Thenn backing and two reported defectors at the Wall**.
- **Cost:** Detaining the Karstark leader creates a governance vacuum; the king cannot conjure an alternative commander just because an alternate heir exists hundreds of miles away.
- **Rejection test:** Do not install Alys in Karhold or appeal to her marriage until a new communication channel exists.

**C3 — News is distorted, delayed or treated as hostile propaganda.**

An inaccurate report reaches the camp claiming the Night's Watch married a highborn Karstark girl to a foreign-god savage. Arnolf can use that version to rally relatives or invoke the memory of Robb's killing of Rickard; equally, other northern families might support Alys's right to avoid coercion.
- **Cost:** A useful defensive marriage may unintentionally fracture the northern coalition and deepen tensions between the Watch and Stannis.
- **Rejection test:** The distorted messenger needs a plausible identity and incentive; no existing chapter states Arnolf already possesses such a report, and no northern chief has reacted to it at the lake.

**Counterexample to all three:** The enemy arrives before any news does. The contingent's immediate decisions could be governed by hunger, fear and Arnolf's orders rather than genealogical argument; later postbattle diplomacy would then carry the marriage's significance.

## 6. Character consequences distinct from a 'hidden clever plan'

- **Alys:** A choice to reject forced marriage and seek a protective ally is already narrated; do not reduce her to proof that Stannis can win an ice battle. She also cares about the survival of hungry Karhold civilians.
- **Jon:** Opens a path toward integrating Thenns into a recognized social order while creating a politically sensitive act outside conventional Watch neutrality. His fall at the end of ADWD makes further communication and command less assured.
- **Sigorn:** His 200 Thenns are an asset of their own people, not interchangeable with a Baratheon garrison. The text shows public marriage, not that every Thenn has signed up to inherit Karhold.
- **Selyse:** Has witnessed the match and possesses a different religious constituency from old-gods clan chiefs. **Her attendance is not a permission slip from Stannis**.
- **Old Flint/Norrey:** Can mediate between Wall and army if a route is earned, but attendance should remain ambiguous as the POV itself treats it.
- **Stannis:** His stated preference for Arnolf and his strong belief in his own kingship conflict with Jon's act if he hears both. He could accept a regency, contest the marriage's implications, or prioritize military survival; no version is forced by canon.
- **Arnolf's soldiers:** They have not spoken individually in a POV. Some may remember Rickard's execution; others may prefer supplies or obey their paid commander. A meaningful split requires **one actual character to choose disobedience**, not a document declaring wholesale revolt.

## 8. After the wedding: Flint and Norrey are skeptical of Free Folk settlement (ADWD 53)

**New source test:** In **ADWD 53 (Jon)**, Jon brings **Old Flint (Torghen Flint) and the Norrey** onto the Wall, alongside Bowen Marsh, Yarwyck, Clydas, Septon Cellador and Leathers, to discuss the arrival of Tormund's people. The very chiefs who attended Alys's wedding in **ADWD 49** challenge the breadth of Jon's policy:

- **Observed skepticism:** The Norrey objects to allowing wildlings onto *his* lands; Old Flint threatens to punish any who leave Watch-controlled holdings. Both distrust the Weeper. Their objections include **land, food, security, and memory of northern violence**, not merely disdain for a R'hllor ceremony.
- **Safeguard dispute:** Jon offers assurances about garrisons in abandoned Wall castles and **a hundred young hostages** from wildling leaders. Old Flint invokes former Winterfell practice of keeping wards/hostages; the Norrey recalls that some who disappointed the Kings of Winter returned without their heads. These are **characters' appeals to a brutal precedent**, not proof Jon will execute children.
- **Implication for Karhold:** **Attending Alys/Sigorn's wedding ≠ embracing large-scale Free Folk immigration or promising that Thenn soldiers will rule northern civilians.** The same elder can treat the marriage as a tolerable emergency alliance yet oppose settlements by other wildlings. This makes a future northern dispute **more** plausible while preventing us from declaring blanket hatred or unanimous acceptance.
- **Message constraint unchanged:** Their **second recorded meeting with Jon** proves ongoing contact with the Watch but still does **not** depict them writing to sons/grandsons at Stannis's lake camp or forwarding testimony about Arnolf.

**Conditional character test:** If younger Norrey/Flint fighters learn of the marriage, they might endorse Alys's protection **while insisting** on concrete limits governing Sigorn's 200 Thenns. That would create a real negotiation over hostages, food, oaths, religion and rights to land. No such negotiation at the lakes is narrated.

## 9. Two overlooked uncertainties: unreliable Karstark counts and a surviving hostage

- **ASOS 20 (Catelyn):** Nearly **300 mounted Karstarks** desert Robb and twice as many mounts vanish. **ASOS 37 (Jaime):** Roose claims **a thousand Karstarks** still range near the Riverlands looking for Jaime. Different times, exaggeration and other detachments could explain the numerical gap. We must not silently convert either to the exact size of Arnolf's later **452**-fighter field force.
- **ADWD 69 (Jon):** **Cregan is still alive in Castle Black's custody** late in the fifth book, behaving abusively in his ice cell. Jon orders him moved to the **Lord Commander's Tower undervault** to avoid his smothering in buried cells; guards haul him out. His imprisonment thus remains an active hostage/legal problem just as Jon's own leadership becomes fatally contested. Nothing shows Cregan already released, traded to Stannis, or suddenly able to issue orders at Karhold.

**Research implication:** These facts enlarge the political story beyond a single messenger. Even after a warning, Stannis needs viable troops; Jon's coalition must handle fiercely independent northern chiefs; and Cregan remains a living rival whom a future party might use in bargaining. All three still require actual travel, messages or decisions before their plots can intersect.
