## Result and necessary correction to an easy reconstruction

**Confirmed by Roose's own speech, with an independent earlier prisoner sighting:** **ASOS 37 (Jaime)** has Roose state that **Harrion Karstark was captive in Harrenhal when Bolton's force took the castle**, and that Roose gave Harrion the Karhold soldiers still with him and sent him with **Robett Glover**. **ACOK 30 (Arya)** had earlier noticed a young prisoner in black with **white suns**, consistent with Harrion's Karstark identity; **AGOT 62 (Tyrion)** independently records his original capture at the Green Fork. Thus there is a strongly grounded *specific* Harrenhal prisoner → Bolton subordinate-command claim, not merely a generic imagined release after the castle changed hands.

**But this is not proof that Wylis Manderly had exactly the same exit.** In **ACOK 30** Arya also knows of a fat, bushy-moustached prisoner wearing a silver-and-sapphire **trident** clasp, strongly consistent with Wylis; critically, she learns he **belongs to Tywin**, whereas the Karstark-cloaked prisoner was taken by a **hedge knight** hoping for ransom. **No released-Wylis conversation, exchange receipt, escort, or appointment by Roose is ever narrated** between ACOK 30 and Roose's **ASOS 49** statement that Wylis commanded the exposed Trident rear guard.

**A second class of prisoners must not be collapsed into these Green Fork captives:** the hundred-plus Northern men brought **later** into Harrenhal in chains by **Vargo Hoat**, headed by **Robett Glover and Aenys Frey**, are lodged **beneath the Widow's Tower** in **ACOK 47 (Arya)**. This appears to be part of the deception by which Vargo and northern conspirators remove Amory Lorch, rather than the original Green Fork highborn group on parole in the **Tower of Dread**.

## 1. Prisoner group A: Green Fork highborn men held by competing captors

- **AGOT 62 (Tyrion), `REPORTED`:** Marbrand names **Harrion Karstark**, **Ser Wylis Manderly**, **Lord Cerwyn**, and **four Freys** as casualties of capture; he does not document the custodian, ransom right or prison site for each man.
- **ACOK 30 (Arya), `POV-OBSERVED/HEARS ABOUT`:** Tower of Dread's upper chambers house approximately **three dozen** Green Fork captives, most allowed freedom inside Harrenhal on promises not to flee. A rich warlord's castle was therefore neither a single sealed dungeon nor a uniform, unguarded prison camp.
- **Different property interests, `REPORTED TO ARYA`:** the **trident-clasp, very fat** Northern man (strongly identified as **Wylis**, `INFERRED`) is said to belong to **Lord Tywin**; the **black cloak with white suns** man (strongly identified as **Harrion**, `INFERRED`) was taken by a **hedge knight intending profit from ransom**. This is especially important because one cannot assume Tywin's high-value hostage was automatically at Roose's disposal when the castle changed hands.
- **Frey contrast, `POV-OBSERVED / NARRATED`:** among the highborn captives are **three Frey brothers and a bastard half-brother** who train together; **two more Freys come under a peace banner with money** and ransom those four, and all six depart. **No equivalent Northern ransom is shown then.** These four are distinct from the later convoy's **Ser Aenys Frey** unless separate evidence joins the identities.
- **Cerwyn, `POV-OBSERVED/IDENTIFIED BY GUARD`:** Lord Cerwyn is confined to a sickbed and subsequently dies; Arya sees his battle-axe-marked shroud taken by the silent sisters. A **Cerwyn battle-axe on one of the later Hoat convoy's men** does not resurrect or re-identify the dead lord.
- **`UNKNOWN`:** Whether Tywin transported Wylis when he left Harrenhal, whether any Northern hostages stayed in the Tower of Dread after that departure, whether they negotiated release with the new governor, and whether the individual captors' ransom rights remained enforceable.

### Prisoner counts are not additive across these episodes

The roughly **36** Green Fork highborn captives are described during Tywin's occupation; the later **more than 100** Northern prisoners in Hoat's convoy are presented after Tywin's army leaves. Different date, custodianship, tower and stated battle origin. A calculation that adds these groups to assert an original 136 captives at the Green Fork (or a mass rescue of all 136 by Bolton) is invalid.

## 2. Prisoner group B: the Hoat convoy as an operational deception

**ACOK 47 (Arya), `POV-OBSERVED`:** Vargo's group returns to Lannister-held Harrenhal with **Robett Glover, Ser Aenys Frey** and over a hundred apparent Northern prisoners, bound and many visibly battered. Vargo **claims** he ambushed and defeated Roose's army and took its commanders. **Ser Amory Lorch**, whom Tywin left as castellan, sends the men to the **cell beneath the Widow's Tower**, expressly refuses to honor Vargo's supposed promise of fair treatment, and threatens to kill resisters.

Arya seeks the release of those men, and Jaqen exploits the situation with Rorge and Biter. The prisoners rush to fight Lorch's garrison and Hoat's company attacks it too. On the same day **Roose takes Harrenhal as new lord**, his banner and the Stark direwolf replace Lannister colors, and **Glover and Aenys appear freshly cleaned and clothed alongside Roose**.

**Interpretive boundary:** Vargo's *story* of a decisive victory over Roose is contradicted by the immediate Bolton takeover. At least a **coordinated change of sides and exploitation of a prisoner pretext** is a strong `INFERRED` explanation. Nevertheless **not everyone in the bound group must have known the plot in advance**. Its ropes, wounds and Lorch's menacing reception are narrated; whether specific rank-and-file were initially genuine captives, voluntarily accepted a ruse, or were deceived themselves is `UNKNOWN`. Similarly, Arya knows she enlisted Jaqen to free them; she does **not** narrate a prior planning meeting with Roose, Hoat, Glover and Aenys.

**Strategic consequence:** a prisoner transport **creates access to a fortified castle**; a threatened prisoner group becomes its **fighting garrison replacement**. This is independently important as a pattern of *custody, intelligence and changing command* in Martin's Northern politics; it does not itself prove the later Trident rear guard was a sacrificial deployment.

## 3. The specific Harrion chain and its limits

| Transition | Evidence | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harrion captured at Green Fork | Addam Marbrand speaking to Tywin (AGOT 62) | `REPORTED` to a Tyrion POV; straightforward named Northern prisoner |
| Harrion likely seen on parole at Harrenhal | Young, bearded man in black cloak with white suns (ACOK 30) | `INFERRED IDENTIFICATION`, very strong together with AGOT 62 and ASOS 37, but Arya does not know his name |
| Harrion still prisoner when Roose takes Harrenhal | Roose to Jaime (ASOS 37) | `CHARACTER CLAIM` about custody; aligns with earlier independent Arya scene |
| Harrion receives remaining Karhold men and is dispatched with Glover | Roose to Jaime (ASOS 37) | `CHARACTER CLAIM` of command transfer and dispatch; **not an original writ, independent departure witness or proof of final arrival** |
| Glover/Tallhart later head toward Duskendale | Roose's directly witnessed instruction in ACOK 64, Arya's observation the force leaves | `DIRECT SPEECH / POV OBSERVED` for **general mission**, not independent proof Harrion was among its actual ranks |
| Disaster at Duskendale | Report and Robb's response (ASOS 35) | `REPORTED DEFEAT`; **Harrion's precise individual fate beyond Roose's stated fears not certified here** |

Roose's speech to Jaime is unusually revealing. Roose knows **which former highborn captive** was in the castle when captured; asserts authority to **redistribute the remaining Karhold men** to that prisoner; and then muses darkly about Harrion potentially dying at Duskendale, which would leave **Alys Karstark** as the last surviving child of Rickard. That observation reveals awareness of **dynastic vulnerability**, not a direct admission he intentionally arranged Harrion's death. Moreover, **Rickard Karstark was still alive when Harrion's earlier Green Fork capture occurred**, and Roose's later speech is after the political situation had changed. Avoid reading a fixed, fully formed assassination plan into the first capture.

**Important qualifier:** Roose also knows Harrion was a prisoner **at the moment he took the castle**, but this does not establish he physically opened the cell, negotiated with the hedge knight, or personally marched Harrion out. The witnessed Duskendale direction and Roose's later reported Karstark reassignment strengthen an operational connection without certifying every transfer step.

### Harrion's next known political trace: Maidenpool report, not a verified casualty

[Harrion's post-Duskendale hostage and succession audit](harrion-karstark-maidenpool-hostage-succession-verification.md) adds a later, **explicitly stale** report. **ADWD 44 (Jon)** records Alys saying Harrion was a **prisoner at Maidenpool** when the family last heard, **almost a year earlier**. This goes beyond Roose's conjecture about Duskendale in ASOS 37, but it is neither a witnessed capture/transfer nor proof of current life. **ADWD 49** records Cregan's contrary death assertion without evidence. Alys also alleges Arnolf hopes pro-Stannis declarations will cause Lannisters to kill Harrion; **ADWD 32** independently supports Arnolf–Bolton collusion but not an observed death order. This is a *different period and prisoner chain* from the Harrenhal exit documented here.

## 4. Wylis: the contrast case rather than Harrion's presumed duplicate

For **Wylis**, the available endpoints are `AGOT 62 GREEN FORK CAPTURE`, `ACOK 30 TYWIN-HELD LIKELY PRISONER`, `ASOS 49 ROOSE REPORTS TRIDENT REAR-GUARD WYLIS`, `AFFC 27 JAIME FINDS WYLIS HELD AFTER THE RIVER`, and `ADWD 29 WYMAN SAYS HIS SON RETURNED`.

In contrast to Harrion, **there is no**:
1. Roose statement naming Wylis as someone he liberated when Harrenhal fell;
2. intercepted or received ransom letter showing how the first custody ended;
3. first-person Wylis report of a release, re-enlistment, troop allocation or ford order.

Even if Wylis physically remained in Harrenhal on Tywin's departure, **Roose might have freed him, exchanged him, paroled him, permitted him to depart, or inherited an already changed custody position**. The books do not choose among those mechanisms. The fact he was later captured **by Gregor** does not imply Gregor was working with Roose during his first release.

**More useful than a single theory:** compare two sons/officers whose initial capture was co-reported at the Green Fork but whose later **custody and command** are documented at different epistemic levels. Roose tells Jaime about Harrion's reassignment; Roose tells Catelyn/Robb about Wylis's defeat; **neither account is an independent eyewitness's explanation of his own release**.

## 5. Command incentives, agency, and tests against over-reading

| Reading | Text supporting it | Serious counterargument / missing bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pragmatic remobilization of Northern captives** | Roose says he reallocated Karhold troops to Harrion (ASOS 37); Glover and Aenys emerge in a Bolton-held castle (ACOK 47). | Northern commanders may genuinely be needed to lead their own banners; retaking Harrenhal restores military capacity as well as Bolton leverage. |
| **Preserving outward legitimacy while exposing rivals** | Wounded/outnumbered rival houses lose officers; Harrion sent toward lethal Duskendale despite Roose's awareness of dynastic stakes (ACOK 64; ASOS 37). | No first-person Harrion statement that he understood the risk or had the option to refuse, nor proof Roose foresaw Tarly's ambush when directing Glover/Tallhart. |
| **A prisoner's service compelled by previous captivity or ransom debt** | Different captors control hostage value; Harrion moves from prisoner to serving under Roose's purported order (ACOK 30; ASOS 37). | The books contain **no documented bargain or coercive oath** tying Harrion's freedom to compliance with Duskendale orders. |
| **Common Roose mechanism explains Wylis too** | Wylis and Harrion appear together in the Green Fork capture list and Harrenhal highborn hostages (AGOT 62; ACOK 30); both later reenter Northern military episodes. | **Distinct initial captor/property claims**; only Harrion's post-capture reassignment is named. Wylis's first release remains `UNKNOWN`. |
| **Hoat–Bolton raid was planned in detail by all captives** | Staged defeat claim; coordinated opening and Bolton's arrival (ACOK 47). | Neither full advance planning nor universal prisoner knowledge is narrated. |

### The next scene Martin could make politically decisive (not an adopted forecast)

If Harrion ever recounts his service, he can directly address **whether Roose's order reached him** and what he knew before marching. If Wylis recounts the river, he can say **who chose his rear-guard role**. These are *two different witnesses* whose accounts could reinforce or undermine a broader attrition hypothesis. Their separate stories cannot be merged into one omniscient survivor accusation without a credible meeting, message, and decision.

The closer five-book theme is **transfer of practical power after custody changes**: who holds a person, who may ransom them, who can order their companies, what the commanding lord subsequently says about the operation, and who survives to challenge that attribution. That is more securely sourced than a single fixed master plan from the Green Fork onward.

## 6. Open tests

1. **Tywin's departure manifests:** was any Tower of Dread hostage, especially the man with the trident clasp, explicitly transferred with Tywin after ACOK 30? No affirmative five-book line located by Wylis's name; check any anonymous guard or custody description before upgrading.
2. **Donnel Locke:** Tyrion includes him in an exchange proposal (ACOK 25); he later dies at the Twins (**ASOS 51 Catelyn**). His custody exit is not demonstrated by the proposal itself. Can another named source identify how he returned to Northern ranks?
3. **Harrion's exact path:** Roose's ASOS 37 account says he dispatched him with Glover; is there a second identified Karstark field witness or post-Duskendale report of his capture or death? Do not treat Roose's fear or wish as verified casualty.
4. **Wylis release mechanics:** Was Tywin's refusal/condition replaced by a new bargain or did Harrenhal's fall make his first custody unenforceable? Neither is established. Family politics in **ADWD 29** concerns his later captivity; do not conflate terms.
5. **Prisoner group's advance knowledge:** distinguish what Glover, Aenys, Jaqen, Hoat and Bolton each demonstrably knew before Amory Lorch was killed.
6. **Duskendale operational order:** Roose's destination is directly witnessed in ACOK 64; the specific messages read by Tallhart, Glover and Harrion, and their accepted chain of command are not.
7. **Numbers:** the Green Fork's highborn ~36 and Hoat's subsequent 100-plus men are different rosters. No reliable house-by-house counts for their deaths or reinstatements exist here.

**Disposition:** `HARRION HELD AT HARRENHAL AND REASSIGNED—ROOSE'S EXPLICIT ACCOUNT SUPPORTED BY EARLIER IDENTIFICATION`; `WYLIS INITIAL CAPTIVITY RELEASE STILL UNKNOWN`; `TWO DIFFERENT HARRENHAL PRISONER GROUPS`; `FREY RANSOM DIRECTLY NARRATED`; `STAGED HOAT VICTORY CLAIM CONTRADICTED BY BOLTON TAKEOVER`; `DELIBERATE ATTRITION OF RELEASED CAPTIVES STILL UNPROVEN`. Independent critical challenge remains open.
