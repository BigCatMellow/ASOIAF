## 3. Source-rooted narrative seeds (not dictated outcomes)

1. **The consequences of legitimacy.** Aegon's claim is being actively promoted by Varys and the Golden Company. Meanwhile, Cersei's government is destabilized by Varys himself. *Seed:* What does Aegon do when a plausible claim brings him supporters whose expectations conflict? (ADWD, Jon Connington chapter 61; epilogue.)
2. **Northern loyalty is not uniform.** Manderly wants a Stark restored; Stannis seeks a kingdom; the Watch exists under other vows; Theon is politically valuable but widely despised. *Seed:* What happens if a military victory leaves all these interests unresolved? (ADWD, Davos 29; Asha 62; Jon 69.)
3. **Rescue may become exploitation.** Davos must find Rickon to secure military support; Rickon is a child with his own needs. *Seed:* What would Davos do if these conflict? (ADWD, Davos 29.)
4. **A promise can require betrayal.** Brienne's oath to Jaime and her wish to protect Podrick appear to place her in direct conflict. *Seed:* How does she respond when keeping one promise means risking someone else's life? (AFFC, Brienne 42; ADWD, Jaime 48.)
5. **The price of magical power is already visible.** Bran's possession of Hodor is nonconsensual, and his visions culminate in ancient blood sacrifice; Dragonbinder's inscriptions warn of death. *Seed:* Who accepts responsibility for magic's costs? (ADWD, Bran 34; Victarion 63.)
6. **Daenerys's governments are fragile even when her intentions are admirable.** Barristan inherits a plague-stricken siege and unstable administration; Daenerys has been carried into another society's power structure. *Seed:* What sacrifices will Daenerys accept to hold or leave Meereen? (ADWD, Barristan 70; Daenerys 71.)
7. **The Stark children are being claimed through identities.** Sansa lives as Alayne; Arya trains to be 'no one'; Jeyne is forced to impersonate Arya; Rickon's identity could become a political instrument. *Seed:* Who has the right to decide what a person is? (AFFC, Alayne 41; ADWD, Arya 64, Theon 51, Davos 29.)
8. **The truth of prophecy is contested inside the story.** Melisandre cannot fully control or read her visions. Sam learns that the Citadel's attitudes toward prophecy may conceal institutional conflict. *Seed:* How does a character act responsibly on incomplete supernatural knowledge? (ADWD, Melisandre 31; AFFC, Sam 45.)

## 4. Revisions to our earlier speculative outline

**Retain as hypotheses only:** the Night Lamp tactical victory; an Aegon coronation; Daenerys destroying King's Landing; a Trial of Seven; Stannis burning Shireen; the decisive Euron blood ritual; a negotiated settlement with the Others; Jon's resurrection mechanics; Bran's eventual kingship.

**Do not assume as established:** Jon returned to life; Stannis won or lost the Battle of Ice; Davos found Rickon; Aegon captured Storm's End; Tyrion already advised Daenerys; Euron performed a successful supernatural ritual; the Three-Eyed Crow and Bloodraven have been proven distinct; the Long Night was once resolved through treaty.

**Research guardrail:** statements from Martin, sample chapters from *The Winds of Winter*, *Dunk and Egg*, *Fire & Blood*, and community theories are not part of this five-novel evidence baseline. They can be added in a separately marked evidence tier later.

## 5. A gardener's next pass

The next useful unit of work is a set of **character decision sheets**, beginning with Jon, Stannis/Theon/Asha, Davos, Daenerys/Barristan/Tyrion, Aegon/Jon Connington, and Jaime/Brienne/Stoneheart. Each should record:

- last incontrovertible scene and relevant earlier scene references;
- what the character *knows* versus what the reader knows;
- the immediate want, fear, duty, and relationships;
- two or three feasible next actions without a preselected winner;
- likely direct consequences and plausible surprises;
- dependencies on the other point-of-view timelines;
- theory claims marked **supported / plausible / unsupported / contradictory**.

Then write or outline *one* decision at a time, carry consequences into the other sheets, and revise the global trajectory. Do not write backward from an assumed final battle or coronation.
