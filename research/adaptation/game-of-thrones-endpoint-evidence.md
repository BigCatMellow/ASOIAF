# Game of Thrones endpoint evidence — non-canonical planning inputs

- Record role: `RESEARCH / ADAPTATION EVIDENCE`.
- Authority: `ADVISORY / NONE`; television is not book canon and cannot override five-book text.
- Purpose: preserve reported Martin-derived possibilities as tests for a character-led garden, not as required destinations.
- Scope: the completed *Game of Thrones* television series and public statements about its relationship to the unwritten novels.

## Operating rule

The show is evidence of one adaptation’s chosen route. A reported Martin disclosure can raise the priority of a **possibility**, but never establish book fact, book causation, a surviving character, or a required sequence. Any use must first pass the five-book character-knowledge, geography, and institutional-legitimacy tests in `research/reconciliation/`.

## Source tiers

| Tier | What it means | Planning weight |
|---|---|---|
| A | Martin’s own public statement, or a direct on-screen/book fact. | A real constraint or possibility, but still distinguish book mechanism from show mechanism. |
| B | Benioff/Weiss or HBO-adjacent reporting about a Martin meeting. | Useful corroboration; require the exact scope of their statement. |
| C | Actor/media recollection or retrospective commentary. | Lead only; do not build an outcome around it without a book-supported route. |
| D | Viewer inference from the finished show. | Creative prompt only, never evidence of Martin’s plan. |

## What the public record supports

| Candidate endpoint element | Evidence tier / source | What may safely be carried forward | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Some show developments overlap Martin’s books, while much diverges | **A** — Martin’s [July 2022 Not a Blog statement](https://georgerrmartin.com/notablog/2022/07/) says some HBO events will appear, perhaps differently, and identifies omitted book POVs as consequential. | The show is worth checking as a possibility generator. | Beat-for-beat identity, shared chronology, or that a show ending is the book ending. |
| Not every show survivor/dying character will share that fate in the books | **A** — same 2022 Martin statement. | Survival/fate must be re-derived from book conditions. | That the show’s final council, offices, couples, or deaths are default outcomes. |
| Stannis sacrificing Shireen | **B** — Benioff/Weiss described it as one of three future-book shocks Martin gave them; contemporary summary in [TIME](https://time.com/4346408/game-of-thrones-george-r-r-martin-3-twists/). | Treat a Stannis–Shireen sacrifice as a high-priority *book possibility* requiring a book-specific causal route. | That Melisandre, weather, location, timing, consent, military context, or Stannis’s exact show reasoning transfers. |
| Hodor’s name/origin and tragic death | **B** — the same showrunner account identifies the Hodor revelation as Martin-derived. | Bran/Hodor time-and-consciousness material deserves high-priority scrutiny. | That the show’s cave attack, enemy mechanics, exact words, or causal loop are book-confirmed. |
| The third “holy-shit” endpoint event | **B/C** — showrunners said a third endgame shock existed, but public reporting does not make its full book mechanism canonical; later reporting often identifies it with a show ending result. | Keep an explicit unresolved “major endpoint disclosure” slot. | That a public inference identifies the event, or that any particular coronation/result is thereby settled. |
| Bran as king / king-like ending | **C/D** unless supported by a direct Martin statement not presently archived here. | Test whether a future political role for Bran can grow from his five-book knowledge, consent, body, and succession constraints. | That Bran must be monarch, that the show’s election/council exists, or that “Bran” means an unchanged Bran. |
| Daenerys destroying King’s Landing, Jon killing Daenerys, northern secession, Arya westward | **D** as adaptation outcomes. | They can be compared as stress tests for character motivation and logistics. | Martin endorsement, book inevitability, or a license to skip Meereen/Dorne/Aegon/Faith/Stoneheart causal chains. |

## Garden protocol for adaptation material

1. State the relevant show outcome without treating it as book evidence.
2. Identify the closest Tier A/B disclosure, if any.
3. Build at least one book-native causal route and one serious route that does **not** reach that outcome.
4. Check who is alive, informed, geographically able to act, and institutionally empowered in the five-book endpoint.
5. Reject the adaptation result if the garden cannot produce it without breaking established characterization or continuity.

## Immediate applications

- **Stannis/Shireen:** The published books leave Stannis snowbound and Shireen at Castle Black, so any sacrifice needs a delivery/command chain; it cannot be imported from television as an already imminent scene.
- **Bran/Hodor:** The books establish Bran’s coercive Hodor skinchanging and ambiguous time-facing vision, enough to make the reported revelation relevant, but not enough to dictate its setting or result.
- **Final sovereignty:** Any Bran, Sansa, Jon, Daenerys, or council endpoint must compete with the living-claimant and information ledgers, not merely reproduce a final tableau.
- **King’s Landing catastrophe:** Cersei, the Faith, Aegon, Dorne, Tyrells, wildfire, dragons, and Varys remain separate book variables. A television event cannot collapse them into one cause.
